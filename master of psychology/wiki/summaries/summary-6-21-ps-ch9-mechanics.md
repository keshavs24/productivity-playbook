# Pre-Suasion Ch.9: The Mechanics of Pre-Suasion — Summary

**Author:** Robert Cialdini
**Source:** *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade* (2016), Chapter 9
**Phase:** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **The basic mechanism: concepts don't just fire when ready, they fire when *readied*.** When an opener concept (German music, weight) receives attention, closely associated secondary concepts (German wine, substance) become more cognitively accessible — more likely to be attended to, to color perceptions, orient thinking, affect motivations, and change behavior.
2. **Simultaneously, unlinked concepts get *suppressed*.** Concepts not associated with the opener become *less* accessible, making them less likely to influence behavior. This dual mechanism (activation + suppression) explains why pre-suasion is so powerful.
3. **If/when-then plans are the most powerful form of self-directed pre-suasion.** These plans (also called "implementation intentions") work by: (1) installing heightened vigilance for a specific cue, and (2) pre-constructing a strong association between that cue and the desired action. They dramatically outperform simple intentions or action plans. Example: opiate addicts in withdrawal — 0% of controls completed an employment history by day's end, vs. 80% of those who formed an if/when-then plan.
4. **Pre-suasive associations can be manufactured through Pavlovian conditioning.** No pre-existing link is needed. Simply pairing a brand with pleasant images 5-6 times creates associations that produce favorable feelings — both immediately and three weeks later. Subliminal exposure to happy faces before tasting a new drink caused people to consume more and pay three times more.
5. **There are corrective mechanisms, but they require resources.** People can correct for pre-suasive biases when: (a) they are *reminded* of the potential bias (weather question, blatant product placement), or (b) they have the time, energy, and motivation for deliberative analysis. Modern life's complexity, pace, and distraction increasingly prevent these correctives from operating.

## Detailed Notes

### Readied and Waiting

The mechanism is cognitive accessibility. When an opener concept receives attention:
- **Linked concepts rise**: closely associated secondary concepts become more accessible in consciousness, coloring perceptions and orienting behavior
- **Unlinked concepts fall**: non-associated concepts get temporarily decommissioned, losing influence

Evidence from video games:
- Violent games plant aggression-related thoughts, producing aggressive behavior (louder noise blasts to annoyed targets)
- Prosocial games (protecting, rescuing, assisting) plant helping thoughts, producing prosocial behavior (cleaning up spills, volunteering time, intervening in harassment)
- Even violent games can *decrease* aggression — if the game requires cooperation to destroy an enemy (the cooperative element suppresses the violent associations)

### Three Questions About Pre-Suasion's Reach

**How soon?** Pre-suasive effects operate from the earliest stages of development. 18-month-old infants shown photographs of individuals standing close together became three times more helpful — spontaneously and without prompting — because togetherness and helpfulness are fundamentally linked in human minds.

**How far?** The strength of pre-suasion depends on the strength of the association between the opener concept and the desired behavior. Cialdini's anti-littering study: a handbill message about littering was most effective at reducing littering (10%); recycling (close association) was nearly as good (15%); turning off lights (moderately far) was less effective (18%); voting (far) was weak (22%); and an unrelated message about an art museum produced the most littering (25%).

**How manufacturable?** Associations can be *created from scratch* via Pavlovian conditioning:
- Superimposing a beer brand on pleasant activity images 5 times increased favorability
- Superimposing a mouthwash brand on nature scenes 6 times increased favorability — and the effect *grew* over 3 weeks
- Subliminal exposure to happy faces 8 times before tasting a new drink caused subjects to consume more and be willing to pay 3x more
- Participants in none of these studies were aware of the influence

### If/When-Then Plans

The most powerful form of self-directed pre-suasion. Standard intentions fail ~50% of the time because: (1) we forget, (2) we don't recognize opportune moments, and (3) we get derailed by distractions.

If/when-then plans solve all three:
- The "if/when" component installs heightened vigilance for a specific cue
- The "then" component pre-constructs a strong cue-action association
- The plan self-tailors the pre-suasive process: we choose our own cues and our own associations

Key evidence:
- **Epilepsy medication**: Adherence rose from 55% to 79% with if/when-then plans
- **Opiate addicts in withdrawal**: 0% of controls completed an employment history by day's end; 80% of those with if/when-then plans did (drug addicts in active withdrawal!)
- **Dieters**: Chronically unsuccessful dieters ate fewer high-calorie foods and lost more weight after forming plans like "If/when I see chocolate displayed in the supermarket, then I will think of my diet"
- **Superiority over simple intentions**: "I intend to lose 5 pounds" and "I plan to cut down on sweets" are considerably less likely to succeed than if/when-then plans

### Correction: Minding the Gap

People can correct for pre-suasive biases via two mechanisms:

**1. Mere reminders**: Simply being reminded of a potential bias source can eliminate it:
- When interviewers asked "How's the weather?" before a life-satisfaction survey, the sunny-vs.-rainy day bias disappeared — the question reminded people that weather might be coloring their mood
- Blatant product placements in *Seinfeld* actually *reduced* purchase intent (47% for subtle vs. 27% for blatant) because the conspicuousness reminded viewers of the influence attempt

**2. Deliberative analysis**: When people have time, energy, and motivation to think carefully, they can overcome rudimentary biases:
- Camera viewers given *unlimited* time chose the higher-quality camera (67%); those given only 2 seconds per feature chose the one with more (inferior) features (83%)

**The problem**: Modern life increasingly deprives us of corrective resources:
- Information overload, complexity, pace, and smartphone alerts
- Infomercials perform better at night because viewers lack mental energy to resist
- False confessions average 16 hours of interrogation (vs. <1 hour for typical interrogations) — fatigue eliminates corrective analysis
- Sleep-deprived combat artillery teams stop challenging orders to fire on hospitals or civilian targets
