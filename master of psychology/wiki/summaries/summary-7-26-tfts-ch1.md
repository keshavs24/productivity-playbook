# Think Faster Talk Smarter Ch.1: Calm — Tame the Anxiety Beast — Summary

**Author:** Matt Abrahams
**Source:** *Think Faster, Talk Smarter* (2023), Chapter 1
**Phase:** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **The goal is not to eradicate anxiety but to manage it.** A moderate level of stress actually helps performance (Yerkes-Dodson Law). The problem is when anxiety overwhelms our ability to cope.
2. **Anxiety symptoms fall into three categories -- the ABCs:** Affective (mood/feelings), Behavioral (physical symptoms like sweating, shaking), and Cognitive (blanking out, negative self-talk, spotlight effect).
3. **Tackle symptoms first, then sources.** Specific techniques address each symptom category. Combine 3-5 favorites into a personalized Anxiety Management Plan (AMP).
4. **The Anxiety Management Plan (AMP) is the core tool.** Pick techniques that address your specific symptoms, create a memorable acronym (e.g., BOOM, ARC, HSM), and rehearse before speaking situations.

## Detailed Notes

### The ABCs of Speaking Anxiety
- **Affective:** Feeling stressed, pressured, vulnerable, overwhelmed, frightened
- **Behavioral:** Sweating, trembling, racing heart, shallow breathing, jittery speech, flushed face, dry mouth ("plumbing reversal" -- palms get wet, mouth goes dry)
- **Cognitive:** Blanking out, forgetting, fixating on audience judgment, negative self-talk ("I can't do this")

### Techniques for Affective Symptoms
- **Practice mindfulness:** Notice and acknowledge unpleasant feelings without judgment. As Stanford professor S. Christian Wheeler notes, "There's me and then there's this anxious feeling" -- creating psychological distance allows observation without attachment
- **Reframe anxiety as excitement:** Professor Alison Wood Brooks showed that saying "I am excited" out loud before speaking improved performance -- both states involve high alertness, so the reframe is psychologically plausible

### Techniques for Behavioral Symptoms
- **Deep breathing (Rule of Lung):** Inhale to count of 3, exhale to count of 6. Longer exhalation calms the nervous system by reducing CO2. Andrew Huberman confirmed the neuroscience on Abrahams' podcast
- **Slow your movements:** Speech pace tracks with gesture pace. Slow down hand motions, head nods, torso movement, and speech naturally slows
- **Cool your body:** Hold something cold (water bottle, ice) in your hands. Hands are thermoregulators; cooling them reduces blushing and perspiration
- **Reactivate salivary glands:** Chew gum, sip warm water, or suck a lozenge before entering a speaking situation

### Techniques for Cognitive Symptoms
- **Positive self-talk / mantras:** "I have value to add," "I've got this," "It's not about me -- my content is compelling." Professional golfers use similar techniques
- **Rewind to go forward:** If you blank out, repeat what you just said to jog memory. Repetition actually helps audiences too
- **Pose a generic question:** Deflect attention temporarily: "Can you think of ways you can share this with your teammates?" This buys time to recover
- **Get rational:** Ask "What's the worst that could happen?" Most consequences are minor. The [[spotlight-effect]] means people aren't paying as much attention to you as you think
- **Inhale to reduce fillers:** Time exhalation to coincide with the end of sentences/phrases. Since speaking requires exhaling, landing phrases on the exhale forces inhalation at natural pause points, physically preventing "um" and "uh"

### The Anxiety Management Plan (AMP)
Select 3-5 techniques, create a memorable acronym. Examples:
- **BOOM:** Be present oriented, Observe your movements, Oxygenate (exhale 2x inhale), Mantra
- **ARC:** Acknowledge anxiety is normal, Rationalize (worst case isn't bad), Cool yourself down
- **HSM (Stephanie's personal AMP):** Heart (service orientation), Speech (slow gestures, ask questions), Mind (odds of blanking are low)

### Case Study: The Onion Interview
Abrahams recounts being asked in a CEO interview, "If you were an onion and I peeled back the first three layers, what would I find?" Despite going into fight-or-flight, he activated his AMP (deep breath + mantra "I have value to offer"), focused on the onion itself, and delivered a creative answer about empathy and emotional openness. He got the job.
