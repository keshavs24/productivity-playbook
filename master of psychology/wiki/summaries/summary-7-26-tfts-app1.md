# Think Faster Talk Smarter App.1: Going Big on Small Talk — Summary

**Author:** Matt Abrahams
**Source:** *Think Faster, Talk Smarter* (2023), Application #1
**Phase:** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **Use [[what-so-what-now-what]] to structure small talk.** Make a point (What), explain its significance (So What), suggest what the audience might do with this (Now What -- often a question that invites them to contribute).
2. **The First Commandment of Small Talk: Make it about them, not you.** Favor support responses (that deepen what the other person said) over shift responses (that redirect to you). Aim for a 3:1 ratio of support to shift.
3. **Small talk is a team sport, not a competition.** Think "hacky sack," not tennis -- keep the conversational ball aloft together.
4. **Use the "white flag approach" for exits.** Signal your departure is coming, ask one final appreciative question, then gracefully disengage.

## Detailed Notes

### Why Small Talk Matters
Small talk forges connections, tests relationships, builds reputations (warmth and empathy), and reveals shared goals and aspirations.

### Structuring Small Talk with What-So What-Now What
- To initiate: ask three questions mapping to What, So What, Now What
- To respond: provide a What (your point), So What (significance), and Now What (question back to partner)
- Example: "Well, I was originally born in Omaha, but I moved southward until settling in Houston [What]. While my move was for work, I have really enjoyed the opportunity to attend lots of sporting events and eat really good food [So What]. So, I am curious to know, have you ever lived in or visited Texas [Now What]?"

### Support vs. Shift Responses
- **Shift response:** Redirects to you ("Yeah, you wouldn't believe what my neighbor's been putting me through")
- **Support response:** Deepens the other person's story ("What excited you about that?" / "How did you feel when that happened?")
- Rachel Greenwald (professional matchmaker): "Tell me more..." is a simple, generous, empathic act

### Tips for Better Small Talk
1. **Seek comparable self-disclosure** -- don't make it 100% about them; share meaningfully when asked
2. **Avoid putting your partner on the spot** -- use open-ended questions, not interrogation-style direct questions
3. **Be a team player** -- set your partner up to succeed on their next turn (paraphrase, bridge topics)
4. **Avoid swiping left too soon** -- resist scanning the room for "better" conversations; commit to the present one
5. **Minimize offense risk** -- reflect, question, and paraphrase rather than making bold declarations on sensitive topics. Show "conversational receptiveness"
