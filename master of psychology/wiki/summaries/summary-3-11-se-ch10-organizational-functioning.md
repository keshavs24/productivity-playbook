# Ch.10 Organizational Functioning — Summary

**Author(s):** Albert Bandura
**Type:** Book chapter
**Phase:** 3
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-14

## Key Takeaways

1. **Self-efficacy is a robust contributor to career development.** It predicts the scope of career options considered, occupational interests and preferences, enrollment in relevant courses, perseverance in difficult fields, academic success, and the choice of cultural milieu in which to pursue one's career -- verified in stringent tests controlling for actual ability, prior preparation, and level of interest (p.427).
2. **Gender differences in occupational self-efficacy arise primarily from socialization rather than actual ability.** Female students judge themselves more efficacious for traditionally female occupations but have weaker efficacy for male-dominated occupations, despite no differences in actual verbal and quantitative ability on standardized tests (Betz & Hackett, 1981; p.432).
3. **Mastery modeling is the most effective method for developing occupational competencies** -- it combines instructive modeling of subskills, guided skill perfection through practice, and self-directed transfer training. It consistently outperforms lecture-based training and computerized tutorials (pp.440-445).
4. **Perceived managerial self-efficacy directly affects the quality of organizational decision making and performance.** In simulated organizations, managers who believed ability was acquirable maintained resilient efficacy, set challenging goals, used good analytic thinking, and achieved progressively higher organizational productivity -- while those who believed ability was inherent showed deteriorating efficacy, aspirations, thinking, and performance (Wood & Bandura, 1989b; pp.453-454).
5. **Entrepreneurial efficacy predicts venture success** beyond personality traits. The venturers who achieved high growth had a vision, firm belief in their efficacy, set challenging goals, and came up with innovative strategies (Baum, 1994; p.455).
6. **Collective organizational efficacy** is an emergent group attribute -- not simply the sum of individual efficacies -- that predicts group performance, productivity, and satisfaction. Perceived team efficacy is a better predictor of subsequent group performance than general potency (Campion, Medsker, & Higgs, 1993; pp.468-470).

## Detailed Notes

### 1. Career Development and Pursuits (pp.422-439)

**Career choice and development (pp.423-427):**
- Higher perceived efficacy to fulfill educational requirements and job functions widens range of career options considered and increases interest (Betz & Hackett, 1981; Lent et al., 1986; Matsui, Ikeda, & Ohnishi, 1989)
- People rapidly eliminate vocations from consideration on the basis of perceived efficacy, whatever benefits they might hold
- Efficacy beliefs predict career options considered viable when variations in actual ability, prior achievement, and vocational interests are controlled
- Perceived mathematical efficacy contributes more to educational and career choices making use of quantitative skills than does amount of math preparation, level of ability, past achievement, or anxiety (Hackett & Betz, 1989)
- Career development includes milestones of accomplishment along the way; strong belief in personal efficacy to surmount major hurdles contributes to perseverance beyond mastering particular subjects (Lent et al., 1986)
- Perceived efficacy contributes to career choices through effects on interests (Lent et al., 1989), persistence, and performance
- Social cognitive theory posits a reciprocal but asymmetric relationship between perceived efficacy and occupational interests, with efficacy beliefs playing the stronger determinant role

**Comparative tests of theories of career choice (pp.425-427):**
- Perceived efficacy made a unique contribution above ability and past achievement in predicting science career options, enrollment, and perseverance (Lent, Brown, & Larkin, 1987)
- Wheeler (1983): Self-efficacy predicted occupational preferences better than expectancy-value theory; when analyzed together, only efficacy beliefs emerged as predictor
- Lent, Lopez, & Bieschke (1993): Perceived mathematical efficacy contributed to prediction of interests, enrollment in math/science courses, and success after controlling for math ability. Outcome expectations predicted interests and enrollment intentions but not course grades
- Singer (1993): Asian students' career decisions mediated by efficacy beliefs regarding managing career in native vs. host culture

**Exploratory decision making and fulfillment of occupational roles (pp.427-429):**
- Career decision making is not simply picking an occupation but developing facility in solving problems when things are unpredictable
- Perceived efficacy for decisional subskills (gathering information, self-appraisal, planning coherent courses of action) affects occupational indecisiveness (Betz, Klein, & Taylor, 1996; Taylor & Betz, 1983)
- Taylor & Popma (1990): Only perceived decision-making efficacy was a significant predictor of occupational indecision among multiple predictors including locus of control
- Computer-assisted career guidance markedly increases perceived efficacy and reduces indecisiveness (Fukuyama, Probert, Neimeyer, Nevill, & Metzler, 1988)

**Perceived efficacy in employability and reemployability (pp.429-430):**
- Tracy & Adams (1984): Training job-search skills through modeling strategies for making contacts and interviewing bolsters perceived efficacy
- Kanfer & Hulin (1985): Perceived efficacy predicted successful reemployment following job loss beyond search behavior; when efficacy beliefs controlled, search behavior had no effect on reemployment
- Clifford (1988): Unemployed individuals assured in their job-search efficacy were more likely to be reemployed; neither cause of termination nor personality characteristics exerted influence

**Gender differences in occupational self-efficacy (pp.430-436):**
- Wide gender disparities exist in career aspirations and pursuits; women make up approximately half the work force but are largely employed in traditionally female fields
- Betz & Hackett (1981): Male college students judge themselves equally efficacious for traditionally male- and female-dominated occupations; female students judge themselves more efficacious for female-dominated occupations but weaker for male-dominated ones, despite no differences in actual ability
- Gender bias operates in classrooms and homes: teachers tend to center criticisms of boys on disruptive behavior and of girls on academic work, attributing boys' failures to insufficient effort and girls' to deficiencies of ability (Dweck, Davidson, Nelson, & Enna, 1978)
- Perceived mathematical efficacy operates as a significant cognitive mediator of interest and achievement in math (Randhawa et al., 1993; Pajares & Miller, 1994a)
- Path analysis (Fig. 10.1, Hackett, 1985): Gender affects perceived mathematical efficacy through math preparation in high school, mathematical achievement, and masculine gender-role orientation
- When differences in math achievement and efficacy beliefs are controlled, gender makes no independent contribution to vocational interests
- Schunk & Lilly (1984): Female students pursuing self-directed instruction in novel math activities raised their perceived efficacy and math skills to the level of male students when structured for mastery with clear feedback
- Computer skills increasingly gender-typed as masculine (Hess & Miura, 1985; Lockheed, 1985); boys more likely to master computers and regard them as important for futures
- Gist, Schwoerer, & Rosen (1989): Helpful modeling, guided practice, and corrective feedback achieved high computer efficacy regardless of initial belief; participant modeling produced higher efficacy and computer proficiency than tutorial training

**Ethnicity and occupational self-efficacy (pp.436-439):**
- Sociostructural barriers for women and ethnic minorities are similar; access to socioeconomic resources lowers some barriers to occupational aspiration
- Bores-Rangel et al. (1990): Among Hispanic students of migrant farm-working families, the stronger children's beliefs in academic efficacy, the higher their educational aspirations and achievement
- Efficacy beliefs equally predictive of range of occupations considered regardless of ethnicity or level of acculturation (Church, Teresa, Rosebrook, & Szendre, 1992)
- Cannon (1988): A program confirming high scholastic efficacy substantially increased minority student enrollment in colleges

### 2. Mastery of Occupational Roles (pp.439-449)

**Development of competencies through mastery modeling (pp.440-443):**
- Two broad classes of skills: fixed (adopted as modeled) and generative (flexibly orchestrated to fit demands)
- Three-element method for best results: (1) model appropriate occupational skills to convey basic rules and strategies, (2) guided practice under simulated conditions to perfect skills, (3) self-directed transfer to real work situations
- Mastery modeling consistently outperforms lecture-based training and computerized tutorials
- Fig. 10.2 (Gist et al., 1989): Videotaped mastery modeling instills uniformly high sense of efficacy for computer software skills regardless of whether managers begin self-assured or self-doubting; computerized tutorial exerts weaker effects and is especially ineffective for those insecure in their efficacy
- Cognitive modeling (models verbalize thought strategies aloud as they problem-solve) produces higher perceived efficacy and performance than verbal instruction conveying the same information (Gist, 1989)

**Guided skill perfection (pp.443-444):**
- Role rehearsal in simulated situations essential: practice handling problems without fear of mistakes
- Cognitive and behavioral rehearsal combined (Bandura, 1986a; Corbin, 1972; Feltz & Landers, 1983)
- Informative feedback should call attention to successes and improvements, correcting deficiencies constructively
- Corrective modeling (Vasta, 1976) -- subskills not adequately learned are identified and modeled by those proficient -- achieves greatest improvements
- Gist, Bavetta, & Stevens (1990): Guided modeling training augmented with self-management component (anticipating stressors, self-incentives) produced better transfer of negotiation skills to new situations

**Transfer training by self-directed success (pp.444-445):**
- New skills unlikely to persist unless people experience sufficient success using them
- Supervisory skills training: Latham & Saari (1979) videotape modeling program teaching motivation, recognition, correcting poor habits, reducing absenteeism, handling complaints -- supervisors who received mastery modeling training maintained superior skills one year later
- Porras and colleagues: Mastery modeling of supervisory skills improved morale and productivity of organizations (Porras & Anderson, 1981)
- Sales training: Meyer & Raich (1983) salespersons learned through videotaped modeling; 7% increase in commissions vs. 3% decline in control stores; 22% of control store salespersons quit vs. 7% in modeling stores

**Self-efficacy in occupational socialization (pp.445-447):**
- Newcomers who come with a secure sense of efficacy learn more and perform better during training
- Saks (1994, 1995): Longitudinal study of newly hired accountants -- lower technical, educational, and interpersonal efficacy predicted more anxiety during organizational entry; structured guidance helped low-efficacy newcomers but made high-efficacy ones even more anxious
- Organizations that provided mastery experiences, successful coworker models, and encouraging performance feedback enhanced occupational efficacy, predicting occupational success and satisfaction many months later
- Ilgen (1994): Role adoption (performing duties) vs. role construction (adding innovative elements) -- employees with high perceived efficacy perform roles innovatively; those with low efficacy discharge roles conventionally
- Jones (1986): Newcomers' perceived efficacy at entry affects responsiveness to different training orientations

**Efficacious adaptability (pp.448-449):**
- Job rotation, transfers, promotions, and geographic relocations require developing whatever skills are needed
- Employees who have cultivated diverse talents handle occupational transitions better than narrow specialists
- Hierarchically structured systems experience declining opportunities for challenging assignments in later career years, eroding efficacy and leading to occupational obsolescence
- Three types of organizational change requiring different forms of efficacy (Binks & Vale, 1990): (1) reactive efficacy -- recognizing and responding to changes, (2) proactive efficacy -- creating incremental improvements through experimentation, (3) innovative efficacy -- creative innovations producing entirely new products or processes
- A high sense of efficacy fosters innovativeness (Locke et al., 1984)

### 3. Self-Efficacy in Organizational Decision Making (pp.450-460)

**Managerial decision making (pp.450-454):**
- Decision making is neither purely rational utility maximization nor purely self-interested; emotional factors, group norms, and self-evaluative outcomes all figure prominently
- Perceived efficacy determines the slate of options given consideration: people do not regard options in domains of perceived inefficacy as worth considering
- Effective analytic thinking requires a strong sense of efficacy to deploy cognitive resources optimally and remain task-oriented amid organizational complexities
- People who judge themselves inefficacious become self-diagnostic rather than task-diagnostic (M. Bandura & Dweck, 1988)
- Simulated management system (Wood & Bailey, 1985): Managers allocate employees to production subfunctions, set goals, use feedback, and discover complex decision rules over time
- Bandura & Jourden (1991); Bandura & Wood (1989); Jourden (1991); Wood & Bandura (1989b): Managers led to believe that complex decision making is an acquirable skill -- maintained resilient efficacy, set increasingly challenging organizational goals, used good analytic thinking, achieved progressively higher productivity. Managers led to believe ability was inherent aptitude -- showed declining efficacy, lowered aspirations, erratic thinking, and deteriorating performance
- These divergent organizational trajectories were largely driven by altered beliefs of managerial efficacy
- In organizational milieus, low sense of efficacy fosters ascribing blame to the deficiencies of others (Ashton & Webb, 1986)

**Appraising opportunities and managing risks (pp.455-456):**
- Krueger & Dickson (1993, 1994): Managers whose perceived efficacy is raised focus on opportunities worth pursuing; those whose efficacy is lowered dwell on risks to be avoided
- Perceived decisional efficacy affects risk taking through its impact on perceptions of opportunities and threats (consistent with Heath & Tversky, 1991: perceived competence increases willingness to take risks under uncertainty)
- Entrepreneurial efficacy: perceived entrepreneurial efficacy higher in business majors intending to start new ventures (Krueger, 1994); having an entrepreneurial parent raises offspring's efficacy to start own business (Scherer, Adams, Carley, & Wiebe, 1989)
- Baum (1994): Among several hundred firms in the architectural woodwork industry, venturers who achieved high growth had a vision, firm belief in efficacy to realize it, set challenging growth goals, had good technical skills, and came up with innovative strategies. General personality traits bore little relationship to success
- Exaggerated efficacy can lead to [[escalation-of-commitment]]: administrators of investment projects whose perceived decisional efficacy is raised are prone to escalate commitment to previous investments in attempt to recoup losses (Whyte, Saks, & Hook, in press). High efficacy similarly predicts greater investment in unproductive ventures by petroleum geologists exploring for oil in sites producing dry wells (Whyte & Saks, 1995)
- Edison quotation: "The trouble with other inventors is that they try a few things and quit. I never quit until I get what I want" (Josephson, 1959)
- Self-efficacious employees exhibited high initiative regardless of organizational controllability; those of lower efficacy showed little initiative under low controllability but were more enterprising with substantial leeway (Speier & Frese, in press)

**Enhancing resilience to managerial stressors (pp.456-458):**
- Construing managerial capability as acquirable skills removes perceived threat of mistakes, fosters development of potentialities, converts threats into challenges
- Managers subscribing to acquirable-skills view construe substandard attainments as instructive guides for improving competencies and sustain self-efficacious outlook amid difficulties (Jourden, 1991; Wood & Bandura, 1989b)
- Social comparison effects can be reduced by judging accomplishments against personal standards of self-improvement rather than against attainments of others
- Self-improvement never follows a uniform course but is characterized by spurts, setbacks, plateaus, and variations in rate of progress
- Framing performance feedback in ways that give salience to gains enhances beliefs of managerial efficacy even when organizational standards are nigh out of reach (Jourden, 1991)
- Belief in controllability of the environment is important: viewing an organization as changeable increases perceived efficacy to manage it (Bandura & Wood, 1989)

**Self-efficacy in policy making and receptivity to innovations (pp.458-460):**
- Jatulis & Newman (1991): Under time pressure and potential risks, program managers with low efficacy sought more information, spent more time talking, and procrastinated longer than high-efficacy managers
- Efficacy beliefs affect not only administrators' receptivity to innovations but also the readiness with which employees adopt them (Hill, Smith, & Mann, 1987)
- Ellen (1988): Business students whose computer efficacy was arbitrarily lowered resisted computer aids; those whose efficacy was raised abandoned manual methods for computer-aided ones
- McDoneld & Siegall (1992): Employees who were self-assured in their learning efficacy during telecommunications technology changeover were more satisfied, committed, and performed better
- Burkhardt & Brass (1990): Longitudinal study showing efficacy beliefs promote adoption of new technologies, which in turn alter organizational network structure -- early adopters gained more influence and centrality within the organization

### 4. Self-Efficacy in Enactment of Occupational Roles (pp.460-468)

**Role enactment and goal setting (pp.460-462):**
- McEnrue (1984): Managers of high and low perceived efficacy do not differ when roles are ambiguously structured, but high-efficacy managers exhibit marked performance gains when role expectations are clear
- Goals improve functioning by providing direction, purpose, and feedback basis
- Goals must be explicit, segmented into attainable progressive subgoals, with good feedback on performance (Locke & Latham, 1984; Pritchard et al., 1988)
- Earley (1986): Comparison of goal setting, strategy training, and supervisor vs. union representative delivery in manufacturing facilities in US and England. Employees taught improved production strategies raised perceived efficacy, set higher goals, increased commitment and productivity

**Participative goal setting (pp.462-463):**
- Participative goal setting increases satisfaction but has no consistent effect on performance beyond assigned goals (Locke & Latham, 1990; Wagner & Gooding, 1987)
- Benefits of participation stem from exchange of ideas rather than how goals are set (Latham, Winters, & Locke, 1994)
- Self-efficacy helps explain diverse effects of working alone vs. together: high-efficacy members expect positive outcomes and perform better when individually identified; low-efficacy members perform worse when singled out (connects to social loafing and free-riding dynamics)

**Creative productivity (pp.463-464):**
- High assurance in investigatory efficacy accounts for research productivity after controlling for experience, rank, and affiliation (Vasil, 1992)
- Taylor et al. (1984): Perceived efficacy contributes to productivity both directly and by influencing goals and flexible involvement in multiple projects
- Faculty members hold weaker beliefs in efficacy for research than for teaching (Schoen & Winocur, 1988)

**Occupational stress and dysfunction (pp.464-468):**
- Perceived self-efficacy to fulfill occupational demands affects level of stress and physical health (McAteer-Early, 1992)
- Low sense of coping efficacy is a central mediator of burnout (Chwalisz et al., 1992; Leiter, 1991)
- Burnout syndrome: emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, lack of personal accomplishment (Maslach, 1982)
- Low sense of personal efficacy to fulfill job demands arouses anxiety; low collective efficacy contributes to job dissatisfaction and intent to quit (Jex & Gudanowski, 1992)
- Fig. 10.3 (Chwalisz et al., 1992): Perceived coping efficacy, not belief about causes of stress nor their changeability, is the central mediator of burnout. High coping efficacy predicts problem-focused coping which prevents burnout; perceived inefficacy predicts emotion-focused coping leading to burnout
- Frayne & Latham (1987): Self-regulatory program for reducing absenteeism -- employees taught to self-monitor, set subgoals, develop coping strategies, self-reward. Training increased self-regulatory efficacy and improved job attendance maintained over nine-month follow-up (Fig. 10.4, Latham & Frayne, 1989)
- Employees who develop self-regulatory efficacy need programs for developing and upgrading skills, helpful feedback systems, sense of mission and purpose from co-workers and efficacious leadership

### 5. Collective Organizational Efficacy (pp.468-476)

- Collective efficacy is an emergent group-level attribute; interactive dynamics create a property that is more than the sum of individual attributes
- Aggregating members' beliefs in their own efficacy is most relevant when the group works independently within an organized structure; aggregating members' beliefs in their group's efficacy is more relevant when group outcome is produced through highly interdependent effort
- Campion, Medsker, & Higgs (1993): Perceived group efficacy was the strongest predictor of employee satisfaction and productivity, positively associated with group self-management, flexibility, shared purposes, communication, managerial support, and sharing of workloads
- Perceived team efficacy tailored to the relevant domain is a better predictor of subsequent group performance than general potency (Lindsley, Mathieu, Heffner, & Brass, 1994)
- Leadership aspirations predicted by perceived efficacy to fulfill leadership demands, not just anticipated benefits of high rank (Singer, 1991)

**Individualistic versus collectivistic efficacy (pp.470-471):**
- Earley (1993): American managers (individualistic culture) expressed highest efficacy and performed best under individual management system; Israeli and Chinese managers (collectivistic cultures) expressed highest efficacy and performed best as members of ethnically homogeneous managerial teams
- Collectivists display a lowered sense of personal and group efficacy and diminished productivity in groups of culturally dissimilar members
- When effects of efficacy beliefs and anticipated outcomes are controlled, national origin explains only about 5 percent of the variation in managerial performance
- Cultural context moderates training effects: employees achieve greatest sense of personal efficacy and productivity when training is congruent with their cultural orientation (Earley, 1994)

**Organizational learning (pp.472-474):**
- Organizational learning occurs through interactive psychosocial processes rather than through reified organizational attributes operating independently
- Four major phases of organizational learning: (1) monitoring and interpreting environmental opportunities, (2) strategic selection or construction of alternative solutions, (3) symbolic exploration by testing alternatives, (4) implementing chosen course of action
- Each subfunction (monitoring, innovating, producing, marketing) is segmented into specialized organizational subsystems, creating social dynamics that can facilitate, retard, or block learning and change
- Three modes of innovation: exploratory experimentation, synthesizing existing knowledge into new ways, serendipity
- Assessment of perceived efficacy for organizational learning should measure members' beliefs about how well their organizational subsystems can perform their various functions and how well they can work together

**Organizational culture (pp.474-476):**
- Perceived collective efficacy is an important feature of an organization's operative culture
- Created and transmitted through social modeling, incentive systems, personnel selection practices, staff development activities, job structuring, and relative success in the marketplace
- Bandura critiques both radical postmodernist views that deny the existence of generalizable organizational factors and purely anecdotal approaches that lack quantitative rigor
- Progress in understanding organizational life may benefit from reframing issues in sociostructural terms rather than through the vague construct of "culture"

## New Concepts Introduced
- [[career-self-efficacy]] -- beliefs about capabilities for career development, occupational choice, and decisional subskills
- [[organizational-efficacy]] -- self-efficacy applied to organizational and managerial functioning

## Contradictions or Tensions
- Perceived efficacy generally enhances performance but can also promote [[escalation-of-commitment]]: raised decisional efficacy leads administrators to escalate investment in failing ventures (Whyte, Saks, & Hook). This is a boundary condition on the adaptive value of self-efficacy
- Participative goal setting increases satisfaction but does not consistently improve performance beyond assigned goals -- contrary to popular assumption (Locke & Latham, 1990)
- Cultural context moderates effects: efficacy-boosting influences that work for individualists may backfire for collectivists and vice versa (Earley, 1993, 1994)

## Quotes Worth Keeping
> "The combined findings of these different lines of research are consistent in showing that perceived efficacy is a robust contributor to career development. It predicts the scope of career options seriously considered, occupational interests and preferences, enrollment in courses of study that provide the knowledge and skills for different careers, perseverance in difficult fields, academic success in those chosen fields, and even the choice of cultural milieu in which to pursue one's occupational career." -- Bandura, 1997, p.427

> "Managers operating under these self-enhancing belief systems displayed a resilient sense of efficacy even when assigned productivity standards were exceedingly difficult to reach. They set themselves increasingly challenging organizational goals. And they used good analytic thinking for discovering managerial rules. Such a self-efficacious orientation paid off in high organizational productivity." -- Bandura, 1997, p.454

> "The trouble with other inventors is that they try a few things and quit. I never quit until I get what I want." -- Thomas Edison, quoted in Bandura, 1997, p.456
