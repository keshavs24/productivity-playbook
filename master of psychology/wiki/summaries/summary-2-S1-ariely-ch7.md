# Predictably Irrational Ch.7: Procrastination and Self-Control — Summary

**Author(s):** [[ariely|Dan Ariely]]
**Type:** Book chapter
**Phase:** 2
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-14

## Key Takeaways
1. Procrastination is the result of choosing immediate gratification over long-term goals -- rooted in the same hot-cold gap from Ch.6
2. **Externally imposed deadlines** produce the best outcomes; **self-imposed deadlines** help but are suboptimal; **no deadlines** produce the worst outcomes
3. **Precommitment devices** -- tools that restrict future choices -- are the most effective way to combat procrastination
4. People recognize their procrastination problem and try to fight it, but they set imperfect restrictions for themselves

## Detailed Notes

### The Three-Class Deadline Experiment (Ariely & Klaus Wertenbroch)
Ariely ran a natural experiment with three sections of his consumer behavior class at MIT. Each class had three papers due during the 12-week semester:

**Class 1 (Self-imposed deadlines):** Students chose their own deadlines at the start of the semester. Late papers penalized 1% per day.

**Class 2 (No deadlines):** All three papers due at the end of the semester. Maximum flexibility, lowest risk of intermediate penalties.

**Class 3 (Dictated deadlines):** Ariely imposed deadlines at weeks 4, 8, and 12. No flexibility.

#### What Happened
- A perfectly rational student would set all deadlines to the last day (maximizing flexibility, as in Class 2)
- But students in Class 1 **did not do this** -- they spaced their deadlines across the semester, acknowledging their procrastination weakness
- Students who did not space their deadlines sufficiently pulled down the class average

#### Results (Grades)
- **Class 3 (dictated deadlines): BEST grades**
- **Class 1 (self-imposed deadlines): MIDDLE grades**
- **Class 2 (no deadlines): WORST grades**

This proves three things:
1. Students **do** procrastinate (Class 2 performed worst)
2. Tightly restricting freedom (externally imposed deadlines) is the best cure
3. Self-imposed deadlines help but are **not as effective** as external ones -- people don't perfectly understand their own procrastination

### Why Self-Imposed Deadlines Are Suboptimal
Not everyone understands their tendency to procrastinate. Even those who do may not set optimal restrictions. Some students in Class 1 bunched their deadlines near the end, negating the benefit of self-commitment.

### Precommitment Mechanisms
Ariely discusses multiple real-world precommitment devices:

- **Automatic paycheck deductions:** If you can't save voluntarily, commit to automatic deduction before you see the money
- **Exercise with friends:** Social commitment makes it harder to skip
- **The "ice glass" method:** Freeze your credit card in a block of ice. When tempted to impulse buy, you must wait for it to thaw -- by then, the impulse passes
- **Debt blogs:** People publicly posting their debt online (like "poorerthanyou.com") -- social accountability as precommitment

### The Self-Control Credit Card Idea
Ariely proposed a credit card that would let users set their own spending limits by category, store, and time frame. Exceeding limits could trigger:
- Card rejection
- Self-imposed tax (donated to charity or savings)
- Alert email to spouse or friend

He pitched this to a major bank. The bankers were enthusiastic -- until they realized it would reduce the $17 billion they make annually in interest charges. They never called back.

### Health Care and Procrastination
- Preventive medicine (colonoscopies, mammograms, cholesterol checks) is procrastinated because the immediate cost (pain, inconvenience) is certain while the long-term benefit (health) is uncertain and distant
- **Ford vs. Honda maintenance:** Honda bundled 18,000 maintenance items into 3 simple service intervals. Ford's 18,000 items were spread across dozens of confusing schedules. Ford copied Honda's approach and service bay utilization went from 40% to full in three years. Simplification defeats procrastination
- **Proposal:** Self-imposed deposits for health checkups (refundable only if you show up on time) replicate the Class 1 deadline condition

### The Interferon Story (Personal)
Ariely's own battle with self-control during hepatitis C treatment:
- Interferon injections caused fever, nausea, and vomiting for 16 hours after each dose
- He was the **only patient** in the six-month trial who followed the protocol perfectly
- His trick: he paired injections with watching movies (immediate reward). Press Play immediately after injecting, before side effects hit -- associating the injection with pleasure rather than pain
- Lesson: **pair immediate positive reinforcement with unpleasant-but-beneficial behaviors** to overcome the delayed-gratification problem

### Broader Implications
- Ralph Keeney (Duke): America's top killer is not cancer or heart disease -- it's our **inability to make smart choices** and overcome self-destructive behaviors
- About half of us will make a lifestyle decision that leads to an early grave
- The right behavioral antidote for each procrastination problem is different, but the principle is universal: pair something we love with something we need to do
