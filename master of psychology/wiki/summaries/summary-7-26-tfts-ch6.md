# Think Faster Talk Smarter Ch.6: Focus — The F-Word of Spontaneous Speaking — Summary

**Author:** Matt Abrahams
**Source:** *Think Faster, Talk Smarter* (2023), Chapter 6
**Phase:** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **Focused messages are precise, relevant, accessible, and concise.** These four dimensions define what it means to communicate with focus, and improving any one dimension enhances overall effectiveness.
2. **Precision means having a clear goal across three dimensions:** what you want people to *know*, *feel*, and *do*. Most communicators think only about informing (know) and neglect emotion (feel) and action (do).
3. **Relevance means audience-centered communication.** Ask: "Why should *they* care?" Consider resistance areas, create curiosity and tension. Jim Koch (Samuel Adams) exemplifies: size up the audience within 30 seconds and tailor the pitch.
4. **Concision is power.** Steve Jobs introduced the iPod in five words: "1,000 songs in your pocket." Google's mission: 12 words. Neurologist Josef Parvizi: fewer words activate fewer processing systems, making messages easier to absorb.

## Detailed Notes

### Dimension #1: Precision ("What's the Point Exactly?")
- **Know, Feel, Do:** Communication goals have three dimensions -- information (know), emotion (feel), and action (do). Most people only clarify the first.
- Baba Shiv (Stanford marketing professor): 90-95% of decisions are shaped nonconsciously by our emotional brain system. Emotionally connected customers are 50%+ more valuable economically.
- Before entering a speaking situation, jot answers to: What do you want people to know? Feel? Do?

### Dimension #2: Relevance ("Why Should I Care?")
- Jim Koch (Samuel Adams): size up the person and bar within 30 seconds, then tailor the pitch. If he couldn't see how the product fit the bar's needs, he moved on.
- Make a habit of asking: "Who is this person? What do they need to hear? How can I frame what I'm about to say so it's most relevant/interesting/urgent for them?"
- Create **common ground** by addressing the question that lingers in every listener's mind: "Why should I care?"
- Create **curiosity and tension:** share unexpected customer feedback before answering, pose a provocative question before delivering information
- Zakary Tormala (Stanford social psychologist): "Taking a more open, agreeable, inclusive, and cooperative type approach with people generally lowers their resistance"

### Dimension #3: Accessibility ("Why Is This Stuff So Complicated?")
- **The curse of knowledge:** we know too much and assume audiences can follow our jargon. The **curse of passion:** we ramble about what excites us
- Reduce jargon: The U.S. military's "GOAT" (Glossary of Acronyms and Terms) illustrates how deep the problem can go
- Hans Rosling's "shortest TED Talk ever" (under 1 minute) used stones on the ground to explain global economic inequality -- visual, simple, powerful
- Justin Kestler (LitCharts/SparkNotes): translating complex ideas into straightforward terms is the job
- **LEGO approach:** LEGO designers have "very deep knowledge of what kids are able to understand at any age" and design instructions accordingly. Apply the "grandmother test" -- could your elderly mother understand your explanation?
- **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front):** Military/journalism technique. Lead with the main point, then add detail. Practice delivering ideas in BLUF before meetings.

### Dimension #4: Concision ("Why Are They So Long-Winded?")
- Steve Jobs: "1,000 songs in your pocket" -- five words that launched a revolution
- Google's mission (crafted by Raymond Nasr): "to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful" -- 12 words for a $1.5 trillion company
- Hilary Price (cartoonist, *Rhymes with Orange*): convey 49% of the information and let the reader fill in the rest. "You never want to say 'apple' if you could show an apple."
- **Brevity challenges:** Summarize in a tweet (140 characters), write haikus, try PechaKucha (20 slides x 20 seconds)
- Review text messages to self-assess: are responses longer than necessary?

### Balance Is Key
Each dimension can be taken too far. Too goal-directed = rigid. Too audience-tailored = narrow. Too accessible = dumbed-down. Too concise = insufficient detail. Aim for the sweet spot.
