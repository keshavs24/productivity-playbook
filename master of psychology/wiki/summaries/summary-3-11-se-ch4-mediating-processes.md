# Ch.4 Mediating Processes — Summary

**Author(s):** Albert Bandura
**Type:** Book chapter
**Phase:** 3
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-14

## Key Takeaways

1. **Self-efficacy beliefs regulate human functioning through four major processes:** cognitive, motivational, affective, and selective (selection) processes. These processes usually operate in concert rather than in isolation.
2. **Cognitive processes:** Efficacy beliefs affect thought patterns that can enhance or undermine performance -- high-efficacy people visualize success scenarios; low-efficacy people dwell on failure scenarios. Conceptions of ability (acquirable skill vs. inherent aptitude) dramatically affect the self-regulatory mechanisms governing decision making and performance.
3. **Motivational processes:** Three forms of cognitive motivators are regulated by efficacy beliefs -- causal attributions (attribution theory), outcome expectancies (expectancy-value theory), and cognized goals (goal theory). Efficacy beliefs mediate the effect of causal attributions on performance almost entirely.
4. **Affective processes:** Efficacy beliefs regulate anxiety and depression through three modes: control over thought (cognitive reappraisal), action (transformational behavior), and affect (palliative regulation). Perceived coping efficacy and thought control efficacy jointly determine anxiety levels.
5. **Selection processes:** By influencing the environments and activities people choose to engage in, efficacy beliefs shape the courses their lives take. These selective processes determine developmental trajectories that are difficult to reverse.

## Detailed Notes

### 1. Cognitive Processes (pp.116-122)

Efficacy beliefs affect thought patterns that can enhance or undermine performance. These cognitive effects take various forms.

**Cognitive constructions** (pp.116-117):
- People with high efficacy view situations as presenting realizable opportunities and visualize success scenarios that provide positive guides for performance
- Those who judge themselves as inefficacious construe uncertain situations as risky and visualize failure scenarios (Krueger & Dickson, 1994)
- Cognitive negativity that dwells on personal deficiencies is a good way to undermine self-motivation and performance
- Numerous studies show that cognitive simulations in which individuals visualize themselves executing activities skillfully enhance subsequent performance (Bandura, 1986a; Corbin, 1972; Feltz & Landers, 1983; Kazdin, 1978)
- Visualizing successful actions improves performances; imaging faulty ones impairs them (Powell, 1973)
- Perceived self-efficacy and cognitive simulation affect each other bidirectionally

**Inferential thinking** (pp.117-118):
- Problem-solving skills require effective cognitive processing of multifaceted information containing many complexities, ambiguities, and uncertainties
- A high sense of efficacy fosters cognitive constructions of effective courses of action, and cognitive enactments of efficacious actions strengthen efficacy beliefs (Bandura & Adams, 1977; Kazdin, 1979)
- The powerful influence of efficacy beliefs on self-regulatory cognitive processes is revealed in the Wood and Bandura (1989a) simulated organization study: managers with strong efficacy remained task-oriented, used efficient analytic strategies, and achieved high organizational performance despite causal ambiguities and judgment failures

**Conceptions of ability** (pp.118-119):
- **Acquirable skill view:** Ability is an acquirable skill that can be increased by gaining knowledge and perfecting competencies. People adopt a functional learning goal, seek challenges, regard errors as natural part of learning, measure success by personal improvement. This view fosters resilient self-efficacy and high performance (Figure 4.1).
- **Inherent aptitude view:** Ability is a fixed, endowed capacity. Errors and deficient performances signify intellectual limitations. People prefer tasks that minimize error display, shun opportunities to learn, and belittle their own accomplishments when others surpass them. This view produces declining efficacy and deteriorating performance under difficulty (Figure 4.1).
- These conceptions of ability can be experimentally instilled and have strong effects on self-regulatory mechanisms (Wood & Bandura, 1989b)
- The same person may view ability differently in different domains

**Perceived controllability of the environment** (pp.120-121):
- People who believe organizations are controllable maintain high self-efficacy, set challenging goals, use good analytic thinking, and achieve high organizational performance even in the face of difficulties (Figure 4.3, Bandura & Wood, 1989)
- Those with an instilled cognitive set that organizations are not easily changeable quickly lost faith and lowered aspirations, and their group's performance deteriorated
- **Path analysis** (Figure 4.4, Wood & Bandura, 1989a): With growing experience, the performance system was regulated more strongly and intricately by efficacy beliefs. Efficacy beliefs influence performance both directly and through their strong effects on personal goal setting and proficient analytic thinking

### 2. Motivational Processes (pp.122-137)

Most human motivation is cognitively generated. Three different forms of cognitive motivators around which different theories have been built: **causal attributions**, **outcome expectancies**, and **cognized goals** (Figure 4.5, p.123).

**Attribution theory** (pp.123-125):
- People who credit their successes to personal capabilities and their failures to insufficient effort will undertake difficult tasks and persist in the face of failure
- Those who ascribe their failures to deficiencies in ability display low strivings and give up readily
- Causal attributions can serve as self-serving excuses that do not affect future performances vs. as genuine belief modifiers
- **Self-efficacy mediates the effect of causal attributions on performance almost entirely** (Relich, Debus, & Walker, 1986; Schunk & Gunn, 1986; Schunk & Rice, 1986). The more the arbitrary reasons raise efficacy beliefs, the higher the subsequent performance attainments.
- Attributions of success to ability are accompanied by heightened beliefs of personal efficacy, which predict subsequent performance attainments
- Self-efficacious individuals view attainments as personally controllable; they ascribe failures to insufficient effort or situational impediments. Those with low efficacy view failures as stemming from lack of ability. This influence is reproducible across academic performances, interpersonal transactions, physical performances, and health habits.
- **Key distinction from attribution theory:** Whereas attribution theory is concerned solely with perceived causes of performance successes and failures, self-efficacy theory encompasses modeling and persuasory and affective sources of efficacy information as well as enactive ones. Self-efficacy considers more factors than the attributional subset and thus has greater predictive power.
- Causal attributions serve as supplementary motivators but not as a primary mode of generating motivation. Sociocognitive programs fostering personal enablement produce uniformly beneficial changes; attribution retraining alone has met with limited success.

**Expectancy-value theory** (pp.125-127):
- The higher the expectancy that certain behavior can secure specific outcomes and the more highly those outcomes are valued, the greater the motivation to perform
- Self-evaluative outcomes from fulfilling personal standards may be valued more than tangible payoffs; when these conflict, self-evaluative outcomes often override tangible rewards (Bandura, 1986a)
- Efficacy beliefs affect the extent to which people act on their outcome expectations. A low sense of efficacy can nullify the motivating potential of alluring outcome expectations. Firm belief in one's efficacy can sustain efforts over prolonged periods in the face of uncertain or negative outcomes.
- When efficacy beliefs are controlled statistically, outcome expectations contribute little to prediction of behavior
- **Perceived self-efficacy is a broader construct than effort expectancy** because it encompasses much more than effort -- people judge capabilities in terms of knowledge, skills, and strategies at their command, not solely exertion (Wood & Bandura, 1989a)
- Recent additions to expectancy-value models (Ajzen's perceived behavioral control, Fishbein's self-efficacy determinant) increase predictive power by essentially adding efficacy-like constructs. When perceived self-efficacy is added to the original set of expectancy predictors, perceived control makes no independent contribution (Dzewaltowski, 1989).

**Goal theory** (pp.128-136):
- Explicit, challenging goals enhance motivation across diverse activity domains, settings, populations, social levels, and time spans (Locke & Latham, 1990)
- Goals operate largely through self-reactive influences rather than regulating motivation and action directly
- Self-efficacy is one of the important self-influences through which personal standards create powerful motivational effects

**Self-reactive influences as mediators of goal motivation** (pp.128-130):
- Three self-reactive influences: (1) affective self-evaluative reactions to one's performance, (2) perceived self-efficacy for goal attainment, and (3) adjustment of personal standards in light of attainments
- The more sources of self-influence individuals brought to bear on themselves, the greater the effort they exerted (Figure 4.6, Bandura & Cervone, 1986)
- Perceived efficacy contributes to motivation at all discrepancy levels; discontent operates as an influential affective motivator when attainments fall substantially or moderately short

**Self-regulation and the negative feedback model** (pp.130-132):
- Negative feedback control systems (Carver & Scheier, 1981; Lord & Hanges, 1987; Powers, 1973) serve only as a discrepancy reduction mechanism -- matching a standard begets inertness
- Human self-motivation relies on both **discrepancy production** (proactive control: setting valued performance standards that create disequilibrium) and **discrepancy reduction** (reactive control: adjusting effort to reach those standards)
- People initially raise their level of motivation by adopting goals before they receive any feedback about their beginning effort (Bandura & Cervone, 1983)
- Bandura's critique: A purely reactive system cannot explain proactive goal-setting, the adoption of ever-higher standards after success, or the deep engrossment in activities that characterizes human motivation

**Goal properties and self-motivation** (pp.133-136):
- **Goal specificity:** Explicit, clear, attainable goals produce higher levels of performance than general intentions (Locke & Latham, 1990; Bandura & Cervone, 1983)
- **Goal challenge:** Strong interest and involvement are sparked by challenges. The higher the goals (if accepted and committed to), the harder people work and the better they perform (Locke & Latham, 1990). When goals are set unrealistically high, strong effort produces repeated failure that eventually takes its toll on personal efficacy
- **Goal proximity:** Proximal subgoals mobilize self-influences and direct what one does in the here and now. Distal goals alone are too far removed to provide effective incentives. Proximal subgoals produce rising efficacy beliefs, achievement gains, and intrinsic interest; distal goals have no demonstrable effects on any of these outcomes (Figure 4.7, Bandura & Schunk, 1981). Subgoal attainments also create intrinsic interest in activities that initially held little attraction.
- **Hierarchical goal systems** (pp.136): Proximal goals invest activities with personal significance by linking them to pursuit of higher level goals. The model of self-motivation as recurrent proximal self-challenge and evaluative reward differs from one powered entirely by a superordinate goal.
- **Causal ordering of efficacy and goal influences** (pp.136-137): Efficacy beliefs influence the level of goals, the strength of commitment, the strategies used, the effort mobilized, and the intensification of effort when accomplishments fall short. During acquisition of competencies, efficacy beliefs influence goals; during ongoing regulation, basing personal goals on perceived capabilities has considerable functional value.

### 3. Affective Processes (pp.137-156)

Self-efficacy plays a pivotal role in the self-regulation of affective states. Three principal ways efficacy beliefs affect emotional experiences: through the exercise of personal control over **thought**, **action**, and **affect**.

**Efficacy regulation of anxiety through attentional and construal processes** (pp.140-141):
- Perceived efficacy to exercise control over potentially threatening events plays a central role in anxiety arousal
- Threat is not a fixed property of situational events but a relational matter concerning the match between perceived coping capabilities and potentially hurtful aspects of the environment
- People who believe they can exercise control over threats do not conjure up calamities; those who believe threats are unmanageable dwell on coping deficiencies, magnify the severity of possible threats, and worry about perils that rarely happen
- Several converging lines of evidence corroborate the influential role of perceived control (Averill, 1973; Levine & Ursin, 1980; Miller, 1980)

**Efficacy regulation of anxiety through transformational actions** (pp.141):
- People with high coping efficacy adopt strategies and courses of action designed to change hazardous environments into more benign ones
- Major changes in aversive social practices are usually achieved through collective rather than individual exercise of efficacy

**Microrelationships between efficacy beliefs and anxiety arousal** (pp.143-144):
- Perceived self-efficacy operates as a cognitive regulator of anxiety arousal (tested directly by creating different levels of perceived efficacy in phobics and relating them to different manifestations of anxiety at a microlevel)
- The perceived mismatch between beliefs of coping efficacy and task demands, rather than properties inherent in the tasks themselves, is the source of variation in anxiety reactions
- Perceived efficacy linked to catecholamine release (Bandura, Taylor, Williams, Mefford, & Barchas, 1985): Phobics exhibit low epinephrine and norepinephrine within their high perceived efficacy range but display substantial increases during threatening activities approaching their perceived coping limits
- After coping efficacy is maximally strengthened, previously intimidating threats no longer elicit differential autonomic or catecholamine reactivity

**Environmental controllability and anxiety arousal** (pp.144-145):
- Perceived coping efficacy is a major contributor to anticipatory anxiety and stress reactions, but it is not the sole determinant
- High-efficacy individuals take on risky activities and create more demanding environments for themselves, potentially generating more extraneous anxiety sources
- In evaluating the role of perceived coping efficacy in anxiety arousal, one must consider how risky and demanding are the activities people choose to pursue

**Regulation of affective states by thought control efficacy** (pp.145-148):
- People possess the capacity to manage their own thought processes
- People with low efficacy to control intrusive thoughts find them more distressing, are more recurrently plagued by them, and engage in more obsessive-compulsive rituals
- Thought control efficacy predicts the distressfulness of intrusive thoughts; frequency of thoughts controlled, the effect of perceived efficacy to manage intrusive thoughts on distress has little effect (Churchill & McMurray, 1989)
- Efforts to suppress unwanted thoughts can paradoxically activate them (Wegner, 1989) -- the suppressive self-instruction "Quit thinking of a giraffe" invariably activates the unwanted thought
- Self-distraction by becoming absorbed in other trains of thought can be more effective than suppression
- Postponement of worrying to a particular time and place reduces worry time (Borkovec, Wilkinson, Folensbee, & Lerman, 1983)
- Enactive immersion in engrossing activities is even more effective than cognitive thought control
- **Dual control of anxiety and behavior** (Ozer & Bandura, 1990): Path analysis of the causal structure of coping behavior involving interpersonal threats reveals two paths -- perceived self-protective efficacy affects perceived vulnerability and risk discernment; cognitive control efficacy affects negative intrusive thoughts. Both paths converge on anxiety which affects avoidant behavior (Figure 4.10).

**Perceived self-inefficacy and depression** (pp.153-156):
- People experience anxiety when they perceive themselves as unable to manage potentially threatening events; they experience depression when they perceive themselves as unable to gain things of value
- Anxiety and depression often co-occur because of the common co-occurrence of privations and threats
- Three major contributors to depression: cognitive, behavioral, and environmental factors (interacting, not independent)
- **Inefficacy and biased cognitive processing:** Low efficacy creates negative biases in how experiences are cognitively organized, and recalled. Three subfunctions affected: (1) self-monitoring and cognitive processing of successes/failures, (2) judging ongoing performances against the standard one seeks to fulfill, and (3) affective self-reactions to one's accomplishments
- Depression-prone people dwell on failures and distort recollections in self-slighting directions; nondepressed display self-enhancing bias
- Dysphoric individuals use unfavorable social comparison information in self-depreciating ways; when exposed to high attainments of others, they judge their own accomplishments as less praiseworthy
- Self-comparative standards (measuring progress against one's own previous performance) provide benefits of personal challenge without the cost of invidious social comparison
- **The price of early success:** Accomplishments that surpass earlier ones bring continued self-satisfaction, but the pace at which activities are mastered can drastically alter self-evaluative reactions (Simon, 1979a). Early spectacular accomplishments can be conducive to later self-dissatisfaction in the face of continuing personal attainments (e.g., Linus Pauling: "Change fields, of course!" after the Nobel Prize; Bob Beamon never jumping again after shattering the record)

**Biological effects of inefficacy in coping with stressors** (pp.156-158):
- Efficacy beliefs affect biological systems that mediate health and disease
- Perceived self-efficacy in exercising control over stressors activates endogenous opioid systems (Bandura et al., 1988)
- Low perceived coping efficacy is accompanied by high levels of subjective distress and autonomic and catecholamine reactivity
- After coping efficacy is developed, previously distressing threats are managed without autonomic or catecholamine arousal
- Perceived self-efficacy affects immune function: efficacy to control cognitive stressors activates cellular components of the immune system (Wiedenfeld, O'Leary, Bandura, Brown, Levine, & Raska, 1990)

### 4. Selection Processes (pp.158-161)

People are partly the products of their environments. By influencing which environments and activities people choose, efficacy beliefs can shape the courses lives take.

**Key findings:**
- People avoid activities and situations they believe exceed their coping capabilities, but they readily undertake activities and select social environments they judge themselves capable of managing
- The social influences operating in selected environments continue to promote certain competencies, values, and interests long after the efficacy decisional determinant has rendered its inaugurating effect
- Career choice and development is one domain in which efficacy beliefs play a prominent role (Betz & Hackett, 1986; Lent & Hackett, 1987): Perceived self-efficacy predicts the range of career options people consider, their persistence in difficult fields, and their academic success
- Women's career development illustrates these effects: women who have a strong sense of efficacy consider a wider range of occupational options, show greater interest in those occupations, and perform better in educational preparation for them
- The higher the perceived self-efficacy, the more career options people consider possible, the greater their interest in them, the better they prepare themselves educationally, and the greater their staying power in challenging career pursuits (Lent, Brown, & Hackett, 1994)
- Through the environments they select, people can influence what they become: efficacious people choose challenging environments that develop new competencies and interests; inefficacious people choose environments that confirm and reinforce self-limiting beliefs
- Judgments of personal efficacy also determine how long people will persevere in occupations they find unsatisfying (Staw & Ross, 1985)

## Contradictions or Tensions
- **Negative feedback models vs. proactive goal theory:** Bandura argues that purely reactive discrepancy-reduction models (Carver & Scheier, Powers) cannot explain proactive motivation -- people create disequilibrium by setting challenging goals, not just reduce existing discrepancies
- **Effort attributions as motivators:** Attribution retraining alone has limited success; sociocognitive programs fostering personal enablement are more effective because they address efficacy directly
- **Coping dichotomy:** Lazarus and Folkman's (1984) distinction between problem management and emotion management is misleading -- successful coping usually requires both, and most situations are at least partly changeable
