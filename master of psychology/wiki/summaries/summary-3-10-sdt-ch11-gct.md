# SDT Ch.11: Goal Contents Theory — Summary

**Author(s):** [[ryan-deci|Richard M. Ryan & Edward L. Deci]]
**Type:** Book chapter
**Phase:** 3
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-14

## Key Takeaways
1. **[[goal-contents-theory]]** (GCT) is the fifth of SDT's six mini-theories. It concerns the *what* of people's goal-directed behaviors (goal content) rather than the *why* (motives/regulation), arguing that not all goals are created equal
2. Life goals fall along an **intrinsic-extrinsic axis**: [[intrinsic-aspirations]] (personal growth, close relationships, community contribution, health) vs. [[extrinsic-aspirations]] (wealth, fame, image/appearance). These differentially relate to well-being
3. **[[relative-aspirations|Relative importance]]** matters more than absolute levels: placing higher relative priority on extrinsic vs. intrinsic goals predicts lower well-being, more ill-being, and more negative social/environmental behaviors
4. Goal contents and autonomous regulation ([[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation|motives]]) make **independent contributions** to predicting well-being -- both the *what* and the *why* of goal pursuit matter
5. **[[aspiration-index|The Aspiration Index (AI)]]** is the primary measurement instrument, developed by Kasser & Ryan (1993, 1996), measuring relative importance and attainment of intrinsic vs. extrinsic life goals
6. **Attaining** intrinsic goals enhances well-being through [[basic-psychological-needs|basic need satisfaction]]; attaining extrinsic goals yields no gains or marginal gains at best, and the process of pursuing them often compromises autonomy and relatedness
7. Extrinsic aspirations frequently develop as **compensatory responses** to [[need-frustration|need thwarting]] during development -- controlling, cold parenting predicts children adopting extrinsic goals
8. **Framing** activities in terms of intrinsic vs. extrinsic goals directly affects engagement, learning quality, and persistence

## Detailed Notes

### Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Aspirations as the Basis for GCT

SDT addresses not only people's *motives* (why they pursue goals) but also the *content* of their goals (what they pursue). From an SDT perspective, expectancy-value theories are ambiguous because the pursuit of a valued goal could be either autonomous or controlled, and the valued goals being pursued could be more or less fulfilling of [[basic-psychological-needs]].

- SDT holds a **critical perspective on goals**: some types of goals will yield satisfaction of the basic needs of the self and some will not
- This perspective is grounded in **eudaimonic theorizing**: the pursuit of what is inherently valued (intrinsic aspirations) is predicted to relate to greater well-being, while focus on extrinsic aspirations is predicted to relate to lower well-being

### Research on Aspirations and Well-Being

#### The Aspiration Index (Kasser & Ryan, 1993, 1996)

- Research began with **Kasser & Ryan (1993)** who distinguished [[intrinsic-aspirations]] (forming close affiliations, personal growth, giving to community) from [[extrinsic-aspirations]] (gaining wealth, fame, image)
- Developed the **[[aspiration-index]]** (AI): participants rate importance and likelihood of attaining various life goals. The AI represents [[relative-aspirations|relative importance]] of each type of life goal.
- **Kasser & Ryan (1993), three initial studies:**
  1. Cross-sectional survey: AI for each of three intrinsic aspirations was positively related to wellness indicators; the higher the relative importance of financial success, the lower the self-actualization and vitality
  2. Replication: Intrinsic aspirations for personal growth and meaningful relationships were negatively related to depression and anxiety; relative extrinsic aspiration for financial success was positively related to these distressing outcomes
  3. 18-year-olds from mixed SES, community sample, clinician-rated: Intrinsic aspirations related positively to global social functioning and social productivity and negatively to conduct disorders; opposite was true for financial success aspirations

#### Expanded Aspiration Index (Kasser & Ryan, 1996)

- Added two extrinsic aspirations: attractiveness (image) and social recognition (fame)
- Higher-order factor analyses confirmed two clear overarching factors: **intrinsic** (personal growth, relationships, community contributions) and **extrinsic** (wealth, image, fame)
- High relative importance on intrinsic aspirations associated with greater self-actualization, vitality, less depression, fewer physical symptoms
- High relative importance on extrinsic aspirations associated with lower self-actualization, lower vitality, more physical symptoms

#### Cross-Cultural Replication

- **Ryan, Chirkov, Little, Sheldon, Timoshina, & Deci (1999):** U.S. and Russian samples -- greater valuing of intrinsic relative to extrinsic goals predicted better wellness in both nations despite differing economic circumstances
- **Schmuck, Kasser, & Ryan (2000):** Germany -- similar effects
- **Martos & Kopp (2014):** Representative sample of Hungarian adults -- intrinsic aspirations predicted greater meaning in life and well-being; extrinsic aspirations contributed to diminished meaning. Findings held within every socioeconomic level.
- **Utvaer, Hammervold, & Haugan (2014):** Scandinavia -- predicted pattern replicated
- **Williams, Hedberg, Cox, & Deci (2000):** U.S. high school students -- strong relative extrinsic aspirations predicted more high-risk behaviors (tobacco, alcohol, marijuana use)
- **Kasser & Ryan (2001):** Midwestern U.S. college students -- strong relative extrinsic aspirations predicted more drug use and more television watching
- **Grouzet et al. (2005):** 15-nation circumplex study -- intrinsic-extrinsic axis was reliable across cultures. The more one focuses on extrinsic goals like fame and wealth, the less likely one is to place importance on community and close relationships. Additional dimensions included hedonism, conformity, spirituality, and a self-focus vs. self-transcendence axis.
- **Tang, Kuang, & Yao (2008):** China; **Kim, Kasser, & Lee (2003):** South Korea -- similar patterns
- **Unanue, Dittmar, Vignoles, & Vansteenkiste (2014):** United Kingdom and Chile -- materialistic extrinsic aspiration negatively associated with well-being and positively with ill-being in both mass-consumer and fast-developing economies, mediated by low need satisfaction and high need frustration

#### Children and Adolescents

- **Easterbrook, Wright, Dittmar, & Banerjee (2014):** 8-15-year-olds who were materialistic, focused on possessions and outward indicators of worth, had low well-being
- **Ku, Dittmar, & Banerjee (2014):** Chinese and British children with strong materialistic aspirations displayed extrinsic motivation for learning, leading to poorer learning outcomes

### Aspirations: Espousing vs. Enacting

- **Sheldon & Krieger (2014b):** People are more likely to espouse aspirations than to act in accordance with them, especially intrinsic goals (personal growth, affiliation, community)
- **Key finding:** The amount of behavior consistent with intrinsic aspirations was more predictive of well-being than the intrinsic aspirations themselves. Similarly, behaviors consistent with extrinsic aspirations were more predictive of ill-being than the extrinsic aspirations themselves.
- Simply stated: **actually living** one's intrinsic aspirations may be critical to experiencing greater need satisfaction and well-being

### Changes in Aspirations Over Time

- **Three longitudinal studies (including one over 12 years)** (Kasser et al., 2014): When people became more intrinsically oriented over time, their well-being increased; when they became more extrinsically oriented, well-being decreased. Changes were mediated by basic psychological need satisfaction.
- **Intervention study:** Highly extrinsically oriented, materialistic adolescents given an intervention to decrease materialism showed increased self-esteem relative to control group
- **Twenge, Campbell, & Freeman (2012):** Across three post-WWII generations, American adolescents became less concerned with others, community, and environment, focusing increasingly on wealth, fame, and image -- a cohort shift toward extrinsic aspirations

### Intrinsic and Extrinsic Goal Attainment

> *GCT Proposition IV:* Progress and success at attaining extrinsic goals will tend to be associated with less enhanced wellness relative to progress and attainment of intrinsic goals. Progress and attainment of intrinsic goals is predicted to yield especially enhanced wellness. These effects are largely mediated by basic psychological need satisfaction.

- **Sheldon & Kasser (1998):** Short-term longitudinal study -- well-being was enhanced by actual attainment of intrinsic goals; success at extrinsic goals provided little well-being enhancement
- **Ryan et al. (1999):** Russian and U.S. participants -- attainment of intrinsic goals correlated more strongly with well-being; extrinsic goal attainments accounted for no incremental variance in wellness beyond intrinsic goals
- **Kasser & Ryan (2001):** Current attainment of intrinsic aspirations was positively associated with well-being; extrinsic aspiration attainment was not
- **Niemiec, Ryan, & Deci (2009):** Longitudinal postcollege study -- attainment of hoped-for intrinsic aspirations led to greater well-being and less ill-being; attainment of hoped-for extrinsic aspirations did not relate to well-being but did predict greater ill-being. The strong relation between intrinsic aspiration attainment and psychological health was mediated by basic need satisfaction.
- **Montasem, Brown, & Harris (2013):** Working dentists -- intrinsic aspiration attainment associated with subjective well-being, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction
- **Van Hiel & Vansteenkiste (2009):** Older adults (mean age 68-75) -- intrinsic goal attainment contributed positively to well-being and ego integrity; extrinsic goal attainment was unrelated to psychological health and contributed positively to despair. In the older cohort, extrinsic attainment was negatively associated with death acceptance.
- **Sheldon & Krieger (2014a):** 1000+ private-firm lawyers (money-focused) vs. public-service lawyers (service-focused) -- money-focused lawyers had higher incomes but more negative affect, lower well-being, and more alcohol consumption

### Goal Contents and Autonomous Regulation: Independent Effects

The *what* (intrinsic vs. extrinsic goal content) and the *why* (autonomous vs. controlled motives) of goal pursuit are related but distinguishable, and both independently predict well-being.

- Intrinsic goals tend to be more autonomously regulated; extrinsic goals tend to be more controlled. But the correlation is imperfect.
- **Sheldon & Kasser (1995):** Both what and why of strivings independently predicted well-being
- **Sheldon, Ryan, Deci, & Kasser (2004):** Series of studies -- goal contents and goal motives each contributed significant independent variance to predicting well-being. Extrinsic aspirations (wealth, fame, image) taken alone significantly negatively predicted well-being even after removing the effects of controlled reasons. In a longitudinal study, the aspirations of college seniors predicted changes in well-being over the subsequent year.
- **Carver & Baird (1998); Srivastava, Locke, & Bartol (2001):** Critics suggested the autonomous regulation confound explained the goal content effects. Sheldon et al. (2004) directly tested and refuted this -- both contribute independently.

### The Match of Personal Goals and Contextual Values

- Some have argued that holding goals consistent with one's social environment (the **match hypothesis**; Sagiv & Schwartz, 2000) would buffer negative effects of extrinsic goals. Research has not supported this:
- **Kasser & Ahuvia (2002):** Business school students -- same negative relations between extrinsic (relative to intrinsic) aspirations and well-being as in other samples
- **Vansteenkiste, Duriez, Simons, & Soenens (2006):** Compared business and education students -- business students rated extrinsic aspirations as more important, but the strength of extrinsic aspirations was nonetheless negatively related to well-being for both groups equally
- **Kasser (1996):** High-security prison residents -- where intrinsic aspiration for physical fitness was supported but self-acceptance and close relationships were not. Physical fitness aspiration related positively to well-being; self-acceptance and affiliation aspirations related negatively (frustration of holding goals one cannot satisfy).

### What If Extrinsic Goals Are Put to Virtuous Use?

- **Landry et al. (2016):** Three categories of reasons for pursuing money -- financial stability (e.g., family support), integrated reasons (e.g., charity), and nonintegrated reasons (e.g., to look better than others). Financial stability reasons were controlled. Integrated reasons predicted greater well-being and lesser ill-being via need satisfaction. Nonintegrated reasons predicted the opposite.
- **Frost & Frost (2000):** Romanians and Americans -- financial success correlated positively with psychological well-being and contributing to community for both. But for Americans, financial success was negatively associated with well-being (related to "power" and "security"), while for Romanians it was more related to "self-direction."
- **Key principle:** When an extrinsic goal like making money is being pursued for the sake of a larger intrinsic goal (giving to community), it is the latter that carries the predictive weight for well-being

### Beyond Wellness: Intrinsic and Extrinsic Goals in Relationships and Society

- **Kasser & Ryan (2001):** Individuals with high extrinsic aspirations experienced intimate relationships as more conflicted, less satisfying, and less trusting
- **Sheldon & Kasser (1995):** Strongly extrinsically oriented people reported being less empathic and more likely to use relationships instrumentally
- **Khanna & Kasser (1999):** Extrinsically oriented individuals more likely to report using friends to get ahead in life, and scored higher on Machiavellianism scales
- **Rodriguez, Hadden, & Knee (2015):** Consistency between intrinsic ideals and matching one's partner's intrinsic ideals strongly predicted high-quality relationship functioning; extrinsic ideal-actual consistency did not
- **Sheldon & McGregor (2000):** "Tragedy of the commons" experiment -- people with more extrinsic life goals were more likely to overharvest scarce resources. Cited *acquisitiveness* as a strong motive in the extrinsic group.
- **Brown & Kasser (2005):** Extrinsic goal pursuit negatively predicted pro-environmental behavior and predicted a larger environmental footprint
- **Sheldon, Sheldon, & Osbaldiston (2000):** Stronger extrinsic aspirations predicted more competition over cooperation when common resources were scarce
- **Duriez, Vansteenkiste, Soenens, & De Witte (2007):** Extrinsic relative to intrinsic goal pursuits associated with more racial and ethnic prejudices and greater tendency toward social dominance

### Extrinsic Aspirations and Materialism in Our Society

- The advertising industry pays billions annually to promote extrinsic aspirations (wealth, fame, image) -- the so-called American Dream
- **Dittmar, Bond, Hurst, & Kasser (2014):** Meta-analysis of 753 effect sizes from 259 independent samples -- materialism yielded an overall effect size of -0.24 on well-being, including more risky health behaviors and negative self-appraisals, mediated by low basic psychological need satisfaction
- **Hurst, Dittmar, Bond, & Kasser (2013):** Meta-analysis of 17 studies (3000+ participants) -- moderately strong negative relation between materialistic values and pro-environmental behaviors/attitudes
- **Unanue, Vignoles, Dittmar, & Vansteenkiste (2016):** UK and Chile -- intrinsic vs. extrinsic life goals predicted environmentally responsible behavior over a 2-year period; pro-environmental attitudes and identification with the environment by themselves did not
- **Pieters (2013):** 5-year longitudinal study in the Netherlands -- loneliness (lack of relatedness satisfaction) was a strong predictor of materialism, and materialistic individuals tended to become more lonely -- a **vicious cycle**
- **Otero-Lopez & Villardefrancos (2015):** Failure to attain intrinsic aspirations predicted people becoming compulsive buyers -- turning to extrinsic aspirations as a compensatory response

#### Material and Experiential Purchases
- **Howell & Hill (2009):** Materialist values negatively related to well-being; experiential purchases (trips, concerts) positively associated with wellness while materialistic purchases (bigger TV, new dress) were not. Mediated by enhanced satisfaction of relatedness need and diminished social comparison.
- **Carter & Gilovich (2012):** Experiential purchases were more closely related to people's sense of true self; observers believed they knew people's true selves better from experiential than material purchases.

### The Development of Extrinsic Aspirations

> *GCT Proposition V:* Individuals whose basic psychological needs have been neglected or frustrated in development are more prone to adopt need substitutes, such as extrinsic life goals, as being personally important. To the extent that they do so, their well-being will be compromised.

- **Kasser, Ryan, Zax, & Sameroff (1995):** Investigated developmental antecedents -- teenagers with mothers who were authoritarian, controlling, and cold placed relatively higher importance on wealth. Trained observers had rated these same mothers' coldness and rigidity with their 4-year-old children, and this index related significantly to the children's relative extrinsic aspirations assessed more than 10 years later.
- **Williams, Cox, Kouides, & Deci (1999):** High school students whose parents were low in autonomy support had significantly stronger relative extrinsic aspirations, which mediated engagement in health-compromising behaviors (tobacco, alcohol, marijuana)
- **Nurturing environments** that supply relatedness and autonomy support facilitate intrinsic aspirations. Parents who are autonomy-supportive and hold intrinsic aspirations tend to have children who emulate those values.
- **Yu, Assor, & Liu (2015):** *Inherent value demonstration* (IVD) -- parents who not only transmitted but authentically engaged in value-consistent behaviors had children who internalized intrinsic values more deeply and showed more self-congruence and subjective well-being
- **Wouters, Duriez, Luyckx, Colpin, Bijttebier, & Verschueren (2014):** Parents promoting intrinsic goals --> children with higher self-esteem via need satisfaction. Parents promoting extrinsic goals --> contingent self-esteem associated with introjected regulation.
- **Cultural influences:** Greater exposure to television predicts adoption of more extrinsic values (Kasser, 2002a), likely through pervasive celebrity role models focused on wealth, fame, and image

### Influencing People's Aspirations

- **Roth & Assor (1999):** Israeli students who perceived parents as valuing prosocial behaviors reported more autonomy and volition for prosocial actions
- **Lekes, Hope, Gouveia, Koestner, & Philippe (2012):** Students encouraged to write about and reflect upon intrinsic aspirations (close friends) rather than extrinsic aspirations (being popular) over a 4-week period evidenced greater well-being and gave greater priority to intrinsic aspirations at month's end

### The Framing of People's Goals

> *GCT Proposition VI:* Motivators can frame goals in more extrinsic versus intrinsic terms. The latter will be more likely to produce sustained engagement and, ultimately, wellness.

- **Vansteenkiste, Simons, Lens, Sheldon, & Deci (2004):** Three studies with Belgian college students. Factorial design: intrinsic vs. extrinsic goal content X autonomy-supportive vs. controlling climate. In each experiment, intrinsic goal framing produced greater engagement, extracurricular learning, and better conceptual performance. An additional interaction emerged: students in the intrinsic goal + autonomy-supportive context scored higher than expected from two main effects alone.
- **Vansteenkiste, Simons, Lens, Soenens, Matos, & Lacante (2004):** Exercise domain with three conditions (intrinsic, extrinsic, both). The expectancy-value position would predict best outcomes for the combined condition. GCT predicted intrinsic-only as best. Results confirmed GCT: intrinsic goal framing alone led to better performance and persistence than extrinsic framing or the combined condition.
- In the intrinsic goal condition, students' persistence was positively correlated with autonomous motivation. In the extrinsic condition, persistence was uncorrelated with autonomous motivation -- persistence resulted from less stable extrinsic reasons.

### All Goals Are Not Created Equal: Applying GCT to Any Goals

> *GCT Proposition VII:* Because all goals can be more or less linked to need satisfaction, the relation of personal goals of any type to wellness-related outcomes is a function of (or is mediated by) need satisfactions.

- **Dweck (2000); Elliot & McGregor (2001):** Learning (mastery) goals vs. performance goals -- performance goals have more risks and fewer benefits, and align with extrinsic orientation. Mastery goals are more autonomous.
- **Nicholls (1984):** Ego goals vs. task goals -- ego involvement is classically controlled, task involvement is more autonomous. These map onto GCT's extrinsic-intrinsic distinction.
- **Crocker (2008):** Eco-centric vs. ego-centric goals -- ego-centric goals focus on appearance, achievement, wealth and are regulated through controlling motivations and introjections; eco-centric goals are often intrinsic and better integrated.

### Mindfulness, Goal Pursuits, and Aspirations

> *GCT Proposition VIII:* Mindfulness, in promoting more integrated functioning, also conduces to a greater focus on intrinsic goal contents relative to extrinsic goal contents.

- **Brown & Kasser (2005):** Higher mindfulness related to greater intrinsic life-goal orientations and both subjective well-being and ecologically responsible behaviors
- **Brown, Kasser, Ryan, Linley, & Orzech (2009):** Series of studies -- mindfulness was associated with smaller financial desire discrepancy (gap between what one has and desires), which partially mediated the positive relations between mindfulness and well-being. Results held independent of economic status. Residential mindfulness meditation training was related to declines in financial desire discrepancy and increases in well-being.

### Well-Being: Living an Authentic, Mindful Life

The chapter concludes that autonomously pursuing and attaining intrinsic aspirations makes people happy, even though happiness is not the direct aim. Both BPNT and GCT show that people's selection of motives and aspirations is significantly influenced by experiences of need support or thwarting in development, but current choices still matter -- not all goals are created equal. Cultivating mindfulness can help individuals make more informed and integrated choices, contributing to flourishing.

## Contradictions or Tensions
- **GCT vs. expectancy-value theories:** Expectancy-value theories predict that attaining any valued goal yields satisfaction. GCT shows that attaining extrinsic goals yields no well-being gains (or negative ones) even when highly valued. Goal content matters independently of value placed on the goal.
- **Match hypothesis refuted:** Sagiv & Schwartz (2000) suggested extrinsic goals would be less harmful in environments that endorse them. Research consistently fails to support this -- extrinsic aspirations predict lower well-being even for business students, prison inmates with supported physical fitness goals, etc.
- **Nuance on extrinsic goals:** When extrinsic goals (e.g., money) are pursued for integrated, intrinsic reasons (e.g., family support, charity), they can relate positively to well-being (Landry et al., 2016). The hierarchical goal structure matters -- the superordinate purpose determines well-being effects.
