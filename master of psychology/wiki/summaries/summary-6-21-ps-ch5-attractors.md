# Pre-Suasion Ch.5: Commanders of Attention 1 — The Attractors — Summary

**Author:** Robert Cialdini
**Source:** *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade* (2016), Chapter 5
**Phase:** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **Three types of stimuli automatically seize attention because of evolutionary survival value: the sexual, the threatening, and the different.** These are "commanders of attention" because they commandeer focus without requiring a communicator's special effort — they draw attention to themselves by their nature.
2. **Sexual stimuli are powerful but conditional.** Sex in advertising works only for sexually related products (cosmetics, cologne, form-fitting clothing). Of *Advertising Age*'s top 100 ad campaigns of the 20th century, only 8 employed sexuality. Furthermore, sexual stimuli only capture attention from people currently in a romantic/sexual mindset — those not looking for a partner don't linger on attractive faces.
3. **Fear-based messaging works best when paired with actionable solutions.** Pure fear can trigger denial ("my grandfather smoked and lived to 80"). The most effective approach: present frightening facts *after* providing information about accessible assistance systems (programs, workshops, help lines) that allow recipients to manage their fear through genuine action rather than self-delusional denial.
4. **Threatening vs. sexual openers drive opposite persuasive strategies.** Fear-based openers make popularity-based appeals ("Join the crowd") more effective (because threat activates the desire to be part of a group for safety). Sexual openers make distinctiveness-based appeals ("Stand out from the crowd") more effective (because romantic interest activates the desire to differentiate oneself).
5. **Novelty and change are the most fundamental attention attractors.** Pavlov discovered that novel stimuli in the lab destroyed his carefully conditioned responses — the "investigatory reflex." The orienting response (O-wave) is a brain-wide pattern of electrical activity that flows across sectors associated with evaluation whenever we detect change.

## Detailed Notes

### The Sexual

- **Valentine Street study (France)**: Men asked for directions to "Valentine Street" (vs. "Martin Street") were twice as likely (40% vs. 20%) to then confront four young toughs to retrieve a woman's stolen phone. The sexually linked concept *Valentine* triggered bravado, but the woman's attractiveness alone was insufficient without the pre-suasive opener.
- **Sex sells selectively**: Sexual content works for products people buy for sexually related purposes (cosmetics, perfume, form-fitting clothing). It does not work for soft drinks, laundry detergents, or kitchen appliances.
- **Attentional prediction of breakups**: The best predictor of relationship dissolution was not love, satisfaction, or desired duration — it was how much partners noticed and were distracted by attractive members of the opposite sex.

### The Threatening

- **Dread risks**: After 9/11, many Americans switched from air to road travel. Highway fatalities are much higher, and an estimated 1,600 additional Americans died in auto accidents — six times the passengers killed in the only US commercial plane crash that year. Similar dread-driven switching occurred after the July 2005 London bombings, producing hundreds of additional bicycle casualties.
- **Fear + feasible action = behavior change**: A Dutch study showed that people informed of high vulnerability to hypoglycemia were *four times* more likely to sign up for a preventive diet workshop when the workshop information was included with the frightening health news. The key: they used the new belief (the workshop would help) rather than denial to manage anxiety.
- **Cigarette warnings**: Large, scary images on cigarette packages have had a double-barreled effect across 12+ countries — convincing nonsmokers to resist and smokers to stop — because they pair fear with actionable steps.

### The Evolutionary Matching Principle

Griskevicius and Cialdini's team realized that threatening and sexual stimuli activate opposite social motivations:
- **Threat** → desire to be part of a group (safety in numbers) → popularity-based appeals work ("Visited by over a million people each year")
- **Sexual arousal** → desire to stand out from the group (romantic differentiation) → distinctiveness-based appeals work ("Stand out from the crowd")
- **Museum ad experiment**: An ad stressing popularity was supercharged among people watching a violent movie but deflated among those watching a romantic one. A distinctiveness ad showed the reverse pattern.

### The Different: Change-O, Presto

- **Pavlov's investigatory reflex**: Pavlov's conditioned dogs failed to salivate for visitors because novel stimuli (new people entering) hijacked attention. Pavlov recognized this as the "investigatory reflex" — so forceful it supersedes all other operations.
- **Doorway effect**: Walking through doorways causes forgetting because the abrupt change in physical surroundings redirects attention and disrupts working memory.
- **Orienting response (O-wave)**: Brain-imaging research shows a pattern of electrical activity that flows across evaluative sectors whenever change is detected.
- **Film cuts**: A successful TV/film producer told Cialdini that shots provide content, but *cuts* are equally important — they swing attention pre-suasively to the most persuasive feature. However, TV advertisers have misunderstood this by increasing scene shifts indiscriminately (50%+ increase over the years), producing confused, irritated viewers with *less* memory for persuasive claims.
- **Distinctiveness-as-attention magnet**: Adding three competitors with durable cushions to a sofa comparison made the one sofa with *soft* cushions (the Dream) leap from 42% to 77% preferred choice — because it became the *distinctive* option, drawing attention and perceived importance.

## New Concepts Introduced

- [[commanders-of-attention]] — Stimuli that automatically seize attention: the sexual, the threatening, and the different
- Dread risks — Risky steps taken to avoid less risky but more focal threats
- Orienting response (O-wave) — Brain-wide evaluative response to detected change
- Evolutionary matching principle — Threat activates conformity motivation; sexual arousal activates differentiation motivation
