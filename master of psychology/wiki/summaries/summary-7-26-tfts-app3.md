# Think Faster Talk Smarter App.3: Make That (Im)perfect Pitch — Summary

**Author:** Matt Abrahams
**Source:** *Think Faster, Talk Smarter* (2023), Application #3
**Phase:** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **Use [[problem-solution-benefit]] for persuasive pitches.** Define the problem/pain point, present a feasible solution, and describe the benefits of adoption.
2. **A bit of imperfection invites collaboration.** Baba Shiv (Stanford): highly polished presentations invite criticism; leaving a small imperfection gives the audience something to improve, increasing buy-in. "If you go for advice, you're going to get advice. If you go for funding, you're going to get money."
3. **Frame benefits in positive terms and address obstacles proactively.** Loss aversion means evoking risk can turn people off. Acknowledge barriers to adoption and offer ways around them.
4. **Use analogies to make each step more concrete.** Compare problems, solutions, and benefits to things the audience already understands.

## Detailed Notes

### The Problem-Solution-Benefit Structure
- **Problem:** Clearly define the challenge, issue, or pain point your audience shares. Frame in terms they resonate with.
- **Solution:** Present a feasible, reasonable fix. Communicate accessibly. Itemize if complex.
- **Benefit:** Rank benefits starting with highest value. Specify and rank clearly.

### Bonus Structure (for pitching ventures)
Complete four sentence starters: "What if you could..." / "So that..." / "For example..." / "And that's not all..."

### Tips for Refining Pitches
1. **Use analogies** -- compare the problem/solution to familiar situations
2. **Offer consistent solutions** -- show precedent ("foot in the door" technique from Cialdini)
3. **Frame benefits positively** -- "75% success rate" beats "25% failure rate"
4. **Address obstacles** -- don't just pitch benefits; acknowledge barriers and offer solutions for them
5. **Tone down perfectionism** -- leave room for audience input and collaboration

### Parting Thought
Abrahams' 12-year-old son spontaneously pitched for an expensive guitar using a perfectly structured Problem-Solution-Benefit argument: (Opportunity) "Aren't you always encouraging me to be more creative?" (Solution) "I can teach myself to read music and practice in my room" (Benefit) "I will learn to play one of your favorite Carlos Santana songs." It worked.
