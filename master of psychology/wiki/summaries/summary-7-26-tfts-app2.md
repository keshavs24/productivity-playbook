# Think Faster Talk Smarter App.2: Toasts That Tantalize — Summary

**Author:** Matt Abrahams
**Source:** *Think Faster, Talk Smarter* (2023), Application #2
**Phase:** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **Use the WHAT structure for toasts, tributes, and introductions:** Why are we here (W), How are you connected (H), Anecdotes or learnings (A), Thank (T).
2. **Think of these occasions as gifts you give to the person being honored** -- not performances about you. Focus on the recipient.
3. **Be brief, emotional, audience-appropriate, and unified.** Long toasts are almost always bad. Keep anecdotes concise and relatable.
4. **Set others up for success.** Think of yourself as the opening act -- "clear the runway" so those who follow can take off smoothly.

## Detailed Notes

### The WHAT Structure
- **W -- Why Are We Here:** Identify the purpose/context of the gathering. Sets expectations and allows emotional expression.
- **H -- How Are You Connected:** Share who you are and your relationship to the honoree. Slip in background about the subject and perhaps humor.
- **A -- Anecdotes or Learnings:** Share stories, humor, emotion, and lessons. Keep them structured, appropriate, concise.
- **T -- Thank:** Express gratitude and well-wishes to the person, group, or event.

### Tips
1. Be brief and to the point -- share only enough to appropriately honor those involved
2. Prepare to be emotional -- have a plan if emotions flow (backup person, closing line ready)
3. Keep the spotlight on the subject -- minimize use of "I"
4. Make anecdotes accessible and appropriate for the whole audience
5. Strive for unity -- seek common ground, avoid polarizing content
6. Set others up for success -- handle logistics, leave people energized for what comes next

### Parting Thought: Edwina's Memorial
Abrahams tells of commemorating a deceased mentor in a spontaneous Zoom gathering. Using the WHAT structure, he kept remarks brief and meaningful -- conveying something authentic about Edwina while inviting others to share. The structure prevented emotional rambling and kept the focus on honoring the person.
