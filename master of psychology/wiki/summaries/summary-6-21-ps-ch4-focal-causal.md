# Pre-Suasion Ch.4: What's Focal Is Causal — Summary

**Author:** Robert Cialdini
**Source:** *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade* (2016), Chapter 4
**Phase:** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **What is focal is not only deemed important but also deemed causal.** Directed attention gives focal elements standing as causes — as answers to the question "Why?" If we see ourselves giving attention to something, we infer it must be a cause.
2. **The Oberholzer-Gee line-cutting study reveals hidden causality.** When people were offered money to cut in line, larger amounts produced more compliance — but almost no one took the money. The visible factor (money) appeared causal but wasn't; the hidden factor (the norm of social responsibility — "he must really need to get to the front fast") was the true driver.
3. **Camera angle bias in interrogations produces false perceptions of guilt.** Shelley Taylor's experiments showed that whichever discussant's face was more visible was judged to be more causal. Daniel Lassiter extended this: a camera pointed at the suspect (standard police practice) makes observers assign the suspect more responsibility for a confession, while a side-angle camera eliminates the bias.
4. **False confessions are a devastating real-world consequence of focal-is-causal.** The Peter Reilly case (1973) illustrates how a combination of focal attention on a suspect, fabricated evidence (false polygraph results), and exhaustive questioning can produce a detailed false confession from an innocent person. Of 125 cases involving fabricated confessions, 81% of suspects who recanted were still convicted.
5. **The "romance of leadership" is explained by focal-is-causal.** Leaders receive disproportionate credit and blame for organizational outcomes because they are visually prominent (the person at the top is psychologically salient). Business research shows other factors (workforce quality, market conditions) have greater impact than CEO actions, yet the average large-corporation employee earns 0.5% of the CEO's pay.

## Detailed Notes

### Taking a Chance — The Tylenol Lottery Numbers

In 1982, after the Chicago Tylenol cyanide poisonings, the production lot numbers of the affected bottles (2880 and 1910) became so heavily publicized that people began playing them as lottery numbers at unprecedented rates. Three states had to halt wagering due to "maximum liability levels." The numbers were ordinary, associated with death, yet people bet on them — because "what is focal is seen to have causal properties — to have the ability to make events occur."

### Taking a Life — Camera Angle and False Confessions

**Taylor's seating experiments**: Observers watched scripted conversations where neither partner dominated. Whichever person's face was more visible (due to the observer's seating angle) was judged to have "had more influence" and been "more causal." The effect was:
- Virtually unmovable and automatic
- Present regardless of gender, importance of the topic, or delay before judging
- Present whether conversations were viewed live or on videotape

**Lassiter's interrogation studies**: A camera aimed at a suspect during interrogation leads observers to assign the suspect greater responsibility for the confession (and greater guilt). The bias:
- Persists regardless of whether watchers are ordinary citizens, law enforcement, or criminal court judges
- Disappears when the camera shows the side view (both faces equally visible)
- Can be reversed when the camera is aimed at the interrogator

**Practical defense**: If ever in a police interrogation, find the camera (usually above and behind the officer) and *move your chair* so the recording shows both faces equally. "Don't allow the what's-focal-is-presumed-causal effect to disadvantage you at trial."

### Peter Reilly's False Confession

Peter Reilly, 18, returned home to find his mother murdered. Despite having no blood on him and weighing only 121 pounds, he was interrogated for 16 hours by rotating teams who: (1) lied about polygraph results ("Definitely. Definitely."), (2) suggested he had repressed the memory, and (3) pressed him to repeatedly imagine committing the crime until the imaginations became "reality." He signed a false confession. Two years later, hidden evidence placed him elsewhere at the time of the crime. The case illustrates the terrifying convergence of focal-is-causal effects: once a confession exists, it becomes the focal piece of evidence, and all other evidence is subordinated to it.

### The Romance of Leadership

CEOs and other leaders are accorded outsized causal responsibility for organizational outcomes because they are visually prominent. Business performance analysts call this the "romance of leadership." The person at the top is psychologically salient and therefore assigned an unduly causal role.

## Key Studies Referenced

- **Oberholzer-Gee (line-cutting study)**: Larger cash offers increased compliance (50% at $1 to 76% at $10) but almost no one accepted the money — the visible factor (money) was less causal than the hidden factor (norm of social responsibility)
- **Taylor et al. (seating/camera angle studies)**: Whichever discussant's face was more visible was judged more causal — practically unmovable, automatic, cross-gender, cross-context
- **Lassiter et al. (interrogation camera angle)**: Camera aimed at suspect increases perceived guilt; side angle eliminates bias; camera aimed at interrogator reverses bias
- **Peter Reilly case (1973)**: 18-year-old innocent person confessed to matricide after 16-hour interrogation using fabricated polygraph results and guided imagination
- **False confession meta-analysis (125 cases)**: 81% of suspects who confessed falsely and later recanted were still convicted at trial
