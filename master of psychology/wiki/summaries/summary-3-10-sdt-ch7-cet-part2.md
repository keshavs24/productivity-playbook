# SDT Ch.7: Cognitive Evaluation Theory, Part II — Summary

**Author(s):** [[ryan-deci|Richard M. Ryan & Edward L. Deci]]
**Type:** Book chapter
**Phase:** 3
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-14

## Key Takeaways
1. The **interpersonal context** in which events occur shapes their [[informational-vs-controlling|functional significance]] -- the same reward can enhance or undermine intrinsic motivation depending on the climate
2. [[autonomy-support]] vs. controlling orientations in teachers, managers, and parents directly predict the [[intrinsic-motivation]] of their students, employees, and children
3. **[[ego-involvement]]** is an intrapersonal controlling state that undermines intrinsic motivation even without external pressure -- people become their own controllers
4. CET Propositions IV and V extend the theory to interpersonal climates and intrapersonal events, respectively
5. Intrinsic motivation produces superior **learning outcomes** (deeper conceptual understanding, not just rote memorization) and higher **creativity** and **performance quality** on complex/heuristic tasks
6. Relatedness support plays a distal but important role in facilitating intrinsic motivation -- a secure relational base enhances exploration

## Detailed Notes

### Interpersonal Contexts and the Functional Significance of Events

The effects of events on intrinsic motivation depend on how individuals interpret them -- their **functional significance**. This is not random or whimsical; people have an ongoing readiness to interpret events in accordance with their basic psychological needs for [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and relatedness.

Key principle: It is not events per se but their **psychological meaning** that determines effects on intrinsic motivation. The same engagement-contingent reward is experienced as more controlling in a pressuring climate and more informational in a supportive one.

#### Interpersonal Contexts and External Events
- The interpersonal climate or ambience of a setting (classroom, home, workplace) influences how specific events are interpreted
- Climates characterized by autonomy support, competence support, and relatedness support produce informational interpretations
- Climates characterized by control, evaluation, and conditional regard produce controlling interpretations

**CET Proposition IV:** Interpersonal contexts can be characterized in terms of the degree to which the motivational climate tends to be controlling, autonomy supportive, or amotivating. This quality of the overarching interpersonal climate both directly impacts motivation and the likely interpretation or functional significance of specific events. Environments most facilitating of intrinsic motivation are those that support people's basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

### Orientations toward Autonomy Support versus Control

#### Deci, Schwartz, Sheinman, & Ryan (1981) -- Teacher Orientation Study
- Assessed motivational orientations of 36 fourth-through-sixth-grade teachers during summer break using "Problems in School" vignettes
- Teachers rated endorsement of controlling vs. autonomy-supportive solutions to student problems
- 2 months into school year: students with more controlling teachers already showed lower intrinsic motivation, perceived competence, and self-esteem
- Effects remained comparable 6 months later
- Follow-up study: Pre-selected highly controlling vs. highly autonomy-supportive teachers; students' intrinsic motivation changed rapidly and systematically within first weeks

#### Ryan & Grolnick (1986) -- Classroom Climate
- Students' perceptions of classroom climates as more origin-like (autonomy-supportive) vs. pawn-like (controlling) predicted intrinsic motivation, self-esteem, feelings of personal control, and perceived cognitive competence
- Projective stories reflected the motivational climate: children from controlling classrooms wrote stories with compliant or rebellious characters; autonomy-supportive classrooms produced self-initiating characters

#### Deci, Connell, & Ryan (1989) -- Workplace Study
- "Problems at Work" survey paralleling the school study
- Workers whose managers were more controlling expressed more alienation and lower job satisfaction
- Employees of controlling managers placed greater importance on extrinsic work factors (pay, promotions)

#### Deci, Driver, Hotchkiss, Robbins, & Wilson (1993) -- Mother-Child Study
- Mothers' autonomy-supportive vs. controlling communication styles during play predicted children's free-choice intrinsic motivation for the target activity
- Children rated the activity as more interesting when mothers were autonomy-supportive

#### Autonomy Thwarting vs. Merely Low Support
- Bartholomew, Ntoumanis, Ryan, Bosch, & Thogersen-Ntoumani (2011): Coaches' autonomy-supportive and autonomy-thwarting styles independently predicted need satisfaction/frustration in athletes
- Autonomy support predicted need satisfaction and positive outcomes; autonomy thwarting predicted need frustration, negative affect, and burnout symptoms
- Athletes of need-thwarting coaches showed elevated secretory immunoglobulin A (SIgA) -- a stress biomarker

### Interpersonal Style as a Moderator of External Events

#### Ryan, Mims, & Koestner (1983) -- Rewards + Climate Study
- Two groups received performance-contingent rewards; two no-reward groups received comparable positive feedback
- Half in each condition received feedback/rewards in a controlling style; half in an autonomy-supportive style
- Results: Controlling delivery lowered intrinsic motivation relative to autonomy-supportive delivery, regardless of reward condition
- Informationally administered performance-contingent rewards actually led to **higher** intrinsic motivation than a neutral no-reward condition

#### Ryan (1982) -- Feedback Style Study
- Positive feedback administered controllingly ("you did just as you should") vs. informationally
- Controlling delivery undermined intrinsic motivation regardless of whether feedback was self- or experimenter-administered

#### Koestner, Ryan, Bernieri, & Holt (1984) -- Limit Setting Study
- Limits set on second-grade children's painting behavior (keep art neat)
- Autonomy-supportive limit setting (minimizing controlling language, acknowledging feelings, providing rationale) maintained children's intrinsic motivation
- Controlling limit setting undermined it
- Key insight: Structure and limits can be provided **without thwarting autonomy**

### Perceived Intentions and Motives of the Motivator

#### Wild, Enzle, & Hawkins (1992) -- Tutor Motivation Study
- Music tutors described as either volunteers (intrinsic) or paid (extrinsic)
- Students who believed tutors were volunteers showed more interest, exploratory behavior, and intrinsic motivation
- Students who believed tutors were paid showed less

#### Social Contagion Effect
- Radel, Sarrazin, Legrain, & Wild (2010): In a physical education context, participants taught by instructors described as paid showed lower interest. Crucially, those participants then taught **new** students who also showed lower interest -- motivational attitudes are contagious across generations

#### Enzle & Anderson (1993) -- Surveillance Intent Study
- Surveillance perceived as evaluative/controlling undermined intrinsic motivation and autonomy
- Surveillance perceived as noncontrolling (curious observer) did not
- Ambiguous surveillance fostered distrust and was assumed controlling -- lowering intrinsic motivation

### Relatedness and Its Support

Relatedness plays a less proximal but important role in facilitating intrinsic motivation:

#### Anderson, Manoogian, & Reznick (1976) -- The Accidental Finding
- Children in a no-reward, no-feedback condition with a cold, unresponsive experimenter showed the **lowest** intrinsic motivation of any group
- The thwarting of relatedness need decimated intrinsic motivation

#### Attachment and Intrinsic Motivation
- Bowlby (1979): Felt security supports exploration (intrinsic motivation in infants)
- Bretherton (1987): Maternal autonomy support and attachment security both associated with exploratory behaviors
- Ryan, Stiller, & Lynch (1994): Students who felt more security with teachers reported more autonomous motivation and greater intrinsic motivation

#### Bao & Lam (2008) -- Choice and Relatedness Interaction
- Chinese children: Making own choices enhanced intrinsic motivation in general
- But children who felt close to adults showed no difference between self-chosen and adult-chosen activities -- closeness allowed them to endorse adult choices willingly
- Children who did not feel close showed less intrinsic motivation when adults made choices for them

#### Autonomy and Relatedness Synergy
- Costa, Ntoumanis, & Bartholomew (2015): A climate of relatedness conduces to feelings of **both** autonomy and relatedness
- When people are autonomy-supportive, they tend to take the other's perspective, which is experienced as caring -- autonomy and relatedness support co-occur

### Intrapersonal Events: Ego Involvement and Internally Controlling States

**CET Proposition V:** Intrapersonal events that bear on the initiation and regulation of behavior can differ in their functional significance. Internally informational events facilitate intrinsic motivation by facilitating I-PLOC and perceived competence. Internally controlling events pressure toward specific outcomes and facilitate E-PLOC, undermining intrinsic motivation. Internally amotivating events make salient incompetence and inability, diminishing both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.

#### Ego Involvement vs. Task Involvement

[[ego-involvement]] pertains to situations where people feel pressure to perform in ways valued by a reference group, with self-worth contingent on the outcome. Definitions (Greenwald, 1982):
1. Striving based in threats to esteem by others -- ego on the line regarding others' evaluation
2. Ego on the line regarding self-evaluation -- maintaining self-worth through demonstrated competence
3. Personal importance (without evaluative pressure) -- not inherently controlling

SDT is primarily concerned with definitions 1 and 2, where ego involvement creates internal pressure to prove worth.

**Task involvement** is the contrasting state: being interested in the task for its own sake, concerned with learning and mastery rather than proving worth.

#### Ryan (1982) -- Ego Involvement Experiment
- Participants introduced to hidden-figures puzzles in either ego-involving ("test of creative intelligence") or task-involving ("experimental puzzle task") conditions
- All received positive feedback
- Ego-involved participants showed **less** intrinsic motivation despite positive feedback and feeling competent
- The ego involvement was self-controlling, diminishing autonomy even when competence was confirmed

#### Replication and Extension
- Butler (1987), Koestner, Zuckerman, & Koestner (1987), Plant & Ryan (1985): All replicated the negative effect of ego involvement on intrinsic motivation
- Exception: Sansone (1986) used personal importance induction (Greenwald's third definition) which is not inherently controlling

#### Further Exploration of Internally Controlling States

##### Public Self-Consciousness
- Plant & Ryan (1985): Placing participants in front of a camera or mirror (inducing objective self-awareness) decreased intrinsic motivation
- Self-objectification creates evaluative stance toward oneself -- internally controlling
- Enzle & Anderson (1993): Controlling surveillance that was perceived as evaluative undermined intrinsic motivation

##### Internally Generated Pressure
- People can be as dictatorial to themselves as others are to them
- Self-pressuring, self-shaming, self-aggrandizing patterns derive from internalized social controls
- These intrapersonal processes result from internalization of contingent approval

#### Ego-Involvement and Negative Feedback

- Ryan (1982): Ego-involved participants given positive feedback felt competent but still showed lower intrinsic motivation (the ego satisfaction fulfilled the extrinsic goal)
- Anderson & Rodin (1989): Ego-involved participants given nonconfirming feedback showed **greater** free-choice persistence -- they persisted to prove self-worth, not out of intrinsic interest
- Baumeister & Tice (1985): Low-self-esteem participants given humiliating feedback showed highest persistence -- desperate attempt to salvage self-worth

#### Ryan, Koestner, & Deci (1991) -- Distinguishing Intrinsic from Ego-Involved Persistence
- Three experiments: Task-involved participants who got positive feedback showed more persistence **correlated** with interest/enjoyment (intrinsic motivation)
- Ego-involved participants who got nonconfirming feedback showed more persistence **uncorrelated** with interest -- they persisted to prove themselves, not from enjoyment
- Key diagnostic: Within-cell correlations between behavior and self-reported affect distinguish intrinsic from controlled persistence

#### Ego-Involved Winning and Losing
- Reeve & Deci (1996): Participants who lost a competition persevered (returning to old problems) but did not explore new ones -- internally controlled persistence
- Winners explored new problems -- intrinsic motivation
- Rawsthorne & Elliot (1999): Meta-analysis of 23 experiments confirmed ego involvement and performance goals led to less intrinsic motivation than task involvement and learning goals
- Under task involvement: confirming feedback led to more intrinsically motivated persistence
- Under ego involvement: nonconfirming feedback led to more persistence (controlled, not intrinsic)

### Intrinsic Motivation and Performance: When Interest Matters

#### Learning Outcomes

##### Benware & Deci (1984) -- Learning to Teach vs. Learning for a Test
- Students studying neuropsychology material to teach others (autonomy-supportive) vs. to be tested (controlling)
- Teaching group: significantly greater conceptual understanding and intrinsic motivation
- Test group: more rote memorization but no better at it after one week
- Rote memorization did not differ between groups

##### Grolnick & Ryan (1987) -- School Learning
- Children told they'd be tested and graded (controlling) vs. told to answer questions about interest (noncontrolling)
- Noncontrolling: more interest, greater conceptual understanding at both immediate and one-week tests
- Controlling: more rote memorization immediately, but **greater deterioration** of memorized material over the week

##### Cordova & Lepper (1996)
- Choice and personal relevance both led to enhanced intrinsic motivation and learning performance

##### Lewthwaite, Chiviacowsky, Drews, & Wulf (2015)
- Peripheral choice elements in motor learning enhanced both intrinsic motivation and performance, even 24 hours later

##### Gottfried, Marcoulides, Gottfried, & Oliver (2009)
- Longitudinal study: Task-intrinsic parenting practices (encouraging enjoyment) predicted higher motivation through age 17
- Task-extrinsic practices (external rewards contingent on performance) predicted adverse effects

#### Performance

##### McGraw & McCullers (1979) -- Rewards and Problem Solving
- Rewarded participants had harder time breaking cognitive set on insight problems
- Rewards created a rigid, focused approach antithetical to creative problem-solving

##### McGraw (1978) -- Heuristic vs. Algorithmic Tasks
- Task-contingent rewards impair performance on interesting, complex, heuristic tasks
- May improve performance on dull, uninteresting, algorithmic tasks

##### Fabes, Moran, & McCullers (1981)
- College students given WAIS problems: rewarded students performed significantly poorer on heuristic items but no different on algorithmic items

##### Ryan, Connell, & Plant (1990) -- Ego Involvement and Reading
- Ego-involved readers demonstrated less comprehension and recall than task-involved readers

##### Standage, Treasure, Hooper, & Kuczka (2007)
- Ego-involved participants showed greater self-handicapping prior to performance

#### Creativity

##### Amabile (1983) -- Intrinsic Motivation Hypothesis of Creativity
- People are more creative under conditions that conduce toward intrinsic motivation than under controlling conditions

##### Amabile (1979, 1982)
- Collages: Expected evaluation reduced creativity (artist-rated)
- Children: Competition for prizes reduced creativity

##### Joussemet & Koestner (1999)
- Rewards led children to generate less appropriate themes and produce less creative products

##### Choice and Creativity
- Amabile & Gitomer (1984): Choice about materials led to more creative products
- Koestner et al. (1984): Minimizing controlling language and acknowledging feelings facilitated children's creativity

##### Utman (1997) -- Meta-Analysis
- 24 experiments: Task-involvement and learning-goal conditions produced higher quality performance than ego-involvement and performance-goal conditions
- Advantage of intrinsic motivation limited to complex tasks requiring open, flexible, creative engagement

### Some Summary Notes

CET evolved from controversial research on rewards undermining intrinsic motivation. Key progression:
1. Early reward studies revealed the undermining effect
2. Other controlling external events (deadlines, evaluations, surveillance, competition) similarly undermined
3. The **functional significance** of events -- whether controlling, informational, or amotivating -- predicted effects
4. Interpersonal climate moderates functional significance: autonomy-supportive contexts buffer negative effects
5. CET extended to **intrapersonal** events: ego involvement and internally controlling states also undermine intrinsic motivation
6. Intrinsic motivation reliably produces better learning, creativity, and performance on complex tasks

## Contradictions or Tensions
- **Ego involvement paradox:** Ego-involved people can show **more** persistence than intrinsically motivated people, but their persistence is pressured, not interested. Behavioral measures alone can be misleading -- must examine within-cell affect-behavior correlations
- **Relatedness as proximal vs. distal:** For solitary activities, direct relatedness is not necessary for intrinsic motivation. But a secure relational base (distal) supports exploration. CET emphasizes autonomy and competence as proximal; relatedness as a facilitative background condition
- **Choice and culture:** Bao & Lam (2008) showed that for Chinese children who felt close to authority figures, adult-made choices were endorsed as willingly as self-made choices. This nuances Western emphasis on individual choice but is consistent with CET: it is the experience of autonomy (volition), not the act of choosing, that matters

## Quotes Worth Keeping
> "It seems that people can be as dictatorial to themselves as others can be to them. Indeed, astute clinicians, coaches, teachers, leaders, and parents can find manifold instances of people being self-pressuring and self-controlling -- people driven to achieve certain standards who are prideful and self-aggrandizing when they succeed and self-shaming and critical when they fail." -- Ryan & Deci, 2017, Ch.7

> "The art of motivation concerns how to value and support creative performances or learning and work endeavors without using controls in an attempt to produce or enhance them." -- Ryan & Deci, 2017, Ch.7
