# Pre-Suasion Ch.7: The Primacy of Associations — Summary

**Author:** Robert Cialdini
**Source:** *Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade* (2016), Chapter 7
**Phase:** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-15

## Key Takeaways

1. **All mental activity arises from associations.** "Just as amino acids can be called the building blocks of life, associations can be called the building blocks of thought." Influence attempts succeed to the extent that the associations they trigger are favorable to change.
2. **Language is primarily a mechanism of influence, not conveyance.** Psycholinguist Gun Semin's conclusion: the main purpose of speech is to direct listeners' attention to a selected sector of reality, where existing associations take over to determine the reaction.
3. **Mere exposure to words shapes subsequent behavior without awareness.** Exposure to violence-related words increased shock intensity by 48% in a subsequent task. Exposure to achievement-related words (*win, attain, succeed, master*) increased task performance and more than doubled willingness to keep working. Fund-raisers given a photo of a runner winning a race raised 60% more money.
4. **Metaphors are "meta-doors to change" because they channel associations.** Framing crime as a "beast" leads people to favor catch-and-cage (enforcement) solutions; framing the identical crime data as a "virus" leads to remove-unhealthy-conditions (societal reform) solutions. The difference from changing a single word (22%) was more than double the effect of the reader's gender (9%) or party affiliation (8%).
5. **Fluency drives liking, trust, and perceived truth.** Easy-to-pronounce names, rhyming slogans, and readable fonts all enhance positive evaluation. Companies with pronounceable names outperform those with hard-to-pronounce names on the stock exchange. "Caution and measure will win you riches" is judged more true than "Caution and measure win you treasure."

## Detailed Notes

### Thinking Is Linking

Associations are the fundamental building blocks of cognition. Influence training programs advise using language that "manages" recipients' thoughts, perceptions, and emotional reactions — but Cialdini refines this: we manage their mental *associations*, and their thoughts, perceptions, and emotions merely proceed from those.

### Speak No Evil, Leak No Evil — SSM Health's Language Policy

SSM Health, a Malcolm Baldrige Award-winning healthcare organization, systematically replaced violent language:
- "Bullet points" → "information points"
- "Attacking a problem" → "approaching a problem"
- Business "targets" → business "goals"
- "Beat the competition" → "outdistance or outpace them"

Initially Cialdini was skeptical ("Geez, this is silly"), but research convinced him ("Geez, this is smart"):
- Arranging violence-linked scrambled sentences increased subsequent shock intensity by 48%
- Arranging neutral sentences produced benign behavior ("door the fix" → "fix the door")

### Incidental Exposure to Words and Images

- **Achievement words**: Multiple studies show that subtly exposing people to words like *win, attain, succeed, master* increases task performance and doubles willingness to keep working
- **Achievement images**: Fund-raisers given information on paper carrying a photo of a runner winning a race raised 60% more money than those given the same information on plain paper
- **Call center posters**: Signs carrying words like OVERCOME, SUCCEED, PERSEVERE, ACHIEVE — previously dismissed by Cialdini as laughably useless — are actually supported by research

### Metaphor Is a Meta-Door (to Change)

- **Crime-as-beast vs. crime-as-virus** (Stanford study): Readers exposed to crime described as a "ravaging beast" recommended enforcement solutions; those exposed to "ravaging virus" recommended societal-reform solutions. The single-word change produced more than double the difference of gender or party affiliation.
- **Ben Feldman, greatest life insurance salesman**: Never pressured prospects — instead used metaphor. People didn't die, they "walked out" of life. "When you walk out, your insurance money walks in." Metaphor channeled associations to the moral responsibility of buying life insurance.
- **Physical metaphors**: Heavy clipboards make job candidates seem more serious; warm beverages make people feel warmer toward strangers and become more cooperative — metaphorical persuasion activated without a word, through touch alone.

### More Hot Stuff — Negative Associations

- A government official resigned after using the word *niggardly* (meaning "miserly") because of its phonetic associations
- Used-car salespeople say "preowned" (not "used") — bridges to possession rather than wear
- IT providers say "investment" (not "cost" or "price") — connects to gain rather than loss
- Airlines replace "your final destination" with "your destination" and "terminal" with "gate" to avoid death associations

### Implicit Egoism and the Self

- People prefer things that share letters with their own names — crackers, chocolates, teas
- Coca-Cola's UK "Share a Coke" campaign (replacing branding with 150 common first names on 100 million packs) produced the first increase in Coke sales in a decade
- *Rolling Stone* magazine ranked "Like a Rolling Stone" and "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction" as the #1 and #2 greatest rock songs — no other comparable list agreed, revealing self-name bias even in organizations

### The South Korean Taliban Negotiation

In 2007, the Afghan Taliban kidnapped 21 South Korean church workers. The key breakthrough: South Korea's intelligence chief replaced the negotiator (who used an Afghan translator) with a South Korean representative who spoke fluent Pashtun. "The key in the negotiations was language... When our counterparts saw that our negotiator was speaking their language, Pashtun, they developed a kind of strong intimacy with us." All 19 remaining hostages were freed.

### "Easy" Does It — Fluency

- **Rhyme enhances perceived truth**: "Caution and measure will win you riches" > "Caution and measure win you treasure"
- **Name pronounceability**: Attorneys with harder-to-pronounce names held lower positions in law firms (independent of foreignness). Hard-to-pronounce drugs and food additives are viewed less favorably.
- **Stock market**: Companies with easy-to-pronounce names and stock ticker codes outperformed those with hard-to-pronounce ones in initial trading
