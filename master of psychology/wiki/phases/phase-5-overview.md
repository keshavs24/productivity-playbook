# Phase 5: Negotiation

## Purpose

Phase 5 applies the psychological foundations of Phases 1-4 to the highest-stakes interpersonal challenge: negotiation. Where Phase 4 addressed one-to-many communication, Phase 5 addresses the interactive, adversarial, and collaborative dynamics of two-party and multi-party dealmaking. The central question: how do you create and claim value in negotiations while managing cognitive biases, emotional dynamics, and power asymmetries?

## Phase 5 Synthesis

Phase 5 presents three complementary negotiation paradigms. Fisher/Ury's principled negotiation provides the foundational framework: separate people from problems, focus on interests, invent options, use objective criteria. Malhotra/Bazerman extend this with strategic sophistication: investigative negotiation, ZOPA analysis, and influence tactics grounded in Phase 2's cognitive bias research. Voss provides the emotional/tactical layer: tactical empathy, mirroring, labeling, and calibrated questions — tools for high-stakes negotiations where rationality alone fails.

The key tension in Phase 5 is between Fisher/Ury's cooperative, rational approach and Voss's emotionally-grounded, sometimes manipulative tactics. The resolution: principled negotiation provides the ethical framework and strategic direction, while tactical empathy provides the interpersonal execution skills. Malhotra bridges the two with the negotiator's dilemma — you must both create AND claim value.
