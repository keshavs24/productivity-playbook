# Phase 4: Communication Foundations

## Purpose

Phase 4 bridges the internal psychology of Phases 1-3 to the external world of communication. Where earlier phases asked how people think, decide, and are motivated, Phase 4 asks: how do you craft messages that are understood, remembered, believed, and acted upon? The foundational question: what makes communication effective — from Aristotle's ancient rhetoric to modern evidence-based frameworks for sticky ideas and difficult conversations?

## Phase 4 Synthesis

Phase 4 provides four complementary lenses on effective communication. Aristotle's *Rhetoric* establishes the foundational taxonomy (ethos/pathos/logos) that all subsequent communication theory builds upon. Munter provides the strategic planning framework — who is the audience, what's the objective, how should the message be structured? The Heath brothers translate these principles into evidence-based practices for making ideas memorable and actionable (SUCCESs). Stone, Patton, and Heen address the hardest communication challenge: conversations where emotions run high, stakes are real, and identities feel threatened.

The phase reveals a powerful convergence: Aristotle's ethos maps to Munter's credibility framework and Heath's Credible principle. Aristotle's pathos maps to Heath's Emotional principle and the Feelings Conversation. Logos maps to Heath's Simple and Concrete principles. The curse of knowledge — the single biggest barrier to effective communication — explains why experts consistently fail at all three modes of persuasion.
