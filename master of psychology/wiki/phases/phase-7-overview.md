# Phase 7: Leadership Communication

## Purpose

Phase 7 culminates the curriculum by integrating everything into the practice of leadership communication. Where earlier phases built the science (cognition, decision, motivation) and the craft (rhetoric, negotiation, persuasion), Phase 7 asks: how do you wield these tools to build exceptional relationships, deliver transformative presentations, and communicate effectively under pressure? The central question: how does a leader communicate in a way that inspires action, builds trust, and creates lasting impact?

## Phase 7 Synthesis

Phase 7 addresses the three modes of leadership communication. Bradford and Robin focus on one-to-one relationship building — the foundation of influence without authority. Duarte provides the framework for one-to-many prepared communication — transforming presentations from information delivery into story-driven experiences that move audiences to action. Abrahams completes the picture with spontaneous speaking — the unscripted moments (Q&A, feedback, small talk, toasts) that often determine a leader's perceived competence and trustworthiness.

The convergence with earlier phases is clear: Duarte's sparkline is Aristotle's three-part oration updated for modern audiences. Abrahams's anxiety management draws on self-efficacy theory (Phase 3). Bradford's exceptional relationships require the autonomy support and need satisfaction from SDT (Phase 3). And all three draw on the foundational communication principles from Phase 4 (SUCCESs, ethos/pathos/logos, three conversations).

The curriculum is now complete — from cognitive foundations through applied leadership communication.
