# Jonah Berger

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 6 (Advanced Persuasion)
**Key Contribution:** Developed the STEPPS framework for understanding and engineering contagious content — why products, ideas, and behaviors catch on through word of mouth and social influence

## Overview

Jonah Berger is the James G. Campbell Assistant Professor of Marketing at The Wharton School at the University of Pennsylvania. His research focuses on the science of social influence, word of mouth, and viral marketing — specifically, what makes certain content, products, and ideas more contagious than others.

Berger's path to studying social influence was unconventional. Raised by parents who didn't believe in TV or sweets (offering educational rewards instead), he became fascinated with logic puzzles and math as a child. He initially planned to study environmental engineering but switched to psychology and sociology in college after realizing the same scientific toolkit used in hard sciences could be applied to understand human social behavior. He completed his PhD and joined the Wharton faculty, where he has spent more than a decade conducting research on social transmission.

What distinguishes Berger's approach is his commitment to rigorous, large-scale empirical research rather than anecdotal case studies. With collaborators including Katherine Milkman, Eric Schwartz, Raghu Iyengar, and others, Berger has analyzed hundreds of years of baby names, thousands of *New York Times* articles, millions of car purchases, hundreds of BzzAgent word-of-mouth campaigns, and countless viral videos and products. His work explicitly challenges the "influentials" thesis popularized by Malcolm Gladwell's *The Tipping Point*, arguing that the message matters more than the messenger — social epidemics are driven by the products and ideas themselves, not by a small handful of special influential people.

Berger's popular accounts have appeared in *The New York Times*, *The Wall Street Journal*, *The Washington Post*, *Science*, *Harvard Business Review*, *Wired*, *BusinessWeek*, and *Fast Company*. He has been named Wharton's "Iron Prof" for excellence in both scholarship and teaching.

## Key Ideas

- **[[stepps-framework]]:** The six-principle framework (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories) for making content contagious
- **[[social-currency]]:** People share things that make them look good — impression management drives word of mouth
- **[[environmental-triggers]]:** Top of mind leads to tip of tongue — environmental stimuli determine what gets talked about
- **[[high-arousal-emotions]]:** The critical variable in sharing is physiological arousal, not emotional valence — anger and anxiety drive sharing, while sadness suppresses it
- **[[behavioral-residue]]:** Physical traces of behavior create ongoing social proof and make the private public
- **[[practical-value]]:** People share useful information to help others; [[prospect-theory]] explains why some deals seem more shareable
- **[[trojan-horse-stories]]:** Stories are vessels that carry information; the key is "valuable virality" — making the brand integral to the narrative
- **Message over messenger:** Contagious content spreads regardless of who is doing the talking — challenging Gladwell's "influentials" thesis
- **Only 7% of word of mouth is online:** Offline, face-to-face conversations are far more prevalent and potentially more impactful than social media sharing
