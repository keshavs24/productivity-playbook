# Roger Fisher, William Ury, and Bruce Patton

**Type:** Person (group entry)
**Phase(s):** 5 — Negotiation
**Key Contribution:** Creators of [[principled-negotiation]], the four-principle method that transformed negotiation from an adversarial art into a structured discipline. Authors of [[getting-to-yes|Getting to Yes]], the most widely read negotiation book in the world.

## Overview

Roger Fisher, William Ury, and Bruce Patton have been working together since 1977 at the Harvard Negotiation Project, a research project at Harvard University that deals with all levels of negotiation — from interpersonal to international. Together they developed the method of principled negotiation (also called "negotiation on the merits"), which rejects the soft/hard dichotomy of positional bargaining in favor of a third way: be soft on the people, hard on the problem.

### Roger Fisher (1922-2012)
Williston Professor of Law at Harvard Law School and Director of the Harvard Negotiation Project. Fisher served in World War II with the U.S. Army Air Force, worked in Paris with the Marshall Plan, and practiced law in Washington with the Department of Justice and as a consultant to the Department of Defense. He was the originator and executive editor of the award-winning PBS series *The Advocates*. He consulted widely with governments, corporations, and individuals through Conflict Management, Inc.

Key works:
- *International Conflict for Beginners* (1969)
- *Dear Israelis, Dear Arabs* (1972)
- *International Crises and the Role of Law* (1978)
- *International Mediation: A Working Guide* (with Ury, 1978)
- *Getting Together: Building Relationships As We Negotiate* (1988)
- *Getting to Yes* (with Ury, 1981; 2nd ed. with Patton, 1991)

### William Ury
Director of the Negotiation Network at Harvard University and Associate Director of the Harvard Negotiation Project. Ury has served as a consultant and third party in disputes ranging from the Palestinian-Israeli conflict to U.S.-Soviet arms control to intracorporate conflicts to labor-management conflicts at a Kentucky coal mine. He holds degrees from Yale in Linguistics and Harvard in anthropology.

Key works:
- *Beyond the Hotline: How Crisis Control Can Prevent Nuclear War* (1985)
- *Getting Disputes Resolved* (with Brett and Goldberg, 1988)
- *Getting Past No: Negotiating with Difficult People* (1991)
- *The Third Side: Why We Fight and How We Can Stop* (2000)
- *The Power of a Positive No* (2007)

### Bruce Patton
Deputy Director of the Harvard Negotiation Project and Thaddeus R. Beal Lecturer on Law at Harvard Law School. Patton teaches negotiation to diplomats and corporate executives worldwide and works as a negotiation consultant and mediator in international, corporate, labor-management, and family settings. He co-founded the Conflict Management organizations in 1984.

Key works:
- *Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most* (with Stone and Heen, 1999) — also a key text in Phase 4 of this curriculum
- *Getting to Yes* (2nd edition, as full co-author, 1991)

## Key Ideas

- **[[principled-negotiation]]** — the four-principle method: separate people from problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, insist on objective criteria
- **[[batna]]** — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement, the measure of negotiating power
- **[[positions-vs-interests]]** — the central insight that positions mask underlying needs
- **[[negotiation-jujitsu]]** — deflecting positional attacks onto the problem
- **[[one-text-procedure]]** — single negotiating text iterated through criticism
- **[[separate-people-from-problem]]** — dealing with perception, emotion, and communication independently from substance
- **[[objective-criteria]]** — using fair standards and fair procedures

## Connections

- **Builds on:** The work draws on Howard Raiffa's decision analysis, the broader field of conflict resolution, and insights from international law and anthropology
- **Extended by:** Ury's *Getting Past No* (1991) extends the "what if they won't play" question into a full book. Patton's *Difficult Conversations* (1999) deepens the "separate people from problem" principle into a complete communication framework. Malhotra and Bazerman's *Negotiation Genius* further develops the analytical toolkit.
- **Contradicts:** The hard-bargaining tradition (e.g., Herb Cohen's *You Can Negotiate Anything*) and zero-sum approaches to negotiation. Also implicitly challenges pure game-theoretic models that assume perfect rationality.
