# Max H. Bazerman

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2, 5
**Key Contribution:** Leading researcher in judgment, decision making, negotiation, and behavioral ethics; author of *Judgment in Managerial Decision Making* (with Don A. Moore)

## Overview

Max H. Bazerman is the Jesse Isidor Straus Professor of Business Administration at Harvard Business School. He is one of the most influential scholars in the field of managerial decision making, bridging the gap between the cognitive biases research of [[tversky]] and [[kahneman]] and practical managerial application. His textbook *[[judgment-in-managerial-decision-making]]* (co-authored with Don A. Moore) has been a standard in MBA programs for decades.

Bazerman's distinctive contribution is the concept of [[bounded-awareness]] (with Dolly Chugh, 2005) -- the systematic failure to notice or focus on obvious, observable, and relevant data during decision making. He also made significant contributions to negotiation research, the winner's curse in auctions, and bounded ethicality.

## Key Ideas

- [[bounded-awareness]] -- the narrowing of attention that causes decision makers to overlook obvious, important, and readily available information (Bazerman & Chugh, 2005)
- The winner's curse in auctions and acquisitions -- the winning bidder in an auction of uncertain-value items has likely overpaid (Bazerman & Samuelson, 1983)
- The "Acquiring a Company" problem -- demonstrates that the rational offer is $0 despite the firm being worth more to the acquirer, due to information asymmetry
- Bounded ethicality -- our ethics are limited in ways of which we are unaware
- Three-part taxonomy of overconfidence (with Moore): [[overprecision]], [[overestimation]], [[overplacement]]
- Multiparty ultimatum games (with Messick and Moore) -- demonstrating bounded awareness of rules and others' decisions
- Slippery slope of unethical behavior (with Gino, 2009) -- gradual ethical lapses are harder to detect than sudden ones

## Connections
- **Builds on:** [[kahneman]], [[tversky]], Herbert Simon (bounded rationality), Richard Thaler (bounded willpower/self-interest)
- **Extended by:** Don A. Moore (co-author), Dolly Chugh (bounded awareness), Francesca Gino (bounded ethicality)
- **Applied in:** Negotiation (Phase 5), managerial decision making, behavioral ethics, organizational behavior

## Negotiation Genius Contributions

As co-author (with [[malhotra|Deepak Malhotra]]) of *[[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]]* (2007), Bazerman applies his behavioral decision research directly to negotiation practice:

- **Five-step preparation framework** for value claiming: assess BATNA, calculate [[reservation-value]], assess other party's BATNA, calculate their RV, evaluate [[zopa]] ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **Value creation through logrolling, adding issues, and [[contingent-contracts]]** ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **[[investigative-negotiation]]** as the practical methodology for overcoming [[fixed-pie-bias]] ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch3-investigative]])
- **[[influence-strategies-in-negotiation]]** — eight offensive and six defensive strategies ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch7-influence]])
- **[[negotiating-from-weakness]]** — strategies for weak-BATNA situations including coalition building and leveraging extreme weakness ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])
