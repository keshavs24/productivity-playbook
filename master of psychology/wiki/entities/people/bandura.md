# Albert Bandura

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 3
**Key Contribution:** Originator of social cognitive theory and self-efficacy theory; demonstrated that beliefs about personal capabilities are central determinants of human motivation, affect, and action

## Overview

Albert Bandura (1925-2021) was a Canadian-American psychologist at Stanford University who is widely regarded as one of the most influential psychologists of the 20th century. He is the originator of social cognitive theory (originally social learning theory) and the construct of [[self-efficacy]] -- perceived beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute courses of action required to produce given attainments.

Bandura's work represents a major departure from both the behaviorist tradition (Skinner, Watson) and trait-based personality theories. His framework of [[reciprocal-determinism]] posits that personal factors, behavior, and environmental events all influence each other bidirectionally, rejecting both pure environmentalism and pure personalism. His concept of [[human-agency]] positions people as proactive, self-organizing, self-reflecting, and self-regulating agents rather than passive reactors to environmental forces or hosts of internal mechanisms orchestrated by environmental events.

His 1997 book *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* ([[self-efficacy-exercise-of-control]]) is the definitive statement of self-efficacy theory, systematically distinguishing the construct from rival frameworks (self-concept, self-esteem, locus of control, effectance motivation, control beliefs) and documenting the massive body of empirical evidence supporting the causal role of efficacy beliefs in human functioning.

## Key Ideas

- [[self-efficacy]] -- Beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute courses of action; the key factor of human agency
- [[reciprocal-determinism]] -- Triadic reciprocal causation among personal factors (P), behavior (B), and environment (E)
- [[human-agency]] -- People as proactive agents who have a hand in shaping their own lives and environments
- [[efficacy-expectations-vs-outcome-expectations]] -- The critical distinction between beliefs about capability and beliefs about consequences
- [[generative-capability]] -- Self-efficacy as a dynamic, integrative capability, not a fixed trait or repertoire of behaviors
- [[self-efficacy-dimensions]] -- Efficacy beliefs vary in level (magnitude), strength, and generality
- Collective efficacy -- Shared belief in group capabilities as an emergent group-level attribute
- Four sources of self-efficacy: enactive mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion, physiological/affective states (detailed in Ch.3)

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Social learning theory (his own earlier work), Tolman's cognitive views of learning, White's effectance motivation (critiqued and refined)
- **Critiques:** Skinner's radical behaviorism, Rotter's [[locus-of-control]], trait theories of personality, Freudian psychodynamics
- **Extended by:** Research on self-regulated learning (Zimmerman), collective efficacy in organizations, health behavior change models
- **Applied in:** Education, health promotion, clinical psychology, organizational behavior, athletic performance, career development
