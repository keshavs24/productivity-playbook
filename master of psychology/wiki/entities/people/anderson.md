# John R. Anderson

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Cognitive architecture (ACT-R), textbook synthesis of cognitive psychology research on problem-solving, expertise, reasoning, and decision-making

## Overview

John R. Anderson is a cognitive psychologist at Carnegie Mellon University, best known for developing the ACT-R (Adaptive Control of Thought—Rational) cognitive architecture — one of the most influential computational models of human cognition. His textbook *Cognitive Psychology and Its Implications* is a comprehensive synthesis of the field, covering perception, memory, problem-solving, expertise, reasoning, and decision-making.

In the context of this curriculum, Anderson's work provides the cognitive science foundations that underpin the heuristics-and-biases program. Where Kahneman and Tversky document *what* goes wrong in human judgment, Anderson explains *how* the cognitive machinery works — problem spaces, means-ends analysis, chunking, proceduralization — giving a mechanistic account of both competence and error.

## Key Ideas

- [[problem-space]] — Newell & Simon's framework for representing problems as initial states, goal states, and operators
- [[means-ends-analysis]] — Core problem-solving heuristic: identify largest difference between current and goal state, find operator to reduce it
- [[chunking]] — Grouping information into meaningful units; the basis of expert pattern recognition
- [[deliberate-practice]] — Structured, feedback-rich practice as the engine of expertise (draws on Ericsson)
- [[expert-intuition]] — Rapid pattern-based judgment built from thousands of hours of chunked experience
- [[proceduralization]] — Conversion of declarative knowledge into efficient procedural skill
- [[mental-model-theory]] — Reasoning via constructed internal simulations rather than formal logic (draws on Johnson-Laird)
- [[bayesian-conservatism]] — Tendency to under-update beliefs relative to Bayes' theorem
- [[iowa-gambling-task]] — Decision paradigm revealing somatic/emotional signals guide choice (draws on Damasio)
