# Nancy Duarte

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Key Contribution:** Codified the structure of transformative presentations by discovering that great speeches and presentations share a common "presentation form" — an alternating pattern of *what is* and *what could be* — and developed the [[sparkline]] as a visual analysis tool to map this form

## Overview

Nancy Duarte is the CEO of Duarte Design, one of the largest presentation design firms in Silicon Valley, which she co-founded with her husband Mark Duarte. Over two decades and dozens of economic cycles, she has attracted extraordinary talent and established the firm as an industry leader, working with half of the world's top fifty brands and many of its most innovative thinkers.

What makes Duarte unique among communication experts is her empirical, pattern-recognition approach to presentations. Rather than offering generic tips, she studied thousands of presentations her firm had created, then turned to screenwriting, literature, mythology, and philosophy to uncover *why* certain presentations caused change and spread broadly. Her breakthrough came when she mapped Steve Jobs's 2007 iPhone launch and Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech onto a shape she had sketched — both mapped perfectly to the same form. She describes crying when this mystery was revealed.

Duarte's first book, *Slide:ology* (2008), focused on visual design of slides. *[[resonate]]* (2010) is its prequel in concept — addressing the deeper problem that comes before slide design: structuring the presentation itself as a story that creates resonance with the audience. Her codified methodology is called the Visual Story methodology.

## Key Ideas

- **[[sparkline]]**: A visual analysis tool that maps the contour of a presentation, showing how content moves between *what is* and *what could be*, with overlaid indicators for emotional contrast, delivery contrast, audience engagement (laughter, applause), and verbal cues
- **[[what-is-what-could-be]]**: The fundamental contrast structure driving all great presentations — alternating between the audience's current reality and the presenter's proposed future
- **[[audience-as-hero]]**: The critical mindset shift that the presenter is the mentor (Yoda), not the hero (Luke Skywalker) — the audience is the hero who will do the heavy lifting
- **[[big-idea]]**: The one key message with three components — unique point of view, clear stakes, complete sentence form
- **[[star-moment]]**: Something They'll Always Remember — a deliberately planned dramatic moment with five subtypes (memorable dramatization, repeatable sound bites, evocative visuals, emotive storytelling, shocking statistics)
- **Presentation Form**: The structural template for presentations with a beginning (what is), two turning points (call to adventure and call to action), a middle (alternating contrast), and an end (new bliss)
- **Nine Rules of Resonance**: (1) Resonance causes change, (2) Story has exponential effect on outcomes, (3) Tune to the audience's frequency, (4) Audiences persist at rest unless compelled, (5) Big idea filters all other frequencies, (6) Structure is greater than the sum of its parts, (7) Memorable moments cover longer distances, (8) Audience interest is proportionate to preparation, (9) Presentations create change when audiences act
