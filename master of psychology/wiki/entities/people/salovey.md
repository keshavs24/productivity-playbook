# Peter Salovey

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 1
**Key Contribution:** Co-originator (with [[mayer|John D. Mayer]]) of the scientific concept of emotional intelligence; defined EI as a measurable set of abilities involving emotional appraisal, regulation, and utilization.

## Overview

Peter Salovey is a psychologist at Yale University who, together with John D. Mayer, published the foundational 1990 paper defining emotional intelligence as a formal psychological construct. Before Salovey and Mayer's work, research on emotion-related skills was scattered across disconnected literatures (alexithymia, nonverbal perception, empathy, mood regulation, affect and cognition). Their contribution was to unify these under a coherent framework and argue that emotional abilities constitute a genuine form of intelligence — a subset of social intelligence as originally conceived by Thorndike.

Salovey's work at Yale focused on the intersection of emotion and cognition, including research on meta-experiences of mood (how people monitor and evaluate their own moods), mood and health-relevant cognitions, and social comparison and jealousy. He co-developed the State and Trait Meta-Mood Scales (SMMS, TMMS), key instruments for measuring emotional self-awareness and regulation.

## Key Ideas

- [[emotional-intelligence]] — The overarching construct unifying emotional appraisal, regulation, and utilization
- [[emotional-intelligence-model]] — The three-branch framework (appraisal/expression, regulation, utilization)
- [[emotion-regulation]] — Meta-experiences of mood as a regulatory system; mood maintenance and mood repair
- [[emotional-perception]] — Verbal and nonverbal appraisal of emotion in self and others
- [[emotional-facilitation]] — Using emotion to enhance cognition: flexible planning, creative thinking, redirected attention, motivation
- [[empathy]] — Included as a core component of emotional perception of others

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[attribution-theory]] (understanding others' emotional states requires causal reasoning), Gardner's theory of multiple intelligences (personal intelligences), Thorndike's social intelligence
- **Extended by:** Goleman's popular treatment of EI (1995); Salovey and Mayer's own later four-branch ability model (1997)
- **Contradicts:** Traditional view of emotions as disorganized interruptions of cognition
- **Applied in:** Clinical assessment of alexithymia, emotional competence training, leadership development
