# Solomon Asch

**Type:** Person — Social Psychologist
**Phase(s):** 1
**Key Contribution:** Pioneered research on impression formation ([[primacy-effect]]) and conformity (line judgment studies). Demonstrated that the order of information fundamentally shapes how people are perceived.

## Overview

Solomon Asch (1907–1996) made foundational contributions to two areas of social psychology: impression formation and conformity. His 1946 impression formation studies showed that the same set of traits, presented in different orders, produced dramatically different impressions — establishing the [[primacy-effect]]. His conformity studies (1951) showed that people would give obviously wrong answers to match a unanimous group — one of the most famous demonstrations in all of psychology.

## Key Ideas
- [[primacy-effect]] — First information disproportionately shapes overall impression (1946)
- Conformity — people yield to group pressure even for obviously incorrect judgments (1951)

## Connections
- **Influenced:** All subsequent research on impression formation, social influence, and conformity
- **Connects forward to:** [[cialdini]]'s social proof principle (Phase 1/6)
