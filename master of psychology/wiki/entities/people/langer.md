# Ellen J. Langer

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Discovered the illusion of control -- the tendency to behave as if one can influence purely chance outcomes when skill-related cues are present

## Overview

Ellen J. Langer is a social psychologist who conducted her early career at Yale University before joining Harvard University, where she became the first woman to receive tenure in the psychology department. She is best known in the judgment and decision making literature for her seminal research on the [[illusion-of-control]] -- demonstrating that people systematically overestimate their ability to influence chance outcomes when situations incorporate features associated with skill (competition, choice, familiarity, involvement).

Langer's later career focused on mindfulness and the psychology of aging, but her illusion of control research remains foundational to the heuristics and biases program.

## Key Ideas

- [[illusion-of-control]] -- People treat chance events as controllable when skill-related cues are present
- Skill-chance confusion is driven by both motivation (need for mastery) and genuine difficulty discriminating the two
- Competition, choice, familiarity, and involvement are the key triggers that induce skill-oriented processing in chance contexts
