# Edward L. Deci

**Type:** Person -- Motivational Psychologist
**Phase(s):** 3
**Key Contribution:** Co-founder (with [[ryan|Richard M. Ryan]]) of [[self-determination-theory-framework|Self-Determination Theory]]; pioneer of early empirical research on the effects of rewards on [[intrinsic-motivation]].

## Overview

Edward L. Deci (1942-2017) was a psychologist at the University of Rochester whose early experiments on the effects of external rewards on intrinsic motivation launched one of the most productive research programs in motivational psychology. Together with [[ryan|Richard M. Ryan]], he developed Self-Determination Theory over more than four decades.

Deci's seminal 1971 experiments demonstrated that extrinsic rewards (money) could undermine intrinsic motivation for interesting activities -- a finding that challenged the fundamental assumptions of both operant theory and drive theory. This work, along with Lepper, Greene, and Nisbett's (1973) parallel findings, became known as the "overjustification effect" and catalyzed decades of research and debate.

From these early experimental findings, Deci and Ryan progressively elaborated SDT's theoretical framework: first formulating Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET; Deci & Ryan, 1980a) to explain the effects of external events on intrinsic motivation, then extending to Organismic Integration Theory (OIT; Ryan, Connell & Deci, 1985), and eventually developing the full six-mini-theory structure of SDT. Their 1985 book *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* was the first comprehensive theoretical statement of SDT.

## Key Ideas

- [[intrinsic-motivation]] -- Deci's early experiments (1971, 1975) were foundational
- [[extrinsic-motivation]] -- The differentiated taxonomy of extrinsic regulatory styles
- [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- Competence, autonomy, relatedness as universal needs
- [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation]] -- The fundamental distinction between motivation types
- Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET) -- The effects of rewards, feedback, and external events on intrinsic motivation
- General Causality Orientations Scale (Deci, 1985a) -- Personality measure for autonomy, control, and impersonal orientations

## Connections

- **Collaborated with:** [[ryan]] -- decades-long partnership developing SDT
- **Built on:** Robert White (effectance motivation, 1959), Richard de Charms (personal causation, 1968), Fritz Heider (attribution theory, 1958)
- **Extended by:** Researchers worldwide applying SDT to education, health care, sport, work, and virtual environments
- **Contradicts:** B.F. Skinner (all behavior is reinforcement-controlled), Cameron & Pierce (1994, who argued rewards do not undermine intrinsic motivation)
