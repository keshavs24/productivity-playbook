# Anthony Pratkanis

**Type:** Person — Social Psychologist
**Phase(s):** 6
**Key Contribution:** Co-author of *Age of Propaganda*; researcher on persuasion, attitude change, and propaganda techniques including the phantom alternative, the pique technique, and the four stratagems of influence.

## Overview

Anthony R. Pratkanis is a social psychologist at the University of California, Santa Cruz. Together with [[aronson|Elliot Aronson]], he authored *Age of Propaganda: The Everyday Use and Abuse of Persuasion*, a comprehensive analysis of how persuasion and propaganda techniques operate in everyday life. Pratkanis's research contributions include the [[phantom-alternative]] (how unavailable options shift preferences), the pique technique (how unusual requests break [[mindlessness-in-persuasion]]), and the [[pre-persuasion-tactics|four stratagems of influence]] framework.

Pratkanis and Aronson's central argument is that modern democracy faces a dilemma: citizens are cognitive misers operating in a message-dense environment that promotes [[elaboration-likelihood-model|peripheral processing]], making them vulnerable to propaganda. Their book catalogs the techniques of both propagandists and ethical persuaders, arguing that the most important defense is understanding how these techniques work.

- [[pre-persuasion-tactics]] — The first of the four stratagems of influence
- [[phantom-alternative]] — How unavailable options shift preferences and decision criteria
- [[mindlessness-in-persuasion]] — The pique technique for breaking mindless scripts
- [[granfalloon]] — Meaningless group identity as a propaganda tool
- [[fear-appeal]] — The four conditions for effective fear-based persuasion
- [[inoculation-theory]] — Building resistance to persuasion through pre-exposure
- [[propaganda-vs-persuasion]] — The ethical distinction between truncating thought and encouraging it
