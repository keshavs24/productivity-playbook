# Dan Lovallo

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Co-developed (with [[kahneman]]) the inside view/outside view framework for understanding systematic biases in organizational forecasting and risk-taking.

## Overview

Dan Lovallo is a behavioral economist and strategy scholar who has made important contributions to the understanding of cognitive biases in managerial decision-making. His most influential work, co-authored with Daniel Kahneman, is "Timid Choices and Bold Forecasts: A Cognitive Perspective on Risk Taking" (1993), which identifies two conflicting biases that stem from the tendency to treat decision problems as unique: overly optimistic forecasts (from adopting the [[inside-view-outside-view|inside view]]) and excessive risk aversion (from [[narrow-framing]]).

Lovallo is also a co-author with Colin Camerer of "Overconfidence and Excess Entry: An Experimental Approach" (Ch. 23 in *Choices, Values, and Frames*), which experimentally demonstrates that overconfidence about relative skill leads to excessive market entry -- a phenomenon they call "reference group neglect."

## Key Ideas

- **Inside view vs. outside view:** The central distinction between case-specific forecasting (which produces optimistic bias) and reference-class forecasting (which is statistically superior but psychologically unnatural) ([[inside-view-outside-view]])
- **Timid choices and bold forecasts:** Two conflicting biases that partially but unreliably cancel each other out; both stem from treating problems as unique ([[summary-2-07-cvf-ch22-timid-choices]])
- **Organizational amplification of bias:** Organizations do not reliably correct individual cognitive biases; adversarial resource competition, groupthink, and blame asymmetry may amplify them
- **Reference group neglect:** Self-selected entrants into competitive settings fail to account for the fact that their competitors are similarly self-selected and similarly overconfident (with [[camerer]])
