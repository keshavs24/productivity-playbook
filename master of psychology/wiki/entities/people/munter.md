# Mary Munter

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 4 (Communication Foundations)
**Key Contribution:** Developed a comprehensive strategic framework for managerial communication built around five interactive variables: communicator, audience, message, channel choice, and culture

## Overview

Mary Munter is a Professor of Management Communication at the Tuck School of Business at Dartmouth College. She is the primary author of *[[guide-to-managerial-communication|Guide to Managerial Communication]]* (co-authored with Lynn Hamilton), now in its 10th edition, which is one of the most widely used textbooks in MBA communication courses.

Munter's central contribution is translating classical rhetoric (particularly [[ethos]], [[pathos]], and [[logos]]) and leadership communication theory (particularly the Tannenbaum-Schmidt leadership continuum) into a practical, actionable framework for business professionals. Her [[communication-strategy-framework]] organizes strategic communication around five interactive variables, and her [[tell-sell-consult-join]] model gives communicators a clear vocabulary for matching communication style to objective.

What distinguishes Munter's approach is its emphasis on the **audience** as the starting point and measure of success. Rather than asking "What do I want to say?", her framework begins with "What response do I want from my audience?" -- making communication inherently strategic rather than expressive.

## Key Ideas

- **[[communication-strategy-framework]]**: Five interactive variables (communicator, audience, message, channel, culture) for planning any communication
- **[[tell-sell-consult-join]]**: Four communication styles on a spectrum from speaker control to audience involvement
- **[[credibility-in-communication]]**: Five-factor model of credibility (rank, goodwill, expertise, image, common ground) distinguishing initial from acquired credibility
- **[[direct-vs-indirect-organization]]**: BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) as the default in 90%+ of business communication, with indirect approach reserved for specific constraints
- **[[audience-analysis]]**: Systematic four-question analysis of audience identity, knowledge, emotions, and persuadability
- **Audience Memory Curve**: Visual representation of primacy/recency effect applied to message structure
- **High Skim Value (HSV)**: Document design principle using headings, white space, and typography so busy readers can scan quickly
- **WIIFT ("What's In It For Them")**: Audience-centered persuasion technique emphasizing benefits from the audience's perspective

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[aristotle]] (rhetoric, [[three-modes-of-persuasion]]), Tannenbaum and Schmidt (leadership continuum), French and Raven (social power theory), Kotter (power and influence)
