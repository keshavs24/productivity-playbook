# Daniel Kahneman

**Type:** Person — Cognitive Psychologist / Behavioral Economist
**Phase(s):** 1, 2
**Key Contribution:** Co-founder (with [[tversky]]) of the heuristics and biases program; Nobel Prize in Economics (2002) for integrating psychological research into economic science.

## Overview

Daniel Kahneman (1934–2024) was a psychologist whose work with [[tversky|Amos Tversky]] transformed the understanding of human judgment, decision-making, and rationality. Born in Tel Aviv and raised in France during WWII, Kahneman spent his early career at Hebrew University before moving to the University of British Columbia, then Princeton.

Kahneman and Tversky's 1974 *Science* paper on heuristics and biases demonstrated that human judgment under uncertainty is governed by mental shortcuts — [[representativeness]], [[availability]], and [[anchoring-and-adjustment]] — that produce systematic, predictable errors. Their 1979 paper on [[prospect-theory]] showed that people evaluate outcomes relative to reference points, are loss-averse, and weight probabilities non-linearly — overturning the expected utility model that dominated economics.

Kahneman received the Nobel Prize in Economics in 2002 (shared with Vernon Smith), the prize committee noting his work "integrated insights from psychological research into economic science, especially concerning human judgment and decision-making under uncertainty." Tversky, who died in 1996, could not share the prize.

His 2011 book *Thinking, Fast and Slow* synthesized decades of research into the dual-process framework ([[system-1-system-2]]) and became a global bestseller, bringing behavioral science to a mass audience.

## Key Ideas
- [[representativeness]] — Judging probability by similarity (co-developed with Tversky)
- [[availability]] — Judging frequency by ease of recall (co-developed with Tversky)
- [[anchoring-and-adjustment]] — Estimating by insufficient adjustment from an anchor (co-developed with Tversky)
- [[prospect-theory]] — Loss aversion, reference dependence, probability weighting (1979 Econometrica paper)
- [[loss-aversion]] — Losses loom ~2-2.5x larger than equivalent gains
- [[framing-effects]] — Same choice, different presentation, different preferences
- [[value-function]] — S-shaped function mapping gains/losses to subjective value
- [[decision-weights]] — Non-linear probability weighting
- [[system-1-system-2]] — Dual-process theory of cognition (upcoming: Phase 2 sources)
- [[heuristics-and-biases-program]] — The overarching research program

## Connections
- **Collaborated with:** [[tversky]] — decades-long partnership; the most consequential collaboration in behavioral science
- **Influenced:** Behavioral economics as a field, [[cialdini]], [[bazerman]], [[ariely]], Richard Thaler, Cass Sunstein, and virtually every researcher in judgment and decision-making
- **Extended by:** Thaler & Sunstein's "Nudge" program, Bazerman's managerial applications, Ariely's consumer behavior research
