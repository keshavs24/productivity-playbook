# Dan Ariely

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Leading behavioral economist who demonstrates through vivid experiments that human irrationality is systematic, predictable, and pervasive -- not random noise around rational behavior.

## Overview

Dan Ariely is James B. Duke Professor of Psychology and Behavioral Economics at Duke University. His research career was shaped by a devastating accident at age 18 when a magnesium flare explosion left 70% of his body covered with third-degree burns. Three years of hospitalization led him to observe and question the systematic errors in how people (including medical professionals) make decisions.

After studying at Tel Aviv University (where Professor Hanan Frenk introduced him to empirical research), Ariely pursued behavioral economics -- the intersection of psychology and economics that challenges the assumption of human rationality. His experiments are notable for their creativity, accessibility, and real-world relevance, covering topics from pricing and consumer behavior to procrastination, honesty, and the influence of emotions on decision-making.

Ariely's central thesis: we are not merely irrational -- we are **predictably irrational**. Our mistakes are systematic, repeated, and can be anticipated and studied scientifically. This predictability means our environments can be designed to help us make better decisions.

## Key Ideas

- **[[arbitrary-coherence]]:** Initial prices are arbitrary but become internally coherent reference points for all future valuations
- **[[decoy-effect]]:** Adding an inferior option systematically shifts preference toward the option it resembles
- **[[zero-price-effect]]:** FREE is not just another price -- it triggers disproportionate emotional attraction
- **[[social-norms-vs-market-norms]]:** Two incompatible systems of exchange; introducing money into social exchanges destroys social norms irreversibly
- **[[hot-cold-empathy-gap]]:** People in calm states cannot predict their behavior in emotional states (and vice versa)
- **[[procrastination-and-precommitment]]:** External constraints outperform self-imposed ones, which outperform no constraints
- **[[virtual-ownership]]:** Ownership feelings begin before actual possession, driving irrational attachment
- **[[endowment-effect]]:** Demonstrated a 14:1 selling-to-buying ratio for emotionally significant goods (Duke basketball tickets)
