# Fritz Heider

**Type:** Person — Social Psychologist
**Phase(s):** 1
**Key Contribution:** Father of [[attribution-theory]]; proposed the fundamental dichotomy of internal vs. external attribution that underlies all subsequent attribution research.

## Overview

Fritz Heider (1896–1988) defined the field of social perception with his 1958 book *The Psychology of Interpersonal Relations*. He proposed that people are like "naive scientists" — amateur psychologists who observe behavior and try to infer its causes. His central insight: the most basic question people ask is whether behavior is caused by something **internal** to the person (disposition, personality) or something **external** (the situation, circumstances).

This simple dichotomy became the foundation for all subsequent attribution research, including Harold Kelley's [[covariation-model]], the [[fundamental-attribution-error]], and the [[self-serving-attribution]].

## Key Ideas
- [[attribution-theory]] — People as naive scientists inferring causes of behavior
- Internal vs. external attribution dichotomy
- [[perceptual-salience]] — people are salient, situations are not, leading to dispositional over-attribution

## Connections
- **Extended by:** Harold Kelley (covariation model), Lee Ross (FAE), Daniel Gilbert (two-step process), Bernard Weiner (attribution and emotion)
