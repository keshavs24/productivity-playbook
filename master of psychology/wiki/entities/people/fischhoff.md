# Baruch Fischhoff

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Discovered and named hindsight bias ("I knew it all along") and articulated its devastating consequences for learning from experience

## Overview

Baruch Fischhoff is a decision researcher at Carnegie Mellon University, affiliated with both the Department of Social and Decision Sciences and the Department of Engineering and Public Policy. He is one of the founding figures of behavioral decision research, known particularly for his work on [[hindsight-bias]] and risk perception/communication.

Fischhoff's "condemned to study the past" paper is a sweeping intellectual analysis that goes far beyond demonstrating a single bias. It examines how retrospection systematically misleads us through overfitting, false pattern detection, and the automatic integration of outcome knowledge into our mental models. His concept of [[creeping-determinism]] captures the insidious, below-awareness nature of hindsight distortion.

## Key Ideas

- [[hindsight-bias]] -- People exaggerate in retrospect what could have been anticipated in foresight
- [[creeping-determinism]] -- The unconscious tendency to view outcomes as having been relatively inevitable
- [[debiasing-taxonomy]] -- Systematic framework for classifying all debiasing strategies by their assumption about the source of bias: faulty tasks, faulty judges, or mismatch between judge and task
- **Destructive testing metaphor** -- Debiasing is the application of destructive testing (from engineering) to judgmental biases: stress the bias to find where it breaks
- Warning people about hindsight bias is ineffective; active counter-arguing against inevitability is partially effective
- "Consider why you might be wrong" (Koriat, Lichtenstein, & Fischhoff, 1980) is the most effective restructuring technique for reducing [[overconfidence-in-calibration]]
- The past is harder to learn from than we think -- "the very outcome knowledge which gives us the feeling that we understand what the past was all about may prevent us from learning anything from it"
- Statistical overfitting is analogous to narrative overfitting in historical explanation
- "People's reservoir of judgmental skills is both half empty and half full" -- a nuanced assessment of human rationality

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[anchoring-and-adjustment]] (Tversky & Kahneman), probability calibration research, Peterson & Beach (1967) intuitive statistician hypothesis
- **Extended by:** Bazerman & Moore's seven debiasing strategies ([[debiasing-strategies]]), Larrick (2004) comprehensive debiasing review, Thaler & Sunstein nudge framework
- **Contradicts:** Naive assumption that the past is transparent and the main challenge is predicting the future; the assumption that simply warning people about biases is sufficient to correct them
- **Applied in:** Risk communication, policy analysis, legal judgment, organizational learning, medical decision-making
