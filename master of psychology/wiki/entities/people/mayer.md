# John D. Mayer

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 1
**Key Contribution:** Co-originator (with [[salovey|Peter Salovey]]) of the scientific concept of emotional intelligence; contributed foundational research on mood, personality, and the role of affect in cognition.

## Overview

John D. Mayer is a psychologist at the University of New Hampshire who, together with Peter Salovey, published the 1990 paper that formally defined emotional intelligence as a psychological construct. Mayer's research background included work on how personality moderates the effects of affect on cognition, the experience and meta-experience of mood, perceiving affective content in ambiguous visual stimuli (a component of EI), and how mood-related communication functions in advice-giving relationships.

Mayer brought to the EI collaboration a deep interest in the interplay between emotion and personality. His work on "A Broader Conception of Mood Experience" (1990, with Salovey, Gomberg-Kaufman, and Blainey) expanded the alexithymia construct beyond negative emotions to include responses to positive, mixed, and neutral emotional situations. He also co-developed the State and Trait Meta-Mood Scales with Salovey, providing empirical instruments for measuring emotional self-awareness.

## Key Ideas

- [[emotional-intelligence]] — The core construct: monitoring, discriminating among, and using emotions to guide thought and action
- [[emotional-intelligence-model]] — The three-branch EI framework
- [[emotional-perception]] — Including perception of affective content in ambiguous stimuli
- [[emotion-regulation]] — Meta-experience of mood; how personality moderates affect's cognitive effects
- [[alexithymia]] — Expanded the construct to address positive and neutral emotional states, not just negative

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Appraisal theories of emotion (Roseman, Smith & Ellsworth), personality and affect interaction research (Larsen, Diener, Emmons)
- **Extended by:** Mayer and Salovey's later four-branch ability model (1997); Mayer-Salovey-Caruso Emotional Intelligence Test (MSCEIT)
- **Contradicts:** Traditional view of emotions as disorganized, non-cognitive phenomena
- **Applied in:** Emotional intelligence assessment, personality research, clinical diagnosis
