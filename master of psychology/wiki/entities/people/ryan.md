# Richard M. Ryan

**Type:** Person -- Motivational Psychologist
**Phase(s):** 3
**Key Contribution:** Co-founder (with [[deci|Edward L. Deci]]) of [[self-determination-theory-framework|Self-Determination Theory]], one of the most widely cited and applied theories of human motivation.

## Overview

Richard M. Ryan is a clinical psychologist and motivational scientist who, together with [[deci|Edward L. Deci]], developed Self-Determination Theory (SDT) over more than four decades of collaborative work. Ryan has been a professor at the University of Rochester and later at the Institute for Positive Psychology and Education at the Australian Catholic University.

Ryan and Deci's collaboration has produced one of the most comprehensive and empirically supported theories of human motivation. SDT's central propositions -- that humans have innate [[basic-psychological-needs]] for [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]], and that motivation varies qualitatively along a [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation|controlled-autonomous continuum]] -- have been supported by thousands of laboratory and field studies across dozens of countries and applied domains.

Their work explicitly positions SDT as an *organismic* and *empirical* theory, distinguishing it from both behaviorist approaches (which view the organism as passive) and prior humanistic theories (which were less empirically rigorous). Ryan's clinical background has particularly informed SDT's applications to psychotherapy, psychopathology, and well-being.

## Key Ideas

- [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- The three innate nutriments for growth, integrity, and well-being
- [[autonomy-need]] -- The need for volitional, self-endorsed action
- [[competence-need]] -- The need for effectance and mastery
- [[relatedness-need]] -- The need for social connection and belonging
- [[intrinsic-motivation]] -- Behaviors performed for inherent satisfaction
- [[extrinsic-motivation]] -- Behaviors performed for separable outcomes, varying in autonomy
- [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation]] -- The continuum differentiating motivation types
- [[organismic-integration]] -- The inherent developmental tendency toward differentiation and integration

## Connections

- **Collaborated with:** [[deci]] -- decades-long partnership developing SDT
- **Intellectual predecessors:** Robert White (effectance motivation), Richard de Charms (personal causation), Fritz Heider (naive psychology and attribution)
- **Extended by:** Maarten Vansteenkiste, Bart Soenens, Kennon Sheldon, Christopher Niemiec, and hundreds of SDT researchers worldwide
- **Contradicts:** B.F. Skinner (operant theory), Albert Bandura (unitary motivation), Markus & Kitayama (autonomy as Western construct)
