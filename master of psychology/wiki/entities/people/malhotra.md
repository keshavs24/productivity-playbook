# Deepak Malhotra

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 5
**Key Contribution:** Harvard Business School professor; co-author of *Negotiation Genius*; developer of the [[investigative-negotiation]] framework

## Overview

Deepak Malhotra is the Eli Goldston Professor of Business Administration at Harvard Business School, where he teaches negotiation in the MBA program and in executive education. He is co-author (with [[bazerman|Max Bazerman]]) of *[[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]]* (2007), which bridges the gap between academic negotiation research and practical application for executives and dealmakers.

Malhotra's distinctive contribution is the concept and methodology of [[investigative-negotiation]] — treating negotiation as an information game in which the goal is to discover the other side's interests, priorities, needs, and constraints through detective-like investigation, even when the other party is reluctant to share. His work emphasizes that the most common negotiation failures stem not from lack of skill at the bargaining table but from lack of preparation and lack of information-gathering *before and during* talks.

His research also covers negotiation from positions of weakness, the role of trust in dealmaking, strategies for confronting lies and deception, and the psychology of competitive arousal in bidding contexts.

## Key Ideas

- [[investigative-negotiation]] — a seven-principle framework for uncovering hidden information and creating value through detective-like inquiry
- [[claiming-vs-creating-value]] — the negotiator's dilemma of simultaneously expanding and dividing the pie
- [[negotiating-from-weakness]] — strategies for weak-BATNA situations including leveraging the other side's weakness, relinquishing power, and shifting the balance of power
- [[influence-strategies-in-negotiation]] — eight offensive and six defensive strategies grounded in psychological research
- [[zopa]] and [[reservation-value]] — operationalizing the negotiation framework with precise quantitative analysis
- Competitive arousal — research showing that rivalry and competitive contexts heighten the desire to "win at any cost," contributing to [[nonrational-escalation-of-commitment]]
