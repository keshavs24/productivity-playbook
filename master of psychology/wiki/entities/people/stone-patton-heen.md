# Douglas Stone, Bruce Patton & Sheila Heen

**Type:** Person (group entry)
**Phase(s):** 4 (Communication Foundations), 5 (Negotiation)
**Key Contribution:** Developed the [[three-conversations-framework]] — the insight that every difficult conversation contains three hidden conversations about facts, feelings, and identity

## Overview

Douglas Stone, Bruce Patton, and Sheila Heen are lecturers at Harvard Law School and members of the Harvard Negotiation Project (HNP), the same research group that produced *Getting to Yes* (Fisher & Ury, 1981). Their collaboration on [[difficult-conversations]] grew out of fifteen years of work at HNP, where they studied how people handle their most challenging interpersonal exchanges.

Bruce Patton is the senior member of the trio — he co-authored *Getting to Yes* with Roger Fisher and William Ury, making him a direct bridge between the interest-based negotiation tradition and the difficult conversations framework. Stone and Heen bring complementary expertise in communication, conflict resolution, and organizational behavior.

Their work fills a critical gap in the negotiation/communication literature. While *Getting to Yes* provides a framework for principled negotiation between parties with differing interests, *Difficult Conversations* addresses the *internal* psychological barriers that make it hard to even begin such negotiations — the competing stories, suppressed emotions, and identity threats that derail conversations before they start.

Sheila Heen later co-authored *Thanks for the Feedback* (2014) with Douglas Stone, extending the difficult conversations framework specifically to the challenge of receiving feedback — arguably the most common type of difficult conversation in professional settings.

## Key Ideas

- **[[three-conversations-framework]]** — Every difficult conversation has three layers: the What Happened? Conversation, the Feelings Conversation, and the Identity Conversation
- **[[contribution-system]]** — Replace blame with joint contribution analysis
- **[[learning-conversation]]** — Shift from delivering messages to mutual exploration
- **[[intent-impact-gap]]** — Disentangle what someone intended from the impact they had
- **[[and-stance]]** — Hold both stories as simultaneously valid
- **[[third-story]]** — Begin conversations from a neutral observer's perspective

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[fisher-ury]] (interest-based negotiation from *Getting to Yes*), Roger Fisher's work at the Harvard Negotiation Project
- **Extended by:** Heen & Stone's *Thanks for the Feedback* (2014), which applies the framework to receiving feedback
- **Contradicts:** Adversarial and positional approaches to conflict that treat conversations as debates to be won
- **Applied in:** Communication training, mediation, couples therapy, workplace conflict resolution, leadership development
