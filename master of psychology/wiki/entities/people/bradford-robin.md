# David Bradford & Carole Robin

**Type:** People
**Phase(s):** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Key Contribution:** Creators of Stanford's Interpersonal Dynamics ("Touchy-Feely") course and authors of *[[connect]]*, a framework for building [[exceptional-relationships]]

## Overview

David Bradford, PhD, is the Eugene D. O'Kelly II Senior Lecturer Emeritus in Leadership at Stanford Graduate School of Business, where he developed the Interpersonal Dynamics course over fifty years ago. He is known as the "Father of Touchy-Feely." He lives in Berkeley, California, with his wife Eva of over fifty-five years.

Carole Robin, PhD, was the Dorothy J. King Lecturer in Leadership at Stanford, where she taught for more than twenty years before co-founding Leaders in Tech, which brings the principles and processes of Interpersonal Dynamics to executives in Silicon Valley. She became known as the "Queen of Touchy-Feely" and helped expand the program, doubling its size. She lives in Palo Alto, California, with her husband Andy of thirty-five years. Carole also jointly developed a one-week executive version of the course for Stanford that senior managers from around the world attend, and now applies the same principles to developing Silicon Valley CEO/founders and investors.

Their Interpersonal Dynamics course remains the most famous and popular course offered in the Stanford MBA program. Over 85 percent of students enroll, and it is consistently rated as the most impactful course in the program. The course uses T-groups — twelve-person groups meeting for about sixty hours over ten weeks — as a learning laboratory. Students practice self-disclosure, feedback, connecting across differences, and mutual influence through real-time peer interaction. Similar groups have been conducted around the world — in Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and Latin America — with similar outcomes.

Beyond academia, both have been consultants and executive coaches in hundreds of for-profit and nonprofit organizations, in many countries, in multiple industries, and for companies ranging from Fortune 100s to startups.

Their own relationship nearly collapsed due to a crisis between them (detailed in Chapter 17 of *[[connect]]*), which they describe as a powerful demonstration that even experts in relationship dynamics can struggle — and that repair and recovery are possible.

## Key Ideas

- [[exceptional-relationships]] — Relationships characterized by six hallmarks: authenticity, [[interpersonal-vulnerability]], trust, honesty, productive conflict, and mutual growth commitment
- [[pinch-crunch-model]] — A framework for recognizing and addressing minor irritants ("pinches") before they escalate into major crises ("crunches")
- [[feedback-model]] — A structured approach to giving and receiving feedback that treats it as a growth tool rather than a weapon
- [[influence-without-authority]] — How to build balanced, mutual influence in relationships without relying on positional power
- [[zone-of-comfort-zone-of-growth]] — The principle that relationship development requires moving beyond comfort zones and treating discomfort as a signal of growth opportunity
- [[learning-mindset]] — The prerequisite disposition for relationship development: curiosity, willingness to make mistakes, and treating challenges as growth opportunities (AFOG — "another f**king opportunity for growth")
