# Jack L. Knetsch

**Type:** Person
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Experimental economist who co-discovered the [[endowment-effect]] with [[kahneman]] and [[thaler]], and co-authored foundational research on [[fairness-as-constraint]] in markets.

## Overview

Jack Knetsch is a professor of economics at Simon Fraser University whose experimental work on loss aversion in riskless choice helped establish behavioral economics as a field. His collaboration with Kahneman and Thaler produced two landmark papers collected in *Choices, Values, and Frames*: Chapter 8 on the endowment effect, loss aversion, and status quo bias, and Chapter 18 on fairness as a constraint on profit seeking.

Knetsch's distinctive contribution is demonstrating that the endowment effect holds with real goods in controlled market settings — not just hypothetical choices. His mug/candy exchange experiments showed that ownership itself creates value, with exchange rates far below what standard economics predicts.

## Key Ideas

- **[[endowment-effect]]** — Co-discoverer with Kahneman and Thaler; demonstrated through real-goods experiments that people demand significantly more to give up an object than they would pay to acquire it
- **Nonreversible indifference curves** (Ch.9 of CVF) — Showed that standard economic indifference curves fail when loss aversion is factored in; the curves are not reversible as theory requires
- **[[fairness-as-constraint]]** — Co-authored research showing that public perceptions of fairness constrain firm pricing, with loss aversion explaining which price increases are perceived as unfair vs. acceptable
- **[[loss-aversion]] in riskless choice** — Extended prospect theory's loss aversion from gambles to everyday ownership and exchange decisions
