# Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 5 — Negotiation
**Key Contribution:** The foundational text on principled negotiation, introducing the four-principle method (separate people from problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, insist on objective criteria) and the concept of BATNA. The most widely read negotiation book in the world.

## Overview

Published in 1981 by Roger Fisher and William Ury (2nd edition 1991 with Bruce Patton as full co-author), *Getting to Yes* emerged from the Harvard Negotiation Project's research on practical methods for reaching good agreements. The book rejects the conventional wisdom that negotiation requires choosing between being soft (accommodating) or hard (competitive), proposing instead a third way: principled negotiation, where participants are problem-solvers who are soft on people and hard on the problem.

The book's central thesis: you can negotiate agreements without giving in. The method suggests that you look for mutual gains wherever possible, and that where interests conflict, you insist that the result be based on some fair standards independent of the will of either side. Principled negotiation is hard on the merits, soft on the people. It employs no tricks and no posturing.

The 2nd edition (1991) is structured in five parts:
- **Part I (The Problem):** Why positional bargaining fails
- **Part II (The Method):** The four principles
- **Part III (Yes, But...):** What to do when they are more powerful, won't play, or use dirty tricks
- **Part IV (Conclusion):** Three closing points
- **Part V (Ten Questions People Ask):** Extended FAQ addressing common objections

## Key Ideas

- **[[principled-negotiation]]** — the four-principle method that is the book's main contribution
- **[[batna]]** — arguably the single most important concept in negotiation theory; the source of negotiating power
- **[[positions-vs-interests]]** — the insight that positions mask underlying needs, desires, concerns, and fears
- **[[separate-people-from-problem]]** — dealing with perception, emotion, and communication as independent dimensions
- **[[invent-options-for-mutual-gain]]** — overcoming [[fixed-pie-bias]] through creative option generation
- **[[objective-criteria]]** — using fair standards and fair procedures to avoid contests of will
- **[[negotiation-jujitsu]]** — deflecting positional attacks onto the problem
- **[[one-text-procedure]]** — single negotiating text for mediation (Camp David as exemplar)
- **The soft/hard/principled table** — the book's signature visual, comparing three approaches across 13 dimensions
- **The "Presently Perceived Choice" analysis** — a tool for understanding the other side's decision (Iranian hostage example)
- **The Circle Chart** — four types of thinking for generating options (Problem → Analysis → Approaches → Action Ideas)
- **Trip wire** — a flexible alternative to a rigid bottom line
