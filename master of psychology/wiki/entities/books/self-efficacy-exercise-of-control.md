# Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 3
**Key Contribution:** The definitive statement of self-efficacy theory by its originator, systematically documenting the origins, structure, processes, and applications of efficacy beliefs

## Overview

*Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control* (1997, W.H. Freeman) is Albert Bandura's magnum opus on self-efficacy theory. The book represents decades of research synthesized into a comprehensive theoretical framework. It covers the philosophical foundations of human agency, the precise definition and measurement of the self-efficacy construct, the four sources of efficacy information, and the application of self-efficacy theory to virtually every domain of human functioning: education, health, athletics, organizations, politics, and collective action.

The book is notable for its systematic differentiation of self-efficacy from every rival construct in the field (self-concept, self-esteem, locus of control, effectance motivation, control beliefs, possible selves), its rigorous treatment of causality (including diverse experimental paradigms demonstrating the causal role of efficacy beliefs), and its integration of individual and collective levels of analysis through the concept of collective efficacy.

Bandura situates self-efficacy within the broader framework of social cognitive theory, grounded in [[reciprocal-determinism]] -- the triadic reciprocal causation among personal factors, behavior, and environment. The book rejects both environmental determinism and trait-based personalism in favor of an agentic perspective in which people are proactive contributors to their own functioning.

## Key Ideas

- [[self-efficacy]] -- The core construct: beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute courses of action
- [[reciprocal-determinism]] -- The metatheoretical framework: triadic reciprocal causation
- [[human-agency]] -- The philosophical foundation: people as intentional, self-reflective agents
- [[efficacy-expectations-vs-outcome-expectations]] -- The critical distinction between capability beliefs and consequence beliefs
- [[generative-capability]] -- Self-efficacy as integrative and dynamic, not fixed or trait-like
- [[self-efficacy-dimensions]] -- Level, strength, and generality as the three measurement dimensions
- Four sources of self-efficacy: mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion, physiological/affective states
- Collective efficacy as emergent group-level agency

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Bandura's earlier works: *Social Learning Theory* (1977), *Social Foundations of Thought and Action* (1986a)
- **Extended by:** Subsequent research on collective efficacy, self-regulated learning, health behavior change, organizational behavior
- **Critiques:** Skinner's environmental determinism, Rotter's locus of control, White's effectance motivation, Markus's possible selves, Skinner's (E.A.) control beliefs
- **Applied in:** Education (Chapters 6, 9), health (Chapters 7, 8), athletics (Chapter 11), organizations (Chapter 10), collective agency (Chapter 12)
