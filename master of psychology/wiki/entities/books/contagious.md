# Contagious: Why Things Catch On

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 6 (Advanced Persuasion)
**Key Contribution:** Provides a research-based framework (STEPPS) for understanding and engineering contagious content — why certain products, ideas, and behaviors catch on through word of mouth and social influence

## Overview

*Contagious: Why Things Catch On* (2013) by [[berger]] is a research-based book that explains the science behind why certain products, ideas, and behaviors spread from person to person. Published by Simon & Schuster, the book distills more than a decade of academic research into an accessible framework for understanding and engineering word of mouth and social influence.

The book's central thesis is that virality isn't born — it's made. Contrary to the popular belief that viral success is random or driven by a handful of influential individuals (as argued in Gladwell's *The Tipping Point*), Berger argues that the same six principles consistently appear across contagious content. These principles — Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, and Stories (STEPPS) — can be applied to make any product, idea, or behavior more contagious, regardless of whether it seems inherently interesting or mundane.

The book bridges academic research and practical application. Each chapter presents a principle, supports it with empirical research (often Berger's own published studies), and illustrates it with vivid case studies ranging from a $100 cheesesteak to anti-drug campaigns that accidentally increased drug use. The research base includes analysis of *New York Times* Most E-Mailed articles, BzzAgent word-of-mouth campaigns, voting behavior at different polling locations, car purchasing patterns, and hundreds of viral and non-viral products and content.

## Key Ideas

- **[[stepps-framework]]:** The six-principle framework for making content contagious
- **Message over messenger:** Social epidemics are driven by the products and ideas themselves, not by special influential people
- **Only 7% of word of mouth is online:** Offline conversations are far more prevalent, yet most marketing attention goes to social media
- **High-arousal emotions drive sharing:** The critical variable is arousal (activation), not valence (positive vs. negative)
- **Triggers sustain word of mouth:** Interesting content gets immediate buzz but environmental triggers sustain ongoing discussion
- **Valuable virality:** Going viral is worthless if the brand isn't integral to the narrative — Evian's Roller Babies got 50M views but sales dropped 25%

## Key Case Studies

- **Barclay Prime's $100 cheesesteak** — All six STEPPS in one product
- **Blendtec's "Will It Blend?"** — Turning a boring product into a viral sensation ($50 budget, 300M views, 700% sales increase)
- **Please Don't Tell** — Hidden bar that leverages secrecy and exclusivity as social currency
- **Kit Kat and coffee** — Growing the trigger habitat ($300M to $500M brand growth)
- **Rebecca Black's "Friday"** — Terrible song that went viral because the day of the week triggered weekly spikes
- **Livestrong wristbands** — Behavioral residue that created ongoing social proof (85M units sold)
- **Anti-drug PSAs** — Making the private public backfired, increasing drug use by normalizing it
- **Dove's "Evolution"** — Trojan horse story that embedded brand in beauty norms conversation
- **Vietnamese nail salons** — Epilogue case study showing all six STEPPS at work in immigrant career clustering
