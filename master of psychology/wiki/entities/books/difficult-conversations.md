# Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 4 — Communication Foundations, 5 — Negotiation
**Key Contribution:** Provides a diagnostic framework for understanding why conversations feel difficult and a practical methodology for handling them more effectively, grounded in the insight that every difficult conversation is really three conversations

## Overview

Published by Viking Penguin in 1999 (with a new preface and chapter added in the 2010 Penguin edition), *Difficult Conversations* is the product of fifteen years of research at the Harvard Negotiation Project. Authors [[stone-patton-heen]] studied hundreds of conversations across contexts — workplace disputes, family arguments, relationship conflicts, medical decisions, racial tensions — and discovered that all difficult conversations share the same underlying three-part structure.

The book's central thesis: what makes a conversation "difficult" is not the topic itself but the hidden psychological dynamics at play. Three conversations run simultaneously beneath the surface: the "What Happened?" Conversation (competing stories about facts, intentions, and blame), the Feelings Conversation (what to do with emotions), and the Identity Conversation (what this says about who I am). Each contains predictable errors that can be identified and corrected.

The book is structured in two parts. Part I ("The Problem," Chapters 1-6) diagnoses the three conversations and the common mistakes within each. Part II ("The Solution," Chapters 7-12) provides practical tools for conducting difficult conversations more effectively — when to raise issues, how to begin from the [[third-story]], how to listen, how to express yourself with power and clarity, and how to problem-solve jointly.

## Key Concepts Introduced

- [[three-conversations-framework]] — The core diagnostic model
- [[what-happened-conversation]] — First conversation: truth, intentions, blame
- [[feelings-conversation]] — Second conversation: emotions
- [[identity-conversation]] — Third conversation: self-image
- [[contribution-system]] — Alternative to blame
- [[learning-conversation]] — Alternative to message delivery
- [[intent-impact-gap]] — Separating what was intended from what was experienced
- [[and-stance]] — Holding both stories as valid
- [[third-story]] — Starting from a neutral observer's perspective

- **Related works:** [[emotional-intelligence]] research (Salovey & Mayer; Goleman), [[attribution-theory]] (the Intention Invention draws on attribution research without naming it), [[fundamental-attribution-error]] (directly relevant to Chapter 3)
