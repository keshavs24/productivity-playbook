# Guide to Managerial Communication (10th Edition)

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 4 -- Communication Foundations
**Key Contribution:** A comprehensive, practical framework for strategic business communication covering writing and speaking, organized around five interactive variables

## Overview

Published by Pearson Education in 2014, *Guide to Managerial Communication: Effective Business Writing and Speaking* by [[munter|Mary Munter]] and Lynn Hamilton is a concise (approximately 190 pages) reference text widely used in MBA communication courses. Now in its 10th edition, the book provides a strategic framework for all forms of managerial communication -- from emails and reports to presentations and meetings.

The book's central thesis is that managerial communication differs from other forms of communication because success is measured by outcomes, not intentions. The most brilliant message fails if it doesn't achieve the desired audience response. This outcome-oriented perspective organizes the entire text around a five-variable strategic framework: communicator strategy, audience strategy, message strategy, channel choice strategy, and culture strategy.

What makes the text distinctive is its brevity and practicality. Rather than extended case studies or theoretical discourse, it provides actionable checklists, clear taxonomies (like the [[tell-sell-consult-join]] model), and concrete examples. It translates classical rhetoric ([[ethos]], [[pathos]], [[logos]]) into modern business language without losing the underlying principles.

The book is organized in a logical progression: Chapter 1 establishes the strategic framework that governs everything else. Chapters 2-4 apply the framework to writing (composing process, macro structure, micro style). Chapters 5-7 apply it to speaking (verbal structure, nonverbal delivery, visual aids).

## Key Themes

- **Audience-centered communication:** Every strategic decision starts with the audience -- who they are, what they know, what they feel, what will persuade them
- **Communication as a circular process:** Success is measured by desired audience response, not by message quality in isolation
- **Style flexibility:** No single "right" communication style; effective communicators use [[tell-sell-consult-join]] flexibly depending on objective
- **Direct approach as default:** BLUF over 90% of the time; indirect approach only for specific constraints
- **Credibility management:** Initial credibility as a "bank account" that must be maintained through ongoing goodwill and expertise
- **Practical tools:** Communication Strategy Checklist, HSV document design, Audience Memory Curve, channels of communication taxonomy
