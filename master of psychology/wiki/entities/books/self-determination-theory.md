# Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 3 -- Motivation Science
**Key Contribution:** The comprehensive, updated theoretical statement of Self-Determination Theory -- a macro-theory of human motivation, development, and wellness organized around three basic psychological needs and six mini-theories.

## Overview

Published by Guilford Press in 2017, this 756-page volume represents [[ryan|Ryan]] and [[deci|Deci]]'s definitive statement of [[self-determination-theory-framework|SDT]], updating their 1985 work *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*. The intervening three decades saw SDT substantially elaborated and refined based on thousands of laboratory and field studies by hundreds of researchers worldwide.

The book's central thesis: human beings are inherently active, growth-oriented organisms who require satisfaction of three [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]] -- to thrive. Motivation is not a unitary quantity but varies qualitatively along a [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation|controlled-autonomous continuum]], with autonomous motivation producing superior outcomes across virtually every domain studied.

## Key Ideas

- [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- Autonomy, competence, and relatedness as universal nutriments
- [[intrinsic-motivation]] -- Behaviors performed for inherent satisfaction
- [[extrinsic-motivation]] -- The differentiated taxonomy of extrinsic regulatory styles
- [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation]] -- The continuum from external regulation to intrinsic motivation
- [[organismic-integration]] -- The inherent developmental tendency toward differentiation and integration
- Six mini-theories: CET, OIT, COT, BPNT, GCT, RMT

## Connections

- **Builds on:** *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (Deci & Ryan, 1985b) -- the prior formal theoretical statement
- **Intellectual predecessors:** White's effectance motivation (1959), de Charms's personal causation (1968), Heider's attribution theory (1958), Piaget's cognitive development, Rogers's humanistic psychology
- **Contradicts:** Operant theory (Skinner), Hull's drive theory, unitary motivation theories (Bandura's self-efficacy), cultural relativism about autonomy
