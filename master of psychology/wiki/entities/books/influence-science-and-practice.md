# Influence: Science and Practice (5th Edition)

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 1 — Cognitive Foundations
**Key Contribution:** The definitive scientific account of the six universal principles of social influence, combining experimental evidence with real-world practitioner observation

## Overview

Published by Pearson in 2009, *Influence: Science and Practice* is the academic edition of Cialdini's landmark work (distinct from the popular edition *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*). The "Science and Practice" version includes research citations, chapter summaries, study questions, and "Reader's Reports" — firsthand accounts from readers who recognized influence principles operating in their own lives.

The book's central thesis: although there are thousands of different compliance tactics, nearly all of them fall into six categories, each governed by a fundamental psychological principle. These principles — reciprocation, commitment/consistency, social proof, liking, authority, and scarcity — work because they trigger automatic psychological responses that are normally adaptive but can be exploited.

## Key Ideas

- **Six principles of influence** — the organizing framework for the entire field of compliance research
- **Click, whirr responding** — the meta-mechanism that makes all six principles work
- **Contrast principle** — a perceptual weapon that operates independently of the six principles
- **Rejection-then-retreat** — the most powerful tactical application of reciprocation, combining reciprocal concessions with the contrast principle
- **Defense framework** — each principle comes with specific countermeasures
