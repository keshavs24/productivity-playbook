# Judgment in Managerial Decision Making

**Type:** Book (8th Edition, 2013)
**Phase(s):** 2
**Key Contribution:** Comprehensive textbook applying Tversky/Kahneman's heuristics and biases research to managerial decision making; introduces bounded awareness and three-part overconfidence taxonomy

## Overview

*Judgment in Managerial Decision Making* by [[bazerman]] and Don A. Moore is a foundational MBA textbook that translates the cognitive science of judgment and decision making into practical managerial contexts. Now in its 8th edition, the book bridges Herbert Simon's bounded rationality, Tversky and Kahneman's heuristics and biases program, and modern behavioral economics. The book takes a descriptive approach -- understanding how decisions are actually made -- as a prerequisite for prescriptive improvement.

The book's distinctive organizational framework identifies five types of bounds on human judgment: bounded rationality (Simon), bounded willpower and bounded self-interest (Thaler), bounded awareness (Bazerman & Chugh), and bounded ethicality.

## Key Ideas
- [[bounded-rationality]] -- Simon's framework: we satisfice rather than optimize
- [[system-1-system-2]] -- Dual-process thinking as the foundation of judgment errors
- [[bounded-awareness]] -- Systematically overlooking obvious, important information
- Three forms of overconfidence: [[overprecision]], [[overestimation]], [[overplacement]]
- [[confirmation-heuristic]] -- Elevated to a fourth general heuristic alongside availability, representativeness, and anchoring
- Twelve common biases mapped to three parent heuristics
- The winner's curse in auctions and acquisitions
- Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze model applied to decision improvement
- [[framing-effects]] -- Asian Disease Problem, [[pseudocertainty]], portfolio irrationality from sequential framing
- [[endowment-effect]] and [[mental-accounting]] -- ownership inflates value, separate mental accounts produce irrational financial behavior
- [[want-should-conflict]] -- dual-self tension between impulsive and reflective decision-making
- [[escalation-of-commitment]] -- four additive causes: perceptual biases, judgmental biases, impression management, competitive irrationality
- [[sunk-cost-fallacy]] -- the psychological difficulty of ignoring irrecoverable past investments
- [[self-serving-bias-in-decisions]] -- unconscious selection of fairness criteria favoring self-interest
- [[bounded-ethicality]] -- systematic unethical behavior driven by cognitive biases operating below awareness
- [[ultimatum-game]] and [[dictator-game]] -- fairness preferences override economic rationality
- [[behavioral-finance]] and [[disposition-effect]] -- cognitive biases applied to investment decisions
- [[fixed-pie-bias]] -- the most destructive cognitive error in negotiation
- [[value-creation-in-negotiation]] -- expanding the pie through differences in preferences and predictions
- [[debiasing-strategies]] -- seven complementary strategies for improving decision making
