# Resonate: Present Visual Stories that Transform Audiences

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 7 — Leadership Communication
**Key Contribution:** Codifies the hidden structure of transformative presentations by revealing that great speeches follow a story-like form alternating between *what is* and *what could be*, and provides a complete methodology for creating presentations that resonate

## Overview

Published by John Wiley & Sons in 2010, *Resonate* by [[duarte|Nancy Duarte]] is a visually rich, 248-page guide to structuring presentations as stories that transform audiences. It is the conceptual prequel to Duarte's first book *Slide:ology* (2008) — where *Slide:ology* addressed how to design slides visually, *Resonate* addresses the deeper, upstream problem: how to structure the presentation itself so that it resonates with audiences and causes change.

The book's central thesis: presentations are broken because they are self-centered, fact-heavy, and structureless. The fix is to apply insights from mythology, cinema, and literature to structure presentations as stories. The presenter is not the hero — the audience is the hero, and the presenter is the mentor. The presentation should alternate between *what is* (current reality) and *what could be* (proposed future), creating dramatic tension that propels the audience to cross the threshold and adopt the idea.

Duarte developed her framework empirically by analyzing thousands of presentations from her firm's work, then overlaying insights from Gustav Freytag's dramatic arc, Syd Field's screenplay paradigm, Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey (via Christopher Vogler), and Robert McKee's *Story*. Her breakthrough discovery was that Steve Jobs's 2007 iPhone launch and Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech both map to the same underlying structural form.

### Nine Rules of Resonance
1. Resonance causes change
2. Incorporating story into presentations has an exponential effect on outcomes
3. If a presenter knows the audience's resonant frequency and tunes to that, the audience will move
4. Every audience will persist in a state of rest unless compelled to change
5. Use the big idea to filter out all frequencies other than the resonant frequency
6. Structure is greater than the sum of its parts
7. Memorable moments are repeated and retransmitted so they cover longer distances
8. Audience interest is directly proportionate to the presenter's preparation
9. A presentation is meant to create change but doesn't become reality until the audience acts
