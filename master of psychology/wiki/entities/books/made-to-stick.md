# Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die

**Type:** Book
**Phase(s):** 4 — Communication Foundations
**Key Contribution:** A practical framework (SUCCESs) for designing ideas that are understood, remembered, and have lasting impact, grounded in research from cognitive psychology, communication studies, and the analysis of naturally sticky ideas

## Overview

*Made to Stick* (2007) by [[heath-brothers|Chip Heath and Dan Heath]] addresses the question of why some ideas thrive while others die. The book identifies six principles — Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories (the [[success-framework|SUCCESs]] acronym) — that characterize ideas that stick. It also identifies the [[curse-of-knowledge]] as the primary villain that prevents communicators from applying these principles effectively.

The book is structured around the six principles, with each chapter devoted to one. Throughout, the Heaths provide "Idea Clinics" — before-and-after message redesigns that demonstrate how the principles work in practice. Topics range from sun exposure awareness and foreign aid advocacy to fundraising presentations and oral rehydration therapy.

The research base draws on Chip Heath's decade of studying urban legends, proverbs, and naturally sticky ideas at Stanford; Dan Heath's experience working with master teachers at Thinkwell; and a wide range of academic research including George Loewenstein's gap theory of curiosity, Paul Ekman's research on facial expressions of emotion, Robert Cialdini's work on influence and science communication, and Amos Tversky and Eldar Shafir's work on decision paralysis.

## Key Ideas

- **[[success-framework]]** — the six-principle checklist for sticky ideas
- **[[curse-of-knowledge]]** — the psychological villain that prevents clear communication
- **[[commanders-intent]]** — finding the essential core of a message
- **[[schema-breaking]]** — violating expectations to grab attention
- **[[gap-theory-of-curiosity]]** — sustaining attention through information gaps
- **[[concreteness-principle]]** — concrete language aids memory, understanding, and coordination
- **Idea Clinics** — practical before-and-after exercises applying the principles

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[aristotle]]'s [[three-modes-of-persuasion]] (ethos/credible, pathos/emotional, logos/stories+simple+concrete); [[cialdini]]'s [[six-principles-of-influence]] (overlap in credibility, social proof, and storytelling); [[tversky]]'s research on decision-making under uncertainty
- **Extended by:** The Heaths' later works (*Switch*, *Decisive*, *The Power of Moments*); [[on-rhetoric]] provides the philosophical foundation that the SUCCESs framework operationalizes
- **Contradicts:** The assumption that message effectiveness depends primarily on delivery (eye contact, posture, repetition) rather than message design
- **Applied in:** Teaching, marketing, organizational strategy, public health communication, political campaigns
