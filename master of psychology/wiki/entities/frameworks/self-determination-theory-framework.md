# Self-Determination Theory (SDT Framework)

**Type:** Framework
**Phase(s):** 3
**Key Contribution:** A comprehensive macro-theory of human motivation, personality development, and wellness, organized around three [[basic-psychological-needs]] and six mini-theories.

## Overview

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) is an organismic, empirically based macro-theory of human motivation developed by [[ryan|Richard M. Ryan]] and [[deci|Edward L. Deci]] beginning in the 1970s. SDT addresses the conditions that facilitate or undermine intrinsic motivation, the internalization of extrinsic motivation, individual differences in motivational orientations, the dynamics of basic psychological needs, the content of life goals, and the quality of interpersonal relationships.

SDT's foundational assumptions:
1. **Organismic perspective:** Humans are inherently active, curious, and growth-oriented
2. **Three basic psychological needs:** [[autonomy-need|Autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]] are universally essential for optimal functioning
3. **Motivation is differentiated:** The [[controlled-vs-autonomous-motivation|type of motivation]] (autonomous vs. controlled) matters more than the amount
4. **Social contexts are critical:** Environments that support or thwart basic needs determine the quality of motivation and functioning

## The Six Mini-Theories

### 1. Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET)
**Formulated:** Deci & Ryan, 1980a
**Concerns:** How social environments influence [[intrinsic-motivation]] -- the effects of rewards, feedback, and other external events
**Key proposition:** External events have three aspects: informational (supports competence/autonomy), controlling (pressures behavior), and amotivating (signals incompetence). The functional significance of an event determines its impact on intrinsic motivation.
**Discussed in:** Chapters 6-7

### 2. Organismic Integration Theory (OIT)
**Formulated:** Ryan, Connell & Deci, 1985
**Concerns:** The development of [[extrinsic-motivation]] through internalization -- how extrinsically motivated behaviors become autonomous
**Key proposition:** Extrinsic motivation varies along a continuum from external regulation through introjection, identification, and integration, with increasing degrees of autonomy
**Discussed in:** Chapter 8

### 3. Causality Orientations Theory (COT)
**Formulated:** Deci & Ryan, 1985b
**Concerns:** Individual differences in motivational orientations
**Key proposition:** Three general causality orientations -- autonomy, controlled, and impersonal -- represent relatively enduring personality characteristics that parallel the three motivational types (autonomous, controlled, amotivated) at a global level
**Measure:** General Causality Orientations Scale (GCOS; Deci, 1985a)
**Discussed in:** Chapter 9

### 4. Basic Psychological Needs Theory (BPNT)
**Formulated:** Ryan, Deci, et al., 2006
**Concerns:** How the dynamics of [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- their satisfaction and frustration -- relate to psychological health and well-being
**Key proposition:** Need satisfaction predicts wellness and vitality; need thwarting predicts psychopathology and ill-being. This operates at both between-person and within-person levels, and across cultures.
**Discussed in:** Chapter 10

### 5. Goal Contents Theory (GCT)
**Formulated:** Kasser & Ryan, 1996
**Concerns:** The relationship between life goals/aspirations and basic need satisfaction
**Key proposition:** *Intrinsic aspirations* (personal growth, relationships, community) relate more to need satisfaction and well-being than *extrinsic aspirations* (wealth, fame, image), which show weaker or negative relations to wellness
**Discussed in:** Chapter 11

### 6. Relationships Motivation Theory (RMT)
**Formulated:** Deci & Ryan, 2014a
**Concerns:** The quality of interpersonal relationships
**Key proposition:** High-quality relationships depend on experiencing not only positivity and regard but also respect for autonomy. [[relatedness-need|Relatedness]] fuels internalization and is reciprocally facilitated by [[autonomy-need|autonomy]].
**Discussed in:** Chapter 12

## Connections

- **Builds on:** White's effectance motivation (1959), de Charms's personal causation (1968), Heider's naive psychology (1958), Piaget's cognitive development, Rogers's humanistic psychology, Bowlby's attachment theory
- **Extended by:** Thousands of studies across education, health care, sport, work, virtual environments, parenting, religion, culture, and politics
- **Contradicts:** Operant theory (Skinner), Hull's drive theory, Bandura's unitary self-efficacy model, cultural relativism about universal needs
- **Applied in:** Classroom interventions, workplace management, clinical treatment, coaching, game design, health behavior change, political analysis
