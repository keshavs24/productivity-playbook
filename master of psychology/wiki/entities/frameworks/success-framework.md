# SUCCESs Framework

**Type:** Framework
**Phase(s):** 4 — Communication Foundations
**Key Contribution:** A six-principle checklist for designing ideas that stick (are understood, remembered, and change behavior), derived from the study of hundreds of naturally sticky ideas and grounded in cognitive psychology research

## Overview

The SUCCESs framework, developed by [[heath-brothers|Chip Heath and Dan Heath]] in [[made-to-stick]], identifies six principles that characterize ideas that stick:

1. **Simple** — Find the essential core and express it compactly. Not dumbing down but forced prioritization. The ideal form is the proverb: compact *and* profound. Central metaphor: [[commanders-intent]].
2. **Unexpected** — Break schemas to grab attention (surprise), then sustain interest by opening knowledge gaps (curiosity). See [[schema-breaking]], [[gap-theory-of-curiosity]].
3. **Concrete** — Express ideas in sensory, tangible terms. Concrete images cling to memory via the Velcro effect; abstract language slides off. See [[concreteness-principle]], [[abstract-vs-concrete-information]].
4. **Credible** — Help ideas carry their own credentials. Let people test ideas for themselves rather than relying on external authority.
5. **Emotional** — Make people feel something. We care about individuals, not abstractions. Connect to identity and self-interest. See [[pathos]].
6. **Stories** — Tell stories that function as mental flight simulators, preparing audiences to respond effectively. See [[ethos]], [[logos]].

The framework is explicitly designed as an antidote to the [[curse-of-knowledge]] — the cognitive bias that makes knowledgeable people systematically poor communicators.

## The Six Principles in Detail

| Principle | Gets people to... | By... | Example |
|-----------|------------------|-------|---------|
| Simple | Understand and prioritize | Finding the core, forced prioritization | Southwest: "THE low-fare airline" |
| Unexpected | Pay attention and stay curious | Breaking schemas, opening knowledge gaps | "No school next Thursday"; Saturn's rings mystery |
| Concrete | Understand, remember, coordinate | Sensory language, tangible goals, Velcro hooks | Boeing 727 specs; brown eyes/blue eyes; ORT packet |
| Credible | Believe | Testable credentials, vivid details | Reagan: "Are you better off?" |
| Emotional | Care | Individual stories, identity appeals | Single needy child vs. statistical millions |
| Stories | Act | Mental flight simulation, plot templates | Firefighter stories; Jared (Subway) |

## Key Evidence

- 89% of award-winning ads fit six creative templates (mostly relating to unexpectedness); only 2% of unsuccessful ads fit those templates
- Novices trained on templates for two hours produced ads rated 50% more creative and 55% more positive toward products
- The Kidney Heist urban legend persists across hundreds of versions with zero resources — because it embodies Unexpected, Concrete, and Emotional
- CSPI's movie-popcorn campaign changed consumer behavior and industry practices using Unexpected + Concrete + Emotional framing

## Connections

- **Builds on:** [[three-modes-of-persuasion]] (ethos -> Credible; pathos -> Emotional; logos -> Stories + Simple + Concrete); George Loewenstein's [[gap-theory-of-curiosity]]; Robert Cialdini's work on mystery as a teaching tool; [[tversky]]'s research on decision paralysis
- **Extended by:** *Switch* (Heath & Heath) extends the ideas to behavior change; *Decisive* extends to decision-making
- **Contradicts:** Traditional communication advice that focuses on delivery (practice, eye contact, repetition) rather than message design
- **Applied in:** Teaching, marketing, organizational strategy, public health, political campaigns, journalism
