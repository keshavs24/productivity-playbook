# Social Norms vs. Market Norms

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Humans operate simultaneously in two distinct exchange systems:

- **Social norms:** Warm, community-oriented exchanges with fuzzy, delayed reciprocity. Governed by trust, goodwill, and shared identity. "Could you help me move?" No explicit payment expected
- **Market norms:** Sharp, transactional exchanges with explicit costs and benefits. Governed by contracts, wages, and cost-benefit analysis. "I'll pay you $50 to help me move"

These systems are **incompatible**: introducing market norms into a social exchange destroys the social norm, and once destroyed, it is extremely difficult to restore.

## Key Findings

- **Circle-dragging experiment ([[summary-2-S1-ariely-ch4-5]]):** Unpaid volunteers (social norms) dragged 168 circles; $5 workers dragged 159; $0.50 workers dragged only 101. People worked **harder for free** than for low pay
- **AARP lawyers:** Refused to work for $30/hour (market norm: too low), but volunteered for free (social norm: generous)
- **Gift vs. cash experiment:** Gifts (Snickers bar, Godiva chocolates) maintained social-norm effort levels (~162-169 circles). But mentioning the gift's price ("a 50-cent Snickers") collapsed performance to market-norm levels. **Mentioning cost converts gifts to market exchange**
- **Israeli day care fines (Gneezy & Rustichini):** Fining late parents replaced guilt (social norm) with a price (market norm). Parents came late **more** often. When the fine was removed, the social norm **did not return** -- lateness persisted or increased
- **Vohs, Mead, & Goode:** Merely unscrambling salary-related sentences made people more self-reliant, less helpful, more individualistic, and more likely to choose solitary tasks. Just **thinking** about money activates market-norm behavior
- **Starburst candy experiment (Ch.5):** At 1 cent each, students took 3.5 candies. When free, students took only 1.1 -- social norms triggered consideration for others. Free activates social behavior; even a penny activates market selfishness

## Mechanisms

1. **Frame activation:** The presence or absence of money signals which normative frame applies. Any mention of money, prices, or costs activates the market frame
2. **Incompatibility:** Social and market norms cannot coexist in the same transaction. Market norms "crowd out" social norms
3. **Asymmetric irreversibility:** Moving from social to market norms is easy (introduce a price). Moving back is extremely difficult (the Israeli day care study proves this)
4. **Effort as intermediate:** Exchanges involving effort (but not money) maintain social norms better than monetary exchanges but not as purely as truly free exchanges

## Applications

- **Employer-employee relations:** Companies that cultivate social norms (loyalty, purpose, flexibility, benefits) get more from employees than those relying purely on market norms (salary, bonuses). But companies that build social relationships then violate them (cutting benefits, outsourcing) face a loyalty collapse. **SDT Ch. 21 confirms this extensively:** Olafsen et al. (2015) found pay level did not predict need satisfaction or intrinsic motivation -- but autonomy support did. Kuvaas et al. (2016) showed performance-contingent pay (market norms) negatively predicted autonomous motivation and increased turnover, while base-pay (closer to social norms) supported autonomous motivation. Harrison et al. (1996) found 75% turnover within a year for pure-commission sales employees. See [[sdt-compensation-design]] for SDT's full framework
- **Customer relationships:** "Like a good neighbor, State Farm is there" builds a social relationship. But a bounced-check fee or impersonal service violates it. You cannot have it both ways
- **Gift giving:** Gifts are socially "inefficient" (cash would maximize recipient utility) but they serve a crucial social function -- maintaining relationships. Never mention the price of a gift
- **Education:** Performance-based pay and standardized testing push education from social norms toward market norms, potentially undermining intrinsic motivation
- **Open source software:** Thrives on social norms. Introducing payment could undermine motivation
- **Volunteerism:** People volunteer more readily when no payment is offered than when a small payment is offered
