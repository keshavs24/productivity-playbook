# Organizational Efficacy

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Organizational efficacy refers to the application of self-efficacy beliefs to organizational functioning, encompassing managerial self-efficacy (beliefs about one's capabilities to manage complex organizational activities), collective organizational efficacy (shared beliefs about an organization's conjoint capabilities), and the efficacy beliefs that shape how employees enact their occupational roles, make decisions, cope with stress, and adapt to change (Bandura, 1997, Ch.10).

## Key Findings

- In simulated organizations, managers who believed complex decision making is an acquirable skill maintained resilient efficacy, set increasingly challenging goals, used good analytic thinking, and achieved progressively higher productivity. Managers who believed ability was inherent aptitude showed declining efficacy, lowered aspirations, erratic thinking, and deteriorating performance (Wood & Bandura, 1989b; Bandura & Jourden, 1991) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- Mastery modeling is the most effective method for developing occupational competencies, consistently outperforming lecture-based training and computerized tutorials (Gist et al., 1989, Fig. 10.2)
- Perceived group efficacy was the strongest predictor of employee satisfaction and productivity (Campion, Medsker, & Higgs, 1993)
- Three types of organizational change require different forms of efficacy (Binks & Vale, 1990): reactive efficacy (responding to changes), proactive efficacy (creating incremental improvements), and innovative efficacy (creative innovations)
- Exaggerated managerial efficacy can lead to [[escalation-of-commitment]]: administrators with raised decisional efficacy are prone to escalate investment in failing ventures (Whyte, Saks, & Hook, in press)
- Perceived decisional efficacy affects risk taking: managers whose efficacy is raised focus on opportunities; those whose efficacy is lowered dwell on risks (Krueger & Dickson, 1993, 1994)
- Belief in controllability of the environment is important: viewing an organization as changeable increases perceived efficacy to manage it (Bandura & Wood, 1989)
- Efficacy beliefs promote adoption of new technologies, which in turn alter organizational network structure -- early adopters gain more influence and centrality (Burkhardt & Brass, 1990)
- Low sense of occupational coping efficacy is the central mediator of burnout (Chwalisz et al., 1992; Leiter, 1991). Problem-focused coping prevents burnout; emotion-focused coping leads to it (Fig. 10.3)
- Self-regulatory efficacy programs (Frayne & Latham, 1987) reduce absenteeism by teaching employees to self-monitor, set subgoals, develop coping strategies, and self-reward (Fig. 10.4)

## Mechanisms

Organizational efficacy operates through the standard four [[self-efficacy-mediating-processes]] applied at the organizational level:

1. **Cognitive:** Efficacy beliefs affect quality of analytic thinking in complex decision-making environments. People who believe strongly in their problem-solving capabilities remain highly efficient in their analytic thinking; those who judge themselves inefficacious become self-diagnostic rather than task-diagnostic (M. Bandura & Dweck, 1988)
2. **Motivational:** Managerial efficacy determines goal level, commitment, and persistence in the face of substandard organizational performance. High-efficacy managers set increasingly challenging goals; low-efficacy managers lower aspirations after setbacks
3. **Affective:** Perceived efficacy to fulfill occupational demands affects stress, burnout vulnerability, and job satisfaction. Construing managerial capability as acquirable skill reduces perceived threat of mistakes
4. **Selection:** Efficacy beliefs determine which organizational opportunities are pursued, what level of innovation is attempted, and whether new technologies are adopted

Key organizational dynamics:
- **Triadic reciprocal causation:** Cognition (beliefs, goals, thinking quality), behavior (managerial choices), and environment (organizational properties, challenge level, employee responsiveness) all mutually influence each other over time
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy:** Managers who believe organizations are controllable and ability is acquirable create increasingly productive organizations; those with the opposite beliefs create deteriorating ones
- **Social comparison effects:** Managers who judge accomplishments against personal standards of self-improvement maintain resilient efficacy; those who compare against others' attainments are vulnerable to demoralization

## Applications

- **Managerial development:** Frame complex decision making as an acquirable skill, not an inherent aptitude. Provide mastery modeling with guided practice and self-directed transfer training
- **Organizational training:** Use the three-element mastery modeling method: (1) instructive modeling of subskills, (2) guided practice in simulated conditions, (3) self-directed transfer to real situations
- **Decision making:** Build analytic efficacy through systematic exploration of decision rules in complex environments. Provide performance feedback highlighting gains rather than shortfalls
- **Stress management:** Address occupational stress through building coping efficacy (problem-focused coping) rather than through palliative measures. Develop self-regulatory programs (Frayne & Latham, 1987)
- **Innovation adoption:** Build learning efficacy for new technologies. Use modeling by peers and early adopters. Create supportive organizational milieu for adoption
- **Team management:** Build collective team efficacy through shared mastery experiences. Tailor efficacy-boosting interventions to cultural orientation -- individually for individualists, group-oriented for collectivists (Earley, 1993, 1994)
