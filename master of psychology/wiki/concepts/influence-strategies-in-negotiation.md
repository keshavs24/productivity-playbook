# Influence Strategies in Negotiation

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Influence strategies in negotiation are psychological tactics that increase the likelihood of compliance with your requests, demands, offers, and proposals without requiring you to change the substance of your offer. Developed by [[malhotra|Malhotra]] and [[bazerman|Bazerman]] in [[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]], they draw primarily on [[cialdini|Robert Cialdini's]] research on persuasion and [[kahneman|Kahneman and Tversky's]] work on [[framing-effects]] and [[loss-aversion]]. The chapter presents eight offensive strategies and six defensive strategies.

## The Eight Strategies

1. **Highlight potential losses, not gains** — leverage [[loss-aversion]] by framing proposals in terms of what the other side stands to lose by rejecting them
2. **Disaggregate gains, aggregate losses** — break good news into installments; deliver bad news all at once (based on Thaler's mental accounting)
3. **Door in the face (DITF)** — make an extreme request (expect rejection), then moderate to your actual target; the contrast effect makes the real request seem reasonable
4. **Foot in the door (FITD)** — get agreement on a small initial request, then escalate; commitment and consistency drive compliance
5. **Leverage the power of justification** — even a trivial "because" dramatically increases compliance (Langer's copy machine study: 60% to 93%)
6. **Leverage social proof** — demonstrate that others are doing what you want them to do ("If operators are busy, please call again")
7. **Make token unilateral concessions** — even small gifts trigger [[reciprocity-principle|reciprocity]] ($1 enclosed bill doubled survey response vs. $50 promised payment)
8. **Use reference points strategically** — the same deal seems great or terrible depending on what it is compared to

## The Six Defense Strategies

1. **Prepare systematically** — thorough BATNA/ZOPA/interest analysis protects against persuasion
2. **Create a scoring system** — objectively evaluate every proposal against your priorities
3. **Separate information from influence** — ask "Did I learn something new? Would I have done this yesterday?"
4. **Rephrase their offer in other terms** — convert loss-frame to gain-frame to test whether your reaction stems from merits or presentation
5. **Appoint a devil's advocate** — someone to question your beliefs and decisions
6. **Avoid negotiating under time pressure** — influence tactics work better when targets must respond quickly

## Mechanisms

These strategies exploit well-documented psychological phenomena from Phase 1 and Phase 2:

- [[loss-aversion]] (Strategies 1, 2)
- [[anchoring-and-adjustment]] (Strategies 3, 8)
- Commitment and consistency (Strategy 4)
- Heuristic processing / mindless compliance (Strategy 5)
- [[social-proof]] (Strategy 6)
- [[reciprocity-principle|Reciprocity]] (Strategy 7)
- [[framing-effects]] (Strategies 1, 2, 8)

## Applications

- **Salary negotiation:** Justify your request with evidence (Strategy 5), highlight what the employer loses by not retaining you (Strategy 1), cite market data showing peer compensation (Strategy 6)
- **Sales:** Start with a high anchor then moderate (Strategy 3), provide a justification for your price (Strategy 5), demonstrate that competitors' offers lack key features (Strategy 1)
- **Defending against manipulation:** Use the six defense strategies proactively, especially separating information from influence and creating a scoring system
