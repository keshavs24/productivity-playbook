# Intrinsic Goal Framing

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Intrinsic goal framing is the practice of presenting learning activities, tasks, or demands in terms of their connection to intrinsic goals -- personal growth, community contribution, health, meaningful relationships -- rather than extrinsic goals such as grades, status, wealth, or approval. Within [[self-determination-theory]], how activities are framed affects students' perceived locus of causality, need satisfaction, and the quality of their engagement and learning.

Intrinsic goal framing contrasts with extrinsic goal framing, which emphasizes external outcomes (performing well on tests, earning high grades, gaining competitive advantage). SDT research demonstrates that even when the same activity is performed, intrinsic framing leads to deeper processing, greater persistence, and better conceptual understanding than extrinsic framing.

This concept is closely related to [[intrinsic-goal-framing|Goal Contents Theory]] (GCT, Ch. 11 of SDT), which examines the differential effects of intrinsic vs. extrinsic life goals. In the educational domain, the framing of activities in terms of these goal types has specific consequences for learning quality.

## Key Findings

### Framing Effects on Learning

- Teachers who make clear the **importance and usefulness** of coursework -- connecting it to students' lives, values, and growth -- directly enhance students' perception of the course's value (Patall, Dent, Oyer, & Wynn, 2012).
- Providing an **autonomy-supportive rationale** for learning activities facilitates [[internalization-in-education|internalization]] and effort. Reeve, Jang, Hardre, & Omura (2002) showed that college students given an autonomy-supportive rationale for learning conversational Chinese more fully internalized the regulation and invested more effort.

### Mastery vs. Performance Goal Contexts

SDT's key insight is that the **motives underlying** achievement goals matter more than the goal type itself:

- **Vansteenkiste et al. (2010):** Performance-approach goal strength did not predict self-regulated learning, achievement, or cheating when autonomous/controlled motives were also entered. The motives, not the goals, carried the explanatory weight.
- **Benita, Roth, & Deci (2014):** Mastery goals in autonomy-supportive contexts yielded more positive emotional experiences and engagement than mastery goals in controlling contexts. The framing context determines the motivational quality of any goal.
- Grading schemes (A-F, 0-100%) function as extrinsic, performance-goal structures that undermine autonomous motivation and promote performance-avoidance goals (Pulfrey, Buchs, & Butera, 2011).

### Connecting Learning to Intrinsic Value

- When schools offer even some opportunities for [[intrinsic-motivation|intrinsically motivated]] activity (e.g., garden-based learning, child-centered play), the motivation and interest can **generalize** to regular classroom courses (Skinner et al., 2012; Ruiz-Gallardo et al., 2013).
- Activities framed as opportunities for personal growth and competence development support autonomy and competence need satisfaction, whereas activities framed as required performance benchmarks tend to feel controlling.

## Mechanisms

1. **Perceived locus of causality (PLOC):** Intrinsic framing supports an internal PLOC, helping students feel that learning is volitional. Extrinsic framing shifts PLOC external, making learning feel coerced.
2. **Need satisfaction:** Intrinsic frames connect to autonomy (learning for personal meaning), competence (mastering valuable skills), and relatedness (contributing to community). Extrinsic frames connect to ego involvement and social comparison.
3. **Depth of processing:** When students see material as personally valuable (intrinsic frame), they process it more deeply. When they see it as a means to an external end (extrinsic frame), they engage in surface-level rote processing sufficient to pass the evaluation (Grolnick & Ryan, 1987; Benware & Deci, 1984).
4. **Internalization pathway:** Intrinsic framing facilitates identified and integrated regulation -- students come to personally value the learning. Extrinsic framing at best produces introjected regulation (studying to avoid guilt/shame) or external regulation (studying only when monitored).

## Applications

- **Lesson design:** Frame learning activities in terms of their relevance to students' lives, personal growth, and community contribution rather than in terms of upcoming tests or grades
- **Rationale provision:** When assigning tasks that are not inherently interesting, provide autonomy-supportive rationale explaining why the skill/knowledge matters for the student's own development
- **Assessment redesign:** Use criterion-referenced assessments (mastery of material) rather than normative comparisons; frame feedback as informational rather than controlling
- **Curriculum communication:** Help students understand how subjects connect to intrinsic life goals rather than framing education primarily as instrumental for career advancement or economic outcomes

## Open Questions

- How can intrinsic goal framing be reconciled with legitimate extrinsic requirements (professional licensing exams, university admissions)?
- Does the effectiveness of intrinsic framing vary by subject area (e.g., easier for humanities than mathematics)?
- Can intrinsic goal framing compensate for other controlling aspects of the classroom (e.g., mandatory curriculum, time constraints)?
