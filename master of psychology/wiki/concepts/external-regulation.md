# External Regulation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

External regulation is the most controlled form of [[extrinsic-motivation]] within [[organismic-integration-theory]]. A behavior is externally regulated when it is motivated by and dependent upon external reward or punishment contingencies. The perceived locus of causality is fully external (E-PLOC). The person performs the behavior because of a separable consequence -- compliance with demands, earning rewards, or avoiding punishments -- not because they value or endorse the behavior.

## Key Findings

- The most studied form of extrinsic motivation, and the principal focus of operant/behavioral psychology
- Behavior is performed **only when the contingency is in effect** -- poor maintenance and transfer (Deci & Ryan, 1985b; Ryan & Deci, 2008b)
- People experience externally regulated behaviors as merely **instrumental** rather than personally valued
- External regulation tends to produce the **least effortful** approach with the **least attention to quality**
- Even external regulation requires some minimal **competence** to carry out the behavior
- External regulation at Time 1 was a **negative predictor** of behavioral persistence at Time 2 and became even weaker over time (Pelletier et al., 2001)
- Despite being the least autonomous, external regulators can be **powerfully motivated in the short term** -- rewards and punishments can compel and seduce people into action

## Mechanisms

External regulation operates through:
- **Contingency dependence** -- behavior occurs only when an expectancy (implicit or explicit) that a contingency is in effect
- **E-PLOC** -- the reason for behavior is the contingency, not personal endorsement
- Even finding rewards pleasant does not prevent the detrimental effect on autonomy

## Applications

- **Workplace:** Incentive systems that rely purely on external regulation produce compliance but not commitment. Connects to [[social-norms-vs-market-norms]] -- introducing market norms shifts motivation to external regulation.
- **Education:** Grades and prizes as sole motivators produce externally regulated learning that ceases without the contingency
- **Parenting:** Reward/punishment-only approaches produce compliance in the presence of surveillance but not genuine internalization
