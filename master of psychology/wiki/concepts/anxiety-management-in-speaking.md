# Anxiety Management in Speaking

**Phase(s):** 7
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Anxiety management in speaking is the systematic practice of identifying, categorizing, and addressing the symptoms and sources of anxiety that arise in spontaneous and public speaking situations. Rather than eliminating anxiety entirely (some anxiety is adaptive per the Yerkes-Dodson Law), the goal is to manage it so it doesn't interfere with communication effectiveness.

## Key Findings

- Speaking anxiety manifests in three categories -- the **ABCs**: Affective (mood), Behavioral (physiological), and Cognitive (mental). Effective management requires techniques that address each category ([[summary-7-26-tfts-ch1]])
- **Reframing anxiety as excitement** (Alison Wood Brooks) improved public speaking performance because both states share the same physiological arousal profile ([[summary-7-26-tfts-ch1]])
- **The spotlight effect** (Gilovich et al.) means we dramatically overestimate how much others notice our anxiety -- rationalizing this reduces cognitive symptoms ([[summary-7-26-tfts-ch1]])
- **Deep breathing with extended exhalation** (inhale 3, exhale 6) calms the nervous system within seconds by reducing CO2 (confirmed by neuroscientist Andrew Huberman) ([[summary-7-26-tfts-ch1]])
- **Synching speech with breath** -- landing phrases on the exhale and inhaling at pauses -- physically prevents filler words ("um," "uh") ([[summary-7-26-tfts-ch1]])

## Mechanisms

### The Anxiety Management Plan (AMP)
The core tool is a personalized **Anxiety Management Plan (AMP)**: select 3-5 techniques from the menu below, create a memorable acronym, and rehearse before entering speaking situations.

### Techniques by Symptom Category

**Affective (mood/feelings):**
- Practice mindfulness -- notice and acknowledge feelings without judgment
- Reframe anxiety as excitement
- Adopt a service orientation -- focus on serving the audience rather than protecting yourself

**Behavioral (physiological):**
- Deep breathing (Rule of Lung): inhale 3, exhale 6
- Slow movements -- gesture pace tracks with speech pace
- Cool your body -- hold something cold in hands
- Reactivate salivary glands -- chew gum or sip warm water before speaking
- Move deliberately -- address different sides of the room to dissipate adrenaline

**Cognitive (mental):**
- Positive mantras -- "I have value to add," "I've got this"
- Rewind to go forward -- repeat what you just said if you blank out
- Pose a generic question to the audience -- buys recovery time
- Get rational -- ask "What's the worst that could happen?" (spotlight effect)
- Inhale to reduce fillers -- time exhalation to sentence endings

### Sample AMPs
- **BOOM:** Be present oriented, Observe movements, Oxygenate, Mantra
- **ARC:** Acknowledge anxiety, Rationalize, Cool yourself down
- **HSM:** Heart (service orientation), Speech (slow gestures, ask questions), Mind (odds of blanking are low)

## Applications

- **Before entering a likely speaking situation:** Rehearse your AMP acronym, prepare a generic question as a safety net, activate salivary glands
- **In the moment of anxiety:** Execute your AMP techniques in sequence; start with breathing
- **After the event:** Reflect on which techniques helped, swap in alternatives if needed, update the acronym
- **Long-term development:** Practice techniques daily outside speaking contexts (e.g., 5 minutes of deep breathing per day)
