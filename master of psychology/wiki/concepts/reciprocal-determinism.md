# Reciprocal Determinism

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Reciprocal determinism (also called triadic reciprocal causation) is the foundational causal model of social cognitive theory. It posits that human functioning is the product of a dynamic interplay among three classes of determinants that influence one another bidirectionally:

- **P** (Person): Internal personal factors -- cognitive, affective, and biological events
- **B** (Behavior): Behavioral patterns and actions
- **E** (Environment): External environmental events and social structures

The term "determinism" is used to signify the production of effects by events operating probabilistically, not the doctrinaire sense that actions are completely determined by a prior sequence of causes independent of the individual. Reciprocity does not mean the three sets of interacting determinants are of equal strength -- their relative influence varies for different activities and under different circumstances. Nor do the mutual influences spring forth simultaneously as a holistic entity -- time lags in the operation of the three factors allow analysis of different segments of reciprocal causation without having to mount a Herculean effort to assess every possible interactant at the same time.

## Key Findings

- People are both producers and products of social systems -- social structures are created by human activity but in turn impose constraints and provide resources ([[summary-3-11-se-ch1-theoretical-perspectives]])
- Neither structural constraints nor enabling resources foreordain what individuals become and do -- within rule structures, there is a lot of personal variation in interpretation, enforcement, adoption, circumvention, or active opposition
- The model rejects both pure environmental determinism (Skinner) and pure personalism -- social influences operate through self-processes that produce the actions
- Freedom is defined positively as the exercise of self-influence to bring about desired results, not negatively as exemption from external influence
- In agentic transactions, people are both producers and products of social systems -- they have a hand in selecting and shaping their environmental contexts

## Mechanisms

Reciprocal determinism operates through several pathways:

1. **P → B:** Cognitive processes (beliefs, expectations, self-evaluative reactions) influence behavioral choices, effort, and persistence
2. **B → P:** Actions and their outcomes alter cognitive and affective states (e.g., mastery experiences build self-efficacy)
3. **E → P:** Environmental influences shape personal factors through modeling, instruction, social persuasion, and structural opportunities/constraints
4. **P → E:** People select, create, and transform environments based on their characteristics, beliefs, and preferences
5. **B → E:** Actions alter environmental conditions and social reactions
6. **E → B:** Environmental incentives, constraints, and opportunities shape behavior

The self system is not merely a conduit for external influences but a genuinely causal contributor. People construct personal standards, exercise self-regulation, and engage in reflective thought that generates novel actions not reducible to environmental inputs.

## Applications

- **Organizational behavior:** Understanding that employees are not merely shaped by organizational structures but actively shape them -- agency and structure are mutually constitutive
- **Education:** Students are not passive recipients of instruction but active agents who shape their learning environments through their efficacy beliefs and behaviors
- **Therapy:** Treatment should address all three nodes -- modifying beliefs (P), building behavioral skills (B), and restructuring environments (E)
- **Social change:** Personal and social change are complementary rather than rival approaches -- people change through both self-development and acting to alter institutional practices
