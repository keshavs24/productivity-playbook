# Autonomous Work Motivation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Autonomous work motivation is the composite of [[intrinsic-motivation]] and well-internalized [[extrinsic-motivation]] (identified and integrated regulation) applied to the work domain. It contrasts with **controlled work motivation**, which comprises external regulation (working for rewards/to avoid punishments) and introjected regulation (working out of guilt, ego-involvement, or contingent self-worth).

SDT holds that autonomous motivation is **necessary and essential** to high-quality engagement and performance at work, even when some controlled motives are also present. The absence of high autonomous motivation (e.g., identification with work) is problematic regardless of the presence of controlled motives (Moran, Diefendorff, Kim, & Liu, 2012).

Most work activities are not intrinsically motivating in themselves. Therefore, [[organismic-integration-theory]] is essential for understanding work motivation -- the critical question is the degree to which employees have internalized and integrated the value and regulation of their work tasks.

## Key Findings

- **Fernet, Austin, & Vallerand (2012):** Over 9 months, autonomously motivated employees showed greater job commitment and less emotional exhaustion; controlled motivation predicted greater emotional exhaustion
- **Otis & Pelletier (2005):** Police officers who perceived supervisors as highly autonomy-supportive displayed more autonomous work motivation, fewer daily hassles, lower physical symptoms, and stronger intentions to remain
- **Gagne & Koestner (2002):** Cross-lag analysis -- Time 1 [[relative-autonomy-index]] predicted Time 2 organizational commitment, but not the reverse. Autonomous motivation is a **base for commitment**, not a consequence of it
- **Grant, Nurmohamed, Ashford, & Dekas (2011):** High autonomous + low controlled motivation fostered proactive behaviors; job applicants received more offers and call center employees generated more revenue
- **White (2015):** In a service industry, employees' autonomous motivation accounted for more than half the variance in positive emotions and quality of service -- directly affecting customer satisfaction
- **Kuvaas et al. (2016):** Autonomous motivation strongly predicted effort and negatively predicted desire to leave; controlled motivation had only weak relation to effort and positively predicted desire to leave
- **Fernet, Guay, & Senecal (2004):** Decisional control on the job made a positive difference only for people high in autonomous motivation; those people also showed low levels of burnout

### Measurement
Autonomous work motivation is typically measured using subscales of the Work Self-Regulation Questionnaire or the Multidimensional Work Motivation Scale, and often summarized via the [[relative-autonomy-index]] (RAI). Doshi & McGregor (2015) translated this into the corporate concept of **ToMo (Total Motivation)**, which weights intrinsic motivation and identification positively and introjection and external regulation negatively.

### Cross-Cultural Validity
- **Levesque, Blais, & Hess (2004):** Gambian teachers with supportive supervisors were more autonomously motivated
- **Nie, Chua, Yeung, Ryan, & Chan (2015):** Chinese government-sponsored school teachers showed the same pattern
- **Deci, Ryan, Gagne, Leone, Usunov, & Kornazheva (2001):** The autonomy support --> need satisfaction --> work engagement model was largely invariant between Bulgaria and the US

## Mechanisms

1. **Need satisfaction:** Autonomous work motivation depends on satisfaction of autonomy, competence, and relatedness needs at work
2. **Internalization:** Through [[organismic-integration-theory]], extrinsic work regulations can be internalized from external through introjected, identified, and integrated forms. Deeper internalization produces more autonomous motivation
3. **Managerial climate:** [[autonomy-supportive-management]] facilitates internalization and autonomous motivation; controlling management fosters only external and introjected regulation
4. **Goal and value alignment:** When organizations promote [[intrinsic-motivation|intrinsic values]] (growth, contribution, relationships) over extrinsic values (wealth, status, image), employees show greater autonomous motivation and engagement

## Applications

- **Hiring:** Select for autonomous motivation profiles, not just controlled compliance
- **Onboarding:** Provide meaningful rationale for organizational practices to facilitate internalization from the start
- **Daily management:** Use informational feedback, perspective-taking, and participative decision-making to maintain autonomous motivation
- **Organizational change:** Introduce change autonomy-supportively (rationale, choice, acknowledgment of feelings) to facilitate acceptance rather than resistance
- **Performance systems:** Design evaluation and compensation systems that do not shift employees toward controlled motivation (see [[sdt-compensation-design]])

## Open Questions
- What is the optimal ratio of autonomous to controlled motivation at work? Is any controlled motivation beneficial (e.g., mild introjection providing additional drive)?
- How does autonomous work motivation interact with job demands -- is there a threshold of demands beyond which even autonomous motivation cannot buffer burnout?
- Can autonomous motivation be "too high" for routine, safety-critical tasks that require strict compliance?
