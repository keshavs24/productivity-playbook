# Introjected Regulation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Introjected regulation is a partially internalized form of [[extrinsic-motivation]] within [[organismic-integration-theory]]. Through introjection, a person takes in a regulation or value without fully transforming or assimilating it. The introject is experienced as a demanding and controlling internal force -- a sense that one "should" or "must" do something, or face anxiety, guilt, shame, or self-disparagement. Although the regulatory pressure is now internal, it is not experienced as truly one's own. Introjection represents internal control rather than autonomous self-regulation.

## Key Findings

- Introjection is the **most instructive instance** of how [[internalization]] is not all-or-nothing -- it shows that internal does not equal autonomous
- Phenomenologically associated with: **guilt, shame, contingent self-esteem, pride (contingent), [[ego-involvement]], self-critical perfectionism**
- More enduring than [[external-regulation]] because based on affective/evaluative contingencies within the individual rather than external surveillance
- Despite being internal, introjected regulation is **vitality draining** and ego depleting even when one succeeds at meeting the introject's demands (Nix, Ryan, Manly, & Deci, 1999; Ryan & Deci, 2008b)
- Produces a kind of **false self-esteem** that is unstable, fluctuating in response to relevant outcomes and evaluations (Kernis & Paradise, 2002; Ryan & Brown, 2006)
- Introjection involves **projection** -- people regulating through introjection tend to project their self-approval/disapproval onto imagined others, experiencing shame and fear of judgment even when no one is watching
- Connects to **self-infiltration** (Kuhl & Kazen, 1994) -- adopting goals that belong to someone else "as if" they were one's own, without genuine self-compatibility checking
- Ryan et al. (1993): Introjected religiosity was positively related to anxiety, depression, and somatization
- Pelletier et al. (2001): Introjected motivation predicted moderate athletic persistence at Time 2 but NOT at Time 3 -- it fades
- Koestner et al. (1996, 2001): Introjected reasons for political/environmental behavior predicted vulnerability to persuasion, reliance on others' opinions, and conflicted emotions

## Mechanisms

Introjection operates through:
1. **Internal contingencies** -- self-esteem, guilt, shame, pride tied to behavioral outcomes
2. **Internal control** -- one part of the person controlling other parts, creating inner division
3. **Projection** -- imagining others' evaluative judgments even when alone
4. **Partial PLOC shift** -- somewhat more internal than external regulation but still experienced as pressure rather than volition

Introjection is the mechanism underlying the [[want-should-conflict]]: the "should" has been partially internalized as guilt/shame pressure, but the person does not fully endorse the value, creating a tug between autonomous desires (want) and introjected demands (should).

## Applications

- **Understanding guilt-driven behavior:** People who exercise "because they should" or study "because they'd feel guilty otherwise" are operating from introjected regulation -- they persist in the short term but with lower quality engagement and higher burnout risk
- **Recognizing false self-esteem:** Achievement driven by introjection produces contingent, fragile self-worth. This explains why some high achievers feel empty despite success.
- **Moving beyond introjection:** The path from introjection to [[identified-regulation]] requires autonomy support -- helping people reflectively explore why a value matters to them personally, rather than simply pressuring compliance

## Open Questions

- Is there a "healthy introjection" -- cases where introjected guilt serves as a useful alarm signal?
- In addiction contexts, introjected regulation sometimes supplements autonomous regulation in predicting positive outcomes (Ryan et al., 1995) -- when and why?
- How does introjection relate to [[cognitive-dissonance]]? Both involve internal tension, but introjection is about not-yet-endorsed values while dissonance is about inconsistency between endorsed beliefs and behavior.
