# Relative Aspirations

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Relative aspirations refers to the core measurement principle in [[goal-contents-theory|GCT]]: it is the *ratio* of [[intrinsic-aspirations|intrinsic]] to [[extrinsic-aspirations|extrinsic]] goal importance that most strongly predicts well-being outcomes, not the absolute level of either type of aspiration. Most people endorse both intrinsic and extrinsic goals to some degree. The critical question is which type takes priority when they compete for time, energy, and attention.

The [[aspiration-index]] operationalizes this by computing the relative importance of each aspiration category. A person who rates both personal growth and financial success as "very important" but rates personal growth even higher has a more intrinsic relative aspiration profile than someone who rates financial success highest.

## Key Findings

- **Kasser & Ryan (1993):** The relative importance of financial success aspirations (compared to intrinsic aspirations) was the key predictor -- not the absolute endorsement of financial goals. People can value financial success moderately without harm to well-being; it is when financial success dominates over intrinsic aspirations that problems emerge.
- **Grouzet et al. (2005):** 15-nation circumplex study confirmed that intrinsic and extrinsic aspirations are antipodal -- the more one focuses on extrinsic goals like fame and wealth, the less likely one is to place importance on community and close relationships. This trade-off structure is universal across cultures.
- **Relative scoring is more robust than absolute scoring** across diverse cultures and economic contexts (Ryan et al., 1999; Schmuck et al., 2000; Martos & Kopp, 2014). The relative metric accounts for cultural variation in baseline endorsement levels.
- **Changes in relative aspirations predict changes in well-being:** Three longitudinal studies (Kasser et al., 2014, including one 12-year study) showed that shifts toward more intrinsic relative aspirations predicted increased well-being, and shifts toward more extrinsic relative aspirations predicted decreased well-being.

## Mechanisms

The relative scoring approach captures a fundamental psychological reality: human attention, time, and energy are finite. When extrinsic goals dominate, they crowd out the pursuit of intrinsic goals that more directly satisfy [[basic-psychological-needs]]. It is this displacement effect -- the opportunity cost of extrinsic focus -- that largely explains the well-being costs of extrinsic aspirations, above and beyond the direct need-frustrating qualities of extrinsic pursuit.

## Applications

- **Self-assessment:** The question is not "do I care about money?" but "do I care about money *more* than personal growth, relationships, and contribution?" The relative priority is what matters.
- **Goal portfolio analysis:** Treat life goals like an investment portfolio -- examine the balance between intrinsic and extrinsic holdings and rebalance toward intrinsic priorities
- **Intervention design:** Effective interventions shift the ratio rather than trying to eliminate extrinsic aspirations entirely (which may be unrealistic)
