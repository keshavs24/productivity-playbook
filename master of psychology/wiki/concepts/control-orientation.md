# Control Orientation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The control orientation is one of three general causality orientations in [[causality-orientations-theory]]. It describes the degree to which people's attention and concerns orient toward external contingencies and controls. Individuals high in the control orientation experience social contexts primarily in terms of rewards and social pressures they must either comply with or defy, often losing sight of their own values and interests. They tend to use [[external-regulation]] and [[introjected-regulation]] as predominant regulatory styles and have low [[intrinsic-motivation]].

## Key Findings

- Positively correlated with **public self-consciousness and Type-A personality** (Deci & Ryan, 1985a)
- Linked to **Machiavellianism** (McHoskey, 1999)
- Associated with **cheating** when given opportunity (Lonky & Reihman, 1990)
- Preference for **image-based advertising** over quality-based (Zuckerman et al., 1988)
- High value on **wealth** relative to intrinsic aspirations (Kasser & Ryan, 1993)
- Related to **normative identity style** -- relying on others' expectations (Soenens et al., 2005)
- Adopt **performance goals** and persist rigidly in ego-involved ways after failure (Koestner & Zuckerman, 1994)
- Associated with **gambling, alcohol problems, disordered gambling** (Neighbors & Larimer, 2004; Neighbors et al., 2004)
- Linked to **road rage, aggressive driving, traffic citations** (Knee et al., 2001; Neighbors et al., 2002)
- Connected to **interpersonal aggression** and tendency to dehumanize others (Moller & Deci, 2010)
- Sports parents higher in control orientation more prone to **anger and aggression** at children's events (Goldstein & Iso-Ahola, 2008)
- **More vulnerable to reward undermining** than autonomy-oriented individuals (Hagger & Chatzisarantis, 2011)
- Not reliably linked to positive wellness outcomes
- Being motivated and persistent, but the form of motivation is **nonoptimal** and predictive of poorer well-being

## Mechanisms

The control orientation operates through:
1. **Controlling interpretation** -- construing events as pressures, demands, contingencies
2. **External focus** -- attending to what others think, external judgments, rewards/punishments
3. **Compliance or defiance** -- either going along with perceived controls or rebelliously opposing them
4. **Loss of authentic self-reference** -- losing touch with one's own values and interests

## Applications

- **Risk factor identification:** High control orientation is a risk factor for problem behaviors (gambling, aggression, alcohol)
- **Workplace dynamics:** Control-oriented employees may "pull on" supervisors to be more controlling, creating self-reinforcing cycles
- **Parenting:** Control-oriented parents tend to use more controlling techniques, fostering introjection in children
