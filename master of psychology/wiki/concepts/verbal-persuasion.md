# Verbal Persuasion

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Verbal persuasion (also called social persuasion) is the third [[sources-of-self-efficacy|source of self-efficacy]], operating through the evaluative feedback and encouragement people receive from others (Bandura, 1997, pp.101-106). It is easier to sustain a sense of efficacy when struggling with difficulties if significant others express faith in one's capabilities than if they convey doubts. Verbal persuasion alone may be limited in its power to create enduring increases in perceived efficacy, but it can bolster self-change if the positive appraisal is within realistic bounds.

## Key Findings

- Persuasory efficacy attributions have their greatest impact on people who have some reason to believe they can produce effects through their actions (Chambliss & Murray, 1979a, 1979b)
- Unrealistic persuasory appraisals that mislead performers to repeated failures only discredit the persuaders and further reinforce performers' belief in their inherent limitations
- **Ability feedback vs. effort feedback:** Telling children they have ability enhances efficacy more than telling them they tried hard (Schunk, 1984b). Being told repeatedly that progress is a product of high effort conveys the message that one's talents are limited (Schunk & Rice, 1986)
- **Framing of feedback matters profoundly** (Jourden, 1991): Identical performance feedback framed as gains (75% progress toward the standard) enhances efficacy, goals, and performance; framed as shortfalls (25% deficit from the standard) diminishes all three (Figure 3.3). Connects to [[framing-effects]].
- Social evaluations of limited capability are often conveyed indirectly: unchallenging task assignments, excessive praise for mediocre performances, treating faulty performance with indifference, and unsolicited help
- Persuasory opinions are only as strong as the recipient's confidence in the persuader -- mediated by perceived credibility and expertness (Crandall & Foddy, 1981; Webster & Sobieszek, 1974)
- Persuasory appraisals are most believable when only moderately beyond what individuals can currently do
- Social persuasion serves best as part of a multifaceted strategy, not as a standalone intervention

**Conditions for effective verbal persuasion:**
1. The persuader must be credible and knowledgeable about the domain
2. The appraisal must be within realistic bounds (only moderately beyond current capability)
3. The persuasion should be accompanied by structured activities that bring success
4. Feedback should frame progress in terms of gains rather than shortfalls
5. Success should be measured in terms of self-improvement rather than triumphs over others

## Mechanisms

Verbal persuasion affects efficacy through:
1. **Direct appraisal:** Others' explicit statements about one's capabilities
2. **Evaluative feedback framing:** How performance information is structured -- gain vs. deficit framing (connects to [[framing-effects]])
3. **Indirect social evaluative signals:** Task assignments, level of recognition, degree of assistance offered
4. **Credibility weighting:** Persuasory impact is modulated by the perceived expertise and trustworthiness of the source
5. **Appraisal disparity:** The optimal gap between stated capability and current level depends on temporal proximity and nature of the activity

During formative years, significant models play a key role in instilling beliefs of potential and power through sustained persuasory influence that shapes basic orientations to life. Skilled efficacy builders do much more than convey positive appraisals -- they structure activities for success and encourage self-improvement metrics.

## Applications

- **Coaching:** Frame feedback in terms of progress and gains, not deficits and shortfalls. Connects to [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]] -- informational feedback that acknowledges competence supports both intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy.
- **Mentoring:** Structure early experiences for success before introducing challenges. Avoid premature placement in failure-producing situations.
- **Feedback design:** Highlight ability rather than effort when attributing success, but be truthful -- fabricated appraisals backfire
- **Indirect signals:** Be aware that task assignments, recognition patterns, and help-giving can inadvertently communicate low capability expectations
- **Autonomy-supportive framing:** Provide efficacy-building feedback within contexts that support autonomy (connects to [[autonomy-support]])
