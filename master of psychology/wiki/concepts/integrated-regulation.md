# Integrated Regulation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Integrated regulation is the most autonomous form of [[extrinsic-motivation]] within [[organismic-integration-theory]]. It represents the fullest type of [[internalization]] -- the person has not only identified with the value of a behavior but has brought that identification into congruence with their other values, goals, needs, and aspects of self. The regulation is experienced as fully authentic (Ryan & Deci, 2004a, 2006). Unlike [[intrinsic-motivation]], integrated regulation is still extrinsic because the activity is done for its importance and congruence with one's values, not for its inherent enjoyment.

## Key Findings

- Integration involves **self-reflection and reciprocal assimilation** -- assimilating the new identification may require modifying other previously held values or attitudes
- Requires passing a **self-compatibility check** (Kazen et al., 2003) -- the identification is holistically embraced, not defensively held
- Results in a **highly stable and mature form of self-regulation** that allows for flexible guidance of action
- Integrated regulation and [[intrinsic-motivation]] share qualities of **flexibility and volition** but differ in whether the activity is done for its own sake (intrinsic) or for its congruence with valued goals (integrated)
- Both intrinsic and integrated forms of regulation are facilitated by supports for [[basic-psychological-needs]]
- **Neural correlates:** Integration likely involves right medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC) for self-knowledge processing and self-compatibility checking (Ryan, Kuhl, & Deci, 1997). Di Domenico et al. (2013, 2016) showed higher need satisfaction associated with MPFC activation
- Legault, Green-Demers, Grant, and Chung (2007): More autonomous internalization of motivation to regulate prejudice predicted lower prejudice on both explicit and implicit measures

## Mechanisms

Integration operates through:
1. **Self-reflection** -- mindful examination of how a value relates to other aspects of one's identity
2. **Reciprocal assimilation** -- bringing the new regulation into congruence with existing self-structures, which may involve modifying both
3. **Holistic endorsement** -- the regulation is not compartmentalized but connected to the broader system of values and needs
4. **Congruence** -- absence of conflict between the regulation and other deeply held values

## Applications

- **Sustained behavior change:** Integrated regulation predicts the most durable behavior change because the behavior is self-concordant
- **Value-driven living:** Activities pursued from integrated regulation feel authentic and meaningful even when not inherently enjoyable
- **Identity coherence:** The process of integration contributes to personality coherence and psychological health
