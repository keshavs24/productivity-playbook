# Overclaiming Credit

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition
The systematic tendency for individuals to overestimate their own contributions to joint endeavors. When members of a group independently estimate the percentage of work they personally performed, the total routinely exceeds 100% -- often significantly. This is a manifestation of [[bounded-ethicality]] rooted in the [[self-serving-bias-in-decisions]].

## Key Findings
- **Ross and Sicoly (1979):** Asked married couples to estimate their percentage of household tasks (dishes, trash, etc.). Combined estimates averaged close to 140%.
- **Caruso, Epley, and Bazerman (2006):** Replicated overclaiming in academia, athletics (Brawley, 1984; Forsyth & Schlenker, 1977), and fundraising (Zander, 1971)
- **Joint ventures:** Overclaiming helps explain why joint ventures so often end in disappointment (Epley, Caruso, & Bazerman, 2006). Each partner overclaims credit, perceives the other as unfair, and reduces their own contribution -- creating a downward spiral.
- **Harvard MBA groups (Caruso, Epley, & Bazerman, 2007):** Group members' claims of work completed averaged 139% per group. Greater overclaiming was associated with less desire to work together in the future.
- **Academic authorship:** In multi-author papers (3-6 authors), overclaiming was rampant.
- **Partial remedy -- "unpacking":** When researchers first asked group members to think about each other member's contributions before estimating their own, the overclaiming total fell from 139% to 121%. Perspective-taking reduces the bias but does not eliminate it.

## Mechanisms
- **Availability bias:** Your own contributions are more salient and memorable to you than others' contributions
- **[[self-serving-bias-in-decisions]]:** Natural tendency to see yourself in a favorable light
- **Asymmetric information:** You have full access to your own effort and intent but only partial visibility into what others did
- Crucially, overclaiming is not dishonesty -- "even honest people tend to believe they contributed more to an enterprise than they actually did"

## Applications
- **Team management:** Before claiming credit or blaming a colleague for undercontributing, consider that you might be overclaiming
- **Conflict prevention:** In partnerships and collaborations, make contributions visible to all parties to reduce the information asymmetry that fuels overclaiming
- **Pre-emptive perspective-taking:** Before estimating your own contribution, deliberately enumerate what each other person did
- **Organizational culture:** Recognize that the greater the magnitude of overclaiming, the less parties want to collaborate in the future
