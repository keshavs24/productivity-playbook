# Calibrated Questions

**Phase(s):** 5 — Negotiation
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Calibrated questions are carefully crafted open-ended questions — primarily beginning with "How" or "What" — that remove aggression from conversations by acknowledging the other side openly. They introduce ideas and requests without sounding pushy, giving the counterpart the [[illusion-of-control]] while actually constraining and directing the conversation. The term "calibrated" reflects the precision required: like a gun sight or measuring scale, these questions are targeted at a specific problem.

## Key Findings

- **Origin**: Discovered after the catastrophic [[Dos Palmas]] hostage crisis (2001-2002). The breakthrough came from an untrained drug dealer in Pittsburgh who instinctively asked "Hey, dog, how do I know she's all right?" — causing the kidnapper to voluntarily put the victim on the phone ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch7-illusion-control]])
- **Construction rules**: Avoid closed-ended verbs ("can," "is," "are," "do," "does"). Start with "what" or "how." Avoid "why" (accusatory in any language). "Who," "when," and "where" merely extract facts without tactical advantage.
- **The master question**: "How am I supposed to do that?" — says "No" without saying the word, invites the other side to participate in your dilemma, and triggers [[forced-empathy]]
- **Implicit message**: Every well-designed calibrated question communicates "I want what you want, but I need your intelligence to overcome the problem." This appeals powerfully to aggressive or egotistical counterparts.
- **Self-control prerequisite**: Without emotional regulation, calibrated questions fail. The negotiator must bite their tongue, pause, and think — training the neocortex to override the reptilian brain and limbic system ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch7-illusion-control]])

## Mechanisms

Calibrated questions work through several psychological mechanisms:

1. **Suspending unbelief** (Kevin Dutton, *Split-Second Persuasion*): "Unbelief" is active resistance to the other side. Calibrated questions bypass this by asking for help rather than making demands. "Once we achieve [suspending unbelief], the game's half-won."
2. **Forced empathy**: "How am I supposed to do that?" forces the counterpart to take a good look at your situation, especially effective if preceded by empathic engagement
3. **Illusion of control**: The counterpart thinks they're in charge because they're answering questions and providing solutions. In reality, the questioner is framing and constraining the conversation.
4. **Reciprocity avoidance**: Unlike closed-ended proof-of-life questions that trigger the reciprocity norm (you asked, they answered, now they expect something back), calibrated questions generate effort and thought from the counterpart without creating a quid pro quo obligation
5. **Internal coordination**: In group scenarios (e.g., kidnappings), calibrated questions force internal deliberation among the opposition, buying time and creating friction

## Key Calibrated Questions

Voss's standbys for nearly every negotiation:

- What about this is important to you?
- How can I help to make this better for us?
- How would you like me to proceed?
- What is it that brought us into this situation?
- How can we solve this problem?
- What's the objective? / What are we trying to accomplish here?
- How am I supposed to do that?
- What's the biggest challenge you face?
- How does this affect the rest of your team?
- How on board are the people not on this call?
- How will we know we're on track?
- How will we address things if we find we're off track?

## Applications

- **Hostage negotiation**: "How do we know the hostage is alive?" replaced closed proof-of-life questions throughout FBI practice after Dos Palmas
- **Business payment collection**: "How am I supposed to do that?" forces deadbeat clients to prioritize your needs ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch7-illusion-control]], Kelly the accountant)
- **Salary negotiation**: "What does it take to be successful here?" turns the interviewer into a stakeholder in your success ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Guaranteeing execution**: "How will we know we're on track?" forces counterparts to articulate implementation in their own words, increasing buy-in ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch8-execution]])
- **Saying "No" gracefully**: Use "How" questions four times before actually saying "No" — "How am I supposed to do that?" / "Your offer is generous, but it just doesn't work for me" / "I'm afraid I just can't do that" / "I'm sorry, no" ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch8-execution]])
