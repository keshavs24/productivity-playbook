# Bayesian Conservatism

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Bayesian conservatism is the tendency to **insufficiently update beliefs** in response to accumulating evidence. Where [[base-rate-neglect]] involves ignoring prior probabilities and over-relying on evidence, conservatism is the opposite: people do not give enough weight to new data. Edwards (1968) coined the term after finding that people update their probability estimates only 1/5 to 1/2 as much as Bayes's theorem prescribes when evidence accumulates incrementally.

## Key Findings

- Edwards (1968) poker chip experiment: two bags of 100 chips (one 70 red/30 blue, one 70 blue/30 red); after drawing one red chip, Bayesian posterior = .70, but participants estimated only ~.60 ([[summary-2-09-anderson-ch11]])
- After 12 draws (8 red, 4 blue), Bayesian posterior = .97 for the red-majority bag, but participants estimated only ~.75
- Edwards estimated people give between 1/5 and 1/2 of the proper weight to the available evidence
- The apparent contradiction with [[base-rate-neglect]]: conservatism says people under-weight evidence while base-rate neglect says they over-weight evidence. Anderson notes this may reflect different experimental conditions: conservatism appears when data accumulate incrementally (each chip drawn one at a time), while base-rate neglect appears when vivid individuating information is presented all at once

## Mechanisms

When evidence arrives incrementally, each piece produces a modest update. People appear to "anchor" on their current estimate and adjust insufficiently with each new data point -- conceptually related to [[anchoring-and-adjustment]]. The incremental nature of the task may make each individual piece of evidence feel less compelling than it should be. In contrast, when a vivid description is presented all at once (as in Kahneman & Tversky's engineer/lawyer study), the description overwhelms the base rate.

## Applications

- **Intelligence analysis:** Analysts receiving information piece by piece may fail to update their assessment sufficiently, maintaining outdated estimates despite accumulating contrary evidence
- **Medical diagnosis:** Doctors may under-update their diagnosis as new test results arrive incrementally, especially when early evidence pointed in a different direction
- **Investment decisions:** Slow-moving trends (gradually worsening fundamentals) may be under-weighted because each individual data point produces insufficient updating
