# STEPPS Framework

**Phase(s):** 6
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The STEPPS framework is [[berger]]'s six-principle model for understanding and engineering contagious content — products, ideas, and behaviors that are talked about, shared, and imitated. The acronym stands for **S**ocial Currency, **T**riggers, **E**motion, **P**ublic, **P**ractical Value, and **S**tories. Each principle represents a distinct psychological driver of word of mouth and social transmission. The framework emerged from Berger's decade-plus of research at the Wharton School analyzing thousands of *New York Times* articles, millions of car purchases, hundreds of BzzAgent campaigns, and hundreds of viral videos and products.

## The Six Principles

| Principle | Core Mechanism | Tagline |
|---|---|---|
| **[[social-currency]]** | We share things that make us look good | "People share things that make us look good" |
| **[[environmental-triggers]]** | Top of mind = tip of tongue | "Top of mind, tip of tongue" |
| **[[high-arousal-emotions]]** | Physiological arousal drives sharing | "When we care, we share" |
| **[[behavioral-residue]]** (Public) | Observable behavior is imitable | "Built to show, built to grow" |
| **[[practical-value]]** | People share useful information | "News you can use" |
| **[[trojan-horse-stories]]** | Information travels inside narratives | "Information travels under the guise of idle chatter" |

## Key Findings

- The principles are **independent and modular** — not all six are required for something to catch on. Think of them as salad toppings rather than a recipe. Any combination can work, but more is better.
- The principles can be built into the **product/idea itself** (Barclay Prime's cheesesteak has inherent social currency; Rebecca Black's "Friday" has a natural trigger) or into the **messaging around it** (Blendtec's "Will It Blend?" videos wrap the blender in a story; Kit Kat's campaign linked the product to coffee as a trigger).
- Social epidemics are driven by the **products and ideas themselves**, not by a small handful of special "influential" people — contrary to the thesis in Gladwell's *The Tipping Point*.
- **Applying the STEPPS is a checklist exercise:** Does talking about your product make people look good? (Social Currency) What cues trigger people to think about it? (Triggers) Does it generate emotion? (Emotion) Can people see others using it? (Public) Does it help people? (Practical Value) Can it be embedded in a story? (Stories)

## Mechanisms

Each principle exploits a different aspect of human psychology:
- **Social Currency** exploits the desire for status and impression management (self-enhancement motive)
- **Triggers** exploits the accessibility heuristic — whatever is top of mind gets talked about ([[availability]])
- **Emotion** exploits physiological arousal — activated states drive action, including sharing
- **Public** exploits [[social-proof]] — observable behavior provides information and social pressure to conform
- **Practical Value** exploits altruistic motivation and reciprocity — helping others feels good and strengthens bonds
- **Stories** exploits narrative processing — humans think in stories, not facts, and stories reduce counterarguing

## Applications

The framework applies to any domain:
- **Marketing:** Making products more contagious (Blendtec, Kit Kat, Barclay Prime)
- **Public health:** Designing campaigns that spread healthy behaviors (fruits/vegetables tray slogan)
- **Nonprofits:** Raising awareness and funds (Movember Foundation, Livestrong wristbands)
- **Politics:** Crafting messages that spread (voting location effects, campaign slogans)
- **Education:** Making lessons memorable and shareable
- **Personal branding:** Crafting stories and content that people want to pass along

## Comparison with Related Frameworks

### STEPPS vs. SUCCESs ([[success-framework]])
Chip and Dan Heath's *Made to Stick* framework (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories) focuses on making ideas **stick** — getting people to remember them. STEPPS focuses on making ideas **spread** — getting people to pass them on. Berger explicitly notes that the Heaths' book was a key influence but that "sticking" and "spreading" are different phenomena. An idea can stick (be memorable) without spreading (being shared), and vice versa. The frameworks are complementary.
