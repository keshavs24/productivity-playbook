# Conditional Regard

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Conditional regard is the practice of making the love, attention, affection, or esteem an individual receives from a relational partner contingent upon the individual doing what the partner demands. It is a common socializing technique, especially in parenting, where parents give attention and warmth when children comply with parental demands and withdraw affection or expressions of love when children fail to do what is wanted.

Within [[self-determination-theory-framework|SDT]] and [[relationships-motivation-theory|RMT]], conditional regard is understood as a controlling technique that forces a trade-off between [[autonomy-need|autonomy]] and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]]: the individual must relinquish autonomy (performing specified behaviors under pressure) in order to maintain relatedness (receiving love and approval). This forced antagonism between needs is the core mechanism by which conditional regard produces harm.

Conditional regard can be separated into two subtypes (Kanat-Maymon, Roth, Assor, & Raizer, 2016):
- **Conditional positive regard:** Giving extra attention and affection contingent on desired behavior
- **Conditional negative regard:** Withdrawing attention and affection contingent on undesired behavior

Both forms predict poorer quality relationships and lower wellness, mediated by need satisfaction.

## Key Findings

### Parental Conditional Regard (PCR)
- **Assor, Roth, & Deci (2004):** College students reporting higher PCR also reported higher internal pressure to perform target behaviors, but with substantial costs: fragile self-esteem, short-lived satisfaction after success, shame and guilt after failure, poorer coping strategies. PCR also damaged the parent-child relationship -- children felt more rejected and reported more anger and resentment toward parents.
- **Assor (2004):** Children exposed to conditional regard develop *contingent self-esteem* -- they learn to value themselves conditionally, depending on whether they display the attributes and behaviors that earned parental approval (Deci & Ryan, 1995).

### Conditional Regard and Peer Relationships
- **Moller, Roth, Niemiec, Kanat-Maymon, & Deci (2016):**
  - Students who perceived parents as conditionally regarding also perceived peer partners as conditionally regarding
  - Parental conditional regard predicted less need satisfaction not only with parents but also with best friends and romantic partners
  - A selection effect was found: students whose parents used conditional regard tended to *choose* peer partners who were themselves conditionally regarding (the correlation between length of time together and partner's conditional regard was not significant, ruling out a "training" effect)
  - An experimental paradigm showed projection: students who rated parents high on conditional regard rated a confederate (who behaved identically toward all participants) as more conditionally regarding

### Conditional Regard in Romantic Relationships
- **Kanat-Maymon, Roth, Assor, & Raizer (2016):** Both conditional positive regard and conditional negative regard predicted poorer quality romantic relationships, even after controlling for perceived negative regard and partners' warmth. Links were mediated by need satisfaction.

### Relationship-Contingent Self-Esteem
- **Knee, Canavello, Bush, & Cook (2008):** When self-esteem is contingent upon one's romantic relationship, it makes people "needy" and highly committed but does not produce closeness, satisfaction, or positive relational outcomes. When one partner is high and the other low in relationship-contingent self-esteem, the low partner shows very low commitment.

## Mechanisms

1. **Need antagonism:** Conditional regard forces a choice -- give up autonomy to maintain relatedness, or assert autonomy and lose approval. Either way, at least one basic need goes unsatisfied.
2. **Introjection:** The individual internalizes the contingency as an internal pressure (introjected regulation), performing behaviors out of guilt, shame, or contingent self-worth rather than genuine endorsement.
3. **Self-esteem fragility:** Because self-worth becomes tied to performing specific behaviors, self-esteem fluctuates with success and failure at those behaviors rather than resting on a stable sense of intrinsic worth.
4. **Relational template formation:** Children who experience PCR develop expectations that relationships are conditional, leading them to select (and project conditional regard onto) future relationship partners.

## Applications

- **Parenting:** Replace conditional regard with unconditional positive regard -- accepting and valuing the child as a person while setting limits autonomy-supportively. Structure and expectations can be communicated without making love contingent on compliance.
- **Romantic relationships:** Partners should be aware of subtle conditional regard patterns (e.g., withdrawing warmth after disagreements, providing affection primarily after the partner does something desired).
- **Education:** Teacher approval and warmth should not be contingent on academic performance. Students should feel valued as persons independent of their grades.
- **Self-awareness:** Adults can examine whether their own self-esteem is contingently based (on relationships, achievements, appearance) and work toward a more unconditional self-regard.
