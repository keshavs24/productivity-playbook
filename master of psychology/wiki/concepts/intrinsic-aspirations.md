# Intrinsic Aspirations

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Intrinsic aspirations are life goals focused on pursuits that are inherently valued and directly satisfying of [[basic-psychological-needs]]. As defined within [[goal-contents-theory|GCT]], the four categories of intrinsic aspirations are:

1. **Personal growth** -- developing as a person, gaining new insights, realizing one's potential
2. **Close relationships** (affiliation) -- forming deep, meaningful bonds with others
3. **Community contribution** (generativity) -- making the world a better place, helping others
4. **Physical health** -- being physically healthy and fit (added in later formulations)

These goals are termed "intrinsic" because they are pursued for their own sake and because their pursuit and attainment inherently involves experiences that satisfy [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], [[competence-need|competence]], and [[relatedness-need|relatedness]]. They are contrasted with [[extrinsic-aspirations]] (wealth, fame, image) which are pursued for instrumental, contingent, or socially comparative reasons.

## Key Findings

- **Kasser & Ryan (1993, 1996):** Initial studies established that placing high [[relative-aspirations|relative importance]] on intrinsic aspirations was associated with greater self-actualization, vitality, less depression, fewer physical symptoms, and lower anxiety
- **Cross-cultural replication:** Intrinsic aspiration effects on well-being confirmed across U.S., Russia (Ryan et al., 1999), Germany (Schmuck et al., 2000), Hungary (Martos & Kopp, 2014), Scandinavia (Utvaer et al., 2014), China (Tang et al., 2008), South Korea (Kim et al., 2003), UK and Chile (Unanue et al., 2014), and 15-nation circumplex study (Grouzet et al., 2005)
- **Attainment matters:** Attaining intrinsic goals enhances well-being, mediated by basic need satisfaction (Sheldon & Kasser, 1998; Niemiec et al., 2009). Attainment effects were significant even controlling for extrinsic attainments.
- **Living the values:** Behaviors consistent with intrinsic aspirations were more predictive of well-being than merely espousing them (Sheldon & Krieger, 2014b)
- **Longitudinal changes:** When people become more intrinsically oriented over time, well-being increases; when they become more extrinsically oriented, well-being decreases (Kasser et al., 2014, three longitudinal studies including one over 12 years)
- **Associated with higher quality relationships** (Kasser & Ryan, 2001), greater school success (Fryer et al., 2014), greater spirituality and less greed (Cozzolino et al., 2004), and pro-environmental behavior (Brown & Kasser, 2005)
- **Children and adolescents:** Results hold for young people as well as adults (Easterbrook et al., 2014; Ku et al., 2014)

## Mechanisms

- Intrinsic goals directly involve activities that satisfy basic psychological needs: connecting with others (relatedness), developing skills and understanding (competence), acting with volition and self-endorsement (autonomy)
- They are more easily integrated into the self and more readily pursued autonomously
- Autonomy-supportive environments and nature exposure promote intrinsic goal valuation (Weinstein, Przybylski, & Ryan, 2009)
- Mindfulness is associated with greater intrinsic goal orientations (Brown & Kasser, 2005)

## Applications

- **Reflection exercises:** Writing about and reflecting on intrinsic aspirations (e.g., having close friends) rather than extrinsic aspirations (e.g., being popular) over 4 weeks increased well-being and strengthened intrinsic priority (Lekes et al., 2012)
- **Goal framing:** Introducing learning tasks in terms of their intrinsic relevance (personal growth, helping others) rather than extrinsic payoffs (income, attractiveness) enhances engagement, persistence, and deep learning (Vansteenkiste et al., 2004)
- **Parenting and education:** Model and authentically live intrinsic values; autonomy-supportive environments naturally cultivate intrinsic aspirations in children
