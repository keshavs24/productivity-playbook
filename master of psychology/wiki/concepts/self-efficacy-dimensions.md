# Self-Efficacy Dimensions

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

[[Self-efficacy]] beliefs vary along three dimensions that have important performance implications and measurement requirements (Bandura, 1997, pp.42-44):

1. **Level (magnitude):** The degree of difficulty of tasks a person believes they can accomplish. Some individuals' perceived efficacy is limited to simple task demands; others extend their perceived efficacy to moderately difficult demands or to the most taxing demands within a domain. Measured against levels of task demands that represent varying degrees of challenge or impediment.

2. **Strength:** How firmly a person holds their efficacy beliefs. Weak efficacy beliefs are easily negated by disconfirming experiences, whereas people with tenacious beliefs persevere despite innumerable difficulties. Strength is not necessarily linearly related to choice behavior -- a certain threshold of self-assurance is needed to attempt a course of action, but higher strengths produce greater perseverance. Measured on a 100-point scale from 0 ("Cannot do") to 100 ("Certain can do"), with unipolar scales (no negative values).

3. **Generality:** The breadth of domains across which a person judges themselves efficacious. People may judge themselves efficacious across a wide range of activities or only in certain domains. Generality can vary on degree of similarity of activities, modalities of capability expression (behavioral, cognitive, affective), qualitative features of situations, and characteristics of persons toward whom behavior is directed.

## Key Findings

- Efficacy strength is generally a more sensitive and informative measure than efficacy level ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Self-efficacy scales should be unipolar, ranging from 0 to maximum strength, with no negative values -- a judgment of complete incapability (0) has no lower gradations
- Scales using too few steps (e.g., 0-10 with single unit intervals) should be avoided because they are less sensitive and less reliable (Steiner & Norman, 1989)
- Items are phrased in terms of *can do* rather than *will do* -- self-efficacy is a judgment of capability, not intention, and the two constructs are conceptually and empirically separable (Ajzen & Madden, 1986; Arch, 1992b)
- The dual-judgment format (first judge whether you can do it, then rate strength) and single-judgment format (rate strength from 0-100 for every item) provide essentially the same information, but the single-judgment format is easier and more convenient
- Descending format ordering (most to least difficult) tends to produce slightly higher self-efficacy appraisals than ascending format; ascending or random orders are preferred ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Domain-specific measures consistently outperform global measures in predictive power -- a self-efficacy measure tailored to the mathematical domain is more predictive than one cast in terms of general academic competence
- Particularized efficacy beliefs are most predictive because they are the types of beliefs that guide which activities are undertaken and how well they are performed

### Discriminative Generalization (pp.50-53)
At least five processes produce some generality in personal efficacy:
1. **Similar subskills:** When different activities are governed by similar subskills, mastery generalizes
2. **Codevelopment:** When competencies are socially structured to develop together, perceived efficacy generalizes even across dissimilar domains
3. **Self-regulatory skills:** Widely applicable metastrategies generalize across activity domains
4. **Construing commonalities:** Structuring commonalities cognitively across diverse activities creates self-efficacy linkages
5. **Transformational restructuring:** Powerful mastery experiences can produce generalized belief that one can mobilize whatever effort it takes to succeed

## Mechanisms

The three dimensions interact to create the profile of efficacy beliefs for any given domain:
- High level + high strength = willingness to attempt and persist at the most demanding tasks
- High level + low strength = initial willingness to attempt demanding tasks but vulnerability to discouragement
- Low level + high strength = firm avoidance of tasks beyond perceived capability
- Generality determines how efficacy in one domain transfers to others

## Applications

- **Assessment design:** Self-efficacy scales must be tailored to the specific domain, include graded levels of task demands, and use the 0-100 strength scale
- **Intervention targeting:** Knowing whether someone's efficacy limitation is in level (they don't believe they can handle difficult versions of the task), strength (they are easily discouraged), or generality (they don't transfer efficacy across domains) determines the intervention approach
- **Avoiding ceiling effects:** Include sufficient impediments and challenges in efficacy items to avoid ceiling effects; if everyone has uniformly high perceived self-efficacy for an activity, the measure lacks differentiation

- How should generality be measured -- as a separate dimension or as a pattern across domain-specific assessments?
