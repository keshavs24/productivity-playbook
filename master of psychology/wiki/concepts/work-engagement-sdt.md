# Work Engagement (SDT)

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Within SDT, work engagement is the outcome of basic psychological [[need-satisfaction-at-work|need satisfaction at work]]. It manifests as sustained, high-quality involvement in work tasks characterized by energy, dedication, and absorption. SDT conceptualizes engagement as emerging from [[autonomous-work-motivation]] -- when employees work because they find the work interesting, meaningful, or aligned with their values, rather than because they feel pressured.

Engagement is contrasted with **burnout and exhaustion**, which SDT predicts result from need frustration -- particularly when autonomy is thwarted through controlling management, excessive pressure, or poorly designed incentive systems.

## Key Findings

### Engagement as a Function of Need Satisfaction

- **Van den Broeck, Vansteenkiste, De Witte, & Lens (2008):** Satisfaction of basic psychological needs partially explained the link from job demands to exhaustion and **fully mediated** the relation between low job resources and exhaustion in a large Belgian sample
- **Fernet, Austin, Trepanier, & Dussault (2013):** Satisfaction of three basic needs mediated the links from job demands to exhaustion/depersonalization and from job resources to engagement
- **Deci, Ryan, Gagne, Leone, Usunov, & Kornazheva (2001):** Perceived autonomy support --> need satisfaction --> work engagement and self-esteem. Model was invariant across the US and Bulgaria
- **Gagne (2003):** In nonprofit volunteers, autonomy orientation + perceived autonomy support predicted need satisfaction, which predicted engagement (hours volunteered, self-reported engagement)

### Engagement vs. Burnout

- **Fernet, Austin, & Vallerand (2012):** Over 9 months, [[autonomous-work-motivation]] predicted greater job commitment and less emotional exhaustion; controlled motivation predicted greater emotional exhaustion
- **Trepanier, Fernet, & Austin (2013a):** Workplace bullying led to greater burnout and less engagement, with need frustration as the mediator
- **Trepanier, Fernet, & Austin (2013b):** Nurses experiencing psychological harassment showed less autonomous motivation, more controlled motivation, lower psychological wellness, and higher turnover intentions
- **Fernet, Guay, Senecal, & Austin (2012):** 800+ teachers -- greater job pressure diminished autonomous motivation, leading to greater emotional exhaustion
- **Richer, Blanchard, & Vallerand (2002):** Perceived autonomy positively predicted job satisfaction and negatively predicted emotional exhaustion; emotional exhaustion predicted turnover intentions, which predicted actual turnover one year later

### Engagement and Performance

- **Kuvaas (2009):** Intrinsic motivation partially mediated the relation between job characteristics and work performance; fully mediated the relation between supervisor support and performance
- **Grant, Nurmohamed, Ashford, & Dekas (2011):** Autonomously motivated employees were more proactive; call center employees generated more revenue
- **White (2015):** Autonomous motivation accounted for more than half the variance in positive emotions and quality of service -- directly affecting customer satisfaction
- **Gozukara & Simsek (2015b):** Job autonomy fully mediated the effect of transformational leadership on work engagement

### The Role of Leadership

- **Transformational leadership** enhances engagement through need satisfaction (Kovjanic et al., 2012, 2013)
- **Bono & Judge (2003):** Followers of transformational leaders adopted more autonomous work goals and were more affectively committed
- Transformational leaders themselves require supports for their own needs (Trepanier, Fernet, & Austin, 2012)

### Cross-Cultural Validity

Ryan, Bernstein, and Brown (2010) found that US employees across varied jobs (construction, secretarial, legal, nursing) reported lower well-being during work hours, explained by lower need satisfaction at work. The model has been confirmed in Belgium, Canada, the Netherlands, China, Gambia, Bulgaria, Turkey, New Zealand, and Norway.

## Mechanisms

1. **Need satisfaction --> Autonomous motivation --> Engagement:** When work environments support autonomy, competence, and relatedness, employees internalize work values and engage autonomously
2. **Need frustration --> Controlled motivation --> Burnout:** When work environments thwart needs through control, pressure, harassment, or poor design, employees shift to controlled motivation and experience exhaustion
3. **The cascade to turnover:** Engagement --> job satisfaction --> low turnover intentions --> retention. Burnout --> emotional exhaustion --> high turnover intentions --> actual turnover (Richer et al., 2002)

## Applications

- **Measure need satisfaction as a leading indicator:** Need satisfaction is more predictive of engagement than pay satisfaction or even job satisfaction
- **Address burnout at its root:** Rather than treating burnout with wellness programs or time off, address the need-thwarting conditions that produce it
- **Design work for engagement:** Ensure jobs provide autonomy (how tasks are done), competence feedback (clear mastery indicators), and relatedness (meaningful collaboration)
- **Train managers:** [[autonomy-supportive-management]] training is the most evidence-based intervention for improving engagement (Deci et al., 1989; Hardre & Reeve, 2009)
- **Promote intrinsic over extrinsic organizational values:** Organizations that emphasize growth, contribution, and meaning over status, wealth, and image produce more engaged employees

## Open Questions
- How does SDT's concept of engagement relate to Csikszentmihalyi's [[flow]]? Both involve intrinsic motivation and optimal challenge, but SDT emphasizes the social-contextual conditions
- Can engagement be sustained indefinitely with need satisfaction, or does habituation set in?
- What is the role of recovery and rest in maintaining engagement -- does SDT predict that non-work time must also be need-satisfying?
- How does remote work affect the relatedness component of engagement?
