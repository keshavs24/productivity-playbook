# Negotiation Jujitsu

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Negotiation jujitsu is a strategy described in [[getting-to-yes|Getting to Yes]] for countering positional bargaining when the other side refuses to engage in [[principled-negotiation]]. Named after the Japanese martial art, the key principle is **don't push back.** Instead of meeting force with force, step aside and deflect their energy onto the problem. When they assert their position, look behind it for interests. When they attack your ideas, invite criticism. When they attack you personally, recast it as an attack on the problem.

## Key Findings

- **Three common attacks and their jujitsu responses:** ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch7-wont-play]])
  1. **They assert their position forcefully** → Don't reject it; treat it as one possible option. Ask about the interests behind it. "What exactly are the budget trade-offs involved?"
  2. **They attack your ideas** → Don't defend them; invite criticism and advice. "What concerns of yours would this proposal fail to take into account?" Turn criticism into an essential ingredient of improvement.
  3. **They attack you personally** → Don't counterattack; recast it as an attack on the problem. "When you say that a strike shows we don't care about the children, I hear your concern about the children's education."

- **Two key tools: questions and silence.** ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch7-wont-play]])
  - **Questions** generate answers rather than resistance. They allow the other side to get their points across, they educate rather than criticize, and they create the impression of advancing a dialogue rather than a debate.
  - **Silence** is one of the most powerful negotiation tools. After asking an honest question, simply wait. People feel uncomfortable with silence and will fill it — often with concessions or explanations.

- **The Jones Realty case provides a complete worked example** of negotiation jujitsu in practice, with stock phrases like "Please correct me if I'm wrong," "What's the principle behind your action?", "Let me see if I understand what you're saying," and "Let me get back to you." ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch7-wont-play]])

## Mechanisms

1. **Breaking the action-reaction cycle:** In positional bargaining, each push from one side provokes an equal push from the other — escalating commitment on both sides ([[commitment-and-consistency]]). Jujitsu breaks this cycle by refusing to push back.
2. **Redirecting energy:** Like the physical martial art, the strategy uses the other side's momentum against them. Their forceful assertion of a position becomes an opportunity to explore interests. Their attack on you becomes an opportunity to demonstrate understanding.
3. **Reframing ([[framing-effects]]):** Each jujitsu move reframes the interaction — from adversarial to collaborative, from personal to substantive, from positional to interest-based.
4. **Questions bypass defensiveness:** Statements trigger counter-statements. Questions trigger thought. By asking rather than telling, you engage the other side's analytical faculties rather than their defensive instincts.

## Applications

- **Salary negotiations with a stubborn boss:** Instead of stating a counter-number, ask "What standard did you use to determine that figure?" and "How does that compare to industry benchmarks?"
- **Customer complaints:** Instead of defending your product, ask "What specifically went wrong?" and "What would a satisfactory resolution look like to you?"
- **Hostile negotiations:** When personally attacked, the jujitsu response ("I hear your concern about X") demonstrates empathy while redirecting to substance — consistently more effective than counterattacking.
