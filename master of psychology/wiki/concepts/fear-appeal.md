# Fear Appeal

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

A fear appeal is a persuasive message that arouses fear in the audience to motivate attitude or behavior change. Research shows that fear appeals are most effective when four conditions are simultaneously met: (1) the message genuinely scares the audience, (2) it offers a **specific recommendation** for overcoming the threat, (3) the recommended action is perceived as **effective**, and (4) the audience believes they **can perform** the recommended action.

## Key Findings

### Leventhal's Smoking and Tetanus Experiments

**Smoking:** High-fear communication (gory lung cancer film) produced greater intention to stop smoking, but without specific instructions (buy a magazine instead of cigarettes, drink water), it produced little behavior change. The combination of high fear + specific instructions was most effective — students smoked less four months later.

**Tetanus shots:** High-fear appeals changed attitudes and stated intentions. But specific instructions (map to health services, suggested appointment times) determined *actual behavior*: 28% with instructions got shots vs. 3% without. No-fear + instructions = 0% — fear was necessary for motivation.

### Self-Esteem as Moderator

Leventhal discovered that self-esteem moderates the fear-action relationship:
- **High self-esteem:** Respond well to high fear — feel capable of coping and act immediately
- **Low self-esteem:** Overwhelmed by high fear; more likely to act on low/moderate fear. (After a delay, they catch up with high-self-esteem individuals)

### The Fear-Then-Relief Effect (Dolinski & Nawrat)

Arousing fear (police whistle at jaywalkers; fake parking tickets) followed by quick relief significantly increased compliance with unrelated subsequent requests. The arousal-relief cycle distracts attention from evaluating the request.

## Mechanisms

The propagandist's fear formula: (1) Arouse fear, channeling all thoughts toward removing the threat; (2) Offer a simple, doable response that happens to be exactly what the propagandist wants. In the frightened state, it is difficult to think about anything other than getting rid of the fear. The propagandist offers the exit.

This formula explains both Jonathan Edwards's sermon ("Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God" + commit to Christ) and Hitler's speeches (Jews and communists will destroy Germany + join the Nazi party).

**Why some fear appeals fail:**
- Nuclear disarmament: High fear, but no simple doable individual response
- "Just say no" to drugs: Not perceived as effective or doable under peer pressure
- Successful: Fallout shelters (high fear + doable response); Lyndon Johnson's "Daisy" ad (nuclear war fear + vote for Johnson)

## Applications

- **Public health:** Combine high fear with specific, easy-to-follow behavioral instructions
- **Detecting propaganda:** When you feel afraid after a message, ask: "How legitimate is this fear?" and "Is the proposed response really the best option, or is someone exploiting my fear?"
- **Self-defense:** High fear can be used to make people with high self-esteem behave like those with low self-esteem — overwhelm them with uncontrollable threats. Recognize this tactic when it is used.
