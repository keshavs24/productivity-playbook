# That's Right Moment

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The "that's right" moment is what [[voss|Chris Voss]] identifies as the breakthrough point in any negotiation -- the instant when your counterpart says "that's right" in response to your summary of their worldview. This signals that they feel fully understood and positively affirmed. They have assessed what you said and pronounced it correct of their own free will, without the feeling of having given in.

"That's right" is fundamentally different from "you're right":
- **"That's right"** = "You understand me. I feel heard. I embrace what you've said." It leads to behavioral change, dissolved barriers, and collaborative action.
- **"You're right"** = "I want you to shut up and go away." It produces zero internalization, zero behavior change, and a happy smile that lasts about 24 hours.

The concept is rooted in Carl Rogers' **unconditional positive regard** and the FBI's **Behavioral Change Stairway Model (BCSM)**: active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change. The "that's right" moment marks the transition from rapport to influence -- the point where unconditional positive regard is established and you can begin exerting genuine influence.

## Key Findings

- **Abu Sabaya / Jeffrey Schilling negotiation (2000-2001):** After months of stalemate, Filipino negotiator Benjie used all six active listening tools to summarize Sabaya's worldview -- the war damages, the five hundred years of oppression, the fishing rights. Sabaya went silent for nearly a minute, then said "That's right." The $10 million demand disappeared permanently. Sabaya never asked for money again.
- **Brandon (Voss's son):** A linebacker who kept hitting blockers head-on despite coaching. "You're right" from Brandon produced zero change. A label -- "You seem to think it's unmanly to dodge a block. You think it's cowardly to get out of someone's way" -- produced "That's right" and immediate behavioral change. He became an exceptional linebacker.
- **Pharmaceutical sales rep:** By summarizing a resistant doctor's passion for his patients rather than pitching her product, a sales rep got "That's right" and broke through months of rejection. The doctor agreed to try her medication.
- **Korean MBA student:** By asking calibrated questions that led his ex-boss to say "That's right," the student uncovered two Black Swans (the boss needed a networking ally and was seeking a VP promotion) and secured approval to transfer divisions.
- **Health behavior analogy:** Study after study shows that 90% of heart surgery patients don't change their lifestyle despite being told it's necessary. The "yes, yes, of course, Doctor" is counterfeit -- commitment "yes" (which requires a "that's right" foundation) is needed for real change.

## Mechanisms

1. **Unconditional positive regard (Carl Rogers):** Most people experience conditional positive regard -- love and approval contingent on "correct" behavior. This creates habits of hiding true selves and calibrating words for approval. When a person feels truly understood and accepted *as they are*, real change becomes possible. The "that's right" moment signals this has occurred.
2. **Summary = paraphrasing + labeling:** The technique for triggering "that's right" is the **summary** -- rearticulating the meaning of what the counterpart said (paraphrasing) combined with acknowledging the emotions underlying that meaning ([[labeling-emotions]]). When you accurately and completely summarize "the world according to [counterpart]," the only possible response is "that's right."
3. **Ownership and self-endorsement:** "That's right" means the counterpart has pronounced your summary correct of their own free will. They own the conclusion. This is why it produces real change -- unlike "you're right," which is a dismissal without ownership.
4. **Barrier dissolution without concession:** "That's right" breaks down barriers that were impeding progress. It creates a realization point where the adversary agrees on a point without the feeling of having given in. It is a "stealth victory."

## Applications

**How to trigger "that's right":**
1. Listen intensely using the six active listening tools:
   - Effective pauses (silence)
   - Minimal encouragers ("Yes," "OK," "Uh-huh," "I see")
   - [[mirroring-technique|Mirroring]] (repeat last 1-3 words)
   - [[labeling-emotions|Labeling]] ("It seems like...", "It sounds like...")
   - Paraphrasing (repeat their meaning in your own words)
   - Summarizing (paraphrasing + labeling = "the world according to...")
2. Build toward a comprehensive summary that captures both their stated position AND the emotions driving it
3. Deliver the summary and wait
4. When they say "that's right," the barrier dissolves and real progress begins

**Warning signs:**
- "You're right" = you've failed; they're dismissing you. Regroup and try again with better listening and a more accurate summary.
- A commitment "yes" after "that's right" is genuine. A "yes" without "that's right" may be counterfeit.
