# Illusion of Validity

**Phase(s):** 1, 2
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

The illusion of validity is the tendency to express **high confidence in predictions based on input that "fits" a pattern** (is highly representative), even when the input is known to have low predictive validity. The internal consistency of the pattern sustains confidence even when the evidence is weak.

## Key Findings

- People make extreme, confident predictions even from unreliable or insufficient data, as long as the input forms a coherent "representative" pattern ([[summary-1-01-judgment-under-uncertainty]])
- **Redundancy increases confidence while decreasing accuracy:** Correlated inputs make the pattern look more consistent (increasing subjective confidence) while providing less independent information (decreasing actual predictive power)
- Experienced professionals (clinicians, interviewers, stock analysts) show the same illusion — expertise does not protect against it
- "People often predict by selecting the outcome... that is most representative of the input. The confidence they have in their prediction depends primarily on the degree of representativeness... with little or no regard for the factors that limit predictive accuracy."

## Mechanisms

The [[representativeness]] heuristic makes a coherent, consistent input feel informative and diagnostic. The mind confuses "this pattern is clear and consistent" with "this pattern is predictive." Internal consistency of the input is mistaken for external validity of the prediction.

## Applications

- **Job interviews:** A candidate whose answers form a consistent narrative ("this person is clearly a leader") produces confident hiring predictions — but unstructured interviews have notoriously low predictive validity
- **Clinical diagnosis:** A coherent symptom picture produces confident diagnoses even when the base rate of the condition is low
- **Investing:** A company with a "coherent story" (great product, charismatic founder, growing market) produces confident predictions of success
- **Self-assessment:** People feel confident about their own abilities in domains where their self-image is internally consistent
