# Need Satisfaction in Relationships

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Within [[relationships-motivation-theory|RMT]], need satisfaction in relationships refers to the degree to which close personal relationships fulfill all three [[basic-psychological-needs]]: [[relatedness-need|relatedness]], [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], and [[competence-need|competence]]. RMT proposes that it is not the mere existence of relationships or the frequency of contact that predicts wellness, but the quality of need satisfaction within those relationships.

Need satisfaction operates at two levels:
- **Between-person level:** Individuals who generally experience more need satisfaction across their relationships show greater overall well-being
- **Within-person level:** The same individual experiences different levels of need satisfaction with different relational partners, and these within-person variations predict relationship-specific outcomes (attachment security, satisfaction, emotional reliance)

## Key Findings

### All Three Needs Contribute Independently
- **Patrick, Knee, Canevello, & Lonsbary (2007):** Each need contributed significant unique variance to personal well-being (self-esteem, positive affect, vitality), relationship well-being (commitment, conflict management), and relationship satisfaction. Relatedness was the strongest predictor, but autonomy and competence contributed significantly over and above relatedness.
- A partner's need satisfaction also contributes to the target individual's relational well-being -- when one's partner is getting their needs met, both partners benefit.

### Need Satisfaction Predicts Attachment Security
- **La Guardia, Ryan, Couchman, & Deci (2000):** Attachment security varies substantially within persons across different relationships, and this variation is systematically predicted by relationship-specific need satisfaction. About 35% of attachment variance is between-person (consistent with attachment theory's working model), but more than half is within-person. Even excluding relatedness, autonomy and competence needs significantly predict attachment security with specific partners.
- **Ducat & Zimmer-Gembeck (2007):** Basic need satisfaction within the relationship mediated the links between adult attachment styles and both well-being and relationship quality.
- **Wei, Shaffer, Young, & Zakalik (2005):** Insecure attachment was related to loneliness, depression, and shame, with these relations mediated by frustration of basic psychological needs.

### Need Satisfaction Mediates Social Support Effects
- Need satisfaction largely mediates the relationship between social support and psychological wellness (Ryan, La Guardia et al., 2005)
- Emotional reliance and basic need satisfaction mediated the positive relationship between emotional reliance on others and mental health, across four cultures (Russia, Turkey, South Korea, U.S.)

### Need Thwarting in Relationships
- Need thwarting takes specific forms: dominance and control (thwarting autonomy), coldness or distancing (thwarting relatedness), criticalness and derogation (thwarting competence)
- **Costa, Ntoumanis, & Bartholomew (2015):** Need frustration within relationships was uniquely associated with rejection sensitivity; need satisfaction better predicted interpersonal competence
- [[Conditional-regard]] is a key mechanism of need thwarting, forcing trade-offs between autonomy and relatedness satisfaction

### Interdependence of Needs
The three needs are mutually supportive within relationships:
- When people experience competence, they feel they have the skills to communicate openly and get other needs met
- When people experience autonomy, they feel authentic and can explore ways to satisfy other needs
- When people experience deep relatedness, they feel secure enough to venture outward with greater confidence and agency
- [[Autonomy-support]] from a partner tends to support all three needs simultaneously, because it involves taking the partner's internal frame of reference and being responsive to their feelings and desires

## Mechanisms

1. **Need satisfaction as quality indicator:** The degree of need satisfaction defines what counts as a "high-quality" relationship -- not shared interests, frequency of contact, or duration
2. **Within-person variation:** People's attachment and relational well-being with any specific partner is a function of need satisfaction *within that specific relationship*, not just their general attachment style
3. **Bidirectional effects:** One partner's need satisfaction spills over to benefit the other partner's relational well-being
4. **Mediating role:** Need satisfaction mediates the links between social support and wellness, between attachment style and outcomes, and between autonomy support and relationship quality

## Applications

- **Relationship assessment:** Evaluate relationships not by surface markers (time spent together, conflict frequency) but by the degree to which each partner experiences autonomy, competence, and relatedness
- **Couples therapy:** Target all three needs -- not just connection/relatedness, but also each partner's sense of volition and competence within the relationship
- **Self-evaluation:** Recognize that feeling differently secure or satisfied across different relationships is normal and informative -- it reflects varying need support from different partners

## Open Questions

- Which need is most "fragile" in close relationships -- i.e., which is most easily thwarted and has the largest impact when frustrated?
- How do need satisfaction patterns shift across relationship stages (formation, maintenance, conflict, dissolution)?
- Can excessive need satisfaction in one domain compensate for deficits in another, or are all three independently necessary?
