# Abstract vs. Concrete Information

**Phase(s):** 2, 4, 6
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The abstract-vs-concrete information principle holds that **vivid, concrete, emotionally interesting, case-based information** has far greater psychological impact on judgment and behavior than **pallid, abstract, statistical, base-rate information** -- even when the latter is logically more informative and diagnostic. Bertrand Russell (1927) proposed that "popular induction depends upon the emotional interest of the instances, not upon their number."

## Key Findings

- **Nisbett & Borgida (1975):** Consensus information about the behavior of others in the shock experiment and helping experiment not only failed to affect subjects' predictions but was never mentioned by a single subject in postexperimental interviews. Subjects instead "seized on particular concrete details of the experimental situation and related them to similar situations in their own histories." ([[summary-2-06-hb-ch9-attribution-shortcomings]])
- **Consumer Reports thought experiment (Borgida & Nisbett, 1977):** Consumer Reports' consensus of hundreds of Volvo owners says it is mechanically superior; but a single vivid anecdote from a friend about his brother-in-law's lemon Volvo can override the statistical evidence. The logical status of the anecdote (the N of Volvo-owning Consumer Reports readers has merely increased by one) is negligible, but its psychological impact is overwhelming.
- **Study VI: Course selection (Borgida & Nisbett, 1977):** Face-to-face comments from 2-3 students about courses had a much larger impact on course choices than base-rate evaluations from the entire population of students who had taken the courses (Ns from 26 to 142). Even when the base-rate group received verbatim transcripts of the same comments and were told they were representative, face-to-face delivery remained more impactful.
- **Real-world examples:** (1) The Green Revolution agricultural techniques spread only when government agents farmed alongside local farmers -- not through pamphlets and statistics. (2) Cancer detection clinic waiting lists surged after Mrs. Ford and Mrs. Rockefeller's mastectomies -- not after statistical reports. (3) Reporters following McGovern's 1972 campaign believed he would lose by no more than 10 points despite all major polls showing a 20-point deficit, because they had seen enthusiastic crowds with their own eyes. (4) A graffiti artist admitted knowing about a boy crushed to death painting trains but dismissed it because "I remember laughing about it."

## Mechanisms

Concrete, emotionally interesting information has greater power to generate inferences because it calls up "scripts" or schemas from the person's prior experience, triggering well-worn associative networks. Abstract information is less rich in potential connections to the associative network by which scripts can be reached.

The mechanism connects to [[availability]]: vivid information is more cognitively available -- easier to recall, imagine, and use as the basis for inference. Statistical information, however logically pertinent, simply lacks the "clout to trigger further cognitive work."

## Applications

- **Persuasion (Phase 6):** If people are unmoved by statistics, effective communication must use vivid cases, personal stories, and concrete demonstrations. Social and technological progress is impeded unless concrete, emotionally interesting ways of communicating conclusions are developed.
- **Communication (Phase 4):** Presentations, reports, and arguments should lead with vivid cases and use statistics as supporting evidence, not the reverse.
- **Negotiation (Phase 5):** Making the human impact of a proposal concrete and vivid will be more persuasive than presenting statistical analyses.
- **Public health:** Health campaigns succeed through personal testimonials and vivid imagery, not through statistics about population risk.
- **Education:** Case-based teaching is more impactful than lecture-based statistical presentation.

## Open Questions

- Does the concrete information advantage diminish with statistical training or domain expertise?
- How does this principle interact with digital media where both vivid stories and statistical data are easily accessible?
