# Preference Reversal

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Preference reversal occurs when the rank ordering of options changes depending on the evaluation context -- particularly whether options are assessed **separately** (one at a time) or **jointly** (compared side by side). This violates the principle of procedure invariance: if you prefer A to B when evaluating them together, you should also value A more when evaluating it alone. Yet systematic reversals are well-documented.

## Key Findings

- **Hsee (1996) salary packages:** Package A ($27K, $26K, $25K, $24K = $102K total, declining) vs. Package B ($23K, $24K, $25K, $26K = $98K total, increasing). Separately, B rated more acceptable (aversion to declining pay). Jointly, A clearly preferred (higher total). ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch6]])
- **Hsee (1998) music dictionaries:** Large (20,000 words, torn cover) vs. Intact (10,000 words, perfect cover). Separately, Intact valued more ($24 vs. $20). Jointly, Large valued more ($27 vs. $19). Cover quality is easy to evaluate alone; word count requires comparison.
- **Kahneman & Ritov (1994):** Environmental causes (spotted owls) beat human causes (skin cancer) in separate evaluation (affect heuristic). Human causes won in joint evaluation (reasoned comparison).
- **Lowenthal (1996) political candidates:** Tax-evading but job-creating candidate rated lower than clean candidate in separate approval polls, but won by 2:1 margin in head-to-head voting.
- **Tversky & Kahneman (1981) concurrent decisions:** 73% chose a dominated portfolio when making two framing-dependent decisions separately, vs. 3% who chose the dominant combination ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch5]])

## Mechanisms

Two complementary explanations:

**1. Want/Should Distinction (Bazerman, Tenbrunsel, & Wade-Benzoni, 1998):**
- Separate evaluation activates the "want" self (System 1, affective, emotional)
- Joint evaluation activates the "should" self (System 2, analytical, comparative)
- The affectively arousing option wins in separate evaluation; the more rational option wins in joint evaluation
- Slovic et al. (2002): the affect heuristic dominates single-option assessments

**2. Evaluability Hypothesis (Bazerman et al., 1992; Hsee, 1996; 1998):**
- Attributes differ in "evaluability" -- how easy it is to assess their quality without comparison data
- Hard-to-evaluate attributes (e.g., number of dictionary words) have little impact in separate evaluation because people cannot judge whether 20,000 is good or bad
- Easy-to-evaluate attributes (e.g., torn cover) dominate in separate evaluation because their quality is immediately apparent
- In joint evaluation, comparison data makes hard-to-evaluate attributes evaluable, shifting their weight

Both processes operate simultaneously: affect-driven responses dominate separate evaluation, and hard-to-evaluate attributes are underweighted without comparison context.

## Applications

- **Hiring decisions:** Evaluating candidates one at a time (separate) vs. comparing finalists (joint) can produce different rank orders. Joint evaluation tends to favor substantive qualifications; separate evaluation favors charisma and affect.
- **Policy design:** Public approval polls (separate) vs. referenda with alternatives (joint) can yield opposite results.
- **Marketing:** Products with hard-to-evaluate advantages (technical specs, durability) benefit from comparison contexts (joint evaluation). Products with easy-to-evaluate advantages (aesthetics, packaging) benefit from standalone presentation.
- **Negotiation:** Presenting options sequentially vs. simultaneously changes which attributes dominate.
