# Tell-Sell-Consult-Join

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Tell-sell-consult-join is a four-style continuum of communication approaches, adapted by [[munter|Mary Munter]] from the Tannenbaum-Schmidt leadership continuum. The model maps two inversely related dimensions: **communicator control over content** (decreasing left to right) and **audience involvement** (increasing left to right).

| Style | What You Do | Your Control | Their Involvement | When to Use |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Tell** | Inform, explain, instruct | Highest | Lowest | You have sufficient information, don't need input, want to control content |
| **Sell** | Persuade, advocate | High | Some (to get buy-in) | You want to change their thinking or behavior; you need some involvement to get them to act |
| **Consult** | Confer, give-and-take | Moderate | Moderate | You need information from them; you want to learn from them yet control the interaction somewhat |
| **Join** | Collaborate, brainstorm | Lowest | Highest | You want to work together to co-create content; everyone's input shapes the outcome |

## Key Findings

### Style Selection Depends on Objective, Not Preference

There is no single "right" style. Effective communicators use all four styles at appropriate times and avoid defaulting to the same style out of habit. The appropriate style depends on your communication objective:

- **Tell:** "As a result of this presentation, my boss will learn the seven accomplishments of our department"
- **Sell:** "As a result of this proposal, my client will sign the contract"
- **Consult:** "As a result of this email, employees will respond by answering the questionnaire"
- **Join:** "As a result of this brainstorming session, the group will come up with a solution"

### Styles Can Be Combined Sequentially

In an ongoing communication project, you may cycle through multiple styles:
1. **Join** to brainstorm ideas
2. **Consult** to choose among those ideas
3. **Sell** to persuade your boss to adopt the chosen idea
4. **Tell** to write up the idea as policy

### Speaking Situations Map to Styles

In Chapter 5, Munter maps the styles to three distinct speaking situations:

| Speaking Situation | Primary Style | Who Speaks Most | Typical Purpose |
|-------------------|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Presentations | Tell/Sell | You | To inform or persuade |
| Q&A Sessions | Tell/Sell (reactive) | You, reacting to audience | To answer questions, get feedback |
| Meetings | Consult/Join | Your audience | To discuss or decide |

Each requires different structural techniques:
- Tell/sell presentations need openings, previews, demarcated main points, and closings
- Q&A sessions need preparation, paraphrasing skills, and strategies for challenging questions
- Consult/join meetings need agenda-setting, role delegation, facilitation techniques, and follow-up plans

## Mechanisms

The model works because it aligns the degree of audience participation with the communication goal. When the goal is information transfer (tell) or attitude change (sell), the communicator's control over message content is paramount. When the goal is information gathering (consult) or co-creation (join), relinquishing control enables better outcomes by tapping into the audience's knowledge, creativity, and commitment.

This connects to [[autonomy-support]] from self-determination theory: consult and join styles inherently provide audience members with voice, choice, and a sense of ownership -- all of which increase [[intrinsic-motivation]] and commitment to implementation. The sell style, by contrast, may rely more on extrinsic persuasion techniques (benefits, credibility appeals, [[framing-effects]]).

## Applications

- **Diagnosing communication failures:** If a presentation "bombed," check whether the wrong style was used (e.g., using tell when sell was needed, or sell when the audience wanted to join)
- **Meeting design:** Before calling a meeting, ask whether you actually need a consult/join format, or whether a tell/sell presentation or email would suffice
- **Managing up vs. managing down:** Different power dynamics may call for different styles -- you may sell upward to your boss but tell downward to your team (though [[autonomy-support]] research suggests consult/join produces better outcomes even when you have authority)
- **Cross-cultural adaptation:** Group-oriented cultures may prefer consult/join; individualistic or autocratic cultures may prefer tell/sell
