# Late-Night FM DJ Voice

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The late-night FM DJ voice is one of three voice tones [[voss|Chris Voss]] identifies as available to negotiators. It is a deep, soft, slow, reassuring, downward-inflecting tone that conveys calm authority and control without triggering defensiveness. Voss describes it as "the voice of calm and reason."

The key mechanics: inflect your voice **downward**, keeping it calm and slow. Downward inflection conveys: "I'm in control." Upward inflection invites a response by introducing uncertainty ("You've made a statement sound like a question. You've left the door open for the other guy to take the lead"). The DJ voice makes you quiet, self-assured -- a statement of fact, not a plea or a question.

## Key Findings

- **Chase Manhattan robbery (1993):** After five hours of stalemate, Voss replaced the NYPD negotiator and opened with the DJ voice: "Joe's gone. This is Chris. You're talking to me now." A downward-inflecting statement. This soothing voice was the key to easing the confrontation and eventually securing all hostages' release.
- **Harlem high-rise standoff (1998):** Voss used the DJ voice for six hours through an apartment door, speaking to three armed fugitives who never responded verbally. The voice alone calmed them enough to surrender: "You calmed us down."
- **Cross-cultural effectiveness:** A Black Swan Group instructor's girlfriend got better deals in Istanbul spice markets. Despite aggressive merchant haggling, her smile and playful demeanor (the positive/playful voice) primed merchants to settle cooperatively. The voice tones work across cultures.
- **Bobby Goodwin's trust:** During the Chase Manhattan standoff, Voss's calm DJ voice built trust with the second hostage-taker (Bobby Goodwin) who was speaking through fabric. After about a minute and a half, the muffled voice came through clearly: "I trust you, Joe" (calling Voss by the previous negotiator's name).

## Mechanisms

1. **Neurological telepathy:** "Think of it as a kind of involuntary neurological telepathy -- each of us in every given moment signaling to the world around us whether we are ready to play or fight, laugh or cry." Our brains process not just words but feelings, intentions, and the social meaning of behavior and emotions.
2. **Emotional contagion:** When you radiate warmth and acceptance, conversations flow. When you enter a room with comfort and enthusiasm, people are attracted. A smile on the street produces a reflexive smile back. This reflex is critical to every negotiating skill.
3. **Cognitive state influence:** When people are in a positive frame of mind, they think more quickly, are more likely to collaborate and problem-solve (instead of fight and resist). The positive/playful voice creates this state. The DJ voice creates calm and trust. Both shift the counterpart's cognitive processing toward cooperation.
4. **Authority without threat:** Downward inflection signals authority and finality without triggering the aggressive or passive-aggressive pushback that the direct/assertive voice produces.

## Applications

**The three negotiator voices:**

| Voice | When to Use | Effect |
|-------|------------|--------|
| **Late-night FM DJ** | Selectively, to make a point; when a statement isn't up for discussion | Creates authority and trustworthiness without triggering defensiveness |
| **Positive/playful** | Default -- most of the time | Light, encouraging; smile while talking; creates mental agility and collaboration |
| **Direct/assertive** | Rarely -- "except in very rare circumstances" | Signals dominance; causes pushback; "like slapping yourself in the face while trying to make progress" |

**Practical guidance:**
- The positive/playful voice should be your **default**. Relax, smile, be light and encouraging.
- Use the DJ voice **selectively** for key statements: "We don't do work-for-hire," "Joe's gone. You're talking to me now," "Nobody's going anywhere. Nobody's gonna get hurt."
- A smile transmits tonally even over the phone -- the other person will pick up on it.
- Downward inflection = statement. Upward inflection = question/invitation. Choose deliberately.
