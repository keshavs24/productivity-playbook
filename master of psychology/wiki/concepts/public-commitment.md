# Public Commitment

**Phase(s):** 1, 5, 6
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

Public commitment refers to the principle that commitments made in the presence of others, or made visible to others, are significantly more binding and lasting than private ones. The mechanism operates through the additional pressure to *appear* consistent to an audience, beyond the internal drive for consistency alone.

## Key Findings

- **Deutsch & Gerard (1955)**: Students who publicly wrote, signed, and turned in line-length estimates were most resistant to changing them when presented with contradictory evidence. Those who wrote privately on a Magic Writing Pad (then erased) were moderately resistant. Those who kept estimates in their heads were most willing to change. Public commitment produces maximum stubbornness.
- **Jury deliberations**: Hung juries are significantly more frequent when jurors vote by visible show of hands vs. secret ballot (Kerr & MacCoun, 1985). Public expression locks in positions.
- **Chinese POW camps**: The Chinese constantly arranged for prisoners' pro-Communist statements to be seen by others — posted around camp, read in discussion groups, broadcast on radio.
- **Gordon Sinclair's restaurant**: Changing from "Please call us if you change your plans" to "Will you *please* call us if you change your plans?" (and waiting for a response) dropped no-shows from 30% to 10%.
- **Weight-loss clinics**: Requiring clients to write down and publicize their goals produces better outcomes than private decisions.
- Works especially well for individuals with high public self-consciousness (Feingstein, Scheier, & Buss, 1975).

## Mechanisms

1. **Impression management**: People want to appear consistent to others. Changing a publicly stated position risks appearing fickle, uncertain, or unreliable (Tedeschi, Schlenker, & Bonoma, 1971; Schlenker et al., 1994).
2. **Social accountability**: Others can hold us to our stated positions, creating external enforcement of consistency.
3. **Self-perception amplification**: Public statements are observed by both the self and others, doubling the sources of belief about one's true attitudes (Schlenker, Dlugolecki, & Doherty, 1994).
4. **Written = inherently public**: Written commitments carry a public quality because they create a permanent, shareable record (Jones & Harris, 1967).

## Applications

- **Personal behavior change**: San Diego woman distributed "I promise I will never smoke" cards to everyone she cared about. Charles DeGaulle publicly announced he'd quit smoking.
- **Goal-setting**: Amway's "write it down" directive; Fred DeLucca printing "10,000 stores by 2001" on Subway napkins.
- **Negotiations**: Extracting verbal commitments from counterparts increases follow-through.
- **Service industry**: Asking customers to verbally confirm commitments (rather than passively receiving instructions) reduces no-shows and cancellations.

## Open Questions

- How does social media (the ultimate public commitment platform) change the dynamics of public commitment? Does posting intentions online increase or decrease follow-through?
- Is there an audience-size threshold? Does commitment strength scale with audience size?
- Can public commitments backfire by creating so much pressure that people rebel against the commitment?
