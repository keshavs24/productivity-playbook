# Anchor Extreme

**Phase(s):** 5 — Negotiation
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Anchor extreme is the negotiation tactic of deliberately setting an initial offer far from your target price to exploit the [[anchoring-and-adjustment]] bias. The extreme opening number recalibrates your counterpart's expectations and makes your subsequent "real" offer seem reasonable by comparison. In the [[ackerman-model]], the first offer is set at 65% of the target price. The technique can also be applied emotionally — anchoring how bad things will be before presenting the actual terms.

## Key Findings

- **The anchor-and-adjustment effect** is one of the most robust cognitive biases: people glimpsing 8x7x6x5x4x3x2x1 estimate a higher result than those seeing 1x2x3x4x5x6x7x8, because we focus on first numbers and extrapolate ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Columbia Business School research**: Job applicants who named a range received significantly higher salaries than those offering a single number, especially with a "bolstering range" where the low number was their actual target ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Extreme anchors induce fight-or-flight** in all but the most experienced negotiators, limiting cognitive abilities and pushing them into rash action ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])
- **The $400 iPhone effect**: An expensive $400 iPhone seems reasonable after being marked down from a crazy $600 ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Emotional anchoring**: Before getting to numbers, emotionally anchor by describing how bad things will be — "I have a lousy proposition for you..." The number then seems like a relief.

## Mechanisms

1. **[[anchoring-and-adjustment]]**: The cognitive bias that causes people to make insufficient adjustments from an initial reference point. Even when the anchor is recognized as extreme, it still pulls judgments toward it.
2. **Contrast effect**: The "real" offer presented after an extreme anchor appears reasonable by comparison — the same principle behind retail "was $600, now $400" pricing
3. **Fight-or-flight disruption**: The shock of an extreme anchor can bring the counterpart right to their price limit by overwhelming their rational processing capacity
4. **Range anchoring**: Presenting a range instead of a single number makes you seem less aggressive while anchoring the counterpart to the top of the range

## Applications

- **Salary negotiation**: "At top places like X Corp, people in this job get between $130,000 and $170,000" — frames the discussion around the high end ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Haiti kidnapping**: $3,000 first offer against a $150,000 demand — the extreme anchor completely rearranged the kidnapper's economic reality ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])
- **Rent negotiation**: Mishary anchored at $1,730/month against a $2,100 ask — the agent laughed but was knocked off the original price ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])
- **Handling an extreme anchor against you**: Deflect with "How am I supposed to accept that?" or pivot to nonmonetary terms. Let the other side go first if possible — you want to see their hand.
