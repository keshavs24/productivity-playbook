# Fourfold Pattern of Risk Attitudes

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The fourfold pattern of risk attitudes is the characteristic prediction of [[prospect-theory]] (especially [[cumulative-prospect-theory]]) that risk preferences depend on the interaction of two factors: the domain (gains vs. losses) and the probability level (high vs. low). The four cells are:

| | High Probability | Low Probability |
|---|---|---|
| **Gains** | Risk aversion | Risk seeking |
| **Losses** | Risk seeking | Risk aversion |

## Key Findings

- **High-probability gains (risk aversion):** People prefer a sure $800 over an 85% chance of $1,000. The certainty effect and concavity of the value function both push toward risk aversion. ([[summary-2-07-cvf-ch3-cumulative-prospect-theory]])
- **Low-probability gains (risk seeking):** People buy lottery tickets, overweighting the small probability of a large win. The overweighting of small probabilities overwhelms the concavity of the value function.
- **High-probability losses (risk seeking):** People prefer an 85% chance of losing $1,000 over a sure loss of $800. The certainty effect in the loss domain produces risk seeking.
- **Low-probability losses (risk aversion):** People buy insurance against unlikely catastrophes, overweighting the small probability of a large loss.
- 22 of 25 subjects in the CPT experiment showed the complete fourfold pattern at the individual level.

## Mechanisms

The pattern emerges from the interaction of two features of [[prospect-theory]]:

1. **The [[value-function]]:** Concave for gains (diminishing sensitivity to increasing gains) and convex for losses (diminishing sensitivity to increasing losses). On its own, this produces risk aversion for gains and risk seeking for losses regardless of probability.

2. **The [[decision-weights]] function:** Overweights small probabilities and underweights moderate-to-high probabilities (inverse S-shape). For small probabilities, the overweighting is strong enough to reverse the effect of the value function: it makes small-probability gains attractive (risk seeking) and small-probability losses frightening (risk aversion).

## Applications

- **Insurance and gambling coexist:** The same person can rationally (by PT standards) both buy insurance (risk aversion for low-probability losses) and lottery tickets (risk seeking for low-probability gains)
- **Litigation:** Plaintiffs with strong cases (high-probability gain) tend to be risk-averse and settle; defendants facing likely losses tend to be risk-seeking and go to trial
- **Negotiation:** Understanding which cell of the fourfold pattern each party occupies predicts their risk tolerance and willingness to accept deals
