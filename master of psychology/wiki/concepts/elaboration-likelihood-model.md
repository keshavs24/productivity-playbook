# Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM), proposed by Richard Petty and John Cacioppo (1986), describes two qualitatively different routes by which persuasive messages change attitudes. The **central route** involves careful, effortful evaluation of argument quality; the **peripheral route** involves low-effort processing guided by simple cues (source attractiveness, number of arguments, emotional tone). The route taken depends primarily on the recipient's motivation and ability to elaborate on the message.

## Key Findings

### The Comprehensive Exam Experiment (Petty, Cacioppo, & Goldman, 1981)

University of Missouri students heard a message advocating a comprehensive senior exam. The researchers varied:
- **Personal relevance:** High (exam next year at their school) vs. low (in ten years or at another school)
- **Source expertise:** High (Carnegie Commission) vs. low (a local high school class)
- **Argument quality:** Strong (statistics, data) vs. weak (personal opinions, anecdotes)

**Results:**
- **High relevance (central route):** Argument quality determined persuasion; source expertise had little effect
- **Low relevance (peripheral route):** Source expertise determined persuasion; argument quality had little effect

This demonstrates that personal involvement determines which route is activated.

### Determinants of Route Selection

**Central route is taken when:**
- The issue is personally relevant or important
- The recipient has sufficient time and cognitive resources
- The recipient is motivated to think carefully (e.g., forewarned of persuasive intent on an important topic)

**Peripheral route is taken when:**
- The issue is personally irrelevant
- The recipient is distracted, fatigued, or cognitively overloaded
- The message environment promotes rapid, low-effort processing (30-second ads, information-dense media)

### Consequences of Each Route

- **Central route attitudes** are more durable, more resistant to counterpersuasion, and more predictive of behavior
- **Peripheral route attitudes** are more fleeting, more susceptible to change, and less predictive of behavior

## Mechanisms

The ELM builds on the **cognitive response approach** to persuasion (developed at Ohio State in the late 1960s): what determines persuasion is not the message itself but the *thoughts running through the recipient's head* as they see or hear the message. The central route engages effortful cognitive responding (counterarguing, elaborating, evaluating); the peripheral route bypasses this, relying instead on heuristic cues and superficial processing.

Modern propaganda is specifically designed to promote peripheral processing — the message-dense environment, thirty-second ads, and constant bombardment make deep processing increasingly rare. As Pratkanis and Aronson note, we are "cognitive misers, forever trying to conserve our cognitive energy."

## Applications

- **Detecting propaganda:** If a message truncates thought and relies on peripheral cues (vivid images, celebrity endorsers, emotional music), it is operating through the peripheral route — a hallmark of propaganda
- **Creating durable attitude change:** Use the central route — present strong arguments to a motivated, involved audience. Central-route attitudes persist and predict behavior
- **Resisting persuasion:** Increase personal involvement with important issues to shift from peripheral to central processing. Ask: "Why should I care about this?" to trigger elaboration
- **Advertising design:** Match the route to the audience — high-involvement products (cars, houses) benefit from strong arguments; low-involvement products (soft drinks, snacks) benefit from attractive imagery and celebrity endorsement
