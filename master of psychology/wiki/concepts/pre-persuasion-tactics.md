# Pre-Persuasion Tactics

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Pre-persuasion is the first of Pratkanis and Aronson's [[four-stratagems-of-influence]]: taking control of the situation and establishing a favorable climate for your message before any arguments are presented. Pre-persuasion defines how an issue is structured, what "everyone knows," and what framework will be used for evaluation. If fully successful, it obtains consent without appearing to persuade at all.

The concept traces to Cicero's *statis* (status of the issue) — the Roman orator's first task was to define the situation most advantageously: deny the facts, challenge the definition, question the quality of the deed, or challenge the tribunal's authority.

## Key Findings

Pre-persuasion operates through multiple channels:

1. **Linguistic framing:** Words and labels define the situation before arguments begin. "75% lean" vs. "25% fat"; "pro-life" vs. "pro-choice"; "collateral damage" vs. "civilian deaths." See [[framing-effects]].

2. **Agenda-setting:** Determining *which* issues are discussed is itself a form of pre-persuasion. Media coverage decides what we think *about*, even if it doesn't tell us what to think.

3. **Defining the decision context:** Lincoln's "Four score and seven years ago" redefined the birth of the nation as 1776 rather than 1789, linking national identity to "all men are created equal" and pre-persuading the audience to accept the moral argument for the war.

4. **Rumor and innuendo:** Establishing false "facts" as context for later arguments. Repeating unsubstantiated claims creates a pseudo-environment in which later arguments seem reasonable.

## Mechanisms

Pre-persuasion works by channeling cognitive responses before the target is aware that persuasion is occurring. By defining the frame, the communicator constrains which thoughts the target will have, which comparisons will seem natural, and which conclusions will feel obvious. The target processes the subsequent message within the pre-established framework, often without recognizing that the framework itself was a persuasive construction.

## Applications

- **Negotiation:** Anchoring the discussion by establishing the first number or defining the zone of possible agreement
- **Sales:** Structuring the showroom so customers encounter expensive items first (contrast effect)
- **Politics:** Defining the terms of debate — whoever controls the labels controls the argument
- **Self-defense:** When presented with a definition of the world, ask: "Why this label? What other definitions would shed more light?"
