# Primacy Effect

**Phase(s):** 1, 4, 6
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

In impression formation, the primacy effect is the finding that **the first traits we learn about a person disproportionately influence how we interpret everything we learn about them later**. First information creates a schema that filters all subsequent information.

## Key Findings

- **Asch (1946):** "Intelligent, industrious, impulsive, critical, stubborn, envious" produced a more positive impression than the same traits reversed. Same content, different order, different impression. ([[summary-1-03-social-psychology-ch4]])
- The positive initial traits create a schema: "impulsive" and "critical" are interpreted charitably (decisive, discerning) when preceded by "intelligent." When preceded by "envious" and "stubborn," the same traits are interpreted negatively.
- The "what is beautiful is good" stereotype is a related phenomenon: physical attractiveness (perceived first) creates a halo through which all other traits are filtered positively (Dion, Berscheid, & Walster, 1972)

## Mechanisms

The first information activates a schema — a mental framework for interpreting new information. Subsequent information is assimilated into the existing schema rather than prompting a revision. This is cognitively efficient (schemas reduce processing load) but creates a confirmation bias where initial impressions become self-reinforcing.

## Applications

- **Persuasion (Phase 6):** Lead with your strongest point. The first argument creates the interpretive frame for everything that follows. Weak opening → unfavorable schema → even strong later arguments are filtered negatively.
- **Communication (Phase 4):** Structure presentations and documents to put the most important, most compelling information first.
- **Negotiation (Phase 5):** First offers and first impressions create anchoring effects and primacy frames that persist throughout the negotiation.
