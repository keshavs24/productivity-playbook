# Focalism

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Focalism (Gilbert, Wilson et al., 2000; Wilson, Wheatley, Meyers, Gilbert, & Axsom, 2000) is the common tendency to **focus too much on a particular event (the "focal event") and too little on other events that are likely to occur concurrently**. As a consequence, people overestimate both the degree to which their future thoughts will be occupied by the focal event and the duration and intensity of their emotional response to it. The closely related **focusing illusion** (Schkade & Kahneman, 1998) describes how attending to a subset of available information leads people to overweight that information and underweight unattended information.

## Key Findings

- People overestimate the impact of wins and losses of preferred sports teams and political candidates on their overall happiness ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch4]])
- People dramatically overestimate the effects of major medical conditions on happiness (Ubel et al., 2001)
- **Affective forecasting errors:** Nelson, Meyvis, and Galak (2009) showed that commercial breaks actually enhance TV enjoyment -- contrary to viewers' predictions -- because breaks interrupt adaptation and re-intensify the experience
- **The focusing illusion:** Schkade and Kahneman (1998) found Californians and Midwesterners reported similar life satisfaction, but both groups rated Californians higher. When comparing locations, climate became salient, but it did not actually predict experienced satisfaction
- **Fox and Tversky (1998) -- NBA tournament study:** Probabilities summed to 218% when assessed at the team level (eight teams), 144% at the division level (four divisions), and ~102% at the conference level (two conferences). Whatever is in focus gets overweighted
- **Tversky and Koehler (1994):** Medical doctors assessing four mutually exclusive prognoses for a patient gave probabilities summing to far more than 100% -- the specific prognosis in focus was overweighted
- **Challenger disaster:** NASA engineers examined temperatures only for the 7 launches with O-ring failures, missing the 17 launches without failure. A logistic regression on all 24 launches showed >99% chance of malfunction at the launch temperature. Focusing on failure cases while ignoring success cases (confirmation heuristic + focalism) cost seven lives

## Mechanisms

Focalism operates through attention allocation: whatever occupies our attention feels more important, more probable, and more emotionally impactful than it actually is. When we think about a future event (getting a raise, losing a game, moving to California), that event fills our mental stage, and we fail to consider all the other things that will also be happening in our lives and diluting its impact. The focusing illusion works similarly at the informational level: the information we are attending to gets overweighted in our judgments because we fail to adjust for the information we are not attending to.

## Applications

- **Decision making:** Before major decisions, deliberately enumerate concurrent factors and events that will dilute the impact of the focal outcome
- **Emotional regulation:** Recognize that both positive and negative future events will affect you less than you predict -- tempering both excessive excitement and dread
- **Data analysis:** When analyzing failures or successes, always include the base-rate comparison cases (successes AND failures, not just one)
- **Negotiations:** Recognize that the focal issue on the table may be overweighted; deliberately consider other interests and issues
- **Strategic planning:** Systematically consider what competitors and other players are doing, not just your own position
