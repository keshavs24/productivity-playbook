# Collective Efficacy

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Perceived collective efficacy is defined as a group's shared belief in its conjoint capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to produce given levels of attainments (Bandura, 1997, p.477). The collective belief centers on the group's operative capabilities. Group functioning is the product of the interactive and coordinative dynamics of its members. Interactive dynamics create an emergent property that is more than the sum of the individual attributes -- a host of factors contribute to the interactive effects including the mix of knowledge and competencies in the group, how the group is structured and coordinated, how well it is led, the strategies it adopts, and whether members interact in mutually facilitory or undermining ways.

## Key Findings

- Perceived personal and collective efficacy differ in the unit of agency, but in both forms efficacy beliefs have similar sources, serve similar functions, and operate through similar processes ([[summary-3-11-se-ch11-collective-efficacy]])
- Two measurement approaches: (a) aggregating members' appraisals of their personal capabilities for functions they perform in the group, (b) aggregating members' holistic appraisals of their group's capability as a whole. Under low system interdependence, aggregate of individual efficacies is more relevant; under high interdependence, holistic judgments are better predictors (Gibson, 1995)
- The stronger the beliefs people hold about their collective capabilities, the more they achieve. This is true regardless of whether the group's sense of efficacy develops naturally or is created experimentally (Bandura, 1993; Hodges & Carron, 1992; Little & Madigan, 1995)
- Bandura (1993): In 79 elementary schools, perceived collective efficacy contributed independently to school-level achievement after controlling for student body composition, SES, teacher characteristics, and prior achievement (Fig. 11.1)
- Prussia & Kinicki (1996): Experimentally demonstrated that sociocognitive determinants operate at the collective level as they do at the individual level -- vicarious experience, feedback, group affective evaluations, and group goals all predicted collective efficacy and group performance (Fig. 11.2)
- Campion, Medsker, & Higgs (1993): Perceived group efficacy was the strongest predictor of employee satisfaction and productivity in work teams
- Perceived team efficacy tailored to the relevant domain is a better predictor of subsequent group performance than general potency (Lindsley, Mathieu, Heffner, & Brass, 1994)
- Collective efficacy is not a monolithic group attribute -- it may vary across different domains of activities and different positions or functions within the same social system (Bandura, 1993)
- Earley (1993): Cultural context moderates collective efficacy effects -- individualists perform best under individual management systems while collectivists perform best as members of ethnically homogeneous teams ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])

## Mechanisms

Collective efficacy operates through the same four mediating processes as individual self-efficacy, but at the group level:

1. **Cognitive:** Shared efficacy beliefs influence the type of futures the group envisions, whether group members think in self-hindering or self-enhancing ways about their collective capability
2. **Motivational:** Collective efficacy influences the goals groups set for themselves, how much effort they put into group endeavors, their staying power when collective efforts fail to produce quick results, and their vulnerability to discouragement
3. **Affective:** Groups with low collective efficacy are more easily demoralized by setbacks and less resilient to difficulties
4. **Selection:** Collective efficacy influences what groups choose to attempt, which challenges they take on, and how they structure their activities

Key dynamics that distinguish collective from individual efficacy:
- **Coordination requirements:** A collection of individually efficacious members does not necessarily form a collectively efficacious group -- they must coordinate skills, strategies, and roles effectively
- **Emergent properties:** The interactive dynamics produce a group capability that cannot be predicted by simply aggregating individual capabilities
- **Reciprocal influence:** Members' beliefs in collective efficacy are influenced by the group's actual performance, which is in turn influenced by collective efficacy -- creating positive or negative spirals

## Applications

- **Team building:** Develop collective efficacy through shared mastery experiences, not just individual skill development. Provide vicarious experience through models of successful collective action
- **Organizational design:** Structure work for interdependence, shared purposes, role clarity, and mutual support -- all of which enhance perceived group efficacy
- **School improvement:** Build collective instructional efficacy among faculty through strong academic leadership, shared commitment to high standards, and collaborative professional community
- **Social change:** Build community-wide efficacy through Alinsky-model enablement -- developing people's capabilities, forging common goals, and demonstrating that collective action can produce results
- **Cross-cultural management:** Tailor efficacy-building interventions to cultural orientation -- individually oriented for individualistic cultures, group-oriented for collectivistic cultures (Earley, 1994)
