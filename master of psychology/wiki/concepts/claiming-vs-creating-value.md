# Claiming vs. Creating Value (The Negotiator's Dilemma)

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The tension between claiming value (getting a bigger share of the pie) and creating value (making the pie bigger) is the fundamental dilemma of negotiation, coined by Lax and Sebenius (1986) as the "negotiator's dilemma." Claiming value requires competitive tactics — aggressive anchoring, concealing information, strategic posturing. Creating value requires cooperative tactics — sharing information, exploring interests, building trust. The two goals demand opposite behaviors, and negotiators must manage both simultaneously.

## Key Findings

- **Most negotiators focus too heavily on claiming:** They leave money on the table and walk away satisfied — but also poor. The Moms.com case demonstrates that compromise across all issues ($5.5M fee, 6 runs = $3.0M total value) is dramatically inferior to value-maximizing logrolling ($6.5M fee, 8 runs, Juniors + contingency = $6.1M total value). ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Value creation is self-interested, not altruistic:** "Even the most self-interested negotiators must rely on others to satisfy their own interests." Giving the other side something they value more than you do (in exchange for reciprocal trades) is how you claim a larger share of a larger pie. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Close relationships can destroy value:** People in close relationships often compromise across all issues to avoid appearing greedy — this systematically destroys value through the avoidance of logrolling. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **One-issue negotiations are inherently zero-sum:** Only multi-issue negotiations allow value creation through logrolling, adding issues, or using [[contingent-contracts]]. When stuck on a single issue (typically price), smart negotiators broaden the scope. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])

## Mechanisms

The dilemma arises because:

1. **Information asymmetry is both a resource and a vulnerability.** Creating value requires sharing information about priorities, but sharing information enables the other side to exploit you.
2. **Cooperative moves can be exploited.** If you share that Issue A matters more to you than Issue B, a competitive counterpart can demand large concessions on Issue A.
3. **Competitive moves prevent creation.** If you refuse to share any information, logrolling opportunities cannot be identified.
4. **Sequential resolution:** Malhotra and Bazerman's practical solution is to create value first (expand the pie through logrolling, adding issues, and contingency contracts), then claim value (through anchoring, haggling, and strategic positioning on the distributive dimension).

## Applications

- **Preparation:** Identify all issues and your relative priorities before entering talks. The more issues available, the more opportunities for value creation.
- **Execution:** Negotiate multiple issues simultaneously (not issue by issue). Make package offers. Share *relative* priorities without revealing *absolute* limits.
- **Post-negotiation:** Use [[post-settlement-settlement]]s to seek additional Pareto improvements after the deal is signed.
