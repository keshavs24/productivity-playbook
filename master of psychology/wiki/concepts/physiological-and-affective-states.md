# Physiological and Affective States

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Physiological and affective states constitute the fourth [[sources-of-self-efficacy|source of self-efficacy]] (Bandura, 1997, pp.106-113). People rely partly on somatic information conveyed by physiological and emotional states in judging their capabilities. Somatic indicators of personal efficacy are especially relevant in domains involving physical accomplishments, health functioning, and coping with stressors. People read their physiological activation in stressful or taxing situations as signs of vulnerability to dysfunction.

As with the other sources, the information conveyed by physiological states is not by itself diagnostic of personal efficacy. Such information affects perceived self-efficacy only through cognitive processing -- how the arousal is perceived, interpreted, and attributed determines its impact on efficacy beliefs.

## Key Findings

- People are more inclined to expect success when not beset by aversive arousal than when tense and viscerally agitated
- Stress reactions to inefficacious control generate further stress through anticipatory self-arousal -- creating a vicious cycle of rising anxiety and declining efficacy
- **Perceived source matters more than actual intensity:** Speakers who ascribe sweating to room discomfort vs. personal distress read their physiology very differently
- **Level of activation is curvilinear:** Moderate levels heighten attentiveness and facilitate skill deployment; high arousal disrupts quality of functioning. The optimum level depends on activity complexity (Hollandsworth, Glazeski, Kirkland, Jones, & van Norman, 1979)
- **High achievers vs. low achievers:** High achievers view arousal as an energizing facilitator; low achievers regard it as a debilitator
- **Construal biases:** Preexisting efficacy beliefs create attentional, interpretive, and memory biases in processing somatic information. Panic-prone individuals misinterpret benign bodily sensations as catastrophic (Ehlers et al., 1988); secure individuals view the same sensations as benign
- Reading arousal as challenge can boost perceived efficacy; treatments that alter catastrophic thinking reduce negative biases (Westling & Ost, 1995)
- **Somatic information comes in different forms** even within the same modality -- people may experience the same somatic activation differently depending on which sensory qualities they selectively attend to (focusing on pain vs. focusing on concrete characteristics of sensation makes exercise less distressing)
- Somatic construal and personal efficacy involve two-way causation -- those who judge themselves physically inefficacious perceive more physiological strain and experience their somatic activation more negatively (McAuley & Courneya, 1992; Rudolf & McAuley, 1996)

**Impact of mood on self-efficacy judgment:**
- Mood provides an additional source of affective information for judging personal efficacy
- **Affective priming** (Bower, 1983): Mood activates mood-congruent memories (connects to [[availability]])
- **Cognitive priming:** Specific successes/failures directly cue thoughts of other similar experiences
- Induced positive mood enhances perceived efficacy; despondent mood diminishes it (Forgas, Bower, & Moylan, 1990; Salovey & Birnbaum, 1989)
- The impact is widely generalized across domains (Kavanagh & Bower, 1985) -- not limited to the content domain of the mood-inducing experience
- But mood cannot transform a mouse into a lion -- memory range sets limits on mood-induced changes
- People who fail under happy mood overestimate; those who succeed under sad mood underestimate (Wright & Mischel, 1982)
- Bidirectional relationship: Low efficacy breeds depression; depression diminishes efficacy

## Mechanisms

Physiological and affective states affect efficacy through cognitive processing:

1. **Somatic information --> Cognitive appraisal:** Bodily states are processed into percepts through attention, interpretation, and attribution
2. **Source attribution:** Whether arousal is attributed to personal inadequacy vs. situational factors vs. energizing challenge determines efficacy impact
3. **Mood-congruent memory activation:** Affective states selectively activate memories consistent with the current mood, biasing the database from which efficacy judgments are constructed (connects to [[availability]])
4. **Confirmatory bias cycle:** Low efficacy --> heightened attention to somatic cues --> catastrophic interpretation --> further reduced efficacy (vicious cycle)

## Applications

- **Reattribution of arousal:** Teach people to interpret pre-performance arousal as energizing readiness rather than debilitating anxiety (e.g., Sir Laurence Olivier's self-efficacy orations before performances)
- **Stress management:** Reduce physiological indicators of inefficacy through relaxation training, physical fitness, or cognitive restructuring
- **Mood management:** Recognize that mood states bias efficacy judgments -- avoid making important capability assessments during negative mood states
- **Somatic attention redirection:** Focus on concrete characteristics of sensations rather than their aversiveness (Ahles, Blanchard, & Leventhal, 1983; Cioffi, 1993)
- **Physical fitness as efficacy intervention:** Enhanced physical status reduces stress levels, improves negative emotional proclivities, and corrects misinterpretations of bodily states (Bandura, 1991a; Cioffi, 1991a)
