# Generative Capability

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Self-efficacy is a generative capability in which cognitive, social, emotional, and behavioral subskills must be organized and effectively orchestrated to serve innumerable purposes (Bandura, 1997). This is a foundational ontological claim about the nature of [[self-efficacy]]: it is not a fixed ability one does or does not have, not a repertoire of motor acts, not a personality trait, and not a decontextualized quantity of perceived ability.

Perceived self-efficacy is concerned not with the number of skills one has, but with what one believes one can do with whatever skills one possesses under a variety of circumstances. There is a marked difference between possessing subskills and being able to integrate them into appropriate courses of action and execute them well under difficult circumstances.

## Key Findings

- People often fail to perform optimally even though they know full well what to do and possess the requisite skills (Schwartz & Gottman, 1976) -- the gap between knowing and doing is bridged by efficacy beliefs ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Collins (1982) found that at each ability level, children who perceived themselves as more efficacious were more successful in solving mathematical problems than children who doubted their abilities -- mathematical ability contributed to performance, but within each ability level, self-efficacy made the difference
- A summation of decontextualized efficacy for subskills would provide a misleading measure of perceived operative capability -- the whole is greater than the sum of its parts ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Perceived efficacy to protect oneself against sexually transmitted diseases is not reducible to the act of donning a condom -- it involves perceived self-regulatory efficacy to surmount interpersonal impediments to condom use
- Driving efficacy is not about subskills (turning ignition, shifting gears) but the generative capability of navigating under varying conditions of challenge

## Mechanisms

The generative nature of self-efficacy operates through:

1. **Integration of subskills:** Effective functioning requires orchestrating multiple subskills into coherent action sequences, not just possessing them individually
2. **Adaptation to circumstances:** The same subskills must be deployed differently depending on situational demands -- even routinized activities are rarely performed in exactly the same way each time
3. **Self-regulatory activation:** Self-referent thought activates cognitive, motivational, and affective processes that govern the translation of knowledge and abilities into proficient action
4. **Self-regulatory efficacy:** Beyond task-specific skills, people need efficacy to regulate their own motivation, effort, and emotional states -- particularly in the face of obstacles and aversive conditions

## Applications

- **Skill assessment:** Measuring someone's component subskills does not adequately predict their integrated performance under real-world conditions -- assessment must capture the generative, integrative dimension
- **Training design:** Training should not just teach subskills but build people's belief in their capacity to orchestrate those subskills under varying and challenging conditions
- **Performance under pressure:** The gap between possessing skills and deploying them effectively under stress is explained by efficacy beliefs about one's generative capability
