# BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement)

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — is the standard against which any proposed agreement should be measured. Coined by [[fisher-ury|Roger Fisher and William Ury]] in [[getting-to-yes|Getting to Yes]], it represents what you will do if you fail to reach agreement in the current negotiation. Your BATNA is not what you *want* to happen or what you *think* might happen — it is the best course of action you can actually pursue if no agreement is reached.

BATNA is arguably the single most important concept in negotiation theory. It is the source of your negotiating power, the floor below which you should not accept an agreement, and the measure by which all proposals should be judged.

## Key Findings

- **BATNA replaces the bottom line as the protective measure.** A bottom line is rigid, inhibits imagination, is almost certain to be set too high or too low, and provides no real measure of what you should accept. BATNA is flexible and permits the exploration of imaginative solutions. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **The better your BATNA, the greater your power.** The relative negotiating power of two parties depends primarily upon how attractive to each is the option of not reaching agreement — not on resources, wealth, or connections per se. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **A weak BATNA can trump a strong resource position.** A small town with an attractive BATNA negotiated a payment from $300,000 to $2,300,000 per year from one of the world's largest corporations, which had no real alternative to reaching agreement. The Bombay vendor with unique brass pots has more negotiating power than the wealthy tourist who needs one. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **BATNA must be developed, not just identified.** Attractive alternatives don't just sit there waiting. Three operations: (1) invent a list of actions if no agreement, (2) improve the most promising into practical alternatives, (3) select the best one. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **Consider the other side's BATNA.** They may be unduly optimistic. The more you can learn about their alternatives, the better prepared you are. If they appear to overestimate their BATNA, you will want to lower their expectations. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **Formulate a trip wire.** A trip wire is not a bottom line but an early warning that a possible agreement is beginning to run the risk of being too unattractive. It provides some margin for maneuver that a rigid bottom line does not. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])
- **The "Ten Questions" section elaborates on BATNA as negotiation power.** Negotiation power has many sources: a good BATNA, good working relationships, elegant options, external legitimacy, clear commitments, and good communication. BATNA is the most fundamental. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch6-power]])

## Mechanisms

BATNA works through several psychological and strategic mechanisms:

1. **Calibration:** It provides an objective measure for evaluating proposals, preventing both over-acceptance (taking a bad deal because you're anchored) and under-acceptance (rejecting a good deal because of unrealistic aspirations). This counters [[anchoring-and-adjustment]].
2. **Confidence:** Knowing you have a strong alternative reduces anxiety and desperation, which in turn makes you less susceptible to pressure tactics and more willing to walk away from unfair proposals.
3. **Power perception:** The other side's perception of your BATNA affects their behavior. If they believe you have attractive alternatives, they will be more forthcoming. This connects to [[framing-effects]].
4. **Preparation discipline:** The process of developing your BATNA forces rigorous preparation — inventorying alternatives, improving them, and selecting the best — which invariably improves negotiation outcomes regardless of the specific alternative identified.

## Applications

- **Salary negotiation:** Your BATNA is your best alternative job offer. Having multiple offers dramatically increases your negotiating power for any single position.
- **Business deals:** Your BATNA might be a competing vendor, doing the work in-house, or walking away from the deal entirely. Develop these alternatives *before* negotiating.
- **Buying/selling:** In real estate, your BATNA is your best alternative property (if buying) or your best alternative buyer/option (if selling). The family considering whether to sell their house should ask: what will we do if we haven't sold it by X date?
- **Dispute resolution:** Your BATNA might be going to court, going to arbitration, or simply living with the status quo. Understanding the true costs of litigation often reveals that negotiation is far more attractive than people assume.
- **International relations:** Countries' BATNAs in treaty negotiations include military action, economic sanctions, alliance formation, or maintenance of the status quo.

## Negotiation Genius Extensions

[[malhotra|Malhotra]] and [[bazerman|Bazerman]] in [[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]] extend the BATNA concept in several critical ways:

- **BATNA is Step 1 of a five-step preparation framework:** Assess your BATNA, calculate your [[reservation-value]], assess the *other party's* BATNA, calculate their RV, evaluate the [[zopa]]. The BATNA alone is insufficient — it must be translated into a reservation value and compared to the other side's position. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **Focus on the other party's BATNA, not just your own.** Negotiators who focus on their own BATNA tend to set low aspirations and are happy getting anything better than their RV. Those who focus on the *other side's* BATNA set higher aspirations and capture more value. Roosevelt's campaign manager succeeded by focusing on the photographer's weak BATNA (no money, no publicity) rather than his own (reprint 3M brochures). ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **When both BATNAs are weak, the ZOPA is large.** This means there is a lot of value at stake, and the party that better leverages claiming strategies captures more of it. Having a weak BATNA is not terribly problematic if the other side's BATNA is also weak. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])
- **Don't reveal a weak BATNA.** Having a weak BATNA is less problematic when the other side does not know it is weak. Avoid language that inadvertently signals desperation. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])
- **Portfolio thinking transforms BATNA assessment.** The BATNA that is relevant is not just your best alternative in the current deal, but the overall impact on your portfolio of negotiations. Being willing to lose some deals (accepting your BATNA) can strengthen your position across all future deals. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])

## Open Questions
- How accurately can people assess their own BATNA? Research on [[overconfidence-in-calibration]] suggests people are systematically overconfident — does this extend to BATNA assessment?
- When should you disclose your BATNA versus keep it private? Fisher and Ury suggest disclosure only when your BATNA is extremely attractive; Malhotra and Bazerman advise never revealing a weak BATNA.
- How does BATNA interact with sunk cost reasoning? People who have invested heavily in a negotiation may irrationally discount their BATNA.
- How does competitive arousal (Malhotra et al.) lead negotiators to abandon their BATNA discipline and escalate commitment to a losing course of action?
