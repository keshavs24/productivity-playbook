# Audience Analysis

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Audience analysis is the systematic process of understanding who your audience is, what they know and expect, what they feel, and what will persuade them -- before you begin crafting your message. [[munter|Munter]] calls it "perhaps the most important aspect of your communication strategy," citing Peter Drucker: "Communication takes place in the mind of the listener, not of the speaker."

Audience analysis is the second variable in the [[communication-strategy-framework]] and drives decisions about all other variables -- communication style ([[tell-sell-consult-join]]), message organization ([[direct-vs-indirect-organization]]), persuasion techniques, and channel choice.

## Key Findings

### Four Dimensions of Audience Analysis

#### 1. Who Are They?

**Primary audience:** Those who directly receive your message. Investigate their:
- *Demographics* -- age, education, organization, geographic location, rank, language fluency
- *Knowledge and beliefs* -- background, values
- *Preferences* -- formality level, preferred channels (email vs. face-to-face)

**Key influencers** (one or more audience members with disproportionate control over outcomes):
- *Decision-makers* -- make the decision directly (client, customer)
- *Opinion leaders* -- influence the decision through credibility or ability to shape opinion
- *Gatekeepers* -- can expedite or block your message (boss who approves slides, assistant who forwards documents)

**Secondary audiences:** People who receive your message indirectly -- cc'd colleagues, forwarded emails, people who see shared slide decks, blog commenters.

#### 2. What Do They Know and Expect?

- **The "problem of knowing too much"** -- after months of work on a project, you may forget what background knowledge your audience lacks
- **Jargon identification** -- language that is commonplace to you may be incomprehensible to others
- **Mixed background strategies:** Provide background material for novices, acknowledge experts explicitly, aim core message at key decision-maker
- **Format expectations:** Timing, visual aids, formality level, document length

#### 3. What Do They Feel?

- **Emotions** about their current situation (economic pressure, morale, time constraints) and about your specific message (pride, excitement, anxiety, fear, jealousy)
- **Interest level:** High interest audiences let you get to the point quickly and build logical arguments; low interest audiences require shorter messages, consult/join style for engagement, and quick action on any attitude changes (which may not be permanent)
- **Probable bias:** Positive/neutral audiences respond to benefit reinforcement; negative audiences require specific techniques -- convince there's a problem first, start with points of agreement, limit scope of request, respond to anticipated objections yourself
- **Difficulty of desired action:** If hard for them, break into smallest possible request (foot-in-the-door), provide tools that make action easy (questionnaires, checklists)

#### 4. What Will Persuade Them?

Three categories of persuasion techniques:

**WIIFT -- "What's In It For Them" (audience benefits):**
- *Tangible* -- profit, results, cost savings, symbolic items
- *Career/task* -- making their job easier, challenge, advancement, prestige
- *Ego* -- self-worth, accomplishment, recognition
- *Personality-matched* -- data for thinkers, credibility for skeptics, energy for emotional people
- *Group* -- group worth, consensus, "everyone else is doing it" (connects to [[social-proof]])
- *Consistency* -- leverage public/written commitments (connects to [[commitment-and-consistency]])

**[[credibility-in-communication|Credibility]]-based techniques:**
- Common ground and shared values (establish early)
- Reciprocity -- exchange favors, offer concessions (connects to [[reciprocation]])
- Liking -- build relationship, uncover similarities (connects to [[liking-principle]])
- Emotionality -- show commitment, connect emotionally (connects to [[pathos]])
- Rank/expertise by association -- cite credible experts, get high-status introductions

**Message structure techniques:**
- Problem/solution structure (leverages "balance theory" / [[cognitive-dissonance]])
- One-sided vs. two-sided arguments
- Inoculation -- present and refute mild opposing views
- Ascending vs. descending argument order
- Foot-in-the-door / door-in-face techniques

## Mechanisms

Audience analysis works because effective communication is fundamentally about the receiver, not the sender. The mechanism is perspective-taking: by modeling your audience's mental state (knowledge, emotions, biases, interests), you can craft messages that align with their existing frameworks and address their concerns proactively.

This connects to [[autonomy-support]] -- understanding and acknowledging the audience's perspective is a core element of autonomy-supportive communication. It also connects to [[ethos]] (credibility assessment requires understanding how the audience perceives you) and [[framing-effects]] (knowing your audience lets you choose the frame most likely to resonate).

## Applications

- **Pre-communication checklist:** Before any important communication, answer Munter's four questions systematically
- **Stakeholder mapping:** Identify primary audiences, key influencers (decision-makers, opinion leaders, gatekeepers), and secondary audiences
- **Persuasion design:** Match persuasion techniques to audience profile -- different audiences respond to different benefit categories and credibility types
- **Mixed-audience management:** Provide novice background material, acknowledge experts, aim at decision-maker

## Open Questions

- How does audience analysis scale to very large or unknown audiences (e.g., viral social media posts, public-facing websites)?
- Is there a systematic bias toward analyzing audiences who are similar to the communicator, leading to blind spots for dissimilar audiences?
- How does audience analysis interact with the [[curse-of-knowledge]] -- even when you know the audience lacks background, the curse makes it difficult to communicate at their level?
