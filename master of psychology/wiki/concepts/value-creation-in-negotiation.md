# Value Creation in Negotiation

**Phase(s):** 2, 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Value creation in negotiation is the process of expanding the total pool of resources available to all parties by identifying and exploiting differences in preferences, priorities, risk tolerance, time preferences, and predictions about the future. It contrasts with value claiming (dividing a fixed pool). Effective negotiation requires doing both simultaneously, but most negotiators focus too heavily on claiming at the expense of creating. The key insight: differences between parties are not obstacles but opportunities.

## Key Findings

- **Camp David Accords (1978, documented in Pruitt & Rubin, 1985):** Egypt wanted sovereignty over the Sinai; Israel wanted military security. Positions appeared incompatible. But trading across these two issues of differential importance produced a solution: return the Sinai to Egypt in exchange for a demilitarized zone and new Israeli air bases. Neither side could have achieved this outcome through positional bargaining. ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch11]])
- **Lax and Sebenius (1986):** Coined the term "creating and claiming value" as the two primary tasks of negotiation. Both occur simultaneously and create a fundamental tension -- the "negotiator's dilemma."
- **MBA simulation evidence:** When teaching negotiation, Bazerman and Moore consistently find that students fail to discover mutually beneficial trades. When shown the data revealing missed value, students blame information asymmetry -- but the information was available through questioning.
- **Six strategies for value creation** (Bazerman & Moore): build trust and share information, ask questions, strategically disclose information, negotiate multiple issues simultaneously, make multiple offers simultaneously, search for [[post-settlement-settlement]]s.

## Mechanisms

Value creation works by exploiting five types of differences between parties:

1. **Different valuations of issues:** The most common source. Trade issues you value less for issues you value more. The purchasing officer who asked "why no exclusivity?" discovered the producer only needed to sell 250 lbs/year to his cousin -- a trivial exception that unlocked the entire deal.

2. **Different risk tolerances:** One partner takes a higher salary (lower risk), the other takes a higher ownership share (higher risk). Both are happier than with a simple 50-50 split.

3. **Different time preferences:** Exploit differences in budget cycles, patience, or discount rates. Give earlier returns to the more impatient party in exchange for concessions favored by the more patient party.

4. **Different predictions about the future:** Use [[contingent-contracts]] to bet on differing predictions rather than arguing about them. If the seller thinks the TV show will get a 9 rating and the buyer thinks 7, make the price contingent on actual ratings.

5. **Different capabilities/skills:** Complementary skills create natural divisions of labor that benefit both parties (Northcraft & Neale, 1993).

## Applications

- **Pre-negotiation research:** Identify what the other party values differently from you. Every difference is a potential trade.
- **"Nothing is settled until everything is settled":** Negotiate multiple issues simultaneously to enable tradeoffs. Issue-by-issue negotiation destroys value creation opportunities.
- **Multiple simultaneous offers:** Present 3-5 package offers of equal value to you. The other party's reactions reveal their preferences, enabling better trades.
- **Ask "why?":** The single most powerful value-creation question. Most negotiators spend their time advocating rather than inquiring.
- **Post-settlement settlement:** After reaching agreement, propose looking for Pareto improvements using the signed deal as a safety net.

## Negotiation Genius Extensions

[[malhotra|Malhotra]] and [[bazerman|Bazerman]] significantly extend value creation theory with operational detail in [[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]]:

- **The Moms.com case demonstrates that compromise destroys value.** When parties compromised at 6 runs per episode (midpoint of 4 and 8), they burned $1.1M in value. Agreeing to 8 runs and adjusting the licensing fee made *both* parties better off — from $3.0M total created to $4.1M. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Adding issues is a core value-creation mechanism.** More issues = more currency for logrolling. In Moms.com, adding the sale of *Juniors* (a second show) and a contingency clause raised total value from $3.0M to $6.1M — more than doubling it. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Post-settlement settlements (PSS)** are a powerful and underutilized tool. After signing the deal, propose revisiting it for Pareto improvements with the signed agreement as the new BATNA. A pharmaceutical CEO used this to get more upfront cash in exchange for right of first refusal — an issue never discussed in the original negotiation. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Four preparation strategies:** Identify your multiple interests, create a scoring system, calculate a package reservation value (PRV), and identify the other party's multiple interests. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Three execution strategies:** Negotiate multiple issues simultaneously (not one at a time), make package offers, and leverage differences of all types (priorities, risk, time, predictions, capabilities). ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **[[investigative-negotiation]] is the information engine for value creation.** The seven principles and five strategies for eliciting information from reticent negotiators provide the practical toolkit for discovering the differences that make value creation possible. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch3-investigative]])

## Open Questions
- What is the optimal balance between value creation and value claiming? Can too much focus on creating value leave you vulnerable to aggressive claimers? (Malhotra and Bazerman recommend creating first, then claiming — but the sequencing is easier to prescribe than to execute.)
- How do cultural differences affect the willingness to share information needed for value creation?
- Can AI-assisted negotiation tools systematically identify value creation opportunities that human negotiators miss?
- How often do real-world negotiators actually attempt post-settlement settlements? What barriers prevent their use?
