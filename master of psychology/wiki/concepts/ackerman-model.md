# Ackerman Model

**Phase(s):** 5 — Negotiation
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The Ackerman Model is a systematized offer-counteroffer bargaining method designed to beat the typical "meet in the middle" dynamic. Named after Mike Ackerman, an ex-CIA operative who founded a kidnap-for-ransom consulting company in Miami, and validated by Harvard's Howard Raiffa, the model uses extreme anchoring, decreasing increments, precise numbers, and nonmonetary items to reach a target price while making the counterpart feel they've squeezed you for every last drop.

## The Six Steps

1. **Set your target price** (your goal — what you actually want to pay/receive)
2. **Set your first offer at 65% of your target price** — this is the [[anchor-extreme|extreme anchor]] that induces fight-or-flight in the counterpart, limiting their cognitive abilities
3. **Calculate three raises of decreasing increments** — to 85%, 95%, and 100% of your target price
4. **Use lots of empathy and different ways of saying "No"** to get the other side to counter before you increase your offer. Try to bait them into bidding against themselves with [[calibrated-questions]]
5. **When calculating the final amount, use precise, nonround numbers** — e.g., $37,893 rather than $38,000. This gives the number credibility and weight, suggesting it's the product of careful calculation
6. **On your final number, throw in a nonmonetary item** (that they probably don't want) to show you're at your limit

## Key Findings

- **The model incorporates reciprocity, extreme anchors, and [[loss-aversion]]** — all without you needing to think about them consciously ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])
- **Decreasing increments signal you're being squeezed** — because they decrease by half each time (65→85 is +20; 85→95 is +10; 95→100 is +5), the counterpart perceives mounting resistance and believes they've extracted everything possible ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])
- **People getting concessions feel better** about the bargaining process than those given a single "fair" offer, even when they end up paying more or receiving less. The process juices their self-esteem ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])
- **$1.99 vs $2.00** — nonround final numbers feel permanent and calculated. "You can't buy anything for $2, but you can buy a million things for $1.99. How does a cent change anything? It doesn't. But it makes a difference every time." ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])

## Mechanisms

1. **[[anchoring-and-adjustment]]**: The 65% opening sets a powerful first reference point. Even experienced counterparts unconsciously adjust from this anchor.
2. **[[reciprocity]]**: Each concession (65→85→95→100) triggers the reciprocity norm — the counterpart feels obligated to make a concession in return, just as people who receive Christmas cards feel compelled to send one back.
3. **Perceived scarcity of concessions**: The diminishing size of increases (20%→10%→5%) convinces the counterpart that further concessions are increasingly unlikely.
4. **Precision bias**: Precise numbers ($37,893) are perceived as the product of rigorous calculation, making them feel more immovable than round numbers ($38,000).
5. **Nonmonetary item as proof of limit**: Throwing in something they don't want (e.g., a CD stereo) signals that you have literally nothing left to give monetarily.

## Applications

### Haiti Kidnapping (Original Case)
- Demand: $150,000
- Target: ~$5,000
- Ackerman sequence: $3,000 → $4,751 (with precise number) + CD stereo
- Result: $4,751 + CD stereo ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch6-bend-reality]])

### Mishary's Rent Negotiation
- Landlord wanted: $2,100/month (10 months) or $2,000/month (12 months)
- Mishary's target: $1,830
- Ackerman sequence: $1,730 → $1,790 → $1,810 → $1,829 (precise number with fake calculations)
- Result: $1,829/month for 12 months ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])

### Voss's 4Runner Purchase
- Sticker price: $36,000
- Target: $30,000
- Used empathy and calibrated questions between positions
- Result: $30,000 ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch9-bargain-hard]])

### Joaquin and Bruno Business Buyout
- Bruno's advisor demanded: EUR 30,812
- Target: EUR 23,000
- Sequence: Series of gentle "No" emails (no counteroffers) → EUR 23,567 with EUR 15,321.37 up front (brilliantly specific numbers)
- Result: Accepted within one hour ([[summary-5-20-nstd-ch8-execution]])
