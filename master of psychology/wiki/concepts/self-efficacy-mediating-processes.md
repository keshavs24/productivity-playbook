# Self-Efficacy Mediating Processes

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Self-efficacy beliefs regulate human functioning through four major mediating processes (Bandura, 1997, Ch.4, pp.116-161). These processes explain HOW efficacy beliefs produce their effects -- the pathways through which believing you can (or cannot) do something translates into actual performance, emotional experience, and life outcomes.

The four mediating processes:
1. **[[efficacy-activated-cognitive-processes]]** -- thought patterns, visualization, inferential thinking, conceptions of ability
2. **[[efficacy-activated-motivational-processes]]** -- causal attributions, outcome expectancies, goal setting
3. **Affective processes** -- anxiety regulation, depression, stress, thought control
4. **Selection processes** -- choice of environments and activities

These processes usually operate in concert rather than in isolation, jointly regulating human functioning.

## Key Findings

### Cognitive Processes
- High-efficacy people visualize success scenarios; low-efficacy people dwell on failure scenarios (Krueger & Dickson, 1994)
- Conceptions of ability as acquirable skill vs. inherent aptitude dramatically affect self-regulatory mechanisms: acquirable skill view maintains resilient efficacy and high performance; inherent aptitude view produces declining efficacy under difficulty (Wood & Bandura, 1989b, Figure 4.1)
- Perceived controllability of the environment strongly affects efficacy and performance (Bandura & Wood, 1989, Figure 4.3)
- Path analysis shows efficacy beliefs influence performance both directly and through effects on personal goals and analytic thinking (Figure 4.4)

### Motivational Processes
- Self-efficacy mediates the effect of causal attributions on performance almost entirely (Relich, Debus, & Walker, 1986; Schunk & Gunn, 1986). Connects to [[attribution-theory]] and [[self-serving-attribution]].
- When efficacy beliefs are controlled, outcome expectations contribute little to prediction of behavior
- Explicit, challenging goals enhance motivation (Locke & Latham, 1990), but efficacy beliefs determine goal level, commitment strength, strategies used, effort mobilized, and effort intensification
- Proximal subgoals are far more effective than distal goals in building efficacy and motivation (Figure 4.7, Bandura & Schunk, 1981)
- Three self-reactive influences mediate goal motivation: affective self-evaluation, perceived efficacy for goal attainment, and adjustment of personal standards (Figure 4.6)

### Affective Processes
- Efficacy beliefs regulate anxiety through three modes: control over thought (cognitive reappraisal), action (transformational behavior), and affect (palliative regulation)
- Threat is not a fixed property of situations but a relational matter concerning the match between perceived coping capabilities and potentially hurtful aspects of the environment
- Perceived coping efficacy linked to catecholamine release: phobics show low catecholamines within their efficacy range but elevated levels approaching their perceived limits (Bandura et al., 1985)
- Thought control efficacy and coping efficacy jointly determine anxiety levels through dual pathways (Figure 4.10, Ozer & Bandura, 1990)
- People experience anxiety when unable to manage threats; depression when unable to gain things of value
- Efficacy beliefs affect immune function through control of cognitive stressors (Wiedenfeld et al., 1990)

### Selection Processes
- By influencing which environments people choose, efficacy beliefs shape developmental trajectories
- Career choice strongly influenced by efficacy beliefs: higher efficacy predicts wider range of career options, greater interest, better educational preparation, greater persistence (Betz & Hackett, 1986; Lent & Hackett, 1987; Lent, Brown, & Hackett, 1994)
- Through selected environments, efficacious people develop new competencies; inefficacious people confirm self-limiting beliefs

## Mechanisms

The four processes create a comprehensive causal architecture:

```
Self-Efficacy Beliefs
    |
    ├── Cognitive Processes
    |   ├── Success/failure visualization
    |   ├── Inferential thinking quality
    |   ├── Conceptions of ability (acquirable vs. inherent)
    |   └── Perceived environmental controllability
    |
    ├── Motivational Processes
    |   ├── Causal attributions (effort vs. ability)
    |   ├── Outcome expectations
    |   ├── Goal level, commitment, and adjustment
    |   └── Self-reactive influences (self-evaluation, self-satisfaction)
    |
    ├── Affective Processes
    |   ├── Anxiety arousal and regulation
    |   ├── Thought control efficacy
    |   ├── Depression vulnerability
    |   └── Biological stress responses (catecholamines, immune function)
    |
    └── Selection Processes
        ├── Activity and environment choice
        ├── Career development
        └── Developmental trajectory shaping
```

A critical insight is that Bandura argues against purely reactive models of self-regulation (e.g., Carver & Scheier's negative feedback control theory). Human self-motivation requires both **discrepancy production** (proactively setting challenging goals that create disequilibrium) and **discrepancy reduction** (reactively adjusting effort to reach those goals).

## Applications

- **Comprehensive intervention design:** Address all four processes simultaneously for maximum impact -- build cognitive schemas of success, set proximal goals, manage affective states, and guide environmental selection
- **Growth mindset connection:** Bandura's acquirable skill vs. inherent aptitude distinction (1989) predates Dweck's popularized growth/fixed mindset framework by decades and provides the empirical foundation
- **Goal setting:** Use proximal subgoals linked to distal aspirations; measure progress against self-comparative standards rather than social comparison
- **Anxiety management:** Develop both coping efficacy (to transform threatening situations) and thought control efficacy (to manage intrusive cognitions)
- **Career counseling:** Assess and build [[career-self-efficacy]] for career options; broaden the range of options considered ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- **Organizational decision making:** Efficacy beliefs directly affect quality of analytic thinking in complex managerial environments. Managers who construe ability as acquirable skill maintain task-oriented analytic thinking; those who view it as inherent aptitude become self-diagnostic and erratic (Wood & Bandura, 1989b) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- **Collective-level operation:** All four mediating processes operate at the collective level as they do at the individual level -- affecting what groups envision, how much effort they invest, their staying power, and their vulnerability to discouragement (Prussia & Kinicki, 1996) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch11-collective-efficacy]])
