# Efficacy-Activated Cognitive Processes

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Efficacy beliefs affect thought patterns that can enhance or undermine performance (Bandura, 1997, pp.116-122). These cognitive effects operate through several mechanisms: the construction of cognitive scenarios (visualizing success vs. failure), the quality of inferential thinking in complex decision situations, and conceptions of ability that frame how experiences are interpreted.

## Key Findings

**Cognitive constructions:**
- People with high efficacy view situations as presenting realizable opportunities and visualize success scenarios that provide positive guides for performance
- Those who judge themselves inefficacious construe uncertain situations as risky and visualize failure scenarios (Krueger & Dickson, 1994)
- Cognitive simulations in which individuals visualize themselves executing activities skillfully enhance subsequent performance (Bandura, 1986a; Corbin, 1972; Feltz & Landers, 1983; Kazdin, 1978)
- Visualizing successful actions improves performances; imaging faulty ones impairs them (Powell, 1973)
- Self-efficacy and cognitive simulation affect each other bidirectionally

**Inferential thinking:**
- A high sense of efficacy fosters cognitive constructions of effective courses of action
- The Wood and Bandura (1989a) simulated organization study demonstrates that managers with strong efficacy remained task-oriented, used efficient analytic strategies, and achieved high organizational performance even under causal ambiguity and judgment failures
- Those with weakened efficacy became increasingly erratic in analytic thinking and lowered aspirations

**Conceptions of ability (pp.118-119):**
- **Acquirable skill view:** Ability can be increased by gaining knowledge and perfecting competencies. People adopt learning goals, seek challenges, view errors as natural, and measure success by personal improvement. Produces resilient self-efficacy and high performance under difficulty (Figure 4.1).
- **Inherent aptitude view:** Ability is a fixed, endowed capacity. Errors signify intellectual limitations. People prefer tasks that minimize error display, shun learning opportunities, and belittle accomplishments when surpassed by others. Produces declining efficacy and deteriorating performance under difficulty (Figure 4.1).
- These conceptions can be experimentally instilled with strong effects (Wood & Bandura, 1989b)
- The same person may hold different conceptions across different domains
- This distinction predates and provides empirical foundation for Dweck's growth/fixed mindset framework

**Perceived controllability (pp.120-121):**
- People who believe their environment is controllable maintain high self-efficacy, set challenging goals, and achieve high performance even facing difficulties (Bandura & Wood, 1989, Figure 4.3)
- Those with an instilled belief that the environment is not easily changeable quickly lose faith and lower aspirations
- Path analysis confirms that with growing experience, efficacy beliefs increasingly regulate the entire performance system (Figure 4.4)

## Mechanisms

Efficacy beliefs affect cognition through:
1. **Scenario construction:** High efficacy --> success scenarios --> positive performance guides; Low efficacy --> failure scenarios --> self-undermining thought patterns
2. **Analytic strategy quality:** High efficacy maintains systematic, efficient analytic thinking under pressure; low efficacy produces erratic, degraded analysis
3. **Ability framing:** Acquirable skill conception --> learning goals, error tolerance, self-improvement focus; Inherent aptitude conception --> performance goals, error avoidance, social comparison focus
4. **Environmental construal:** Controllable environment belief --> proactive engagement; Uncontrollable environment belief --> passive resignation

## Applications

- **Cognitive rehearsal:** Systematically visualize successful execution of challenging tasks before performing them
- **Instill acquirable skill conceptions:** Frame abilities as developable competencies rather than fixed traits -- relevant to teaching, coaching, and organizational leadership
- **Controllability framing:** Present challenging environments as responsive to strategic effort rather than as determined by forces beyond control
- **Protect analytic quality:** Recognize that declining self-efficacy degrades the quality of strategic thinking; intervene to restore efficacy before performance spirals
