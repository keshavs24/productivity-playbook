# Commanders of Attention

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Commanders of attention are features of information that naturally draw and/or hold human attention without requiring a communicator's special effort. They function as pre-suasive devices because, by their nature, they automatically capture focus — and whatever captures focus gains perceived importance ([[focusing-illusion]]) and perceived causality ([[focal-attention-is-causal]]).

Cialdini divides them into two categories:

### Attractors (Ch.5) — Draw initial attention
1. **The Sexual**: Sexual stimuli commandeer attention due to evolutionary connections to reproduction. However, they are *conditional* — sex in advertising works only for sexually related products, and sexual stimuli only capture attention from people in a romantic/sexual mindset.
2. **The Threatening**: Violence and threats draw attention due to survival-linked connections to avoiding harm. Fear-based health messages work best when paired with actionable solutions (to prevent denial). **Dread risks** — risky behaviors adopted to avoid focal but less dangerous threats — are a dangerous consequence (e.g., post-9/11 road deaths).
3. **The Different**: Change and novelty automatically trigger the **orienting response** (O-wave) — Pavlov's "investigatory reflex." Scene cuts in film/TV, novelty in print, and distinctiveness among competitors all exploit this. But indiscriminate use (too many scene cuts) produces confusion rather than persuasion.

### Magnetizers (Ch.6) — Hold sustained attention
1. **The Self-Relevant**: Information about oneself is the "superglue of attention." Self-relevant messages (using "you," tailoring to individual characteristics) are more attended to, remembered, and acted upon. The **next-in-line effect** shows how self-focus can block processing of adjacent information.
2. **The Unfinished**: The [[zeigarnik-effect]] — unfinished tasks are better remembered and more attention-holding than completed ones. The drive for cognitive closure keeps attention magnetized to incomplete activities, unanswered questions, and unachieved goals. Unfinished ads, uncertain romantic interest, and incomplete writing sessions all exploit this.
3. **The Mysterious**: Mystery stories combine the pulling power of the unfinished (the unsolved puzzle) with inherent fascination. Cialdini found that the most successful popular science writing began with mysteries. In classrooms, beginning with mysteries kept students engaged past the bell, produced better test scores, and facilitated deeper processing through explanation-seeking rather than mere description or argumentation.

## Key Finding: Evolutionary Matching Principle

The type of attractor determines which persuasive appeal will succeed:
- **Threat-focused** audiences favor **popularity-based** appeals ("Join the crowd") — because threat activates the desire for group safety
- **Sexually-focused** audiences favor **distinctiveness-based** appeals ("Stand out") — because romantic interest activates the desire to differentiate
- This matching was demonstrated in the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art ad experiment (Griskevicius, Goldstein, & Cialdini)

## Applications

- **Advertising**: Match the type of ad appeal (popularity vs. distinctiveness) to the emotional context of the surrounding media content
- **Teaching**: Begin lectures with mystery stories; never finish a writing session at a natural stopping point
- **Meetings**: Sit across from (not next to) the key decision-maker to leverage both the what's-focal-is-causal effect and escape the next-in-line effect
- **Health communication**: Pair fear-based messages with actionable steps to prevent denial
- **Marketing**: Use self-relevant cues ("you" instead of "people") as openers, but only when the subsequent case is strong
