# Relationships Motivation Theory (RMT)

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Relationships Motivation Theory (RMT) is the sixth and final mini-theory within [[self-determination-theory-framework|self-determination theory]]. It concerns the qualities of close personal relationships and their consequences for psychological wellness. RMT proposes that the [[relatedness-need]] is intrinsic and inclines people to be volitionally engaged in close relationships. The theory specifies that high-quality relationships require satisfaction of all three [[basic-psychological-needs]] -- relatedness, [[autonomy-need|autonomy]], and [[competence-need|competence]] -- and that [[autonomy-support]] within relationships is the key contextual factor facilitating this satisfaction.

RMT's distinctive contribution is its insistence that [[autonomy-in-relationships|autonomy and relatedness are complementary, not opposing]]. When social contexts such as [[conditional-regard]] pit these two needs against each other, both relationship quality and individual wellness suffer.

## Key Propositions

RMT is organized around seven formal propositions:

1. **Proposition I:** People have a basic psychological need for relatedness, essential to growth, integrity, and wellness. Its frustration causes ill-being.
2. **Proposition II:** High-quality relationships require not just contact but [[autonomous-motivation]] within and for the relationship. Autonomous motivation contributes to satisfaction and wellness in both partners.
3. **Proposition IIIa:** Satisfaction of all three basic needs within a relationship promotes relationship quality, attachment security, and well-being at both within-person and between-person levels.
4. **Proposition IIIb:** Frustration of any basic need within a relationship contributes to relationship dysfunction, defense, dissatisfaction, insecurity, and ill-being.
5. **Proposition IVa:** [[Autonomy-support]] from a partner increases willingness to emotionally rely on that partner (emotional reliance).
6. **Proposition IVb:** Autonomy support within a relationship enables the person to be authentic, transparent, and closer to their own ideals.
7. **Proposition V:** Mutuality of [[autonomy-support]] in close relationships amplifies relationship satisfaction, attachment security, and well-being for both partners.
8. **Proposition VI:** When the social environment forces a trade-off between two needs (e.g., relinquishing autonomy to get relatedness, as in [[conditional-regard]]), poorer relationship quality and lower wellness result.
9. **Proposition VII:** Objectifying a partner -- relating to them as an object, stereotype, or thing rather than as a person intrinsically worthy of respect -- thwarts basic needs, degrading relationship quality and wellness.

## Key Findings

### Autonomous Motivation in Relationships
- Couples with greater relational autonomy show more dyadic adjustment, satisfaction, and willingness to work through problems (Blais et al., 1990)
- Relational autonomy predicts more satisfaction after disagreements, greater partner understanding, and less defensiveness (Knee, Lonsbary, Canevello, & Patrick, 2005)
- Pro-relationship behaviors performed autonomously predict more satisfaction, closeness, vitality, and self-esteem (Patrick, 2007)
- Relational autonomy (acting for the other willingly) explains positive outcomes over and above personal autonomy for being in the relationship (Gore, Cross, & Kanagawa, 2009)

### Need Satisfaction and Attachment Security
- Attachment security varies substantially *within* persons across relationships, systematically predicted by relationship-specific need satisfaction (La Guardia, Ryan, Couchman, & Deci, 2000)
- About 35% of attachment variance is between-person (working model); more than half is within-person and explained by need satisfaction with specific partners
- All three needs contribute independently, with relatedness the strongest predictor followed by autonomy

### Autonomy Support in Relationships
- Autonomy support predicts emotional reliance across four cultures (Ryan, La Guardia, Solky-Butzel, Chirkov, & Kim, 2005)
- People closer to their ideal self (Big Five traits) when with autonomy-supportive partners, across Russia, U.S., and China (Lynch et al., 2009)
- *Giving* autonomy support relates more strongly to the giver's well-being than *receiving* it (Deci, La Guardia, Moller, Scheiner, & Ryan, 2006)

### Conditional Regard
- Parental conditional regard produces introjected motivation, fragile self-esteem, shame/guilt, and damages the parent-child relationship (Assor, Roth, & Deci, 2004)
- Adults who experienced parental conditional regard tend to select (and project onto) peers who are similarly conditionally regarding (Moller, Roth, Niemiec, Kanat-Maymon, & Deci, 2016)

## Mechanisms

RMT operates through several interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Need satisfaction as mediator:** The relation between social support/relationship quality and psychological wellness is largely mediated by the degree to which relationships satisfy basic psychological needs.
2. **Autonomy-relatedness synergy:** Autonomy support from a partner inherently supports relatedness (by taking the partner's perspective) and competence (by implicitly conveying trust in the partner's ability). These needs are naturally synergistic.
3. **Conditional regard as need-antagonism mechanism:** When love is made contingent on compliance, satisfying the relatedness need requires sacrificing autonomy. This forced trade-off guarantees deprivation of at least one need, producing introjected regulation, fragile self-esteem, and resentment.
4. **Emotional reliance and authenticity:** Autonomy support creates conditions where people willingly turn to partners for emotional support and feel free to be their authentic selves -- both of which deepen relational bonds and support wellness.

## Applications

- **Romantic relationships:** Both partners benefit from cultivating mutual autonomy support -- acknowledging each other's perspectives, providing choices, avoiding controlling language
- **Friendships:** Quality friendships are characterized by autonomy support mutuality, not just contact frequency or shared activities
- **Parenting:** Unconditional regard (accepting the child's self, not just approved behaviors) simultaneously satisfies both autonomy and relatedness needs. Conditional regard damages both.
- **Clinical practice:** Therapeutic alliance quality depends on the therapist's autonomy support -- facilitating clients' own exploration rather than imposing agendas
