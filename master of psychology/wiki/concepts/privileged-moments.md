# Privileged Moments

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Privileged moments are identifiable points in time when an individual is particularly receptive to a communicator's message. The term carries a double meaning: (1) a **time-limited period** — the window of opportunity following a pre-suasive opener, when a proposal's power is greatest; and (2) a **unique leveraging force** (from physics) — a singular force that can bring about unprecedented movement along a psychological dimension.

Critically, privileged moments are *temporary*. Pre-suasive actions create windows of receptivity that are far from propped open permanently. The receptivity fades once the moment passes, which is why timing the request to coincide with the privileged moment is essential.

## Key Findings

- **The associate dean's maneuver**: Cialdini was pre-suaded into agreeing to teach an MBA course (which torpedoed his book project) because the request came in the privileged moment immediately after receiving a string of generous favors. Had the request come a day before or after, he could have declined.
- **Cult recruitment**: Cult recruiters create privileged moments by asking "Are you unhappy?" — a single-chute question that, via the [[positive-test-strategy]], makes people focus on dissatisfactions and declare themselves unhappy. In that privileged moment, the recruiter strikes: "Well, if you're unhappy, you'd want to change that, right?"
- **Bolkan and Andersen studies**: Asking "Do you consider yourself helpful/adventurous?" created a privileged moment in which compliance roughly tripled (29% → 77.3% for helpfulness; 33% → 75.7% for adventurousness)
- **The factor most likely to determine a person's choice is often not the one that counsels most wisely — it is the one elevated in attention at the moment of decision**

## Mechanisms

Privileged moments work because [[pre-suasion|pre-suasive]] actions temporarily alter what is focal in consciousness. During the privileged moment:
1. The focal concept gains perceived importance ([[focusing-illusion]])
2. The focal concept gains perceived causality ([[focal-attention-is-causal]])
3. Associated concepts become cognitively accessible ([[associative-priming]])
4. Competing concepts get temporarily suppressed
5. The audience's identity, mood, or orientation has been shifted in a direction favorable to the communicator's message

## Applications

- **Sales**: Time the close to immediately follow trust-building or value-demonstration openers
- **Negotiation**: Make requests immediately after delivering concessions or favors ([[reciprocation]])
- **Marketing research**: Pre-suasive single-chute questions ("Do you consider yourself helpful?") dramatically increase survey participation
- **Self-influence**: Recognize and create privileged moments for personal goals — e.g., reviewing past successes before high-stakes performances
