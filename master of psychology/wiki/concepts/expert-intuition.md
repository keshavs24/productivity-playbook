# Expert Intuition

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Expert intuition is the ability of experts to rapidly recognize patterns in their domain and immediately "see" the appropriate response without deliberate analysis or search. It is not a mystical faculty but rather the product of extensive [[chunking]] and [[procedural knowledge|proceduralization]] -- experts have stored so many pattern-action pairs that they can recognize situations and retrieve appropriate responses from memory rather than computing solutions from scratch.

## Key Findings

- Chess masters consider about the same number of possible moves as duffers (and sometimes fewer), yet choose much better moves -- they "see" the right move through pattern recognition rather than exhaustive search (de Groot, 1965) ([[summary-2-09-anderson-ch9]])
- Newell and Simon (1972) proposed that in addition to learning ~50,000 chess patterns ([[chunking]]), masters have learned what to do in the presence of each pattern. The chunk triggers the action.
- Expert radiologists correctly interpret ambiguous X-ray shadows by perceiving deep features (size, shape, context) rather than relying on the most common association, as novices do (Lesgold et al., 1988)
- Physics experts recognize problems by deep principles ("conservation of energy") rather than surface features ("inclined plane"), enabling them to immediately apply the correct solution method (Chi, Feltovich, & Glaser, 1981)
- Expert intuition develops through the three phases of skill acquisition: declarative -> associative -> autonomous. In the autonomous phase, pattern recognition replaces deliberate retrieval ([[proceduralization]])
- Computers achieve chess expertise through a fundamentally different path -- brute-force search of millions of positions per second -- demonstrating that "intelligence" can be achieved multiple ways

## Mechanisms

Expert intuition arises from the convergence of three expertise mechanisms:
1. **[[Chunking]]:** Experts have built a vast library of domain-specific patterns through thousands of hours of experience
2. **[[Proceduralization]]:** Each recognized pattern is associated with an appropriate response, bypassing the need for declarative retrieval
3. **Strategic learning:** Experts have learned the overall structure of problems in their domain, enabling them to organize their approach efficiently (forward reasoning in physics, breadth-first decomposition in programming)

The result is that what looks like "intuition" to an outside observer is actually rapid, automatic pattern matching against a massive database of previously encountered situations.

## Applications

- Do not trust expert intuition outside the expert's specific domain -- chess grandmasters are not better general thinkers
- Developing expert intuition requires [[deliberate-practice]], not just experience -- many people play chess for decades without developing intuitive mastery
- When building expertise, focus on learning patterns and their associated responses, not just rules or procedures
- Expert intuition is reliable in well-structured domains with regular patterns (chess, radiology, physics); it may be less reliable in domains with less predictable structure
