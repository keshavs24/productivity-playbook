# Impersonal Orientation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The impersonal orientation is one of three general causality orientations in [[causality-orientations-theory]]. It describes the degree to which people orient toward obstacles to goal attainment, readily experience anxiety and incompetence, and react to their lack of control over outcomes. Drawn from Heider's (1958) concept of impersonal causality, people high in this orientation tend to lack intentionality, initiative, and a sense of personal causation. They are relatively passive and easily overwhelmed by environmental forces and internal drives. This is the least healthy and effective of the three orientations, associated with amotivation and akrasia.

## Key Findings

- Positively related to **social anxiety, public self-consciousness, self-derogation, depressive symptoms, lower self-esteem** (Deci & Ryan, 1985a)
- Related to lower **ego development** (Loevinger, 1976) and **powerlessness/self-estrangement** (McHoskey, 1999)
- Associated with **performance goals combined with low confidence** (Koestner & Zuckerman, 1994)
- Related to **diffuse-avoidant identity style** -- procrastination, denial of internal conflicts, maladaptive coping (Soenens et al., 2005; Duriez et al., 2004)
- Women with **restrictive anorexia nervosa** scored significantly higher on impersonal orientation (Strauss & Ryan, 1987)
- Individuals with **schizophrenia** showed lower autonomy and higher impersonal orientations (Cooper, Lavaysse, & Gard, 2015)
- Develops through considerable degree of **unpredictable thwarting** of basic psychological needs
- Often fosters **amotivation or akrasia** -- inability to master or take command of situations

## Mechanisms

The impersonal orientation operates through:
1. **Amotivating interpretation** -- construing environments as uncontrollable
2. **Helplessness focus** -- attending to cues of incompetence and inability to influence outcomes
3. **Passivity** -- lacking initiative and sense of personal causation
4. **Need thwarting cycles** -- unpredictable early environments produce impersonal orientation, which leads to further need frustration

## Applications

- **Clinical identification:** High impersonal orientation is a marker of compromised functioning warranting clinical attention
- **Therapeutic targets:** Addressing impersonal orientation involves building experiences of competence and agency in supportive environments
- **Understanding amotivation:** Distinguishing between "can't do it" (competence-based) and "don't care" (autonomy-based) amotivation helps tailor interventions
