# Click, Whirr (Automatic Responding)

**Phase(s):** All (meta-concept underlying all six principles)
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

"Click, whirr" is Cialdini's metaphor for automatic, trigger-feature-based human responding — the tendency to react to a single feature of a situation with a stereotyped sequence of behavior, without engaging in thorough analysis. The term draws an analogy to animal fixed-action patterns: *click* (the trigger feature is detected) and *whirr* (the automatic behavioral sequence plays out).

## Key Findings

- **Fixed-action patterns in animals**: Turkey mothers respond automatically to the "cheep-cheep" sound — accepting a stuffed polecat that plays the sound, rejecting their own chicks that don't (Fox, 1974). Male robins attack red feather clumps but ignore perfect replicas without red (Lack, 1943).
- **Human parallel — Langer's Xerox study** (1978): The word "because" alone triggered 93% compliance for cutting in line, even when followed by a placebic (non-informative) reason. Without "because": only 60%.
- **Expensive = good**: Turquoise jewelry sold out at 2x the intended price because customers used price as an automatic trigger for quality (supported by Rao & Monroe, 1989; Cronley et al., 2005; Plassmann et al., 2008 brain-scan study)
- **Coupons without savings** (Zimmatore, 1983): Coupons that offered no actual discount produced the same response rate as real discount coupons — the coupon itself was the trigger
- **Automatic responding is the default**: People use controlled processing only when they have both the desire *and* the ability to analyze (Petty, Cacioppo, & Goldman, 1981; Chaiken & Trope, 1999)

## Mechanisms

Click, whirr responding is not a flaw — it is an **adaptive necessity**. The environment is too complex for thorough analysis of every situation (Gigerenzer & Goldstein, 1996; Fiske & Neuberg, 1990). Shortcuts work correctly most of the time because the trigger features they rely on are usually reliable indicators.

The danger arises from two sources:
1. **Profiteers** who know where the trigger features are and can mimic or manipulate them (like Photuris fireflies mimicking Photinus mating signals)
2. **Modern complexity** — even on personally important topics, the pace of life may prevent controlled processing (Cohen, 1978; Milgram, 1970)

**Key distinction**: Human click-whirr responses are more flexible than animal fixed-action patterns — they are learned rather than inborn, and responsive to a larger number of triggers. But they share the same core property: a single feature activates a whole behavioral sequence.

## Applications

- All six principles of influence ([[reciprocation]], [[commitment-and-consistency]], [[social-proof]], [[liking-principle]], [[authority-principle]], [[scarcity-principle]]) work by exploiting click-whirr responding
- The [[contrast-principle]] operates as a perceptual click-whirr: present something extreme first, and the subsequent item is automatically judged relative to it
- Defense requires recognizing the trigger moment — the instant you feel automatic compliance kicking in — and pausing to ask whether the response serves your interests

## Open Questions

- How does click-whirr interact with expertise? Do domain experts use *different* triggers rather than fewer?
- As information overload increases, does the threshold for controlled processing keep rising — making click-whirr the dominant mode even for important decisions?
- How does digital technology (algorithmic feeds, notification systems) create new trigger features?
