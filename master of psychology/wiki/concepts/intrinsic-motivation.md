# Intrinsic Motivation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Intrinsic motivation is the inherent human propensity to seek out novelty and challenges, to extend and exercise one's capacities, to explore, and to learn -- for the satisfaction inherent in the activity itself, not for separable consequences. It represents a primary and spontaneous tendency of organisms, especially mammals, to develop through play, exploration, and manipulation. Within [[self-determination-theory]], intrinsic motivation is a prototypical manifestation of integrative organismic tendencies and is among the most important inner resources evolution has provided (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Hawley, 2016).

Intrinsic motivation is not the whole of human motivation -- many important behaviors are extrinsically motivated. But the study of intrinsic motivation revealed that the human organism is **active** by nature (not passive, as assumed by behaviorist and drive theories), and that this active growth tendency can be either supported or thwarted by social conditions.

Operationally, intrinsic motivation is measured through the **free-choice behavioral paradigm** (time spent on a task when free to do anything) and self-report instruments like the **Intrinsic Motivation Inventory** (IMI).

## Key Findings

### What Enhances Intrinsic Motivation
- **Choice:** Providing autonomy in what, how, and when to do activities (Zuckerman et al., 1978; Patall et al., 2008 meta-analysis of 41 studies)
- **Optimal challenge:** Tasks that stretch existing skills without being overwhelming (Deci, 1975; Danner & Lonky, 1981)
- **Positive informational feedback:** Competence-affirming feedback delivered non-controllingly (Deci et al., 1999 meta-analysis: d=0.43 for college students)
- **Autonomy-supportive climates:** Teachers, parents, and managers who support autonomy predict higher intrinsic motivation in their charges (Deci, Schwartz, et al., 1981)
- **Relatedness:** A secure relational base (attachment security, felt closeness to authorities) supports exploration and intrinsic motivation (Ryan, Stiller, & Lynch, 1994)

### What Undermines Intrinsic Motivation (the [[undermining-effect]])
- **Tangible contingent rewards:** Expected, salient, contingent rewards shift PLOC from internal to external (Deci, 1971; Lepper et al., 1973; meta-analysis d=-0.34)
- **Evaluations:** Even positive evaluations undermine when experienced as controlling (Smith, 1975; Grolnick & Ryan, 1987)
- **Surveillance:** Being watched undermines intrinsic motivation (Lepper & Greene, 1975; Plant & Ryan, 1985)
- **Deadlines and imposed goals:** External time pressure undermines (Amabile et al., 1976; Mossholder, 1980)
- **Controlling competition:** Pressure to win undermines (Deci et al., 1981)
- **[[ego-involvement]]:** Internal self-evaluative pressure undermines even without external controls (Ryan, 1982)

### Performance Outcomes
- **Learning:** Intrinsic motivation produces deeper conceptual understanding, not just rote memorization (Benware & Deci, 1984; Grolnick & Ryan, 1987)
- **Creativity:** People are more creative under intrinsically motivating conditions (Amabile, 1983; Utman, 1997 meta-analysis)
- **Complex performance:** Intrinsic motivation enhances performance on heuristic tasks while rewards can impair it (McGraw & McCullers, 1979; McGraw, 1978)
- **Persistence:** Intrinsic motivation predicts genuine interested persistence (correlated with enjoyment), distinguishable from ego-involved controlled persistence (Ryan, Koestner, & Deci, 1991)

### Educational Outcomes (Ch. 14)
- **Decline across school years:** Intrinsic motivation declines steadily from grades 3-8 (Harter, 1981; Lepper et al., 2005) and ages 9-16 (Gillet et al., 2012; Gnambs & Hanfstingl, 2016), substantially accounted for by school environments that fail to satisfy basic psychological needs
- **Academic achievement:** Taylor et al. (2014) meta-analysis: intrinsic motivation was the only motivation type consistently positively associated with academic achievement over 1 year, controlling for baseline. Froiland & Worrell (2016): robustly predicted engagement and GPA across diverse samples
- **Grading undermines:** Grolnick & Ryan (1987): students expecting to be graded showed less interest and poorer conceptual learning than noncontrolling conditions, though rote memorization was comparable. Klapp (2015): 8,000+ Swedish students -- grading in primary school negatively affected subsequent achievement, especially for low-ability students
- **Generalization:** Intrinsic motivation from one activity (e.g., garden-based learning) can generalize to improved achievement in regular courses (Skinner et al., 2012; Ruiz-Gallardo et al., 2013)
- See [[autonomy-support-in-education]] and [[summary-3-10-sdt-ch14-schools]] for extensive evidence

### Two Necessary Conditions
Both [[autonomy-need|autonomy]] and [[competence-need|competence]] satisfaction are necessary for intrinsic motivation. Satisfying competence alone (e.g., positive feedback in a controlling context) does not enhance intrinsic motivation (Ryan, 1982). Autonomy alone without competence (e.g., choice on an impossibly difficult task) also fails to sustain it.

## Mechanisms

1. **Active organism assumption:** Intrinsic motivation is not a drive that builds up and must be discharged, but an inherent tendency toward growth, exploration, and mastery
2. **Need satisfaction:** Intrinsic motivation is energized and sustained when the basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and (distally) relatedness are satisfied
3. **Perceived locus of causality (PLOC):** Intrinsic motivation requires an internal PLOC -- the person must experience behavior as volitional and self-endorsed
4. **Optimal challenge:** A growth function that occurs at the leading edge of competence, where effort is required but success is possible
5. **Neural substrates:** Intrinsic motivation is associated with anterior insular cortex (AIC) activation and reward-network engagement independent of external incentives (Lee & Reeve, 2013; Murayama et al., 2010)

## Applications

- **Education:** Foster intrinsic motivation through autonomy support, optimal challenge, informational feedback, and relatedness support. Minimize controlling practices (high-stakes testing, performance-contingent rewards)
- **Workplace:** Support autonomy in how tasks are performed; create conditions for optimal challenge and mastery; provide informational feedback
- **Parenting:** Encourage play, exploration, and curiosity. Avoid converting inherently interesting activities into reward-contingent ones
- **Game design:** Align with intrinsic motivation principles: optimal challenge, meaningful choice, competence feedback, narrative autonomy
- **Health behavior:** Intrinsic motivation for exercise, healthy eating, etc. predicts sustained behavior change better than extrinsic motivation

## Open Questions
- What is the relationship between intrinsic motivation and flow states (Csikszentmihalyi)? SDT argues flow requires autonomy, not just skill-challenge balance
- Can intrinsic motivation be developed for activities that are initially uninteresting, or does it only apply to naturally interesting activities? (Addressed in OIT/Chapter 8)
- How stable is intrinsic motivation as a trait vs. a state? Ch. 14 evidence shows it declines systematically across school years (ages 9-16), largely due to need-thwarting school environments rather than developmental inevitability. Gillet et al. (2012) found an increase after age 15 when students gained more choice over subjects.
