# Accusation Audit

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

An accusation audit is a preparation technique within [[tactical-empathy]] in which the negotiator lists every terrible thing the counterpart *could* say about them, then says those things aloud before the counterpart can. It is a systematic, preemptive application of [[labeling-emotions]] at scale -- "taking the sting out" by front-loading all potential negative perceptions.

The technique borrows from defense lawyers who mention everything their client is accused of, and all the weaknesses of their case, in the opening statement. By addressing the negatives first, you defuse them and prevent them from becoming landmines later.

Because the accusations often sound exaggerated when spoken aloud, the counterpart is psychologically compelled to claim the opposite is true -- "No, no, we don't think that" -- which shifts them toward a more collaborative stance.

## Key Findings

- **Anna's government contractor negotiation:** Anna needed to cut subcontractor ABC Corp.'s pay while improving the relationship. She and partner Mark listed every negative charge ABC could level: "You are the typical prime contractor trying to force out the small guy," "You promised us we would have all this work and you reneged," "You could have told us about this issue weeks ago." They role-played, practiced timing, and planned meaningful pauses. At the meeting, Anna opened by acknowledging ABC's biggest gripes. ABC nodded emphatically. The negatives defused, the conversation turned to problem-solving. Result: contract adjusted, $1 million saved, and ABC said: "We appreciate the fact that you are acknowledging what happened, and we don't feel like you are mistreating us."
- **Voss's classroom exercise:** "In case you're worried about volunteering to role-play with me in front of the class, I want to tell you in advance... it's going to be horrible." By labeling the fear preemptively, he gets more volunteers than he needs. Prefacing with "how much worse can something be than 'horrible'?" makes the unreasonable seem less forbidding.
- **Canada country-clearance incident:** Voss entered Canada without notifying the top FBI official. He called and opened: "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned." By naming the negative dynamic immediately, he diffused the official's anger and secured clearance.

## Mechanisms

1. **Preemptive amygdala disruption:** By naming fears and accusations before they are raised, you interrupt the counterpart's amygdala response before it fully activates. The fears are "bathed in sunlight, bleaching them of their power."
2. **Exaggeration effect:** When you voice the worst accusations out loud, they often sound more extreme than what the other person actually thinks. This compels them to moderate: "No, no, it's not that bad." You've anchored their perception at the negative extreme, making their real position feel more reasonable by comparison.
3. **Initiative seizure:** By going first with the negatives, you control the framing. The counterpart responds to your characterization rather than constructing their own accusations fresh. This shifts from adversarial (they attack, you defend) to collaborative (you've acknowledged, now let's solve).
4. **Trust acceleration:** Acknowledging potential wrongdoing or shortcomings demonstrates good faith and self-awareness. It satisfies the counterpart's need to feel heard and understood before they've even spoken.

## Applications

**How to conduct an accusation audit:**
1. Before the negotiation, sit down and list every negative thing your counterpart could say about you, your position, your organization, or your history together
2. Prioritize the biggest, most emotionally charged accusations
3. Role-play delivering them with a partner -- practice saying them slowly and naturally, with [[labeling-emotions]] phrasing: "You're going to think we are...", "It seems like we have...", "You may feel like..."
4. Plan when to include meaningful pauses after each label
5. At the meeting, open by addressing the biggest gripes first -- before any proposals or asks
6. After the accusations are on the table, shift to problem-solving

**When to use:**
- Before any negotiation where you anticipate hostility, resentment, or unresolved grievances
- When apologizing for a mistake or delivering bad news
- When you are at a perceived power disadvantage and need to level the playing field
- Before asking for something uncomfortable (volunteers, favors, difficult tasks)
