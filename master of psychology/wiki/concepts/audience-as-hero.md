# Audience as Hero

**Phase(s):** 7
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Audience as Hero is the foundational mindset shift in [[duarte|Nancy Duarte]]'s *[[resonate]]*: **the presenter is not the hero of the presentation — the audience is.** The presenter's role is that of the *mentor* (Yoda to the audience's Luke Skywalker, Mr. Miyagi to their Daniel, The Oracle to their Neo). The audience is the one who will do the heavy lifting — adopting the idea, changing behavior, and transforming the world. The presenter merely provides guidance, confidence, insight, and tools so the audience (hero) can succeed on their journey.

## Key Findings

- Most presentations start with "me-ness" — the dreaded "it's all about me" slide featuring company history, market cap, number of employees, and product features. This self-centered approach alienates audiences the same way a person at a party who talks only about themselves drives everyone away.
- Screenwriter Chad Hodge (Harvard Business Review) argues that we should "[help] people to see themselves as the hero of the story, whether the plot involves beating the bad guys or achieving some great business objective."
- Successful Hollywood screenwriter Blake Snyder coined the "save the cat" moment — where we first meet the hero and he does something likable. The presentation equivalent: make the audience feel understood, valued, and personally addressed from the outset.
- When you change your stance from hero to mentor, it alters your viewpoint. You come from a place of humility, the aide-de-camp to your audience. A mentor has a selfless nature and is willing to make personal sacrifices so the hero can reach the reward.

## Mechanisms

The concept works through several psychological mechanisms:

1. **Empathy and perspective-taking:** Thinking of the audience as the hero forces the presenter to adopt the audience's perspective — understanding their fears, desires, knowledge, and values. This connects to [[audience-analysis]] and the creation of [[common-ground]].
2. **Humility as credibility:** Self-centered presentations trigger resistance. Humble, audience-centered presentations create connection. "Audience insights and resonance can only occur when a presenter takes a stance of humility."
3. **Hero's Journey structure:** Drawing from Joseph Campbell and Christopher Vogler, the audience-as-hero framework maps the presentation onto the mythological structure. The audience begins in their ordinary world, receives a call to adventure from the mentor (presenter), crosses the threshold, faces tests, and returns transformed with an elixir. Connects to [[story-as-simulation]].

## Applications

- Before creating any presentation, profile the audience (the hero) across six dimensions: Lifestyle, Knowledge, Motivation and Desire, Values, Influence, Respect
- Profile yourself as the mentor across three dimensions: Guidance (insights and knowledge), Confidence (bolstering so they are not reluctant), Tools (skills or magical gifts for their journey)
- Rewrite "about us" slides to focus on how the company serves the audience's needs
- Frame benefits in terms of what the audience gains, not what the presenter offers
