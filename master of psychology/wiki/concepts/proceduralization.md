# Proceduralization

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Proceduralization is the process by which people transition from **explicit retrieval and application of declarative knowledge** (rules, facts, instructions) to **direct, automatic application of procedural knowledge** (pattern-driven action without conscious thought). It marks the transition from the cognitive phase to the associative phase of skill acquisition and is a key mechanism underlying the development of expertise.

## Key Findings

- Anderson (1982) documented a student learning geometry proofs: initially, the student spent long pauses laboriously retrieving postulate definitions from memory, rehearsing them verbally, and matching them piece by piece to the problem. By the sixth problem, the student instantly recognized which postulate applied without any verbal rehearsal. ([[summary-2-09-anderson-ch9]])
- After 5 days of math practice, brain imaging shows the largest activation decrease is in the **prefrontal region** (associated with declarative retrieval), dropping from 0.17% to 0.04% above baseline. Motor and ACC activation remained stable (Anderson, 2007).
- Poldrack and Gabrieli (2001) found increased basal ganglia activity and decreased hippocampal activation as learning progressed -- consistent with the shift from declarative to procedural memory systems
- Declarative and procedural knowledge can coexist -- speakers of a foreign language may be fluent (procedural) while still knowing grammar rules explicitly (declarative). However, procedural knowledge governs skilled performance.
- Logan (1988) proposed that a key mechanism of proceduralization is the shift from **computation** to **memory retrieval** -- after enough practice, answers are simply remembered rather than computed

## Mechanisms

During the cognitive phase, learners encode task knowledge declaratively (as facts and rules in working memory). Performing the task requires retrieving these facts, interpreting them, and applying them step by step -- a slow, error-prone process that loads working memory heavily. With practice, the interpretive steps are compiled into direct pattern-action associations stored as procedural knowledge. The task can then be performed without retrieving or rehearsing the underlying rules.

Brain imaging confirms this: prefrontal regions (declarative retrieval) show dramatic activity decreases with practice, while motor regions (execution) remain constant.

## Applications

- In learning any new skill, expect the initial phase to feel slow, effortful, and error-prone -- this is the necessary cognitive phase before proceduralization can occur
- Design learning environments that support the declarative-to-procedural transition: start with clear rules, provide extensive practice with feedback, and progressively reduce scaffolding
- Recognize that "knowing the rules" (declarative) and "being able to perform" (procedural) are different knowledge states -- practice is required to bridge them
