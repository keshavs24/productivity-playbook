# Identified Regulation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Identified regulation is a relatively autonomous form of [[extrinsic-motivation]] within [[organismic-integration-theory]]. Through identification, a person has consciously endorsed the value and personal importance of a behavior. The person acts willingly because they see the behavior as personally important, not because of external pressure or internal guilt. The perceived locus of causality is more internal (I-PLOC), and the experience is characterized by greater volition and less conflict than [[introjected-regulation]].

## Key Findings

- Identification does not necessarily imply full **self-compatibility checking** -- the person accepts the importance of an action but may not have fully examined its relation to other aspects of their identity (Kazen et al., 2003)
- **Compartmentalization** can occur: a strong identification may be defensively isolated from other identifications, perceptions, and needs
- Regulation through identification is **more stable, persistent, and associated with positive affect** than external or introjected regulation
- For **non-enjoyable but important activities** (e.g., voting, exercising), identification may be a stronger predictor of sustained behavior than [[intrinsic-motivation]] (Koestner & Losier, 1999)
- Identified regulation predicted **active information seeking and complex positions** on political issues; introjected regulation predicted vulnerability to persuasion (Koestner et al., 1996)
- Ryan et al. (1993): Identified regulation for religious behaviors positively related to self-actualization, self-esteem, and identity integration

## Mechanisms

Identification involves:
1. **Conscious endorsement** -- the person accepts the value as "personally important for me"
2. **Relative volition** -- acting out of belief in personal importance rather than pressure or guilt
3. **More internal PLOC** -- but not yet fully integrated with other values and self-structures
4. **Reduced conflict** -- less tension between the regulation and the person's desires compared to introjection

The transition from introjection to identification is facilitated by autonomy support -- helping people reflectively explore *why* a behavior matters to them personally.

## Applications

- **Goal setting:** Goals pursued for identified reasons produce sustained effort and higher probability of attainment (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)
- **Health behavior:** Patients who identify with the value of diet/exercise persist long-term, unlike those externally or introjectedly motivated (Williams et al., 1996)
- **Education:** Students who see schoolwork as personally important show deeper engagement
- **Connection to [[commitment-and-consistency]]:** Identified regulation resembles internalized commitment -- the person has consciously accepted a value and acts accordingly. The Cialdini mechanism of public commitment may facilitate the transition from external to identified regulation.
