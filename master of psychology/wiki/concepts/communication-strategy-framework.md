# Communication Strategy Framework

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The communication strategy framework is a five-variable model for planning effective managerial communication, developed by [[munter|Mary Munter]] and [[guide-to-managerial-communication|presented in her textbook]]. The five interactive variables are:

1. **Communicator strategy** -- your objective, communication style ([[tell-sell-consult-join]]), and [[credibility-in-communication|credibility]]
2. **[[audience-analysis|Audience strategy]]** -- who they are, what they know and expect, what they feel, and what will persuade them
3. **Message strategy** -- how to emphasize and organize your ideas, including [[direct-vs-indirect-organization]] and storytelling
4. **Channel choice strategy** -- whether to write, present, or speak individually, and which specific medium to use
5. **Culture strategy** -- how cultural norms affect all other variables

The model is circular rather than linear: communication success is measured by the desired audience response, and the five variables interact with each other rather than proceeding in lockstep order. Your audience analysis affects your style choice; your channel choice affects your message; culture affects channel choice; and so on.

## Key Findings

### The Circular Model

Traditional communication models (Shannon-Weaver) depict communication as a straight line from sender to receiver. Munter argues this is inadequate for managerial communication because it ignores the outcome. In business, the most brilliant message is worthless unless it achieves the desired response. The circular model makes the audience response the starting point of strategy: "As a result of this communication, my audience will..."

### Five Variables Are Interactive

The variables do not follow a fixed sequence. Audience analysis may change your chosen style; culture may dictate your channel; your credibility may determine whether you use [[direct-vs-indirect-organization|direct or indirect]] organization. Strategic communicators assess all five variables and adjust each one based on the others.

### Practical Application

Munter provides a **Communication Strategy Checklist** that encapsulates the entire framework:

**Communicator Strategy:**
1. What is your communication objective?
2. What communication style do you choose?
3. What is your credibility?

**Audience Strategy:**
1. Who are they?
2. What do they know and expect?
3. What do they feel?
4. What will persuade them?

**Message Strategy:**
1. How can you harness the power of beginnings and endings? Direct or indirect?
2. How can you overcome the retention dip in the middle?
3. How should you organize your message?
4. How might you connect through stories?

**Channel Choice Strategy:**
1. Written-only channels
2. Oral-only channels
3. Blended channels

**Culture Strategy:**
- What are the cultural attitudes toward time, fate, credibility, audience selection, persuasion, message structure, channel choice, and nonverbal behavior?

## Mechanisms

The framework works because it forces communicators to shift from a sender-centered perspective ("What do I want to say?") to a receiver-centered perspective ("What response do I want to achieve?"). This aligns with the rhetorical tradition going back to [[aristotle]] and [[ethos]], [[pathos]], [[logos]] -- but operationalizes it into a practical checklist for modern business contexts.

The five-variable structure also prevents the common error of optimizing for one dimension while neglecting others. A perfectly structured message (variable 3) delivered through the wrong channel (variable 4) to a poorly understood audience (variable 2) will fail regardless of message quality.

## Applications

- **Before any presentation:** Run through the five-variable checklist
- **Before any important email or document:** Consider audience strategy (who will read this? what do they already know? how do they feel about this topic?)
- **When communication fails:** Diagnose which variable(s) were neglected
- **For team alignment:** Use the checklist as a shared planning tool before group communications

## Open Questions

- How does the framework apply to digital-native communication channels (social media, messaging apps, video) that didn't exist when the model was first developed?
- How does the framework interact with cross-cultural virtual teams where multiple cultures are present simultaneously?
- The framework treats culture as one of five variables, but some scholars (e.g., Hofstede, Hall) would argue culture is the meta-variable that shapes all others.
