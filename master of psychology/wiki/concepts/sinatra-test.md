# Sinatra Test

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The Sinatra Test is a credibility heuristic named after Frank Sinatra's classic lyric "If I can make it there, I'll make it anywhere." An example passes the Sinatra Test when a single example alone is enough to establish credibility in an entire domain. The underlying logic: if you have succeeded in the most demanding possible context, your competence in any lesser context is implied. It is a form of internal credibility -- the message itself carries the proof, rather than relying on external endorsement or raw statistics.

## Key Findings

- The concept was introduced by [[heath-brothers]] in [[made-to-stick]] as the third method of building internal credibility (alongside vivid details and human-scale statistics)
- A single Sinatra Test example can outperform both statistical evidence and expert endorsements in establishing believability
- The power comes from the concreteness of the example rather than from numbers or authority -- these stories make you think, "If they can make it there, they can make it anywhere"
- Sinatra Test examples work because they tap into the audience's existing schemas about what constitutes a demanding challenge

## Mechanisms

1. **Schema activation:** The example activates the audience's understanding of what makes a particular domain challenging, allowing them to infer competence across all lesser challenges
2. **Concreteness advantage:** A specific, vivid example is more memorable and persuasive than abstract claims of reliability or competence (see [[abstract-vs-concrete-information]])
3. **Implicit logic:** The audience performs their own reasoning -- "if they could handle X, they can certainly handle Y" -- which is more persuasive than being told the conclusion directly
4. **Narrative transportation:** Sinatra Test examples are often mini-stories that engage the audience emotionally, bypassing analytical resistance

## Applications

- **Business credibility:** If you catered a White House function, you can compete for any catering contract. If you secured Fort Knox, you can compete for any security contract
- **Safexpress shipping (India):** Rubal Jain used two Sinatra Test stories to win a Bollywood studio account: (1) delivering every Harry Potter book to every bookstore in India by 8 A.M. on release day with zero leaks, and (2) delivering high school board examination papers securely. Both examples were more persuasive than the statistic "98.84% on-time delivery"
- **Bill McDonough's edible fabrics:** An environmentalist who eliminated toxic chemicals from textile manufacturing -- the factory's output water became cleaner than its input water. If he can make a textile factory that clean, he can improve environmental practices in any industry
- **Sales and marketing:** Lead with your single most impressive credential rather than a list of statistics. One extraordinary example establishes more credibility than a hundred ordinary ones
- **Job interviews and resumes:** Identify the single most demanding accomplishment in your background and lead with it

## Open Questions

- At what point does the Sinatra Test fail -- when the demanding context is too far removed from the audience's domain for the inference to hold?
- How does the Sinatra Test interact with the [[availability]] heuristic -- does a vivid example create an inflated sense of competence?
- Can Sinatra Test examples backfire if the audience perceives them as cherry-picked success stories?
