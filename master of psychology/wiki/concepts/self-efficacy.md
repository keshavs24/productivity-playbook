# Self-Efficacy

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Perceived self-efficacy refers to beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to produce given attainments (Bandura, 1997, p.3). It is not a measure of the skills one has, but a belief about what one can do with whatever skills one possesses under a variety of circumstances. Self-efficacy is a [[generative-capability]] -- cognitive, social, emotional, and behavioral subskills must be organized and effectively orchestrated to serve innumerable purposes.

Self-efficacy is distinct from:
- **Self-esteem** (judgments of self-worth, not capability)
- **Self-concept** (global composite self-image, too undifferentiated to predict behavior)
- **[[locus-of-control]]** (whether actions affect outcomes, not whether one can perform the actions)
- **Outcome expectations** (consequences of performance, not capability to perform)
- **Effectance motivation** (a postulated drive, not a measurable belief)

## Key Findings

- Self-efficacy is a uniformly good predictor of diverse forms of behavior across domains: academic performance, health habits, pain management, phobic behavior, athletic performance, career choice, organizational functioning ([[summary-3-11-se-ch1-theoretical-perspectives]])
- Efficacy beliefs account for most of the variance in expected outcomes -- when efficacy beliefs are controlled, outcome expectations make little or no independent contribution to prediction of behavior ([[summary-3-11-se-ch1-theoretical-perspectives]])
- Self-efficacy is empirically uncorrelated with locus of control (Bandura, 1991b); locus of control is generally a weak or inconsistent predictor of the same behaviors that self-efficacy predicts well
- Dispositional measures of perceived control and optimism derive their predictiveness largely from their redundancy with efficacy beliefs -- when efficacy is controlled, perceived control and optimism add nothing ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Diverse experimental paradigms demonstrate causal effects: vicarious efficacy induction, arbitrary anchoring of self-appraisals, bogus feedback and social comparison, contravening experimental designs, and multivariate panel designs ([[summary-3-11-se-ch2-nature-structure]])
- Optimistic self-efficacy appraisal is generally adaptive: it promotes persistence, effort, resilience, and accomplishment; depressed individuals who appraise themselves "realistically" show worse outcomes than nondepressed individuals with self-enhancing biases

## Sources of Self-Efficacy (Ch.3)

Self-efficacy beliefs are constructed from four principal [[sources-of-self-efficacy|sources of information]] (Bandura, 1997, Ch.3):

1. **[[mastery-experience]]** (enactive mastery) -- the most powerful source; direct performance accomplishments that provide authentic evidence of capability
2. **[[vicarious-experience]]** (modeling) -- observing similar others succeed or fail; governed by model similarity, competence, and diversity; coping models vs. mastery models
3. **[[verbal-persuasion]]** (social persuasion) -- evaluative feedback from credible others; gain-framed feedback supports efficacy, deficit-framed feedback undermines it; limited alone but effective as part of multifaceted interventions
4. **[[physiological-and-affective-states]]** (somatic information) -- people read their physiological activation and mood states as cues about capability; what matters is not the intensity of arousal but how it is perceived and interpreted

These sources are not inherently enlightening -- they become instructive only through cognitive processing and reflective thought. Preexisting self-schemata create interpretive biases that contribute to the stability of efficacy beliefs.

## Mediating Processes (Ch.4)

Self-efficacy beliefs affect human functioning through four major [[self-efficacy-mediating-processes|mediating processes]] (Bandura, 1997, Ch.4):

1. **[[efficacy-activated-cognitive-processes|Cognitive processes]]:** Efficacy beliefs influence whether thought patterns are self-hindering or self-aiding, the quality of analytic thinking, conceptions of ability (acquirable skill vs. inherent aptitude), and visualization of success vs. failure scenarios
2. **[[efficacy-activated-motivational-processes|Motivational processes]]:** Efficacy beliefs regulate motivation through causal attributions (mediating [[attribution-theory]] effects almost entirely), outcome expectancies, and cognized goals (goal level, commitment, proximal subgoals)
3. **Affective processes:** Efficacy beliefs influence anxiety, depression, and stress through three modes -- control over thought (cognitive reappraisal), action (transformational behavior), and affect (palliative regulation). Perceived coping efficacy and thought control efficacy jointly determine anxiety levels.
4. **Selection processes:** Efficacy beliefs influence which environments and activities people choose to engage in, shaping developmental trajectories and career paths

Self-efficacy operates within [[reciprocal-determinism]] -- the triadic reciprocal causation among personal factors (P), behavior (B), and environment (E). Efficacy beliefs are both products and producers of experiences.

## Applications

- **Skill acquisition:** Some self-doubt during the preparatory phase motivates deeper preparation, whereas strong efficacy during performance enables proficient execution (the preparatory vs. performance efficacy distinction) -- relevant to [[deliberate-practice]]
- **Resilience building:** Building self-efficacy through mastery experiences is the most powerful way to create a strong, resilient sense of efficacy
- **Collective action:** [[collective-efficacy]] -- the shared belief in group capabilities -- is an emergent group-level attribute crucial for collective agency. It predicts group performance across schools, organizations, and athletic teams (Bandura, 1993; Hodges & Carron, 1992) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch11-collective-efficacy]])
- **Cross-cultural application:** Efficacy beliefs regulate functioning in both individualistic and collectivistic social systems (Earley, 1993, 1994; Matsui & Onglatco, 1992). Cultural context moderates whether individually-oriented or group-oriented efficacy boosts produce better effects ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- **Innovation and entrepreneurship:** A resilient sense of efficacy is necessary to persist through the numerous rejections and setbacks that innovation requires. Entrepreneurial efficacy predicts venture success beyond personality traits (Baum, 1994) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- **Academic functioning:** [[academic-self-efficacy]] is a better predictor of intellectual performance than skills alone (Collins, 1982). [[teacher-efficacy]] partly determines how teachers structure classrooms and affects student achievement trajectories. Collective school efficacy predicts school-level achievement after controlling for SES and prior achievement (Bandura, 1993) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch6-cognitive-functioning]])
- **Organizational functioning:** Self-efficacy in organizational decision making is directly linked to quality of analytic thinking and organizational productivity. Managers who construe ability as acquirable skill maintain resilient efficacy; those who view it as inherent aptitude show deteriorating performance (Wood & Bandura, 1989b) ([[summary-3-11-se-ch10-organizational-functioning]])
- **Political efficacy:** [[political-efficacy]] -- beliefs about influencing the political system through political action -- determines the form and level of political participation. Participatory dilemmas in collective action connect to [[commons-dilemma]] ([[summary-3-11-se-ch11-collective-efficacy]])

## Open Questions
- How does self-efficacy interact with [[bounded-rationality]]? Do efficacy beliefs help or hinder rational decision-making?
- What is the boundary between adaptive optimistic self-efficacy and maladaptive [[overconfidence-in-calibration]]? Is the distinction purely domain-dependent? Ch.10 provides evidence that exaggerated efficacy can lead to [[escalation-of-commitment]] in organizational decision making (Whyte, Saks, & Hook), suggesting a real boundary condition.
- How does [[collective-efficacy]] emerge from individual efficacy beliefs? What are the coordinative and interactive dynamics? Ch.11 provides partial answers: it is an emergent group-level property shaped by the mix of competencies, coordination quality, leadership, and whether members interact facilitatively or underminingly.
- How do the relative weights of the four [[sources-of-self-efficacy]] shift across different domains (academic, athletic, social, health)?
- How does Bandura's acquirable skill vs. inherent aptitude distinction relate to Dweck's growth vs. fixed mindset framework?
- How does the threshold model of intrinsic interest (moderate efficacy optimal, from Ch.6) interact with [[deliberate-practice]] frameworks that emphasize continued challenge at the edge of capability?
