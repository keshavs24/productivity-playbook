# SUCCESs Framework

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The SUCCESs framework is a six-principle checklist for designing ideas that stick — that are understood, remembered, and have a lasting impact on opinions or behavior. Developed by [[heath-brothers|Chip Heath and Dan Heath]] in [[made-to-stick]], the framework was derived from studying hundreds of naturally sticky ideas (urban legends, proverbs, conspiracy theories, effective campaigns) and distilling the common traits that made them successful.

The six principles form the acronym **SUCCESs**:

1. **Simple** — Find the essential core of the idea and express it compactly. Not "dumbing down" but forced prioritization. The ideal is a proverb: both compact and profound. See [[commanders-intent]].
2. **Unexpected** — Break people's existing schemas to grab attention (surprise), then sustain interest by opening knowledge gaps (curiosity). See [[schema-breaking]], [[gap-theory-of-curiosity]].
3. **Concrete** — Express ideas in terms of human actions and sensory information. Concrete images cling to memory like Velcro hooks; abstract language slides off. See [[concreteness-principle]], [[abstract-vs-concrete-information]].
4. **Credible** — Help ideas carry their own credentials so people can test them for themselves, even without the authority of an expert or celebrity endorser.
5. **Emotional** — Make people *feel* something. People care about individuals, not abstractions. The statistic "37 grams of saturated fat" elicits nothing; a table full of greasy food elicits disgust. See [[pathos]].
6. **Stories** — Drive people to act by telling stories that function as mental flight simulators, preparing audiences to respond more quickly and effectively in real situations.

## Key Findings

- **No formula guarantees stickiness.** The six principles are like the attributes of a great basketball player (height, speed, agility) — having all of them doesn't guarantee greatness, and you don't need all of them to be great. But having more increases likelihood of success.
- **The principles are learnable.** Israeli researchers found that 89% of award-winning ads fit six basic creative templates (most relating to unexpectedness), while only 2% of unsuccessful ads fit those templates. Novices trained on templates for two hours produced ads rated 50% more creative.
- **The [[curse-of-knowledge]] is the primary obstacle.** Once you know something, you find it hard to imagine not knowing it. This makes it systematically difficult to design simple, concrete, unexpected messages. The six principles serve as tools to fight this villain.
- **The framework is complementary to the [[three-modes-of-persuasion]].** Credibility maps roughly to [[ethos]], Emotional maps to [[pathos]], and the remaining principles (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Stories) collectively serve [[logos]] by making arguments understandable and compelling. The SUCCESs framework is more granular and applied.

## Mechanisms

The framework works by targeting different aspects of how people process and remember information:

- **Simple** reduces cognitive load and provides a decision filter ([[anchoring-and-adjustment]] — the core message anchors subsequent interpretation)
- **Unexpected** triggers the surprise response (schema violation), which activates focused attention and deeper processing
- **Concrete** activates multiple memory systems simultaneously (visual, auditory, kinesthetic, emotional) — the Velcro theory of memory
- **Credible** satisfies the audience's need for verification, bypassing skepticism
- **Emotional** activates the affective system, which drives caring and action (people donate more to a single identified victim than to statistical abstractions)
- **Stories** provide mental simulation, allowing audiences to rehearse responses before encountering real situations

## Applications

- **Message design:** Use the checklist to audit any important communication — which principles does it hit? Which does it miss?
- **Idea clinics:** The Heaths provide before-and-after examples (sun exposure, foreign aid, fundraising, oral rehydration therapy) showing how the same content can be restructured to hit more principles
- **Teaching:** The principles apply to lesson design — the best teachers intuitively use unexpectedness (Nora Ephron's journalism teacher), concreteness (Asian math with tiles), and stories
- **Strategy communication:** Commander's Intent and Southwest's "THE low-fare airline" show how the Simple principle guides organizational behavior at scale

## Open Questions

- How does the SUCCESs framework interact with the Elaboration Likelihood Model? Simple/Concrete messages might work via the peripheral route, while Unexpected/Credible messages might engage the central route.
- The framework was derived primarily from American examples. How well do the six principles transfer across cultures? Proverbs appear universal, but the specific schemas that must be violated may be culturally specific.
- What is the relationship between stickiness and truth? The Heaths acknowledge that false ideas (Halloween candy tampering, Kidney Heist) can be stickier than true ones. The framework is morally neutral — like Aristotle's rhetoric.
