# Negotiator Overconfidence

**Phase(s):** 2, 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Negotiator overconfidence is the systematic tendency for negotiators to overestimate the likelihood that their positions will prevail, that their offers will be accepted, and that their own value or contribution justifies the outcomes they seek. This bias leads negotiators to set unrealistic aspirations, reject reasonable deals, and miss agreements that fall within the positive bargaining zone.

## Key Findings

- **Bazerman and Neale (1982):** In final-offer arbitration studies where the objective probability was 50%, the average negotiator estimated a 68% probability that their own offer would be accepted. This inflated confidence leads to more aggressive positions and more frequent impasse. ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch12]])
- **Neale and Bazerman (1985):** "Appropriately confident" negotiators -- those with calibrated confidence -- exhibited more concessionary behavior, reached more agreements, and achieved better outcomes than overly confident negotiators. Moderate uncertainty promotes flexibility.
- **Matt Harrington case:** A top MLB draft prospect who refused every offer for 7 consecutive years, each year dropping in draft position. His agent's overconfident demand of $4.95M destroyed a career that could have earned millions. Overconfidence about one's BATNA when that BATNA is objectively terrible is catastrophic.
- Overconfidence is most dangerous when knowledge is limited. As [[overconfidence-in-calibration]] research shows, "When in doubt, be overconfident" is the intuitive but irrational cognitive rule most people follow.

## Mechanisms

Negotiator overconfidence draws on several underlying biases:
1. **[[overconfidence-in-calibration]]:** General tendency to overestimate the precision of one's knowledge
2. **[[self-serving-bias-in-decisions]]:** People generate arguments favoring their own position more easily than arguments against it
3. **[[illusion-of-validity]]:** Confidence in predictions exceeds their accuracy, especially in ambiguous situations
4. **Selective information processing:** Negotiators attend more to information supporting their position (Babcock, Loewenstein, Issacharoff, & Camerer, 1995)

## Applications

- **Pre-negotiation assessment:** Before negotiating, seek objective value assessments from neutral third parties. Your intuitive assessment is likely biased upward.
- **Calibration exercise:** Explicitly estimate the probability that you will get what you want. Then consider: what would a neutral observer say?
- **BATNA analysis:** Rigorously assess your BATNA before entering negotiation. Overconfident negotiators often overestimate the value of their alternatives, leading them to reject deals they should accept.
- **Phase 5 negotiation training:** Practice making and evaluating offers in simulations with feedback to calibrate confidence.

## Open Questions
- Does repeated negotiation experience reduce overconfidence, or do successful negotiators become even more overconfident due to survivorship bias?
- How does negotiator overconfidence interact with power asymmetry? Are negotiators with weaker BATNAs more or less prone to overconfidence?
- Can structured pre-negotiation protocols (checklists, devil's advocate exercises) reliably reduce overconfidence?
