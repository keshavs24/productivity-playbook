# Informational vs. Controlling (Functional Significance)

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Within [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]], every external event relevant to behavior has a **functional significance** -- a psychological meaning to the person experiencing it. This functional significance has three possible aspects:

1. **Informational:** The event conveys information about the person's self-determined competence, supports an internal perceived locus of causality (I-PLOC), and enhances [[intrinsic-motivation]]
2. **Controlling:** The event pressures the person to think, feel, or behave in particular ways, shifts toward an external perceived locus of causality (E-PLOC), and undermines intrinsic motivation
3. **Amotivating:** The event signals incompetence or lack of value, undermining both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation and producing [[amotivation]]

The same objective event (e.g., a monetary bonus, a deadline, positive feedback) can have different functional significance depending on: how it is administered, the interpersonal context, the person's interpretation, and what it is contingent upon.

This is CET Proposition III: the relative salience of the informational, controlling, and amotivating aspects determines the event's impact on intrinsic motivation.

## Key Findings

- **Ryan (1982):** Positive feedback delivered informationally ("You did really well on that") enhanced intrinsic motivation. The same positive feedback delivered controllingly ("You did just as you should") undermined it -- regardless of self- vs. experimenter delivery
- **Ryan, Mims, & Koestner (1983):** Performance-contingent rewards administered in an autonomy-supportive manner produced higher intrinsic motivation than a neutral control. The same rewards in a controlling manner undermined intrinsic motivation
- **Kast & Connor (1988):** Controlling wording in positive feedback neutralized or reversed its competence-enhancing effects
- **Koestner, Ryan, Bernieri, & Holt (1984):** Behavioral limits set with autonomy-supportive language maintained intrinsic motivation; the same limits set controllingly undermined it
- **Enzle & Anderson (1993):** Surveillance perceived as evaluative/controlling undermined intrinsic motivation; surveillance perceived as noncontrolling (curious observer) did not
- **Deci et al. (1999) meta-analysis:** Unexpected tangible rewards (informational) showed d=0.01 (no undermining); expected contingent rewards (controlling) showed d=-0.36 (significant undermining)

## Mechanisms

1. **PLOC modulation:** Informational events maintain or strengthen I-PLOC; controlling events shift to E-PLOC
2. **Need satisfaction vs. thwarting:** Informational events satisfy [[autonomy-need]] and [[competence-need]]; controlling events thwart autonomy; amotivating events thwart competence
3. **Context dependence:** The interpersonal climate (autonomy-supportive vs. controlling) acts as a lens that colors the interpretation of specific events
4. **Intent perception:** People read the perceived intentions of the motivator -- if the intent appears controlling, the event is experienced as controlling (Wild, Enzle, & Hawkins, 1992)

## Applications

- **Feedback delivery:** The most critical application. The same feedback content can enhance or undermine motivation depending on style:
  - Informational: "Here's what you did well and here's an area to work on" (acknowledges competence, provides direction)
  - Controlling: "You did what you should have" or "You need to do better" (implies external standards, pressure)
- **Reward design:** Rewards can be made more informational by: making them unexpected, tying them to acknowledgment rather than control, administering them in autonomy-supportive contexts
- **Limit setting:** Limits and rules can be set informationally (with rationale, acknowledgment of feelings, minimal controlling language) rather than controllingly (demands, threats, "you must")
- **Goal setting:** Goals can be autonomy-supportive (self-endorsed, rationale-provided) or controlling (externally imposed, pressuring). The same goal structure has different motivational effects depending on delivery
