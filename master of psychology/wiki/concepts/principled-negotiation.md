# Principled Negotiation

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Principled negotiation (also called *negotiation on the merits*) is a method of negotiation developed at the Harvard Negotiation Project by [[fisher-ury|Roger Fisher and William Ury]]. It is explicitly designed to produce wise outcomes efficiently and amicably, in contrast to positional bargaining (whether hard or soft). The method rests on four fundamental principles:

1. **[[separate-people-from-problem|Separate the people from the problem]]** — Deal with relationship issues directly; don't try to solve them with substantive concessions
2. **[[positions-vs-interests|Focus on interests, not positions]]** — Look behind stated positions to identify the underlying needs, desires, concerns, and fears
3. **[[invent-options-for-mutual-gain|Invent options for mutual gain]]** — Generate a wide range of possibilities before deciding; overcome [[fixed-pie-bias]]
4. **[[objective-criteria|Insist on using objective criteria]]** — Base outcomes on fair standards and fair procedures independent of either side's will

## Key Findings

- Principled negotiation is an **all-purpose strategy.** Unlike almost all other strategies, if the other side learns this one, it does not become more difficult to use; it becomes easier. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch1-positions]])
- The method applies across contexts: two parties or many, one issue or several, prescribed ritual or impromptu, experienced or inexperienced negotiator, hard or friendly. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch1-positions]])
- The method applies throughout three stages — **analysis** (diagnosing), **planning** (generating ideas), and **discussion** (communicating back and forth). The same four elements are the best subjects to discuss at each stage. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch1-positions]])
- When the other side won't play, two additional techniques extend the method: [[negotiation-jujitsu]] (deflecting attacks onto the problem) and the [[one-text-procedure]] (a third party mediates through iterative drafts). ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch7-wont-play]])
- When the other side uses dirty tricks, the same four principles are applied to the negotiation *procedure* itself — you negotiate about the rules of the game using principled negotiation. ([[summary-5-16-gty-ch8-dirty-tricks]])

## Mechanisms

Principled negotiation works by addressing four distinct failure modes of positional bargaining:

1. **The people problem:** Emotions become entangled with the objective merits of the problem. Egos become identified with positions. By separating relationship issues from substance, negotiators can deal with each set on its own legitimate merits. ([[self-serving-bias-in-negotiation]], [[reactive-devaluation]])
2. **The positions trap:** Stated positions often obscure what people actually want. Compromising between positions does not necessarily produce an agreement that meets underlying human needs. The Camp David example proves that apparently incompatible positions can mask compatible interests. ([[fixed-pie-bias]])
3. **The creativity deficit:** Trying to decide in the presence of an adversary narrows your vision. Having a lot at stake inhibits creativity. Setting aside a designated time to brainstorm (separating inventing from deciding) overcomes this. ([[value-creation-in-negotiation]])
4. **The power problem:** Without objective criteria, negotiation becomes a contest of will — whoever is more stubborn wins, regardless of the merits. Objective criteria transform the dynamic from "who will give in?" to "what does fairness/precedent/market value suggest?" ([[anchoring-and-adjustment]])

## Applications

- **Labor-management negotiations:** Union-management disputes, salary negotiations, contract renegotiations
- **International diplomacy:** Camp David (Egypt-Israel), Law of the Sea Conference, arms control
- **Business deals:** Mergers, acquisitions, vendor contracts, joint ventures
- **Family disputes:** Divorce settlements, inheritance, household decisions
- **Everyday life:** Landlord-tenant disputes (the Jones Realty case), insurance claims (the "It's Company Policy" case), consumer purchases

## Negotiation Genius Extensions

[[malhotra|Malhotra]] and [[bazerman|Bazerman]] in [[negotiation-genius|Negotiation Genius]] build directly on principled negotiation while extending it in several dimensions:

- **Tactical specificity for value claiming:** While principled negotiation focuses on interests and creativity, Negotiation Genius provides detailed tactical guidance for anchoring, first offers, responding to anchors, haggling, and managing concessions — the competitive side of negotiation that Getting to Yes deliberately underemphasizes. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **[[investigative-negotiation]] as the operational method for Principle 2:** Fisher and Ury's "focus on interests, not positions" is extended into a systematic seven-principle framework for *discovering* interests when the other side is reticent or unwilling to share. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch3-investigative]])
- **Strategies for power asymmetry:** Where Fisher and Ury say "no method can guarantee success if all the leverage lies on the other side," Malhotra and Bazerman provide specific strategies for [[negotiating-from-weakness]]: concealing weakness, leveraging the other side's weakness, building coalitions, leveraging extreme weakness, and attacking the source of power. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])
- **Psychological influence as a complement:** The eight [[influence-strategies-in-negotiation]] provide tools for "selling" your proposals alongside the principled approach of structuring them well. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch7-influence]])

## Open Questions
- How does principled negotiation perform against *strategic* positional bargainers who understand the method and deliberately exploit its openness? (Fisher/Ury assert it performs well; Malhotra/Bazerman provide more specific defensive strategies)
- To what extent do cultural differences affect the applicability of the four principles?
- How does principled negotiation interact with power asymmetries beyond the BATNA analysis? (Malhotra and Bazerman address this directly in Ch. 11 with strategies for negotiating from weakness)
- Is there a tension between Fisher/Ury's openness and Malhotra/Bazerman's advice to conceal weak BATNAs? How should a negotiator reconcile these?
