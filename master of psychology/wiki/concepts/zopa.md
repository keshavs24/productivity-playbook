# ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA) is the range between the seller's [[reservation-value]] and the buyer's reservation value within which a mutually acceptable deal can be struck. Any point inside the ZOPA represents a possible agreement; any point outside it will be rejected by at least one party. The ZOPA exists when the buyer's maximum willingness to pay exceeds the seller's minimum willingness to accept; when the reverse is true, there is no ZOPA and no deal should be made.

## Key Findings

- **Hamilton Real Estate case:** The seller's RV was $42.65M and the buyer's estimated RV was $48M, yielding an estimated ZOPA of $5.35M. But the buyer's actual RV was $60M (due to commercial development plans the seller failed to discover), making the true ZOPA $17.35M — the seller left millions on the table by not investigating. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **ZOPA estimation is inherently uncertain:** You can rarely know the other side's exact reservation value. The ZOPA is always an *estimate* that must be revised as you gather information during the negotiation. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **First offers should fall outside the ZOPA:** If your first offer is already inside the ZOPA, you have surrendered value from the start. The goal is to force the other party to negotiate *into* the ZOPA. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **When both BATNAs are weak, the ZOPA is large:** This means a lot of value is at stake. The party that better leverages claiming strategies captures more of this large ZOPA. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch11-weakness]])

## Mechanisms

The ZOPA is defined by two reservation values, each derived from a [[batna]]:

1. **Seller's RV** = the value of the seller's best alternative (BATNA) — the minimum the seller will accept
2. **Buyer's RV** = the value of the buyer's best alternative (BATNA) — the maximum the buyer will pay
3. **ZOPA** = Buyer's RV - Seller's RV (if positive, a deal is possible; if negative, no ZOPA exists)

The ZOPA is not fixed — it can change during negotiation as new information is revealed, as BATNAs improve or deteriorate, or as new issues are added to the negotiation.

## Applications

- **Pre-negotiation preparation:** Evaluating the ZOPA is Step 5 of Malhotra and Bazerman's five-step preparation framework. It tells you whether a deal is possible and approximately how much value is at stake.
- **Multi-issue negotiations:** In multi-issue negotiations, each issue may have its own ZOPA. Value creation occurs by finding trades across issues where the ZOPAs overlap differently for each party.
- **Portfolio strategy:** When your BATNA is weak across multiple negotiations, consider the ZOPA across your entire portfolio — being willing to lose some deals (no ZOPA) can strengthen your position in others.
