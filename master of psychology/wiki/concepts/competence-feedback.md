# Competence Feedback

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Within [[cognitive-evaluation-theory]], competence feedback refers to information that conveys to a person how effectively they are performing an activity. Positive competence feedback (informational feedback that signals mastery or progress) enhances [[intrinsic-motivation]] by satisfying the basic psychological [[competence-need|need for competence]]. Negative competence feedback (information signaling failure or incompetence) diminishes intrinsic motivation.

Critically, the motivational effects of feedback depend on its **[[informational-vs-controlling|functional significance]]**: positive feedback enhances intrinsic motivation only when experienced as informational (acknowledging competence) rather than controlling (evaluative, pressuring). Both **autonomy** and **competence** satisfaction must be present for intrinsic motivation to be enhanced.

## Key Findings

### Positive Feedback
- **Deci (1971, 1972b); Deci, Cascio, & Krusell (1975):** Positive feedback enhanced free-choice persistence -- the earliest demonstrations
- **Deci et al. (1999) meta-analysis:** Verbal rewards (positive feedback) significantly enhanced intrinsic motivation overall (d=0.33). College students: d=0.43. Children: d=0.11 (nonsignificant -- praise may be experienced as controlling by children)
- **Henderlong & Lepper (2002):** Comprehensive review concluded informational praise facilitates intrinsic motivation; evaluative/controlling praise does not
- **Hagger, Koch, & Chatzisarantis (2015):** Positive, competence-enhancing feedback on a puzzle task confirmed enhancement of intrinsic motivation via behavioral free-choice measure

### Feedback Style Matters
- **Ryan (1982):** Positive feedback delivered informationally enhanced intrinsic motivation; the same content delivered controllingly ("you did just as you should") undermined it
- **Kast & Connor (1988):** Controlling wording undermined positive feedback effects
- **Smith (1975):** Positive feedback in an evaluative context undermined intrinsic motivation -- it was the evaluation, not the content, that was controlling
- **Hewett & Conway (2015):** Making positive feedback very salient caused it to be experienced as controlling, decreasing intrinsic motivation

### Two Types of Positive Feedback
1. **Task-inherent feedback:** The activity itself provides feedback (crossword letters fit, climbing progress is visible). Naturally informational, not controlling
2. **Other-mediated feedback:** Delivered by another person. Can be informational or controlling depending on style, intent, and context

### Negative Feedback
- **Deci & Cascio (1972); Karniol & Ross (1977); Vallerand & Reid (1984):** Negative feedback decreases intrinsic motivation relative to positive and no-feedback conditions
- **Abramson, Seligman, & Teasdale (1978):** Negative feedback implying incompetence can produce amotivation (learned helplessness)
- **Carpentier & Mageau (2013):** Negative feedback delivered autonomy-supportively and constructively can enhance rather than diminish motivation
- **Mouratidis et al. (2010):** Coaches' perceived autonomy-supportive vs. controlling styles in giving constructive feedback predicted different motivational outcomes
- A modest amount of negative feedback on optimally challenging activities can actually serve as challenge and motivation

### Feedback and Optimal Challenge
- **Danner & Lonky (1981):** Children naturally chose tasks one step ahead of their ability -- optimal challenge where competence feedback is most meaningful
- Positive feedback enhances intrinsic motivation for optimally challenging tasks but not for tasks that are too easy (Danner & Lonky, 1981)
- Feedback on easy tasks may be experienced as hollow or controlling

## Mechanisms

1. **Competence satisfaction:** Positive feedback satisfies the need for competence, energizing continued engagement
2. **Vitality and energy:** Positive feedback radiates to feelings of vitality and enhanced concentration (Mouratidis et al., 2008; Grouzet et al., 2004)
3. **Mediating role:** Perceived competence mediates the relationship between positive feedback and intrinsic motivation (Grouzet et al., 2004; Vallerand & Reid, 1984; Vansteenkiste & Deci, 2003)
4. **Autonomy requirement:** Competence satisfaction alone is insufficient -- the person must also feel autonomous (I-PLOC). Positive feedback in controlling contexts does not enhance intrinsic motivation

## Applications

- **Teaching:** Provide specific, informational feedback about what the student did well and what to work on. Avoid generic praise ("good job") which can feel hollow or controlling. Focus on effort and process, not just outcomes
- **Parenting:** Acknowledge children's accomplishments without converting feedback into evaluation or pressure. Avoid "you did what you should" language
- **Management:** Performance reviews should be informational (here is what you're doing well, here is where to grow) not controlling (you must improve or face consequences)
- **Game design:** Task-inherent feedback (progress bars, level completion signals, puzzle fit) sustains intrinsic motivation better than external evaluative feedback
