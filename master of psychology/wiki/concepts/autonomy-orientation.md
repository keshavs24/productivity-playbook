# Autonomy Orientation

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The autonomy orientation is one of three general causality orientations in [[causality-orientations-theory]]. It describes the degree to which people orient toward their environments by treating them as sources of relevant information, taking interest in both external events and accompanying inner experiences, and experiencing choice with respect to their actions. When autonomy-oriented, people are "interest-taking" -- finding or creating opportunities for engagement with what they find interesting and important. They tend to use [[identified-regulation]], [[integrated-regulation]], and [[intrinsic-motivation]] as their predominant regulatory styles.

## Key Findings

- Positively correlated with **self-esteem, ego development, self-actualization** (Deci & Ryan, 1985a)
- Associated with **learning goals** rather than performance goals (Koestner & Zuckerman, 1994)
- Related to **informational identity style** -- actively constructing identity through experimentation (Soenens et al., 2005)
- **Buffers against reward undermining:** People high in autonomy orientation did not show significant decrement in intrinsic motivation following contingent rewards (Hagger & Chatzisarantis, 2011)
- Predicts **less self-serving bias, less self-handicapping, less avoidant coping** (Knee & Zuckerman, 1996, 1998)
- Predicts **greater openness and honesty** in relationships, less defensiveness, more complex apologies (Hodgins et al., 1996, 2003)
- Mothers of **securely attached** infants score higher on autonomy orientation (Bridges et al., 1983)
- Predicts **prosocial behavior** (Gagne, 2003) and tendency to **support others' autonomy** (Deci & Ryan, 1985a)
- People maintain **stable positive affect** regardless of social comparison outcomes (Neighbors & Knee, 2003)
- After disturbing experiences, people high in autonomy show **better coping, ownership of feelings, lower energy costs** (Weinstein & Hodgins, 2009)
- Associated with **greater personality integration** -- higher congruence among attitudes, behaviors, feelings, and peer perceptions (Koestner, Bernieri, & Zuckerman, 1992)

### Distinction: Reflective vs. Reactive Autonomy

A critical distinction (Koestner & Losier, 1996):
- **Reflective autonomy** (SDT sense) = volition, sense of choice. Measured by GCOS. Compatible with positive relationships, credible authority, and relatedness.
- **Reactive autonomy** = independence, resistance. Measured by ACL. Associated with negative interactions with authorities, rejection of credible advice, incompatible with positive relationships.

## Mechanisms

The autonomy orientation operates through:
1. **Informational interpretation** -- construing events as informational rather than controlling
2. **Interest-taking** -- actively seeking what is interesting and important
3. **Self-concordance** -- pursuing goals aligned with authentic values
4. **Openness to experience** -- non-defensive engagement with both positive and negative aspects of self and world

## Applications

- **Leadership:** Autonomy-oriented leaders tend to create autonomy-supportive environments
- **Resilience:** High autonomy orientation provides natural resilience against controlling contexts
- **Relationships:** Autonomy orientation predicts relationship quality through openness and non-defensiveness
- **Self-development:** Cultivating autonomy orientation through sustained autonomy-supportive environments
