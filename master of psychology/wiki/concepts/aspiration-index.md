# Aspiration Index

**Phase(s):** 3
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

The Aspiration Index (AI) is the primary psychometric instrument developed by Kasser & Ryan (1993, 1996) to measure [[goal-contents-theory|Goal Contents Theory (GCT)]] constructs. It assesses the [[relative-aspirations|relative importance]] individuals place on different categories of life goals, as well as their perceived likelihood of attaining them and their current level of attainment.

The AI asks participants to rate the importance of various life goals or aspirations and their beliefs about attaining them, then derives a **relative importance** score representing how much each type of goal matters relative to others. This relative scoring is critical: it is the ratio of [[intrinsic-aspirations|intrinsic]] to [[extrinsic-aspirations|extrinsic]] aspiration importance, not the absolute level of either, that most strongly predicts well-being outcomes.

## Key Findings

- **Original AI (Kasser & Ryan, 1993):** Three intrinsic aspirations (personal growth, relationships, community involvement) measured relative to extrinsic aspiration for financial success. Across three studies, higher relative intrinsic aspirations predicted wellness; higher relative financial success aspiration predicted lower self-actualization, lower vitality, more depression, and more anxiety.
- **Expanded AI (Kasser & Ryan, 1996):** Added two extrinsic aspirations -- attractiveness (image) and social recognition (fame) -- and health as an intrinsic aspiration. Higher-order factor analyses confirmed two clear overarching factors: intrinsic (personal growth, relationships, community contributions) and extrinsic (wealth, image, fame).
- **15-nation circumplex study (Grouzet et al., 2005):** The intrinsic-extrinsic axis was reliable across cultures. A circumplex model emerged with intrinsic and extrinsic aspirations at opposite poles, plus additional dimensions (hedonism, conformity, spirituality, self-focus vs. self-transcendence). The intrinsic-extrinsic distinction proved universal.
- The AI has been used in studies across at least 15 countries spanning diverse economic and cultural contexts, consistently yielding the predicted pattern: relative intrinsic aspiration importance predicts wellness; relative extrinsic aspiration importance predicts ill-being.

## Mechanisms

The AI captures individual differences in life goal prioritization. Its predictive power derives from:
1. **Relative scoring:** The *ratio* of intrinsic to extrinsic importance is more predictive than absolute levels of either, because most people endorse both types of goals to some degree. The question is which type takes priority.
2. **Need satisfaction mediation:** The link from AI scores to well-being is mediated by basic need satisfaction -- intrinsic aspirations are more closely associated with need-satisfying activities and experiences.
3. **Developmental sensitivity:** AI scores reflect developmental history (need-supportive vs. need-thwarting environments) and are responsive to intervention (mindfulness training, aspiration reflection exercises).

## Applications

- **Research instrument:** Used in hundreds of studies worldwide to assess the well-being implications of goal content
- **Clinical and coaching tool:** Can help individuals examine their life goal priorities and identify whether extrinsic aspirations have displaced intrinsic ones
- **Intervention assessment:** Pre/post AI scores can evaluate whether interventions (e.g., mindfulness training, value clarification exercises) shift aspiration profiles toward more intrinsic orientations
