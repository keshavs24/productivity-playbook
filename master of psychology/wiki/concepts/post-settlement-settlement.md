# Post-Settlement Settlement

**Phase(s):** 2, 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

A post-settlement settlement (PSS) is the practice of revisiting a completed agreement to search for Pareto-superior alternatives -- agreements that make at least one party better off without making anyone worse off. The original signed agreement serves as a safety net: both parties can veto any proposed change and revert to the original terms. This eliminates risk and encourages exploration of improvements that might not have been discoverable during the initial negotiation.

## Key Findings

- **Raiffa (1985):** Proposed that after reaching a mutually acceptable agreement, negotiators should employ a third party to search for a Pareto-superior agreement. Either side retains the right to veto any new proposal, making the process risk-free. ([[summary-2-08-bazerman-ch11]])
- **Bazerman, Russ, and Yakura (1987):** Extended Raiffa's insight, arguing that PSS should be a standard final step in any important negotiation. The PSS process does not require a third party -- the negotiators themselves can initiate it.
- **Malhotra and Bazerman (2007):** Suggest opening the PSS conversation with: "Congratulations! I think our hard work has really paid off in a great deal... What if we spent a few more minutes talking about potential improvements to the deal that would make both of us better off?"
- Most negotiators are reluctant to reopen completed negotiations -- but the signed agreement as a safety net makes PSS low-risk and potentially high-reward.

## Mechanisms

PSS works because:
1. **Trust has been established:** The signed agreement confirms mutual goodwill and ability to cooperate
2. **Pressure is reduced:** With a deal already in place, parties can explore creatively without fear of losing everything
3. **New information emerges:** The negotiation process itself reveals information about preferences that was not available at the start
4. **Anchoring shifts:** The existing agreement serves as a new (often more constructive) reference point

## Applications

- **After any complex deal:** Ask whether there are improvements that could benefit both sides
- **Contract renegotiation:** Frame renegotiation as a PSS rather than a reopening of disputes
- **Organizational restructuring:** When teams have reached a workable (but suboptimal) arrangement, propose PSS-style optimization
- **Phase 5 negotiation practice:** Build PSS into standard negotiation protocol as the final step
