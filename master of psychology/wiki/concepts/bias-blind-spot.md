# Bias Blind Spot

**Phase(s):** 1
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

The bias blind spot is the tendency to **believe that other people are more susceptible to cognitive and attributional biases than we ourselves are**. We see biases in others but not in ourselves.

## Key Findings

- Pronin et al. (2002): Participants rated themselves as only "somewhat" susceptible to self-serving attributions and victim blaming, but rated the "average American" as much more susceptible ([[summary-1-03-social-psychology-ch4]])
- This is itself a self-serving belief — ironically demonstrating the very bias it denies
- Knowing about biases does not automatically protect against them

## Applications

- **Self-improvement:** The first step in debiasing is recognizing that you are not exempt. The bias blind spot is the barrier to all other bias correction.
- **Negotiation:** Each side assumes the other is more biased than they are — creating mutual distrust and difficulty finding common ground.
