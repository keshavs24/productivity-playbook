# Reservation Value

**Phase(s):** 5
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

A reservation value (RV), also called the walk-away price or indifference point, is the precise point at which a negotiator is indifferent between accepting the deal and pursuing their [[batna]]. For a seller, the RV is the lowest offer they would accept; for a buyer, the RV is the highest price they would pay. The reservation value is *derived from* the BATNA — it is the BATNA translated into the terms of the current negotiation.

## Key Findings

- **Reservation value is distinct from BATNA:** The BATNA is the *course of action* you will pursue if no deal is reached; the reservation value is the *quantified threshold* below (or above) which you should walk away. In the Hamilton case, the seller's BATNA was to finalize a deal with Quincy Developments; the reservation value was $42.65M (the estimated value of that deal after further negotiation). ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **Common mistake — confusing RV with other anchors:** People often confuse their reservation value with what they *think is fair*, what they *originally paid*, or what they *hope to achieve*. These are not the reservation value. Your RV is the *reality* you face if you reach no deal in the current negotiation. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])
- **Package reservation value (PRV):** In multi-issue negotiations, setting per-issue reservation values is limiting. A PRV provides a single threshold for the total package, enabling greater flexibility and better trades across issues. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch2-creating-value]])
- **Risk tolerance affects the exact RV:** Within a range defined by the BATNA, risk-averse negotiators lean toward the more conservative end, while risk-tolerant negotiators lean toward the aggressive end. ([[summary-5-17-ng-ch1-claiming-value]])

## Mechanisms

1. **Assessment:** Start by identifying your BATNA, then translate it into the terms of the current negotiation to get your RV.
2. **Both sides have an RV:** The difference between the two RVs defines the [[zopa]]. If seller's RV < buyer's RV, a deal is possible.
3. **RV provides the floor (seller) or ceiling (buyer):** You should never accept an offer worse than your RV, and you should always accept an offer better than it (assuming no further negotiation is possible).
4. **Satisfaction management:** After negotiation, shift your reference point from your target price (aspiration) to your RV — this increases post-negotiation satisfaction.

## Applications

- **Salary negotiation:** Your RV is the value of your best alternative offer (or status quo). Calculate it before entering talks.
- **Business deals:** Quantify your BATNA to determine the worst deal you should accept. This prevents both over-accepting and under-accepting.
- **Multi-issue deals:** Calculate a PRV rather than per-issue limits. This preserves flexibility for logrolling.
