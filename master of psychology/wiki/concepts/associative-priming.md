# Associative Priming

**Phase(s):** 6 — Advanced Persuasion
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Associative priming is the process by which exposure to one concept activates closely linked concepts in memory, making them more cognitively accessible and more likely to influence subsequent perception, judgment, and behavior. In the context of [[pre-suasion]], associative priming is the core mechanism through which openers work: by channeling attention to a selected concept, a communicator readies associated concepts for action while simultaneously suppressing unassociated concepts.

Cialdini's formulation emphasizes that all mental activity arises from patterns of associations — "just as amino acids can be called the building blocks of life, associations can be called the building blocks of thought." Language is primarily a mechanism of *influence* (not conveyance) because its main function is to direct attention to selected sectors of reality, where pre-existing associations take over.

## Key Findings

- **Violence-word priming**: Arranging scrambled sentences containing aggression-related words increased subsequent shock intensity by 48% in an unrelated task
- **Achievement-word priming**: Exposure to words like *win, attain, succeed, master* increases task performance and more than doubles willingness to keep working
- **Achievement-image priming**: Fund-raisers given a photo of a runner winning a race raised 60% more money than those given plain paper
- **Metaphorical priming**: Framing crime as a "beast" (vs. "virus") shifted solution preferences (enforcement vs. societal reform) — the single-word change produced more than double the effect of gender or party affiliation
- **Physical metaphor priming**: Heavy clipboards increase perceived seriousness of job candidates; warm drinks increase interpersonal warmth and cooperation — without a word spoken
- **Pavlovian manufacturing**: Pairing brands with pleasant images 5-6 times creates favorable associations that persist for weeks; subliminal happy faces increase consumption and willingness to pay 3x more
- **Littering gradient study**: Pre-suasive effectiveness declines linearly with associative distance — a message about littering reduced littering most (10%), recycling (close) nearly as much (15%), turning off lights (moderate) somewhat (18%), voting (far) weakly (22%), an art museum (unrelated) produced the most littering (25%)

## Mechanisms

The mechanism is **cognitive accessibility** — a dual process:
1. **Activation**: When an opener concept receives attention, closely associated secondary concepts become more accessible in consciousness, elevating their capacity to color perceptions, orient thinking, affect motivations, and change behavior
2. **Suppression**: Simultaneously, concepts *not* linked to the opener become *less* accessible, making them less likely to influence behavior

This explains why pre-suasive effects are so powerful: they don't just boost one thing — they simultaneously suppress alternatives. Concepts don't just fire when *ready*; they fire when *readied*.

## Applications

- **Organizational language**: SSM Health replaced violent language (bullet points, targets, beat) with achievement language (information points, goals, outdistance) — supported by research showing violent word exposure increases aggressive behavior
- **Advertising**: Match background cues (images, music, colors) to the associations you want to activate; avoid cues that activate competing associations
- **Negotiation**: Use metaphors and language that channel associations toward your preferred framing
- **Self-influence**: Surround yourself with cues (images, words, objects) associated with desired states; use [[if-then-plans]] to install custom cue-action associations
- **Personal names**: Leverage implicit egoism (Coca-Cola's "Share a Coke" campaign; shared birthdays, initials)

## Open Questions

- How durable are priming effects? Some studies show decay within minutes; Cialdini's conditioning studies show effects persisting for weeks. The duration likely depends on the strength and repetition of the association.
- The "replication crisis" in social priming research (e.g., Bargh's elderly walking study) raises questions about the reliability of some findings, though Cialdini emphasizes that the core mechanism (cognitive accessibility) is well-established
