# Story as Inspiration

**Phase(s):** 4
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Story as inspiration is the second of the two powers of narrative (alongside [[story-as-simulation]]). While simulation provides knowledge about how to act, inspiration provides the motivation to act. Inspirational stories energize and motivate people by tapping into universal emotional patterns. The [[heath-brothers]] identify three fundamental plot types that account for over 80% of inspirational stories: the Challenge Plot, the Connection Plot, and the Creativity Plot. Each type inspires a different kind of action. The most effective communicators learn to spot these story types in the world around them rather than trying to invent them from scratch.

## Key Findings

- **Chicken Soup and People analysis:** The Heaths analyzed hundreds of inspirational stories from the Chicken Soup for the Soul series and People magazine profiles of non-celebrities. Three basic plots -- Challenge, Connection, Creativity -- classified over 80% of them
- **Aristotle to modern screenwriting:** Aristotle identified four primary dramatic plots; Robert McKee lists twenty-five types. The Heaths' three-plot framework is narrower because it focuses specifically on inspirational stories, not all dramatic narratives
- **Jared Fogle as the complete sticky story:** Jared's story scored on every element of the SUCCESs checklist -- Simple (eat subs, lose weight), Unexpected (lose weight eating fast food), Concrete (oversized pants, specific sandwiches), Credible (antiauthority), Emotional (individual, not statistic), Story (Challenge plot). The competing "7 Under 6" campaign scored on almost none and was merely a modest success
- **Spotting vs. creating:** The Jared story was not invented -- it was spotted by franchise owner Bob Ocwieja, pursued by creative director Richard Coad, and produced for free by ad agency president Barry Krause. The chain of spotting, championing, and executing required multiple acts of courage and judgment

### The Three Plot Types

#### The Challenge Plot
- **Pattern:** A protagonist overcomes formidable obstacles through perseverance and courage
- **Archetypes:** David and Goliath, rags-to-riches, underdog stories, triumph of willpower over adversity
- **The key element:** The obstacles must seem daunting to the protagonist. Jared losing 245 pounds is a Challenge plot; his neighbor shaving an inch is not
- **Examples:** Rose Blumkin (Russian immigrant who built a $100 million furniture business from $500), the 1980 U.S. Olympic hockey team, Rosa Parks, Jared Fogle, the Alamo, Horatio Alger tales
- **Emotional response:** Makes us want to work harder, take on new challenges, overcome obstacles. Challenge plots inspire perseverance and courage
- **Best used for:** Project kickoffs, rallying teams facing difficult goals, situations requiring sustained effort

#### The Connection Plot
- **Pattern:** A relationship develops that bridges a gap -- racial, class, ethnic, religious, demographic, or otherwise
- **Archetype:** The Good Samaritan -- a despised outsider (Samaritan) helps a wounded traveler when respectable authorities (priest, Levite) pass by. Modern equivalent: an "atheist biker gang member" stopping to help
- **Examples:** Mean Joe Greene Coke commercial (scrawny white fan and towering black athlete connected by a Coke), Romeo and Juliet, Titanic
- **Emotional response:** Makes us want to help others, be more tolerant, work with others, love others. Connection plots inspire social behavior
- **The most common kind of plot in Chicken Soup for the Soul**
- **Best used for:** Team building, company celebrations, any situation requiring social cohesion and cooperation

#### The Creativity Plot
- **Pattern:** Someone makes a mental breakthrough, solves a long-standing puzzle, or attacks a problem in an innovative way
- **Archetype:** Newton's apple falling on his head; the MacGyver plot
- **Examples:** Ingersoll-Rand's Grinder Team "Drag Test" (tying plastic and metal samples to a rental car bumper instead of running months of lab tests), Shackleton grouping whiners in his own tent to neutralize them
- **Emotional response:** Makes us want to do something different, be creative, experiment with new approaches. Creativity plots inspire innovation
- **Best used for:** Culture change initiatives, innovation programs, situations requiring people to break from established routines

### Springboard Stories

- Coined by Stephen Denning at the World Bank, a springboard story is a specific type of story that lets people see how an existing problem might change
- Denning's Zambia story (a health worker in rural Africa using the CDC website to fight malaria) served as a springboard for the idea of knowledge management -- each listener imagined how the principle applied to their own context
- **Key advantages:** Springboard stories combat skepticism by engaging the audience's internal critic, giving it something constructive to do. They are exercises in mass customization -- each listener launches from the story to a slightly different destination
- **Denning's insight:** "Don't ignore the little voice [inside the listener's head]. Work in harmony with it. Engage it by giving it something to do. Tell a story in a way that elicits a second story from the little voice"

## Mechanisms

1. **Emotional resonance:** Each plot type activates a specific emotional response -- perseverance (Challenge), compassion (Connection), or creativity (Creativity) -- that primes the audience for a corresponding type of action
2. **Identity modeling:** Inspirational stories implicitly model behavior. When we hear about Jared, we imagine ourselves capable of similar transformation. When we hear about the Good Samaritan, we imagine ourselves acting with similar compassion. This connects to James March's identity model: "What would someone like me do?"
3. **Motivation to act:** While [[story-as-simulation]] teaches us how to act, inspiration gives us the will. Both are needed for action -- knowledge without motivation leads to passivity; motivation without knowledge leads to floundering
4. **Defeating the Curse of Knowledge:** Stories naturally embody most of the SUCCESs framework (they are almost always Concrete, usually Emotional and Unexpected). Conference presenters who distilled their experiences into abstract maxims like "Keep the lines of communication open" were furious when Klein's team extracted their stories instead -- but the stories were far more useful than the abstractions

## Applications

- **Leadership communication:** Match the plot type to your goal. Challenge plots for project kickoffs. Connection plots for team celebrations. Creativity plots for culture change
- **Story spotting:** The most powerful stories do not need to be invented -- they need to be spotted. Train frontline employees to recognize when life hands them a Jared or a Good Samaritan or a Drag Test, and create channels for these stories to surface
- **Organizational change:** Denning's springboard story technique is specifically designed for change management -- tell a story that lets people imagine a better future and figure out for themselves how to get there
- **Persuasive communication:** Stories defeat analytical resistance. When Denning made arguments about knowledge management, people fought back. When he told the Zambia story, executives raced up afterward with implementation ideas. The story had become their idea
- **Content selection:** Know which plot type you need and filter for it. Not every interesting story serves your purpose. A Connection plot before a battle would be a mismatch; use a Challenge plot instead

## Open Questions

- Are there cultural variations in which plot types resonate most strongly? Do Challenge plots work better in individualistic cultures and Connection plots in collectivist ones?
- How do the three plot types map onto other narrative taxonomies (Aristotle's four plots, McKee's twenty-five, Booker's seven basic plots)?
- Can artificial or obviously manufactured stories still inspire, or does the audience need to believe the story is true for the inspirational effect to hold?
