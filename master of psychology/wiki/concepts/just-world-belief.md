# Belief in a Just World

**Phase(s):** 1
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

The belief in a just world is the assumption that **people get what they deserve and deserve what they get** — bad things happen to bad people, good things happen to good people. A defensive attribution that protects the believer's sense that the world is safe, orderly, and predictable.

## Key Findings

- Lerner (1980, 1998): people blame victims of crimes and accidents to maintain the illusion that such tragedies couldn't happen to them ([[summary-1-03-social-psychology-ch4]])
- Victims of rape and domestic abuse are often seen as contributing to their own fate
- More common in cultures with extreme wealth inequality (Dalbert & Yamauchi, 1994)
- Connected to the concept of karma in Hindu/Buddhist traditions — good moral behavior is rewarded, bad actions punished (White et al., 2017)

## Applications

- **Persuasion:** "Success stories" implicitly activate just-world beliefs — if this person succeeded, they must have done something right, and you can too. Powerful in marketing.
- **Understanding resistance to systemic change:** Just-world beliefs lead people to see inequality as deserved, creating resistance to redistributive policies or structural interventions.
