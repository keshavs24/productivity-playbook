# High-Arousal Emotions

**Phase(s):** 6
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

High-arousal emotions are emotions that activate the body's physiological arousal system — increasing heart rate, blood pressure, and readiness for action. [[berger]] discovered that the critical variable determining whether emotional content gets shared is not whether the emotion is positive or negative, but whether it is **high-arousal** (activating) or **low-arousal** (deactivating). High-arousal emotions drive sharing; low-arousal emotions suppress it. This is the third principle of the [[stepps-framework]]: "When we care, we share."

## The Arousal Framework

| | HIGH AROUSAL (Drives Sharing) | LOW AROUSAL (Suppresses Sharing) |
|---|---|---|
| **POSITIVE** | Awe, Excitement, Amusement/Humor | Contentment |
| **NEGATIVE** | Anger, Anxiety, Disgust | Sadness |

## Key Findings

- **Awe-inspiring articles were 30% more likely to make the *New York Times* Most E-Mailed list**, even after controlling for interest, usefulness, and positioning. Science articles disproportionately make the list because they frequently evoke awe.
- **Sadder articles were 16% *less* likely to make the Most E-Mailed list.** Sadness is low-arousal and deactivates rather than activates.
- **Anger and anxiety *increased* sharing**, contradicting the simple positive-good, negative-bad model. Articles about Wall Street fat cats getting hefty bonuses (anger) or the stock market tanking (anxiety) were more likely to be shared.
- **Physical exercise increases sharing even when it has nothing to do with the content.** Students who jogged for 60 seconds shared an article at nearly twice the rate (75%) of those who sat still. Any physiological arousal — emotional or physical — can boost transmission.
- **Funny content goes viral because amusement is high-arousal.** "David After Dentist," "Charlie Bit My Finger," and "Charlie the Unicorn" collectively accumulated 600+ million views.
- **Dave Carroll's "United Breaks Guitars"** song went viral (3+ million views in ten days) because viewer anger at United's treatment of a customer was highly arousing. United's stock dropped 10% ($180M equivalent).
- **BMW's *The Hire* film series** evoked fear and anxiety (kidnappings, FBI raids, near-death experiences) — high-arousal negative emotions — and received 11+ million views, increasing BMW sales by 12%.

## Mechanisms

**Physiological arousal** is a state of activation and readiness for action. Evolutionarily, it comes from the fight-or-flight response. When aroused:
- Heart beats faster, blood pressure rises
- Muscles tense, senses heighten
- People feel compelled to *do something* — pump fists, pace, wring hands, share content

High-arousal emotions kindle this fire. They activate people and drive them to take action, including talking about and sharing content. Low-arousal emotions have the opposite effect — they deactivate. Sad people curl up on the couch; content people relax. Neither state promotes action or sharing.

The arousal effect is not specific to emotional arousal. **Any source of physiological activation** — exercise, a scary movie, a turbulent plane ride, an exciting game show — can spill over and increase sharing of whatever content is encountered during or shortly after the arousal.

### Practical Implications of Arousal Spillover
- Ads during exciting TV shows or sports games should get more sharing
- People at the gym are more likely to share content on their phones
- Work groups may benefit from taking walks together — the physical activation encourages sharing of ideas
- People on turbulent flights or treadmills may overshare personal information
- Finding people when they are already fired up is one way to generate word of mouth

## Applications

- **On the positive side:** Excite people, inspire them, show them they can make a difference. Make the polar bear story get them fired up, not sad.
- **On the negative side:** Make people mad, not sad. Anxiety and anger drive more sharing than melancholy or disappointment.
- **Focus on feelings, not features.** Use the "Three Whys" technique to drill past features to the emotional core: Why is online search important? To find information quickly. Why? To connect with people. Why? To achieve goals and fulfill dreams. *Now* it's emotional.
- **Monitor for high-arousal negative language** ("pissed off," "angry," "mad") to identify potential viral negative word of mouth before it snowballs.
- **Simply adding more arousal to content boosts sharing.** Making an ad funnier or an article more anger-provoking or a story more anxiety-inducing all increase transmission.
