# Hedonic Editing

**Phase(s):** 2
**Confidence:** 0.7

## Definition

Hedonic editing refers to the rules people use (or should use) to mentally combine or separate outcomes in order to maximize subjective well-being, given the shape of the [[value-function]] from [[prospect-theory]]. Because the value function is concave for gains and convex for losses, the way outcomes are grouped or separated affects their total perceived value.

## Key Findings

- **Four hedonic editing rules** derived from the value function ([[summary-2-07-cvf-ch14-mental-accounting-matters]]):
  1. **Segregate gains:** Two separate gains feel better than one combined gain of the same total (concavity). Give gifts over time, not all at once.
  2. **Integrate losses:** One combined loss feels less bad than two separate losses of the same total (convexity). Pay bills in a lump sum when possible.
  3. **Integrate small losses with larger gains:** The "silver lining" -- a small loss is absorbed by a larger gain with minimal pain.
  4. **Segregate small gains from larger losses:** A small gain provides disproportionate joy even alongside a large loss (the "silver lining" principle).
- **Empirical test (Thaler & Johnson, 1990):** People spontaneously apply Rules 1 and 2 more consistently than Rules 3 and 4. The theory describes normative hedonic editing better than descriptive editing.
- **Practical applications:** Compensation design (annual bonuses as segregated gains), marketing (breaking discounts into components), pricing (bundling costs), and gift-giving strategy.

## Mechanisms

The hedonic editing rules follow directly from the mathematical properties of the [[value-function]]:
- **Concavity for gains** means v(x) + v(y) > v(x + y) when both are positive, so segregating gains increases total subjective value
- **Convexity for losses** means v(-x) + v(-y) < v(-x - y) when both are negative, so integrating losses reduces total subjective pain
- **[[loss-aversion]]** means a small loss paired with a larger gain is best absorbed into the gain, since the marginal pain of the loss exceeds the marginal pleasure of the equivalent gain

## Applications

- **Gift-giving:** Multiple small gifts over time beat one large gift of the same total value
- **Compensation:** Annual bonuses (segregated gain) feel better than equivalent salary increases (integrated into existing income stream)
- **Bad news delivery:** Deliver all bad news at once (integrate losses) rather than spread it out
- **Pricing:** Bundle costs into a single payment rather than itemizing; highlight each benefit separately
