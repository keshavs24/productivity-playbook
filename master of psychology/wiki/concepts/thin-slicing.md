# Thin-Slicing

**Phase(s):** 1, 4, 7
**Confidence:** 0.5

## Definition

Thin-slicing is drawing meaningful conclusions about another person's personality, abilities, or skills **based on an extremely brief sample of behavior** — often just seconds of observation. The term was introduced by Nalini Ambady and Robert Rosenthal.

## Key Findings

- First impressions form in **under 100 milliseconds** from facial appearance alone (Bar, Neta, & Linz, 2006; Willis & Todorov, 2006) ([[summary-1-03-social-psychology-ch4]])
- Children as young as 3 show this tendency (Cogsdill et al., 2014)
- **Teacher study:** Thin-sliced impressions from 10-second silent video clips of teachers correlated significantly with end-of-semester evaluations. Even 6-second clips worked. Brief, nonverbal impressions predicted months of experience.
- **Baby-face effect:** Childlike features (big eyes, small chin, high forehead) trigger perceptions of warmth, naivety, submissiveness
- **Political prediction:** First-impression ratings of candidates' powerfulness and warmth predicted actual election outcomes (Rule & Ambady, 2010)

## Mechanisms

Thin-slicing works because nonverbal cues carry genuine information about personality traits, emotional states, and social orientation. The rapid impression draws on evolved pattern-recognition that detects trustworthiness, dominance, and competence from physical and behavioral signals. But thin-slicing also activates schemas and stereotypes, meaning it can be both informative and misleading.

## Applications

- **Presentations/Speaking (Phase 7):** The opening seconds of any presentation set the audience's impression. Nonverbal confidence cues in the first thin-slice determine how the content is received.
- **Job interviews:** Handshake quality, dress, eye contact, and posture in the first seconds shape the entire evaluation. Impression management in these moments is disproportionately valuable.
- **Sales:** The first few seconds of a customer interaction set the frame. Thin-sliced competence and warmth predict persuasion success.
- **Negotiation (Phase 5):** First impressions of the counterpart form immediately and persist through [[belief-perseverance]] — making pre-meeting preparation of one's nonverbal presentation critical.
